# 9. Global Context — India's CBDC vs The World

## Global CBDC Landscape (as of 2025)

- **130+ countries** exploring CBDCs (representing 98% of global GDP).
- Only a handful have launched: Bahamas, Nigeria, Jamaica, Eastern Caribbean.
- Major economies (US, EU, UK, Japan) remain in research/exploration phases.
- India is arguably **the most ambitious democratic CBDC experimenter** given its 1.4 billion population scale.

---

## Country-by-Country Comparison

### China — Digital Yuan (e-CNY)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Status** | Most advanced large-country CBDC pilot globally |
| **Launch** | Pilot began April 2020 |
| **Cumulative transactions** | Exceeded 7 trillion yuan (~$1 trillion) by mid-2024 |
| **Wallets opened** | ~260 million |
| **Integration** | Integrated into WeChat Pay and Alipay ecosystems |
| **Use cases** | Government stimulus, public transit, retail, salary payments |
| **Adoption challenge** | Despite massive government push, most Chinese consumers still prefer Alipay/WeChat Pay |

**Key lesson for India:** Even with aggressive state promotion and integration into dominant platforms, replacing well-established digital payment habits is extremely difficult.

---

### Nigeria — eNaira

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Status** | Widely regarded as a **cautionary tale** |
| **Launch** | October 2021 |
| **Adoption** | Less than 2% of Nigerians adopted eNaira by 2023 |
| **Approach** | Aggressive — included limiting cash withdrawals to push digital adoption |
| **Failure reasons** | Poor UX, distrust of government surveillance, well-functioning mobile money ecosystem (private players) |

**Key lesson for India:** Coercive adoption strategies backfire. Trust and genuine value proposition are essential.

---

### Bahamas — Sand Dollar

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Status** | First country to launch a CBDC (October 2020) |
| **Population** | ~400,000 (very small scale) |
| **Adoption** | Modest — most Bahamians still prefer cash or card |
| **Value** | Useful for island communities with limited banking access |

**Key lesson for India:** Financial inclusion is a legitimate CBDC use case, but it alone may not drive mass adoption.

### Jamaica — JAM-DEX

| Aspect | Details |

## India's Unique Position

### Strengths
1. **World's best real-time payment system (UPI)** as infrastructure foundation — no other CBDC country has this advantage.
2. **Massive digital identity system (Aadhaar)** — enables seamless KYC for CBDC wallets.
3. **Large-scale digital payments experience** — RBI and banks have deep operational expertise.
4. **Population scale** — 1.4 billion people; if successful, India's CBDC becomes a global reference model.
5. **Strong central bank** — RBI has credibility and institutional capacity to execute.

### Challenges (Unique to India)
1. **UPI's dominance is paradoxically the biggest obstacle** — the very success that positions India well also means there's less unmet demand for a new digital payment method.
2. **Diverse population** — from tech-savvy metro youth to rural elderly with feature phones; one-size-fits-all won't work.
3. **Connectivity gaps** — offline functionality is critical but technically challenging.
4. **Privacy expectations** — India's citizenry is increasingly privacy-aware post-Aadhaar debates.

### Key Insight
> **No large democracy has achieved mass CBDC adoption.** Even China's massive push has yielded modest usage relative to existing digital payment systems. India's cautious approach may actually be the wisest strategy.
