# 10. Future Roadmap

## RBI's Stated Priorities (in order)

### 1. Full UPI Interoperability
- **Status:** Partially live
- Complete integration so e-₹ wallets can transact at **any UPI QR code merchant** seamlessly.
- This is the most immediate catalyst for adoption — leveraging UPI's 100+ million merchant network.

### 2. Offline Functionality
- **Status:** Pilot/experimental
- NFC or Bluetooth-based solutions for transactions without internet.
- Critical for India's **rural and semi-urban population** that still faces connectivity issues.
- Testing with NFC-enabled smart cards (metro card-like form factor).
- Key technical challenge: preventing double-spending offline.

### 3. Programmable Money / Smart Contracts
- **Status:** Under active exploration
- Priority use cases:
  - **Government subsidy disbursement** — purpose-bound tokens (fertilizer, LPG, education)
  - **Conditional payments** — escrow, milestone-based releases
  - **Time-bound vouchers** — expiring stimulus tokens
  - **Automated insurance payouts** — triggered by verified events

### 4. Structured Anonymity
- **Status:** Not yet implemented
- Implementing cash-like anonymity for **small-value transactions** (possibly below ₹10,000 or ₹50,000 threshold).
- Larger transactions subject to standard KYC/AML oversight.
- This could be a genuine differentiator from UPI (where banks see every transaction).

### 5. Cross-Border Payments
- **Status:** Active participation in international initiatives
- **Project mBridge** — multi-CBDC platform with China, Hong Kong, Thailand, UAE, Saudi Arabia.
- Bilateral arrangements with UAE and other trade partners.
- Could dramatically reduce **remittance costs** (from 4-5% to near-zero).
- Timeline: **medium-term (2026-2028)**.

### 6. Expanded Use Cases
- Government salary disbursement in e-₹
- Corporate treasury management
- Tokenized government bonds settled in e-₹
- IoT and machine-to-machine payments
- Smart city infrastructure payments

## Expected Full Rollout Timeline

| Phase | Estimated Timeline | Description |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| Current | 2022-2025 | Pilot phase — limited cities, banks, features |
| Near-term | 2025-2026 | UPI integration complete, offline pilot expansion, programmability trials |
| Medium-term | 2026-2028 | Cross-border CBDC pilots, structured anonymity, broader city coverage |
| Full rollout | 2028+ (estimated) | Nationwide availability, mature feature set |

> **RBI has not committed to a specific full-launch date.** Most analysts estimate full nationwide rollout is unlikely before 2027-2028 at the earliest.

> RBI's approach: *"We will expand when we are satisfied the design is right."*

## What Would Drive Mass Adoption?

Based on the friction analysis and global lessons, these factors could trigger widespread adoption:

1. **Killer use case that UPI can't serve** — most likely offline payments in rural areas or programmable government subsidies.
2. **Cashback/rewards** — incentivize first transactions (the UPI playbook that drove its adoption).
3. **Integration into existing apps** — e-₹ as a payment option within Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm (not separate apps).
4. **Government mandating certain payments in e-₹** — e.g., all DBT transfers in e-₹ (though this carries coercion risks — Nigeria lesson).
5. **Privacy advantage** — if structured anonymity for small transactions is genuinely implemented, privacy-conscious users may prefer e-₹ over UPI.
6. **Cross-border remittance** — if sending money from abroad via e-₹ is cheaper and faster, the diaspora could drive adoption.

---

## RBI's Guiding Philosophy

> *"The Indian approach to CBDC is calibrated and gradual. We want to learn from other countries' experiences, test thoroughly in our context, and ensure that the CBDC adds genuine value rather than duplicating existing infrastructure."*

This cautious, iterative approach — while slower — may prove wiser than the aggressive rollouts that have struggled elsewhere (Nigeria, even China to some extent).
