# 7. Friction & Challenges in India's CBDC System

## Why Hasn't Adoption Been Massive?

Despite the technological promise, India's CBDC has faced significant friction. Here's a deep dive into every major pain point.

---

## 1. Technical & UX Issues

### Separate App Required
- Users must download a **dedicated e-₹ wallet app** from their participating bank.
- This is a major barrier when UPI works seamlessly within existing apps (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, bank apps).
- Users don't want yet another payment app on their phone.

### Clunky Onboarding
- Wallet activation process is **more cumbersome** than near-instant UPI setup.
- Multiple steps: download app → register → OTP → link bank account → set PIN → load funds.
- UPI setup is far simpler and more familiar.

### Initial Lack of UPI Interoperability
- At launch, e-₹ could NOT interact with UPI merchants.
- Users needed merchants specifically onboarded for e-₹ — an extremely limited network.
- UPI QR code compatibility was introduced in late 2023, but real-world merchant adoption remained limited.

### Offline Functionality Gaps
- RBI has discussed offline capability (critical for rural India), but as of mid-2025 it is still in **experimental/pilot stages**, not broadly available.

---

## 2. Economic Disincentives

### No Interest on Holdings
- Money in e-₹ wallet earns **0% interest**.
- Money in a savings account earns **2.7-4% interest**.
- Rational users have no reason to park money in e-₹ when bank deposits are safer for savings and UPI provides the same payment convenience.

### No Cashback or Rewards
- UPI apps (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm) aggressively offer **cashbacks, rewards, scratch cards, and gamification**.
- e-₹ offered **virtually no such incentives** during the pilot.
- For the average consumer, there is no financial motivation to switch.

### Transaction Limits
- Wallet loading limits were relatively low during the pilot phase.
- No clear advantage over UPI's limits for everyday use.

---

## 3. Merchant Reluctance

- Merchants already have established UPI infrastructure — **no incentive to add another system**.
- Accepting e-₹ initially required separate integration or QR codes.
- No lower fees vs UPI (which is already zero-MDR for most transactions).
- Lack of awareness and training at the retail level.
- Merchants ask: "Why should I accept this when everyone uses UPI?"

---

## 4. The UPI Problem — CBDC's Biggest Competitor

This is India's unique challenge. Most countries exploring CBDC don't have a payment system as successful as UPI.

| Metric | UPI | e-₹ (CBDC) |
|--------|-----|------------|
| Monthly transactions | 14-16 billion | ~30,000-50,000/day |
| Users | 350+ million | ~5-6 million wallets |
| Merchants | 100+ million | ~400,000-500,000 |
| Setup time | Minutes | Longer, more steps |
| Cashback/rewards | Extensive | None |
| App ecosystem | Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, bank apps | Bank-specific wallet apps |

> e-₹'s ~30,000-50,000 daily transactions is approximately **0.006%** of UPI's daily volume — essentially a rounding error.

### The Core Question
For the average user in 2025, there is **almost no compelling reason** to choose e-₹ over UPI for everyday payments. The future differentiators (offline, programmability, privacy, cross-border) are not yet operational.

---

## 5. Privacy Concerns

### Surveillance Risk
- Unlike physical cash, **every e-₹ transaction creates a digital trail**.
- Civil liberties organizations have raised concerns about **mass financial surveillance**.
- The RBI can theoretically see all transactions — powers it doesn't have with physical cash.

### Centralized Control
- RBI could theoretically **freeze wallets, reverse transactions, or impose restrictions**.
- This "killswitch" capability, while useful for law enforcement, raises concerns about government overreach.

### Unfulfilled Privacy Promise
- RBI proposed "structured anonymity" — small-value transactions with cash-like privacy.
- **Not yet implemented in the pilot** — current implementation requires full KYC for all wallet holders.
- Trust remains low among privacy-conscious users.

### Data Protection Uncertainty
- India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (2023) provides a framework.
- Its **application to CBDC transaction data remains unclear**.

---

## 6. Pilot Fatigue

- Initial spike in wallet activations driven by bank promotion campaigns (some banks incentivized employees/customers).
- **Activity dropped significantly** after initial curiosity wore off.
- Many wallets are activated but dormant — "ghost wallets."
- Classic pattern: banks pushed staff to create wallets to meet targets, but no sustained usage followed.

---

## 7. Awareness Gap

- Most Indians have **never heard of the Digital Rupee** or don't understand what it is.
- Confusion with cryptocurrency, UPI, and digital banking.
- No significant public awareness campaign by the RBI or government.
- Even within pilot cities, awareness is extremely low outside of banking/fintech circles.

---

## Summary: Root Causes of Low Adoption

| Factor | Severity | Status |
|--------|----------|--------|
| UPI dominance (no compelling alternative value) | Critical | Structural challenge |
| No interest on holdings | High | By design — unlikely to change |
| No rewards/cashbacks | High | Could be addressed with incentive programs |
| Separate app requirement | High | Partially addressed via UPI interoperability |
| Limited merchant network | High | Improving with UPI QR compatibility |
| Offline not yet available | Medium | Under development |
| Privacy promises unfulfilled | Medium | Structured anonymity not yet implemented |
| Lack of public awareness | Medium | Could be addressed with marketing |
| Programmability not yet live | Medium | Under development |
