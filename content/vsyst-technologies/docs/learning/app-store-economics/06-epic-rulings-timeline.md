# 6. Epic Lawsuits — How the Store Rules Got Cracked Open (2020–2026)

Why this matters: the anti-steering rules quoted in [[04-apple-app-store-rules]] and [[05-google-play-store-rules]] were absolute until Epic Games sued both stores. The carve-outs (US link-outs, fee caps) all trace to these cases.

## The Trigger (August 2020)

Epic slipped a direct-payment option into **Fortnite** on iOS and Android (V-Bucks 20% cheaper). Both stores removed Fortnite within hours. Epic filed antitrust suits against both the same day.

## Epic v. Apple

| Date             | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Sep 2021         | Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers: Apple is **not** a monopolist under federal law, but its **anti-steering rules violate California law** → nationwide injunction: apps must be allowed to link out        |
| Jan 2024         | SCOTUS declines both appeals; injunction takes effect. Apple "complies" with a **27% fee on link-out purchases** + scare screens                                                                       |
| **Apr 30, 2025** | **Contempt ruling**: Rogers finds Apple willfully violated the injunction. Apple must allow US link-outs with **no fee, no scare screens**; executive referred for possible criminal contempt          |
| May 2025         | Apple updates US App Store rules; Spotify, Kindle, Patreon ship external purchase links within days                                                                                                    |
| Dec 2025         | Ninth Circuit **largely upholds** the contempt ruling, but says Apple may pursue a "reasonable commission" tied to external-link costs — framework remanded to the district court                      |
| Apr 2026         | Ninth Circuit reverses a stay in Epic's favor — the **zero-fee link-out regime stays in force** while the district court works out what (if any) fee Apple may charge; Apple can still petition SCOTUS |

**Net effect today:** US-storefront apps may freely link to external purchase; Apple currently collects **nothing** on those sales. Every other storefront (including **India**) still runs under the old strict no-steering rules.

## Epic v. Google

| Date            | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dec 2023        | **Jury verdict: Google Play is an illegal monopoly** (Google settled with US states separately)                                                                                                       |
| Oct 2024        | Judge James Donato's injunction: open Play's catalog to rival stores, allow external billing/links, stop anti-steering — stayed pending appeal                                                        |
| Jul 31, 2025    | Ninth Circuit affirms in full                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Oct 2025        | Supreme Court declines to stay → injunction becomes effective                                                                                                                                         |
| Nov 2025        | Google proposes compliance + settlement: capped service fees (9% / 20% tiers)                                                                                                                         |
| **Dec 9, 2025** | External payment links + alternative billing go **live for US Play apps**                                                                                                                             |
| **Mar 4, 2026** | **Epic–Google settlement**: Donato's US-only injunction becomes a **global framework through June 2032** — capped fees (≤20%, with a 9% tier), Play billing itself priced as a separate ~5% component |
| Jun–Jul 2026    | Google notifies developers: Play catalog will be shared with third-party US app stores unless they opt out by Jul 22, 2026                                                                            |

## Sidebar: Europe (DMA)

The EU's Digital Markets Act forced Apple to allow alternative app stores, sideloading, and link-outs in the EU (2024), with a €500M fine (Apr 2025) over anti-steering and a revised fee structure (Jun 2025). Google made parallel DMA changes. EU rules ≠ US rules ≠ rest-of-world rules — **the store rulebook is now regional**.

## The Meta-Lesson

Epic spent five years and enormous legal fees to win what Figma-style businesses always had for free: **the right to transact with their own customers off-device**. If the transaction can live on the web, put it there and skip the fight entirely (see [[02-how-figma-avoids-store-tax]]).
