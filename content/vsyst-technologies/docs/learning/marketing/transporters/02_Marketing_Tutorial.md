# Marketing Tutorial — From First Principles

**Purpose:** a crash course in marketing for founders who are technically sharp but new to marketing. Read once top-to-bottom; return to sections as you build. Every claim has a citation in `99_References.md`.

---

## Executive summary

1. Marketing is the system by which you identify who needs what you make, design an offer they will pay for, and make sure they find it, understand it, and pick it. Everything else is tactics.
2. The classic 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) extend to 7Ps for services. Your SaaS is a service; use the 7Ps.
3. STP (Segmentation → Targeting → Positioning) is the strategic layer above tactics. Do it before you spend money on anything.
4. Funnels organise content; flywheels describe compounding. B2B SaaS economics live in retention, not in the funnel.
5. India's Bharat SMB market breaks most imported playbooks. WhatsApp is a channel; regional-language content is non-negotiable; trust trumps features.
6. Typical newbie mistakes: vanity metrics, premature paid scale, ignoring retention, shipping a pretty website before distribution.

---

## 1. What marketing actually is

Marketing is **the system** by which a company identifies who needs what it makes, designs an offer they'll pay for, and ensures they can find it, understand it, and choose it over alternatives. Everything else is a sub-discipline.

Adjacent disciplines are **not** synonyms:

- **Sales** — one-to-one conversations that close a specific deal. Marketing creates conditions where sales scales.
- **Advertising** — paid media (Google, LinkedIn, print). One channel inside Promotion.
- **Branding** — the associations, visual language, and trust signals that live in a buyer's head when your name comes up. Brand is the long-term asset; marketing is the activity that builds and spends it.
- **PR** — earned third-party coverage.
- **Growth** — an operating model: experiments across the full funnel, blending product + marketing + data.

### 1.1 The 4Ps and 7Ps

**4Ps** (McCarthy, 1960): Product, Price, Place, Promotion. For a SaaS, Product is what you build; Price is subscription + usage; Place is your website, app stores, partners; Promotion is ads, content, PR, sales collateral ([Professional Academy](99_References.md#profacademy-4ps-7ps); [Meltwater](99_References.md#meltwater-marketing-mix)).

**7Ps** (Booms & Bitner, 1981) adds three for services:

- **People** — humans who deliver the service (reps, CS, implementation)
- **Process** — the actual steps a customer experiences (onboarding, support SLAs)
- **Physical evidence** — tangible proof it works (dashboards, reports, case studies, branded invoices, office)

For DZZLO, the 7Ps matter more than the 4Ps. An Indian transporter is buying a *relationship* alongside a product.

### 1.2 STP — Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning

Philip Kotler's classic strategic layer ([Adobe STP](99_References.md#adobe-stp); [Smart Insights STP](99_References.md#smartinsights-stp)):

1. **Segmentation** — cut the market into coherent groups (geographic, demographic, firmographic, behavioural; for B2B add industry, company size, tech stack, buying role).
2. **Targeting** — pick which to pursue. Kotler's **DAMP** filter: Distinctive, Accessible, Measurable, Profitable.
3. **Positioning** — decide the mental slot you want to occupy, relative to alternatives.

Positioning deserves its own doc. See `07_Positioning_and_Messaging.md`.

### 1.3 Funnels and flywheels

**Funnel — TOFU / MOFU / BOFU**:

- **TOFU** — awareness; doesn't know you exist. Content: blogs, SEO, LinkedIn posts, webinars.
- **MOFU** — consideration; has a problem, comparing. Content: comparison guides, case studies, calculators.
- **BOFU** — decision; validating shortlist. Content: demos, pricing, trials, references.

([HubSpot](99_References.md#hubspot-b2b-funnel); [SmartBug](99_References.md#smartbug-tofu-mofu-bofu))

**Flywheel critique (HubSpot):** the funnel ends when a customer buys. In SaaS the happy renewing, expanding, referring customer is where the economics live. Flywheel replaces funnel: Attract → Engage → Delight → customers become force that attracts new customers.

For DZZLO — a two-sided network of dealers and transporters — the flywheel framing is critical. Word-of-mouth between pump owners *is* the growth channel.

### 1.4 AARRR / Pirate Metrics

Dave McClure's 2007 framework ([Inc. AARRR](99_References.md#inc-aarrr); [PostHog AARRR](99_References.md#posthog-aarrr)):

- **Acquisition** — did a user arrive?
- **Activation** — did they have a good first experience?
- **Retention** — do they come back?
- **Referral** — do they tell others?
- **Revenue** — are they paying?

For Indian SMB SaaS, **Retention is the north star** — churn is the silent killer of all your CAC.

## 2. How to curate a marketing strategy — the founder's workflow

Ten steps; do them in sequence.

### Step 1: Situation analysis

- **SWOT** — internal Strengths/Weaknesses, external Opportunities/Threats
- **PESTLE** — macro forces (Political, Economic, Social, Tech, Legal, Environmental)
- **Porter's Five Forces** — threat of new entrants, buyer power, supplier power, substitutes, rivalry

([Vanderbilt on SWOT/PESTLE/Porter](99_References.md#vanderbilt-swot-pestle-porter); [Texila](99_References.md#texila-strategy-review))

### Step 2: Define the ICP

An ICP is the *company* most likely to buy, extract value, stay, refer. Not a persona. For DZZLO, this is covered in `04_Target_Customer_Transporters.md`.

### Step 3: Jobs-to-be-Done

For each ICP, answer: *"When [situation], they want to [motivation], so they can [expected outcome]."* The customer isn't buying SaaS; they're buying the outcome it enables ([Strategyzer](99_References.md#strategyzer-vpc); [Innodyn JTBD](99_References.md#innodyn-jtbd)).

### Step 4: Value Proposition Canvas

Alexander Osterwalder's two-panel tool. Right side: Customer Jobs / Pains / Gains. Left side: Products / Pain Relievers / Gain Creators. **Fit** = every pain on the right has a pain-reliever on the left. If you can't draw the lines, you have a product not a value prop.

### Step 5: Positioning statement

Moore's formula — see `07_Positioning_and_Messaging.md` for the DZZLO version. Dunford's update: positioning is *five deliberate choices*, not a template ([Dunford](99_References.md#lenny-dunford-b2b-positioning); [Heinz 10-step](99_References.md#heinz-10-step-positioning)).

### Step 6: Messaging hierarchy

Top: brand promise. Middle: 3–5 pillars. Bottom: proof per pillar ([Wynter](99_References.md#wynter-messaging-hierarchy); [CopyGuide](99_References.md#copyguide-messaging-hierarchy)).

### Step 7: Channel selection

2–3 channels max. For Indian B2B SaaS targeting SMB, candidates:

- Content / SEO (slow, compounds)
- Paid search / social (fast, expensive, needs PMF)
- Outbound (WhatsApp, LinkedIn, calls)
- Field sales (mandatory for Bharat SMB)
- Events / associations (dealer associations, AIMTC state chapters)
- Partnerships (OMCs, fleet-card issuers, tyre brands, lubricants)
- Community / WhatsApp groups

Lenny Rachitsky's study of 30 B2B SaaS GTMs: content / SEO wins long-term for durable growth ([Lenny GTM motions](99_References.md#lenny-gtm-motions)).

### Step 8: Budget allocation

~70% into one proven channel, ~20% into a second promising one, ~10% into experiments. Do not spread thin. Do not budget paid media until retention is healthy.

### Step 9: OKRs and KPIs

- **OKRs** — 2–3 quarterly Objectives, each with 3–4 Key Results
- **KPIs** — continuous operational metrics (MQLs, SQLs, CAC, NRR, GRR)

([Pipedrive OKRs](99_References.md#pipedrive-marketing-okrs); [Product School OKRs](99_References.md#productschool-marketing-okrs))

### Step 10: Measurement plan

Instrument before launch. Minimum: site analytics, CRM with agreed stage definitions, product analytics for activation/retention, attribution tagging, weekly dashboard founder reads.

## 3. B2B vs B2C — why the playbook differs

- **Longer sales cycles** — weeks to many months. Nurture, not impulse.
- **Multi-stakeholder buying committees** — Gartner benchmarks ~11 people average. Champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, end-user, procurement gate ([Zoominfo ABM](99_References.md#zoominfo-abm); [Buying Committee](99_References.md#smarketers-buying-committee)).
- **Smaller TAM, higher ACV.**
- **Rational + emotional** — B2B buyers are rational about features, emotional about career risk.
- **Content-led demand gen** — rarely convert a stranger first-click ([Revenue Inc](99_References.md#revenue-inc-demand-gen)).
- **ABM** — instead of casting wide, pick a named 20–200 target accounts and coordinate marketing + sales.
- **Bottom-up (PLG) vs top-down (SLG)** — pure bottom-up is hard in Indian fuel retail; top-down via OMC / dealer association, with bottom-up hook for attendants, is more realistic.

## 4. B2B SaaS playbooks specifically

### 4.1 PLG vs SLG vs hybrid

- **PLG** — product as primary acquisition; freemium / free-trial; self-serve. Best when value is obvious in minutes.
- **SLG** — quota-carrying reps run the cycle; demos, proposals, contracts.
- **Hybrid / Product-Led Sales** — freemium lands a user, sales expands to paid team (Notion, Atlassian). Virtually 100% of mature PLG companies add sales eventually.

([ProductLed PLG vs SLG](99_References.md#productled-plg-vs-slg); [McKinsey PLG](99_References.md#mckinsey-plg))

For DZZLO's Indian SMB profile: **SLG-primary with PLG-assist**.

### 4.2 Freemium and land-and-expand

- **Freemium** — permanent free tier that generates top-of-funnel volume. Risky if free users cost more to serve than they return.
- **Land-and-expand** — start narrow (one outlet, one module), then expand. Measured by **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)** — the single most important SaaS metric after PMF.

### 4.3 The SaaS metric set

([RevPartners cheat sheet](99_References.md#revpartners-saas-metrics); [High Alpha 2025](99_References.md#highalpha-benchmarks-2025); [a16z 11 GTM metrics](99_References.md#a16z-11-gtm-metrics))

- **GRR** — revenue kept from existing customers excluding expansion. World-class ≥95%, healthy ≥90%.
- **NRR** — GRR + expansion. World-class ≥120%, healthy ≥110%.
- **Rule of 40** — growth % + EBITDA margin % ≥ 40.
- **CAC Payback** — months to recover CAC. Target <12; early-stage median ~8.
- **Magic Number** — (ΔARR × 4) / prior-quarter S&M. >0.75 green, >1 excellent.
- **LTV:CAC** — target ≥3:1.

### 4.4 Foundational reading

- April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* — clearest positioning book for B2B tech. Read first.
- Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* — why early adopters and pragmatists behave differently; pick a beachhead ([B2U](99_References.md#b2u-crossing-chasm); [Wikipedia](99_References.md#wiki-crossing-chasm)).
- Lenny Rachitsky's newsletter — weekly reader.
- a16z SaaS/GTM library — especially [11 Key GTM Metrics](99_References.md#a16z-11-gtm-metrics).

## 5. Indian B2B context — marketing to SMB / unorganised sector

This section is where most imported Western playbooks break.

### 5.1 Bharat vs India divide

India has ~950M internet users, ~half from Tier 2/3 towns; ~98% consume content in Indian languages; UPI adoption >75% even in Tier-3. The next 200M digital buyers are not English-first, not metro-based, not procurement-committee-driven ([Rahul Malodia Bharat](99_References.md#rahul-malodia-bharat); [Adgully Bharat](99_References.md#adgully-bharat); [Medianews4u](99_References.md#medianews4u-regional)).

### 5.2 How Indian SMB decides

- **Trust is gating, not features.** Peer referrals beat any landing page.
- **Experts > ads.** Indian B2B buyers trust experts, peer groups, associations more than branded ads ([Tensai India B2B](99_References.md#tensai-india-b2b)).
- **Price sensitivity is structural.** Why many Indian B2B SaaS pivot abroad for dollar subscriptions. Plan lower ACVs, annual prepay, obsessive ROI-per-rupee messaging ([YourStory SME India](99_References.md#yourstory-b2b-sme)).
- **Vernacular content is not a nice-to-have.**

### 5.3 WhatsApp is the channel

84% of Indian SMBs say WhatsApp helps them communicate; 80% say it helps them grow. Fuel retail already uses it natively. For DZZLO, WhatsApp is the **primary** acquisition, onboarding, and support channel — not email ([WhatsApp Business](99_References.md#whatsapp-business); [The Ken WhatsApp](99_References.md#ken-whatsapp-india); [Indian Retailer WhatsApp](99_References.md#indian-retailer-whatsapp)).

### 5.4 Field sales and the dealer network

Feet-on-street is mandatory. Inside sales works for lower ACV; larger dealer groups and OMC partnerships require field. Build:

- A **channel-partner layer** — pump equipment vendors, automation installers, CAs serving pump owners, lubricant distributors, tyre dealers
- **Dealer / transporter associations** — one keynote at an AGM > 10,000 LinkedIn impressions
- **Reference accounts** — one well-known dealer group = your best demo

## 6. Newbie pitfalls to avoid

1. **Pretty website before distribution.** Ship a functional one-pager; spend the time on the first 10 customer conversations.
2. **Vanity metrics.** Impressions, followers, likes — replace with activated-user count, paid logos, NRR, CAC payback ([MarTech vanity](99_References.md#martech-vanity); [Improvado vanity](99_References.md#improvado-vanity)).
3. **Premature scaling of paid.** Ads amplify demand; they don't create it. Fix retention (≥30% user retention, steady organic pull) before scaling spend ([ScaleWithFuture](99_References.md#scalewithfuture-paid)).
4. **Ignoring retention.** SaaS leaky-bucket destroys acquisition faster than weak acquisition does.
5. **Too many channels at once.** Two done well > seven done badly.
6. **Features vs benefits.** "Real-time reconciliation" is a feature; "you stop losing ₹40,000/month" is a benefit. Buyers pay for the second.
7. **No positioning.** "AI-powered platform for fuel retailers" tells a buyer nothing.
8. **Over-indexing on content, under-indexing on distribution.** For every hour writing, spend an hour distributing.
9. **Treating marketing as a one-shot launch.** Launches are events; marketing is a compounding system.
10. **Sales-marketing misalignment.** Write the MQL/SQL SLA.
11. **Copying US playbooks into Bharat SMB.** A gated English whitepaper dies where a 2-minute Hindi reel on WhatsApp wins.
12. **Founder disengagement.** In the first 100 customers, the founder *is* the marketer.

## 7. Recommended learning path for a founder new to marketing

**Week 1 (foundation, 5 hrs):**
- Read this doc, twice.
- Read April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (3-hour read).

**Week 2–3 (frameworks, 6 hrs):**
- Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (summary is fine; full book optional).
- Work through Value Proposition Canvas for DZZLO with your co-founder.

**Month 2 (applied, 10 hrs):**
- Lenny Rachitsky — GTM motions of 30 B2B SaaS companies ([Lenny GTM motions](99_References.md#lenny-gtm-motions)).
- a16z — Fintech Scales Vertical SaaS ([a16z](99_References.md#a16z-fintech-scales-vsaas)).

**Quarterly ritual:**
- 1 hour reading [Bessemer State of the Cloud](99_References.md#bessemer-state-of-cloud-2024).
- 1 hour reviewing your own CAC, NRR, GRR, Rule of 40.

**Total: ~25 hours to go from "new to marketing" to "literate founder-marketer."**
