Enter the [PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/) method, introduced by Tiago Forte. This system, outlined in his book _The PARA Method: Simplify , Organize and Master your Digital Life_, is the simple structure I needed when it comes to organizing my digital life. PARA stands for Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives. The beauty of this method is that it works across any file or document storage system, making it perfect for someone like me who’s constantly experimenting with different tools. The best part? It’s straight forward, which is probably why it works so well for me.

![[PARA_method.png]]
**Projects**:

This folder holds anything I'm actively working on right now. Each project gets its own folder, and I usually give them descriptive titles - something like "Painting the Powder Room" or "Replanting the Backyard." Once the project is done, I'll either move some of the notes to Resources if they're worth keeping for future reference, or just archive the rest. Projects come and go, so this folder is constantly rotating.

**Areas**:

Unlike Projects, Areas are ongoing parts of my life that require consistent attention. These are more general and permanent. For example, I have an

"Areas" folder for Home, which breaks down into subfolders like Medical, Receipts, and even random stuff like recipe collections. Another big folder is Family, where I keep notes on outings, plans, and activities we want to do together. Personal wellness? Yep, that's here too, including subfolders for things like fitness and entertainment.

**Resources**:

This folder is like my digital library. It's where I keep everything that serves as a reference or a how-to guide. I've got sections for Technology, Business Definitions, Books to Read, Graphic Novels to Get, and even Science Reference Notes. If it's something I might want to refer back to in the future, it goes here. Resources are things that don't change often but are valuable long-term.

**Archives**:

The last folder, Archives, is where I stash all the completed projects. It's not exactly the most exciting folder, but it's nice to know that if I ever need to revisit something from a finished project, I can just dig into this folder. It's essentially a parking lot for anything I'm done with.
