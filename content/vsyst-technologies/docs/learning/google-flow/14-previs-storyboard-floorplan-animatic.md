## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

Before the robot crew arrives, you make the whole film out of paper. First, a map of each room from above, with a numbered X wherever a camera will stand. Then a comic strip, with one picture per shot and two when something moves. Then you play the strip on a timeline like a flip-book, reading the lines and buzzing your phone on the desk.

When the flip-book tells the story in 90 seconds, you write down which picture each crew gets, in what order, at what price. Each picture becomes the first frame a crew animates. The crews still forget everything after each shot, but the paper remembers, and every mistake it caught cost an eraser, not coins.

## 1. The One Idea — Previs Is the Film, Made Where Mistakes Are Free

**Previs** (previsualisation) is picturing scenes before making them, so staging and timing can be tested for free. In Flow no clip remembers the last ([[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] §1), so the plan is the only crew member with a memory. Each rung of the ladder catches a different mistake, and none costs a video coin:

| Rung | You make | It catches | Secures |
| ---- | -------- | ---------- | ------- |
| 1 Breakdown | a sheet per scene | a prop, state or sound nobody planned; the risky shots | causal |
| 2 Floor plan | a map per location | a flipped axis or eyeline; light from the wrong side | perceptual: space, light |
| 3 Boards + review | 48 stills | face, wardrobe and prop drift; jump cuts; mismatched singles | perceptual: identity, state |
| 4 Animatic | a timed reel with scratch sound | drag; lines too long for their shots; unmotivated cuts; a story that fails muted | emotional, sonic |
| 5 Locked list | method, length, tier per shot | anything still undecided | — |
| 6 Generation plan | an order and a price | coins spent before the hardest shot is proven | all four |

The last column names the track's four continuities ([[11-directors-track-roadmap]]). A mistake that slips past the paper costs ⏣10 to redraft on Lite, ⏣20 on Fast or ⏣100 on Quality, and the board still needs fixing.

Boards come from **Nano Banana inside Flow**, where only NB 2 Lite is stated free; Veo 3.1 Quality takes **no Ingredients**; Jump To is in no current Flow Help page; and Veo Frames clips run **4, 6 or 8 s** ([[11-directors-track-roadmap]] §8).

## 2. Rung 1 — The Scene Breakdown Sheet

A **scene breakdown sheet** is one page per scene listing every **element** it needs: "an object, person, or process" needed to produce it (StudioBinder). Crews file elements under cast, props, costumes, vehicles, effects, sound and music. Keep what a video model can get wrong, and add **risk**. Start from the scene card ([[12-story-engine-seamless-storytelling]] §5) and walk the shot list row by row.

**Risk tags** set each shot's draft count (§7) and its place in the shooting order (§8):

| Tag | Means | Why it costs drafts |
| --- | ----- | ------------------- |
| **TALK** | an on-camera line | lip-sync: Google DeepMind says consistent speech, "particularly for shorter speech segments, remains an area of active development"; *"Bas?"* is short |
| **TWO** | two people in one frame | identities can blend ([[16-directing-performance-and-dialogue-scenes]]) |
| **HAND** | hands doing one precise thing | a pen circling twice, a thumb tap |
| **MOVE** | travel that must go one way on screen | the tanker's direction carries story |
| **SCREEN** | a phone screen to be composited | needs a plain glow, never a drawn app |
| **MATCH** | a frame that must equal another | bookends, matched singles |
| **PHYS** | physics the model fumbles | a creeping phone, steam, ink |
| **HERO / PEAK** | a Quality face; the tightest shot | ⏣100 rides on it |

```
SCENE BREAKDOWN — Scene 2 "The Dispute"                         sheet 2 of 4
Day / place   D1 · 9 PM, continuous · LOC-A office; Kishan at the doorway, camera-right
Runtime       22 s · 8 generated shots (S2-01 … S2-08)
Turn          "I can settle this" → "my own register can't answer"
CAST + STATE  Ravi — STATE D1 · Kishan — identity line only · phone voice, off-screen
PROPS         three fat red cloth-bound registers — open, pages flipping
              black phone in a teal case — at his right ear, lowered in S2-07
              steel chai glass — full, cold, untouched · Casio calculator
VEHICLE       white fuel tanker, single blue stripe — idling, headlights on
LIGHT         LOOK "DZZLO night office" + the tanker's headlights behind Kishan
SOUND         room tone · thin phone voice · 3 lines · page flips · footsteps ·
              door click on the cut · engine idle from S2-02, carried into scene 3
IN / OUT      in: phone arriving at his ear (S1-06) · out: cold chai, engine, tanker
RISK          TALK S2-02 S2-03 S2-07 · HAND S2-03 S2-04 · TWO+MOVE S2-08 ·
              MATCH S2-06 · PEAK S2-07
```

## 3. Rung 2 — The Floor Plan

A **floor plan** (or overhead) is the location from above: walls, openings, the furniture that matters, people as dots with arrows for their gaze, the **axis** ([[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] §5.4), the lights, and each camera as a numbered mark pointing where it looks. In Flow it is the only place the space exists. Two rules make it readable:

1. **Draw every camera at the bottom of the page.** A camera looking up the page sees page-left as **frame-left**.
2. **For any other camera, turn the page until its arrow points up.** What crosses its view left-to-right crosses the screen left-to-right.

Camera numbers mean the same in both locations: **1** wide, **2** Ravi's single, **3** hands, **4** objects, **5** Kishan's single.

```
LOC-A · the pump office, from above · cameras at the bottom of the page

             back wall · SHELF (the registers end up here in scene 4)
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                  [8]→  ▓ window
   │     RAVI ●→ ══════════ axis ══════════ ←● KISHAN [6]→  ║ door
   │     ┌────────────────┐                                 │
   │     │☼ DESK  [3] [4] │                                 │
   │     └────────────────┘                                 │
   │                                                        │
   │              [5]↗                        [2]↖          │
   │    [7]↗                                                │
   │                      [2′]↖                             │
   │ [1]↗ high, in the corner                               │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        room side: cameras stay below the axis; 6 and 8 stand on a line
```

- **2 and 5 are crossed singles**: each sits near the *other* man's end of the line, so Ravi looks frame-right and Kishan frame-left, both near the lens.
- **6 and 8 stand on a line, looking out**: 6 at the door shows what Kishan sees (S2-06), 8 at the window what Ravi sees (S4-04). Both stand just inside the room, so the door frame or window frame shows at the edges of the shot. A shot along someone's gaze flips nobody.
- **The lamp ☼ is at the desk's left end and the window on the right wall**, so every eye-level night board is warm from frame-left and cool from frame-right.

```
LOC-B · the forecourt, from above · cameras at the bottom of the page

          WEST: the last sun, low, beyond the cabin and the pump
   ┌────────────── office cabin: the background of every LOC-B shot ────────────┐
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     ╔══════════════════════════════ white canopy ═════════════════════════════╗
       PUMP ── hose ─────────────────────────────┐
   exit ◄═══                          ▣ cab ═[fill port]═ TANKER ═══◄═══ entry
         RAVI ●→ ═════════ axis ═════════ ←● KISHAN
     ╚═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
          [3]↑     [5]↗            [2]↖          [4]↑
                          [1]↑ wide
          camera side (east): no camera ever stands above the axis
```

- **2 and 5 mirror each other** across the middle of the axis (same distance, height and lens). That symmetry makes S3-02/S3-03, S3-05/S3-06 and S3-09/S3-10 **matched singles**.
- **The plan decides where the cab stops.** The tanker rolls in leftward, cab first, and Kishan climbs down beside the cab. Had the cab rolled past Ravi's pump, Kishan would land on Ravi's *left* and every scene-3 eyeline would flip. So the nose stops short of the pump, and the hose reaches back to the fill port.
- **Every camera looks west into the low sun**: an amber rim on both men, as the dusk Look Sentence asks.

**Reading screen direction.** The tanker must arrive right-to-left (S3-01) and leave left-to-right (S4-04):

```
SITE PLAN · north is up ↑ · how the two locations sit

                                         entry
         ┌────────────────┐                │
         │  LOC-A office  ▓ window         ▼    tanker lane, one way, north → south
         │   desk   [8]→  ▓         ┌──────┴──────┐
         │                ║ door    │  LOC-B bay  │  ←[1] looks west
         │                ║         │   canopy    │
         └────────────────┘         └──────┬──────┘
                                           ▼  exit

   sun rises in the EAST → through the window: scene 4's key light
   sun sets in the WEST  → behind the office: scene 3's key from behind
```

Turn the page so camera 8 points up: north is on its left, so a north → south drive crosses **left to right**, leaving. Point LOC-B's camera 1 up: north is on its right, so the same drive crosses **right to left**, arriving. Move camera 1 to the office side and the arrival also reads left-to-right, losing the "in, then out" rhyme: free to spot on paper, ⏣40 of Lite and Fast on screen.

**A catch only a plan shows.** Camera 3 looks straight down; with the register upright as Ravi reads it, the lamp lands **frame-right**, the one night set-up where the warm light swaps sides. Keep it (his point of view, 2–3 s), but write "lamp light from frame-right" into its light line.

A **set-up** is one camera position plus one lighting arrangement; a new one starts when the camera moves (not a push-in within the shot) or the light changes. Crews schedule by set-up so the lights move once. This film has **18**, coded `location+camera·story day`: `A2·D1` is LOC-A, camera 2, day 1, and the day fixes the time and Ravi's wardrobe.

## 4. Rung 3 — The Boards: One Consistent Still Set

A **board** here isn't a sketch. It's the approved 9:16 still that becomes the shot's Frames→Video **start frame**, plus an **end-frame** board if the shot moves.

**4.1 Anchors.** First make the masters (`LOC-A_night_master.png`, `LOC-B_dusk_master.png`, `LOC-A_morning_master.png`) and sheets (`CHAR_ravi_D1.png`, `_D2`, `_D3`, `CHAR_kishan.png`), as file 10 §7.4 and [[05-phase-5-character-consistency|Phase 5]] §3 show. Make the morning master by **editing the night master**, so the room can't change, and Ravi's D2 and D3 sheets by **editing only D1's wardrobe**: state changes, identity never does ([[15-continuity-bible-script-supervisor]] §3). Park the tanker in the dusk master, so all tanker boards share one picture of it.

**4.2 Tool and cost.** In your Flow project: prompt box → model name → **Image**. Set **Image aspect ratio** to vertical, **Number of outputs** to 1–2 (stills only; video stays at 1, file 21 §7), and the model to **Nano Banana Pro** or **Nano Banana 2**.

- **Not NB 2 Lite for finals.** Google calls it "not optimized for multiple reference inputs or multi-turn sequential editing", a board's whole job. It's the one image model Flow states is free, so keep it for reference-free thumbnails.
- **Measure the price.** Flow lists no image prices. Check your balance (profile picture) before and after one board, or read **Settings** at the top right of the prompt box, and multiply by ~75 (48 boards, anchors, rejects).
- **Skip the Agent.** It "routes your request to the best image model"; one still set needs one model. Flow Help disagrees on the default model, so read the name first.

**4.3 References and order.** Drag in two or three references from the project (Flow states no limit): the room, and one sheet per person in frame. Per set-up, make the **key board** first from master and sheet (the hero or Quality shot, if any), then the other boards from the key board, then each last board from its own first board.

**4.4 Prompts.** A board prompt is the shot's video prompt skeleton (file 15 §5) with [ACTION] frozen to one instant and [AUDIO] swapped for a still-frame line, pasted as plain sentences: no tags, no bible labels. The key board for S1-04 (references `LOC-A_night_master.png`, `CHAR_ravi_D1.png`):

```
Storyboard frame: one photoreal film still, vertical 9:16.
Image 1 is the room and its light. Image 2 is the man. Keep both exactly.
Medium close-up, eye level, 35 mm lens, from camera 2: in front of the desk near the door, looking back at the man.
Ravi, a friendly Indian man, early 30s, short black hair, light stubble. Crumpled pale-blue cotton shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow, steel watch on his left wrist, tired eyes.
Frozen instant: he rubs his eyes with his left hand.
The pump office at 9 PM. In frame: three fat red cloth-bound registers, open; a black phone in a teal case, face-down, by his right hand. The desk lamp at frame-left.
Camera on the room side; the door and the window are camera-right. He sits behind the desk, turned a little camera-right, toward the door.
Shot on 35 mm, shallow depth of field, soft low-contrast film look with fine grain; warm tungsten desk lamp as key light, cool blue night through the window as fill; muted palette with one teal accent; eye-level, tripod-steady camera. 9:16.
A still frame. No captions, no on-screen text.
```

A **derived board** (S2-07) attaches `S1-04_board_v1.png` as "a frame from this camera position: keep its room, props, light and angle", plus the sheet. It rewrites only [CAMERA] ("Close-up from the same position") and [ACTION] ("the phone at his right ear in his right hand, eyes toward the doorway; mouth closed"). A **last board** is one edit of its own first board — here S2-07's:

```
Same picture. Keep the man, his clothes, the room, the props, the light and
the framing exactly. Change one thing: the phone is lowered out of frame and
his eyes are down on the open register; mouth closed.
```

A second one-change edit, "Same picture, framed slightly tighter", gives the push-in its end. One change per edit is our habit, not Google's rule (Google recommends multi-turn editing); three changes at once tend to move the rest of the picture, as exercise 12.4 tests. The **bookend**, S4-06, is an edit of `S1-02_board_v1.png` with `CHAR_ravi_D3.png` ([[13-visual-language-composition-blocking-light]] §8 gives the method and the prompt); add `LOC-A_morning_master.png` as a third reference if the room drifts. One clash: the Look Sentence says "eye-level", but S1-01, S1-02, S1-05, S2-04 and S4-06 aren't. Never edit the Look Sentence: the camera sentence comes first, states the angle, and the board carries it, so check those five boards first.

**4.5 Two boards per moving shot.** Storyboard artists draw a shot's key moments: the extremes of a motion and the clearest pose (Goldman et al.). In Flow those are the first and last frames, Frames→Video's two inputs. Go to model name → **Video** → **Frames**, drag the boards onto **+ Add start frame** and **+ Add end frame**, and "describe the transition".

**A shot gets a last board when the next cut depends on how it ends**: a move, an exit, an entrance or a look. Seventeen shots do: S1-01, S1-03, S1-04, S1-05, S1-06, S2-01, S2-05, S2-07, S2-08, S3-01, S3-02, S3-04, S3-05, S3-08, S4-01, S4-04 and S4-05, for **48 boards**. Dialogue shots keep one board so the line has the whole clip — except S2-07, whose push-in and lowered phone need an end frame (file 16 may add a closed-mouth last board).

Boards also **design the join** (file 10 §5.7). A's last board and B's first show one instant from two set-ups, so draw them as a pair: S1-06's last from camera 2, S2-01's first from camera 2′. A `J_` join still (file 10 §3.2 ①) carries one continuous moment across two clips; this film's 31 joins (29 cuts, one dissolve, one fade) need none. Name boards `S1-06_board_v1.png` and `S1-06_board_v1_last.png`; a fix bumps both to `_v2`.

**4.6 Annotate a copy** (in Slides or on a print), never the file Flow animates, or Veo animates your arrows. **Subject arrows** are thin, beside the subject, along its path; skip tiny motions. **Camera arrows** are thick, labelled, and touch or cross the frame edge. **Under each panel** go the action, the sound and the **Out**.

```
┌────────────────┐
│↘              ↙│   S1-04 · outer frame = first board (MCU)
│   ┌────────┐   │   inner frame = where the push-in ends (last board)
│   │  face  │   │   corner arrows in = PUSH-IN: the camera travels,
│   └────────┘   │   it does not zoom
│↗              ↖│
└────────────────┘
S2-08 · WS two-shot · use 2 s · thin arrow: Kishan exits frame-right
SOUND footsteps, door click; idle carries over · OUT → HARD CUT on the click → S3-01
```

In India, Flow's visible watermark is on every board (Flow FAQ). Whether it carries into the animated clip is undocumented, so check your first Lite draft, and never crop, blur or paint it out.

## 5. Board Review — Read the Row Before You Roll

In a Drive or Finder folder sorted by name, grid view shows the first boards one scene per row, in story order, near phone size. Read each row like a sentence, with the cut words between pictures:

```
S1-01 ─action─ S1-02 ─J: buzz─ S1-03 ─look─ S1-04 ─action─ S1-05 ─sound─ S1-06 ─action─ S2-01

BOARD REVIEW — first boards, one row per scene
[ ] AXIS     Ravi looks frame-right, Kishan frame-left, in every board
[ ] HANDS    phone in Ravi's right hand; steel watch on his left wrist
[ ] STATE    shirt = story day; glass, registers, sleeves, gamchha per file 15
[ ] LIGHT    key and fill on the set-up's sides (§8); LOC-A window frame-right
[ ] ANGLE    S1-01, S1-02, S1-05, S2-04, S4-06 obey [CAMERA], not "eye-level"
[ ] 30°      one person in two shots in a row: ≥ 30° or two sizes apart
[ ] PAIRS    S2-03/S2-05, S3-02/S3-03, S3-05/S3-06, S3-09/S3-10 (file 16 §8):
             same size, same eye height, mirrored
[ ] PEAK     S2-07 is the tightest face in the film
[ ] JOINS    each cut on action or look: last and first boards agree
[ ] BOOKEND  S1-02 under S4-06 at 50 % opacity: desk, shelf, window align
[ ] TANKER   white, one blue stripe; in right-to-left, out left-to-right
[ ] SCREENS  a plain glow with four visible corners; no drawn app
[ ] SAFE     nothing the viewer must see in the bottom 35 % (file 20)
```

Three catches on this film:

1. **S1-06 → S2-01 is a jump cut on paper.** Same man, same camera, MCU to CU with no change of angle, so the 30° rule fails ([[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] §5.4). Fix it for 0 coins: S2-01 gets **camera 2′**, swung at least 30° toward the front of the desk to favour the phone at his right ear.
2. **The matched singles don't match.** A typical miss: Kishan's eyes sit higher in S3-09 than Ravi's in S3-10. Re-board camera 5 at his eye height.
3. **S3-04 sprouts a fake order screen**, the garbled UI [[01-phase-1-the-big-picture|Phase 1]] §7 warns about. Edit the board to "the screen is a plain white glow, nothing on it, all four corners visible". The real recording is composited later ([[20-colour-finishing-and-delivery]] §8.3).

To check a last board for drift, put it over its first board in Resolve and set Inspector → Composite → **Composite Mode** → **Difference**. Only the change you asked for should light up.

## 6. Rung 4 — The Animatic

An **animatic** is "a sequence of still images … displayed in sync with rough dialogue (i.e., scratch vocals) or rough soundtrack". A scene cut here is never paid for. A **scratch track** is temporary sound recorded only to time the cut; **temp music** is a stand-in that never ships.

**6.1 Build it in DaVinci Resolve.** Every step works in the free edition.

1. File → Project Settings → Master Settings: Timeline resolution 1920 × 1080 HD, **Use vertical resolution** ticked (giving 1080 × 1920), frame rate **24**. Set them before importing; the frame rate can't change later.
2. DaVinci Resolve → Preferences → User → Editing → General Settings → **Standard still duration**: 3 s, the length of 16 of the 32 shots.
3. Import the boards, one bin per scene, sorted by name. If numbered stills arrive as one clip, use the Media Storage option menu → **Frame Display Mode** → Individual. Then right-click → **Append Selected Clips to Timeline**.
4. Press **T** (Trim tool) so changes ripple, select clips that share a length, then Clip → **Change Clip Duration** (⌘D). A one-board shot gets its whole Use; a two-board shot gives two-thirds to the first board and one-third to the last (3 s = 48 + 24 frames). The end card comes from Effects Library → Titles → **Text**.
5. Record scratch lines in your own Hinglish: Cut page → **Voiceover** icon in the viewer, with its Voice Isolation off (that's Studio-only). Record your phone buzzing on wood, and its chime. The **Sound Library** panel downloads Blackmagic's free Fairlight library (500+ foley sounds; search it for footsteps, the door, the engine). Make a 90 s temp bed in Google Flow Music (check that your plan downloads it); [[19-sound-edit-design-and-mix|file 19]] places the real cue.
6. File → **Quick Export** → pick a preset → Export, and watch it on your phone. For the timing sheet: Edit Index option menu → **Export Edit Index** → .csv, opened in Sheets.

**6.2 Vids, the quicker route.** One board per scene in a 9:16 video, each scene's edge dragged to length (**Show timing** shows the tracks; no typed duration is documented). Add AI voice-over for scratch lines (unlimited on AI Pro, Hindi included) and music on a track spanning scenes, then Download as MP4. Keep the timing sheet in Resolve.

**6.3 What you test.** Watch it three times, once muted:

| Test | It passes when |
| ---- | -------------- |
| Runtime | 1:30 total; scenes run 18, 22, 28 and 22 s |
| Hook | something moves and sounds before 0:02 |
| Muted | you can say who wants what and why Kishan leaves (file 12) |
| Drag | you press **M** wherever your attention leaves; a spot marked twice gets cut on paper |
| Lines | each scratch line ends before its Out, at 2–3 words a second ([[06-phase-6-voice-lipsync-audio\|Phase 6]]) |
| Motives | you can say each cut's Out aloud: action, look or sound. If you can't, re-board or remove the cut |
| Phone | the faces in the MCUs read on a phone in your hand |

**6.4 Edit before you shoot: scene 2.** First animatics run long; re-ordering, shortening and dropping cost nothing here. An illustrative first pass of scene 2, and the trims that land on the master list:

## 7. Rung 5 — Lock the Shot List: the Last Columns

**Locked** means every shot has approved boards, a method, a generated length, a tier, a draft count and a price. After this, a change costs coins and goes back down the ladder ([[21-long-form-multi-scene-production]] §8 runs these gates at scale).

1. **Every shot is Frames→Video from its boards**: start frame always, end frame when there is one. No Extend, no Jump To; all 31 joins are designed in advance: 29 straight cuts, one dissolve, one fade.
2. **A Quality shot must have a board.** Quality takes no Ingredients, so the board is its only identity lock. Only a Fast shot whose board won't come right may fall back to Ingredients→Video (Lite or Fast, 8 s only).
3. **Length follows the action**: 4 s for one quick action, 6 s for an action and a settle, 8 s for push-ins, lines and every Quality shot (the credits page prices Quality as "8s videos", the models page says 4–8 s: check the length menu). Lite and Fast cost the same at any length. A first + last pair stretches the move across the clip, so if a draft crawls, drop a length; if it rushes, add one.
4. **Board, prompt and length stay identical from Lite to Fast to Quality**, or the draft stops predicting the lock.

Key: **Boards** 1 = start frame, 1+L = start + last. **L·F·Q** = Lite drafts · Fast renders · Quality locks.

## 8. Rung 6 — The Generation Plan: Shoot by Set-up

Crews order a shot list "in shooting order, not narrative order" to cut set-ups. Flow has no lights to move, but grouping still pays: a set-up's shots share one key board and the same [SUBJECT], [SCENE], [AXIS] and [LOOK] blocks (less drift, file 10 §7.3); you judge their takes side by side; one sitting keeps them on one model version while Google re-tunes Flow (file 21); and one corrected light line fixes them all.

Three order rules:

- **Riskiest set-up first.** Learn the hard shot fails before easy ones spend the money.
- **Faces before inserts.** Make the insert match the face, never the reverse.
- **One tier and one set-up per sitting.** If the daily 50 (5 Lite drafts) can't cover a set-up, finish it on consecutive days.

## 10. When to Skip Rungs

| Piece | Breakdown | Floor plan | Boards | Animatic |
| ----- | --------- | ---------- | ------ | -------- |
| One 8 s social post | in your head | no | 1 | no |
| 15–30 s ad, one person | 5 minutes | only if a second person or a vehicle moves | every shot | optional, in Vids |
| Tutorial | yes | no | presenter shots | screen recording + scratch VO |
| 60–90 s story | every scene | every location | every shot | yes |
| 2–5 min film or series | yes | yes | yes | per sequence, + file 21 |

A floor plan earns its place once **a second person, a vehicle or a second angle** shares the space; an animatic, once **the timing of a cut carries meaning** (dialogue, a cut on action, music). For a quick three-beat piece, one Veo clip with timestamped beats (`[00:00-00:04] … [00:04-00:08] …`) can stand in for both, at the cost of per-shot control.

## 11. What Previs Cannot Do

- **A board pins frames, not the motion between them.** Veo still invents the movement; boards reduce drift, they don't remove it.
- **The animatic tests timing, not performance.** Your scratch read isn't Veo's delivery, so re-time after the Lite pass.
- **The image model drifts too.** Re-anchor each new set-up on the master and the sheet, not on the last board.
- **Flow publishes no image prices and no reference limit.** Measure, and keep references few.
- **Every board and clip made in India carries the visible watermark.** Whether a board's mark animates into its clip is undocumented. Check, and never remove or hide it (disclosure: file 20).
- **Flow has no floor-plan, timing or animatic tool**, and Scenebuilder has no audio, so previs lives on paper, in Sheets, and in Resolve or Vids.
