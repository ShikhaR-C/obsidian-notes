## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

The robot film crew ([[00_README]]) now has eleven notebooks from you — map, story, pictures, plan, bible, acting notes, cut, rhythm, sound, colour, calendar. Nobody on a real set carries eleven notebooks. They carry a **call sheet**: one page saying which shots are made today, what must be signed first, how much money is left, and when the day is done.

This file is that page. The crews still forget everything after eight seconds. The page doesn't.

## 1. The One Idea — One Film, Run Once, From One Page

A film feels **seamless** when four continuities hold at once — **causal**, **perceptual**, **emotional** and **sonic** ([[11-directors-track-roadmap]] §1). The director secures the first three before a coin is spent; the editing director secures them again after generation and adds the fourth. This workbook teaches nothing new. It is the **run**: what to do on which day, which document must exist before the next step, what it costs, and the one check that lets you move on.

**Two ways to use it.** Make *Saaf Hisaab* once, as planned, to learn the method — every number here is the film's. Or run your own 60–90-second film through the same gates from the blank pack (§7); where this page says S2-07, read "my peak". "14 §7" means file 14, section 7; every file is linked in §10.

**An honest note.** The course's authors have **not** generated *Saaf Hisaab*. It is a fully planned production — 32 shots, 89 renders, ⏣2,000 — on paper. Your first run is its first test, and it will differ: a set-up that needs five drafts, a line Veo can't say. Log each difference in the post-mortem ([[21-long-form-multi-scene-production]] §12); that record is worth more than this plan.

## 2. The Run, Gate by Gate

### 4.2 The paste rule

1. **Bodies only** — plain sentences, one block per line, in skeleton order: camera, subject, action, scene, axis, look, audio. No square-bracket tags, no `LOOK — "…":` label; quotation marks are for speech, brackets for timestamps (11 §5, 15 §5). A wrapped bible line is one line; a name leads where needed (`Ravi, a friendly…`, `Ravi has a warm…`).
2. **Camera first.** On the top-down S1-01, S1-05, S2-04 and the high-angle S1-02, S4-06, the camera sentence states the angle and the board carries it; the Look Sentence's "eye-level" is never edited.
3. **From a board**, the still carries what *is*, the words what *happens*: subject becomes `Ravi is the man in the image.`, scene keeps place, time and what moves; the rest stays (15 §5).
4. **Generated quiet** — the line and room tone, the one in-picture hit, or room tone only, always ending "No music. No subtitles." (11 §5, 19 §3).
5. **Never cut** a frozen block, the Out, a hand, an eyeline, a line or that closing pair; before pasting, paste each frozen body into Find on the prompt — a body not found whole was paraphrased (15 §6) — or let the Sheet's per-block check do it (21 §11).

| 5 | AXIS | the LOC-A body, then eyeline and hands from the state table |
| 6 | LOOK | the night-office body, verbatim |
| 7 | AUDIO | room tone, VOICE-ravi, the delivery, the line, "No music. No subtitles." |

## 5. The Rubric

Four levels, as in [[11-directors-track-roadmap]] §2. Another person can mark every row from your artefacts or your cut.

**The scoring line.** Mark each row pass or fail on the artefact, never the intention. A level is passed when its test and every row pass on your own work; one row may stand as *accepted* if its failure and reason are dated in your change log (21 §5). Pass the levels in order.

**Level 1 · Beginner — storyteller.** Test: a stranger retells your story from the boards alone, with no sound and no captions (11 §2, run as 12 §8.1).

| # | Passes when | Back to |
| - | ----------- | ------- |
| 1.1 | The logline underlines a person, a want, an obstacle and a change | 12 §2.1 |
| 1.2 | Every link of the beat chain reads BUT or THEREFORE | 12 §3 |
| 1.3 | Every scene card's VALUE line changes charge from first shot to last | 12 §5 |
| 1.4 | Every featured plant has a pay-off, and every pay-off a plant | 12 §7 |
| 1.5 | Every board keeps the eyes near the upper third and nothing that matters below y 1,248; the squinted colour script shows one family per scene | 13 §2, §7 |
| 1.6 | The assembly is 1080 × 1920 at 24 fps, and every shot's use sits inside its clip's window, a second clear of each end | 17 §2, §4 |

**Level 2 · Intermediate — planner.** Test: every cut in a 30-second piece passes the frame-pair check, and the runtime is within 10 % of the animatic's (11 §2).

| # | Passes when | Back to |

| 2.2 | Every row of first boards passes the board-review checklist | 14 §5 |
| 2.3 | Every prompt sent holds each frozen body it uses, character for character, and every NG in the take log names the block that failed | 15 §5–7, 21 §11 |
| 2.4 | No neighbours in a scene sit more than one ladder step apart without an on-screen cause | 16 §5 |
| 2.5 | Every line has closed-mouth time before and after it; no word is cut by the clip's end | 16 §7 |
| 2.6 | Eyes closed, the track never drops to digital silence, and no bed changes on a picture cut except a planned hard break | 19 §4–5 |

**Level 3 · Advanced — finisher.** Test: a 60–90-second film with dialogue passes the technical QC (20 §11) and a test screening (18 §13).

| # | Passes when | Back to |

| 3.2 | With the downbeats marked, the cuts are not all on downbeats: lengths vary and only a few cuts are moved onto beats; dialogue is cut to the performance | 18 §3 |

## 6. The Failure Clinic

The fixes live in [[09-reference]] §8.1; the clinic finds *which* one you need. From what someone noticed, two or three yes / no checks lead to the continuity that broke, the stage, the file and the price.

**Price:** **FREE** = paper or the edit · **CHEAP** = a still, a Lite draft (⏣10) or a Fast retake (⏣20) · **EXPENSIVE** = a lock (⏣100), a Quality pickup (⏣120) or a reopened gate. **+** = EXPENSIVE if the shot is a Quality face.

```

├─ 1 Can a viewer say whose story it was and what he wanted?
│    no  → CAUSAL · story · 12 §1–2 ................................ FREE
├─ 2 Asked "What happened?", do they say "because" or "and then"?
│    "and then" → CAUSAL · story · 12 §3 · FREE on paper; after the
│                 locks a missing cause is a missing shot ....... EXPENSIVE
└─ 3 Muted, does it still read?
     no  → CAUSAL, carried by lines, not pictures · boards · 12 §8 . CHEAP

│    not the app, decide? no → CAUSAL · story · 12 §3.1, §10 ........ FREE

│    yes → SONIC · sound edit · 19 §3–5 ............................ FREE

│    no, only the timing → cut frame · 17 §8.1 ..................... FREE
│    no, wrong hand or side → regenerate; no flip, 18 §10 ......... CHEAP+
└─ 3 Ghost the pair: does the point of interest jump over ≈ 640 px?
```

## 8. What This Workbook Cannot Promise

- **The plan is untested.** Draft counts, lengths, timings, the 75 hours and a 100 % first-pass yield (21 §12) are assumptions until your post-mortem replaces them.
- **Hindi speech is unevaluated.** Google's Veo and Omni pages say languages other than English "have not been evaluated" (11 §8, 16 §6): hear every line on a Lite draft before any lock.
- **The tools will move.** Prices, models, lengths and which jar empties first change without notice: run the tool check before D1 and D20 and re-verify any number here (21 §6).
- **The visible mark stays.** For people living in India, Flow marks every output. Nothing in this course removes, crops out, covers, blurs or mirrors it; a punch-in is composition, never a way round it, and a flip is closed on every marked clip (18 §9–10, 20 §2.3).
- **Disclosure is your duty.** YouTube's "AI use", Meta's AI label, India's IT Rules amendment (law since 2026-02-20) and ASCI's guidelines (self-regulation, from about end-December 2026) are in 20 §12 — information, not legal advice.
- **Rights are only partly stated.** Google lists "commercial use rights" among AI Pro's Flow Music benefits, but no page read licenses Flow or Veo video: read the terms before paid use; rights are not disclosure (19 §13).
- **Free DaVinci Resolve 21.1 is the ceiling.** Film Grain as a ResolveFX, video noise reduction, automatic subtitles, Speed Warp, the Beat Detector and Voice Isolation are Studio-only; files 18–20 give the free routes.
- **The rubric is the course's own**, not a film school's; one viewer from your audience outranks it.

## 9. Projects

**9.1 — Beginner: one scene, no words, 15–20 seconds (0 coins).** *Brief:* a wordless single scene from clips you already own, or from free Nano Banana 2 Lite stills (14 §4.2). Shape it on 12 §4's 15-second row: one turn, 5–6 shots, a bookend if you can. *Gates:* G1 (logline, chain, scene card), G3 (the timed stills are the film), G6 (a locked 1080 × 1920, 24 fps timeline). *Must pass:* Level 1. *Artefact:* the export, and a stranger's retelling written down.

**9.2 — Intermediate: two scenes, one line, 30 seconds (~⏣450).** *Brief:* your own two-scene story on 12 §4's 30-second row — one turn, one spoken line, 10–11 shots. *Budget:* ≈ ⏣450 — nine shots at a Lite draft and a Fast lock (⏣270), the line on 14 §7's dialogue pattern of three Lite drafts, a Fast rehearsal and a Quality lock (⏣150), ⏣30 spare. *Gates:* all seven, small — a floor plan only if a second person or a vehicle moves, the animatic in Vids if you like (14 §6.2, §10). *Must pass:* Levels 1 and 2. *Artefact:* the master, the take log and the frame-pair sheets.

**9.3 — Advanced → Pro: the full film (≈ ⏣2,000).** *Brief:* *Saaf Hisaab* exactly as §2–§4 plan it, or your own 60–90-second film from the blank pack — four scenes at most, one point-of-view character, a bookend. *Calendar:* 21 §7's twenty working days and weeks 5–6, or two relaxed months. *Gates:* all seven, signed. *Must pass:* Level 3; Level 4's rows now, and its test on your next film. *Artefact:* the G7 bible index, masters, QC record and post-mortem.

**9.4 — The 60 and the 30 (0 coins).** Duplicate `saaf-hisaab_lock_v01` twice (18 §8). The **60** keeps 23 shots, dropping S1-04, S1-05, S1-06, S2-03, S2-05, S3-02, S3-03, S4-02 and S4-03. The **30** keeps 11 and cold-opens on the peak: S2-07, S2-08, S3-01, S3-04, S3-05, S3-07, S3-09, S3-10, S3-11, S4-02, S4-07. Show each, muted, to someone new and ask 12 §8.1's four questions; the 60 is the cut for WhatsApp Status and a Shorts ad (20 §10). *Artefact:* two timelines and a "what we lost" list.

**9.5 — Brief 01 through the story engine (0 coins).** Brief 01 in `flow-content/briefs.md`, "Why the driver OTP?", is four true statements and nobody who wants anything (12 §14.1). Work in a copy and leave the brief as it is. Use only its product facts — the OTP verified at the fill point, the fill that waits for it, the invoice that follows it, one ledger both sides see, any phone and no app — and none of its figures, which are unverified.
