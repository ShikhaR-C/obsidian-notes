> Level: all — start here | Time: ~30 min | Outcome: you know what the eleven files after this one teach, in what order to take them, which document each one leaves you holding, and the fixed facts of the one film every file works on — plus what changed in Flow during 2026 | Status: written & web-verified **2026-10-01** against Flow Help, the Gemini API docs, Google Vids Help, the DaVinci Resolve 21 manual and the sources in §10.

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

The robot film crew ([[00_README]]) can shoot a lovely eight seconds. But every crew that turns up has **never met the last one**. Hand ten crews ten notes and you get ten lovely clips that do not belong together — a different room, a different light, a different mood, a different voice.

A film needs two more people, and neither of them is a robot:

- The **director** decides *before* the crews arrive: what the story is, what every picture looks like, where everyone stands, what must never change. The director writes it all down, because the crews can't remember.
- The **editing director** works *after* the crews have gone: chooses the best pieces, joins them so nobody feels the join, lays one sound and one colour over everything, and sends it out.

You are both. This track teaches each job from the first day to professional practice.

## 1. The One Idea — Four Continuities, Two Hats

A film feels **seamless** when four things hold at once:

| # | Continuity | What it means | Breaks as | Taught in |
| - | ---------- | ------------- | --------- | --------- |
| 1 | **Causal** | Each scene happens *because of* the last | "Nice shots, but what was it about?" | 12 |
| 2 | **Perceptual** | Look, light, space, props and faces match across every cut | "Was that the same room?" | 13, 14, 15, 20 — and [[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] |
| 3 | **Emotional** | Performance and pace rise and fall on one curve | "He was calm, then furious, then calm" | 16, 18 |
| 4 | **Sonic** | One unbroken sound world carries the eye over every join | "It sounds stitched" | 19 |

The **director's hat** secures the first three *before* a video coin is spent — on paper and in stills, where a mistake costs little. The **editor's hat** secures them again *after* generation — in the cut — and adds the fourth. File 10 gave you the rules of the join. This track is everything around the join: the story that makes it necessary, the plan that makes it cheap, and the finish that makes it invisible.

## 5. The Track Film — *Saaf Hisaab*

Every file takes its worked examples from **one** film, so you watch a single project travel from a sentence to a delivered master. These facts are fixed; no file changes them.

**"Saaf Hisaab"** (साफ़ हिसाब — "clean accounts") · a story-led brand short · **≈ 90 seconds · 9:16 · 24 fps** · it grows the four-beat DZZLO promo of [[01-phase-1-the-big-picture|Phase 1]] §7 into four scenes.

**Logline.** A pump dealer who ends every night fighting his own paper registers nearly loses a trusted driver over one missing entry — until an order that arrives already verified lets him close the books for good.

**Controlling idea.** Trust comes from a record nobody has to argue about.

| Scene | Title | Story day · time | Location | What turns |
| ----- | ----- | ---------------- | -------- | ---------- |
| 1 | **The 9 PM Register** (≈ 0–18 s) | D1 · 9 PM | LOC-A, the office | "just another late night" → "this is costing me" |
| 2 | **The Dispute** (≈ 18–40 s) | D1 · continuous | LOC-A, Kishan at the doorway | "I can settle this" → "my own register can't answer" |
| 3 | **The Verified Order** (≈ 40–68 s) | D2 · dusk | LOC-B, the forecourt | suspicion → trust |
| 4 | **Morning Chai** (≈ 68–90 s) | D3 · 7 AM | LOC-A, the office | relief; bookends scene 1 |

**The six spoken moments** (each one breath):

- S2-01 — phone voice, off-screen: *"Payment ho gaya tha, Ravi ji!"*
- S2-02 — Kishan: *"Sahab, gaadi khadi hai. Kab tak?"*
- S2-03 — Ravi: *"Kaun si gaadi thi? Kaun sa driver?"*
- S2-07 — Ravi, quietly, to the register: *"Register mein nahin hai."*
- S3-09 — Kishan: *"Bas?"* · S3-10 — Ravi: *"Bas."*

**Product truth — use only this.** An order arrives in the app already verified — company, manager, driver, vehicle; the ledger reads like a bank statement; the credit limit is visible; the dealer approves. **The app screen is never generated.** It is a real screen recording composited in the edit ([[20-colour-finishing-and-delivery]] §8).

**Characters.** Identity never changes; *state* changes by story day ([[15-continuity-bible-script-supervisor]] §3).

```
CHARACTER — "Ravi": a friendly Indian man, early 30s, short black hair, light stubble.
STATE D1 (night, scenes 1–2): crumpled pale-blue cotton shirt, sleeves rolled to the
  elbow, steel watch on his left wrist, tired eyes.
STATE D2 (dusk, scene 3): cream short-sleeved shirt, steel watch on his left wrist,
  alert and guarded.
STATE D3 (morning, scene 4): navy DZZLO polo shirt, steel watch on his left wrist,
  rested, warm approachable smile.

CHARACTER — "Kishan": a lean Indian man, late 40s, grey stubble, deep-set eyes,
  faded khaki uniform shirt, a red-checked gamchha over his left shoulder.
```

D3 carries the [[05-phase-5-character-consistency|Phase 5]] §4 bible line — the polo, the smile — plus the watch: the film ends on the brand's spokesperson. **Fixed details:** Ravi holds the phone in his **right** hand, always; the watch is on his **left** wrist, always; Kishan drives **a white fuel tanker with a single blue stripe**.

**Locations and the line.**

- **LOC-A — the pump office.** A small glass-fronted cabin. Ravi sits behind the desk. The **window and the door to the forecourt are camera-right**. Every camera stays on the room side of the line: Ravi looks camera-right toward the pump; Kishan stands in the doorway on the right and looks camera-left.
- **LOC-B — the forecourt.** One tanker bay under a white canopy, the cabin behind. **Ravi frame-left looking right, Kishan frame-right looking left** — the same screen sides as in the office.
- The tanker **arrives right-to-left** (S3-01) and **leaves left-to-right** (S4-04). The floor plans are in [[14-previs-storyboard-floorplan-animatic]] §3.

**Props** ([[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] §7.4, verbatim):

```
PROPS — three fat red cloth-bound registers; a black phone in a teal case;
a steel chai glass; a Casio calculator; the window showing the pump's white
canopy lights.
```

**Motifs.** The **chai glass** (full, cold, untouched → steaming, in his hand) · the **phone sound** (a harsh buzz on wood → a soft chime) · the **registers** (open and sprawled → closed and stacked) · the **teal accent** (the phone case → finally the logo).

**Look Sentences** — one per story day, pasted verbatim, never rewritten:

```
LOOK — "DZZLO night office": shot on 35 mm, shallow depth of field, soft
low-contrast film look with fine grain; warm tungsten desk lamp as key light,
cool blue night through the window as fill; muted palette with one teal
accent; eye-level, tripod-steady camera. 9:16.

LOOK — "DZZLO dusk forecourt": shot on 35 mm, shallow depth of field, soft
low-contrast film look with fine grain; last amber sunlight low from behind
the pump as key light, cool white canopy lights as fill; muted palette with
one teal accent; eye-level, tripod-steady camera. 9:16.

LOOK — "DZZLO morning office": shot on 35 mm, shallow depth of field, soft
low-contrast film look with fine grain; soft warm morning daylight through
the window as key light, pale bounce off the white wall as fill; muted
palette with one teal accent; eye-level, tripod-steady camera. 9:16.
```

**Camera personality.** "Tripod & push-ins" ([[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] §7.5): static, or a slow push-in. There are exactly four push-ins — S1-04, S2-07, S3-05, S4-05.

**Names.**

| Thing | Form | Example |
| ----- | ---- | ------- |
| Shot | `S<scene>-<two digits>` | `S2-04` |
| Take | shot + `_t<two digits>` + tier letter `L` / `F` / `Q`, numbered in the order generated | `S2-04_t02_F.mp4` |
| Board (the shot's start still) | shot + `_board_v<n>`; the end still adds `_last` | `S2-07_board_v1.png`, `S2-07_board_v1_last.png` |
| Location master still | | `LOC-A_night_master.png`, `LOC-A_morning_master.png`, `LOC-B_dusk_master.png` |
| Character sheet | | `CHAR_ravi_D1.png`, `CHAR_kishan.png` |
| Edit versions | | `saaf-hisaab_cut_v03`, `saaf-hisaab_lock_v01`, `saaf-hisaab_master_v01` |

**The prompt skeleton** — the [[02-phase-2-prompting-basics|Phase 2]] §5 template with the frozen blocks slotted in. One subject, one action, one camera move:

```
[CAMERA]    size · angle · move — one sentence
[SUBJECT]   CHARACTER line + the STATE line for this story day
[ACTION]    one action, present tense — and how the shot ENDS (the "Out")
[SCENE]     location + time; only the props in frame, in bible words
[AXIS]      side of the line · eyeline direction · which hand
[LOOK]      the Look Sentence
[AUDIO]     quiet room tone; the line in the colon format, or the one effect to keep; "No music. No subtitles."
```

Three rules travel with it; [[15-continuity-bible-script-supervisor]] §5 gives the reasons:

1. **The tags and the labels are for you, not for Veo.** Paste the *bodies* as plain sentences, one block per line, in this order — no square-bracket tags, no `LOOK — "…":` label. Quotation marks in a prompt are reserved for speech. On a shot animated from a board, the subject and scene lines shrink to a naming clause ("Ravi is the man in the image." · "The pump office at 9 PM."), because the picture already carries them.
2. **The camera sentence comes first and wins.** Five shots are not at eye level (S1-01, S1-05, S2-04 top-down; S1-02, S4-06 high angle). The Look Sentence still goes in unedited; the camera sentence states the angle and the board carries it.
3. **Shots are generated quiet.** A dialogue shot asks for its line and room tone only; an insert asks for the one in-picture sound you mean to keep (the pen, the nozzle); every other shot asks for quiet room tone. The engine, the pages, the beds and the music are laid in the mix ([[19-sound-edit-design-and-mix]] §3).

## 7. How the Film Is Made, and What It Costs

- **Stills first.** Every shot starts as a **board** — a 9:16 still made with Nano Banana inside Flow — and is animated with **Frames→Video** (first frame, or first + last). Identity, wardrobe, set and light are decided in the still. It is also the only route that reaches Veo 3.1 Quality, which takes no Ingredients (§8).
- **One film, one picture model.** Veo 3.1 throughout: drafts on Lite, locks on Fast or Quality, so a draft predicts its lock. **Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 is the second unit** — cheap blocking tests for action and timing, the 40-credit prompt edit as a rescue, and the saved-voice route that [[16-directing-performance-and-dialogue-scenes]] §11 weighs.
- **No Extend.** All 31 joins are designed in advance: 29 straight cuts, one dissolve, one fade.
- **The visible mark stays.** For people living in India, Flow applies a visible watermark to every output automatically. Nothing in this track removes, crops, covers or hides it, and a reframe is never taught as a way round it. What you must disclose, and where, is in [[20-colour-finishing-and-delivery]] §12.
- **The envelope: ≈ ⏣2,000**, in four lines. The per-shot plan and the one allocation of the spare are in [[14-previs-storyboard-floorplan-animatic]] §7–9; the calendar that fits it into AI Pro's monthly and daily credits is in [[21-long-form-multi-scene-production]] §7.

The still-join, the seven questions for breaking a scene, the 180° and 30° rules, the transition catalogue and the cohesion stack in [[10-deep-dive-scene-continuity]] are the foundation this track builds on — and *draft cheap, commit expensive* still governs every coin.

## 9. How to Work Through the Track

1. **Read before you spend.** Files 12–20 can be read and exercised in about two weeks of evenings, almost entirely at 0 coins — their exercises use paper, free stills, and clips you already own.
2. **Then make one film.** Either *Saaf Hisaab* itself or your own 60–90-second story, on the calendar in [[21-long-form-multi-scene-production]] §7 with [[22-capstone-saaf-hisaab-workbook]] open beside you.
3. **Keep the order of the hats.** Story, pictures, plan, bible, performance — then generate — then cut, sound, colour. Every hour moved earlier in that list saves coins later in it.
4. **Pass each level's test** (§2) on your own work before moving up. A level you cannot demonstrate is a level you have only read.
