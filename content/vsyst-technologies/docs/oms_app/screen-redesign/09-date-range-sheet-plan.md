## 1. What is on the screen today, and why it cannot be patched

Facts, from the source:

1. **It is two pickers, not a range.** `DailySummary/components/RangeButtons.js` (104 lines) draws v1's `src/components/DatePicker/DTBS.js` (755 lines): a From button and a To button, each opening its own sheet. `useScreenModel.changeRange` (`:198–204`) commits **each end on its own** — a request per end.
2. **The shift buttons set one end's clock, nothing more.** `DTBS.js:450–462`: "6 - 6 AM" is `setHours(6, 0, 0, 0)` on the end being edited, "12 - 12 AM" is `setHours(0, 0, 0, 0)`. The date does not move and the other end does not move, so a 6–6 day is four actions across two sheets and two requests.
3. **Device-local time.** Those `setHours` calls ask the phone which zone it is in. The request path (`helpers/DailySummary/range.js`) and the server (`api_v4/readmodels/dailySummary.js:313–332`) work in fixed IST. On a phone set outside India the button and the window it produces disagree. Spec 02's P‑16 accepted this "until the redesign", and `range.js:170–240` is the adapter that papers over it.
4. **Nothing v2 reaches it.** `DTBS.js` takes colours from Paper's `useTheme` (`:59`, `:346`), its words are English literals ("Set Time ( Shift-Wise ) :", `:547`), its sheets are fixed at `snapPoints: ['90%']` with `enableDynamicSizing={false}` (`:226–228` and three more), and it reads no window class and no type scale. That is the user's list exactly: orientation, larger text, Hindi, theme. At 320 dp its chips run off the right edge (seen on the emulator, 2026-09-16).
5. **It is heavy.** Each `DTBS` mounts **four** `BottomSheetModal`s (`:223, :249, :280, :305`). The phone's quick return draws the chrome block a second time (`DailySummary/index.js:406–412`, mounted at `:442` and again at `:476`), so eight modals exist while it shows — plan 08's RT‑3.
6. **Three callers share it** — this screen, v1's Daily Summary (`Common/DailySummary/component/index.js:18`) and Reports (`Common/Reports/DailyReport/index.js:23`). A change inside `DTBS` lands on all three, and v1 files are not touched.
7. **A redesign is already parked beside it** — `components/DateTimeRange.js` (577 lines) and its test (788 lines), unmounted since P‑16. It has no named choices and no shift time, so it is not the answer either; its **pure rules** (`combineIst`, `toBoundary`, `clampRange` in `range.js`) are right and get a second life here.

So: a new component, v1 left alone.

### 2.2 How the sheet behaves

- **It is a draft.** Opening the sheet copies what is applied. Rows, shift buttons and fields change the draft and the summary — never the list. **Apply** commits the draft: one request, the sheet closes, the list becomes a new list (page 1, top, nothing selected — the screen's existing rule for a new window).
- **Apply with nothing changed** closes the sheet and sends nothing.
- **Every other way out discards the draft:** Close, the backdrop, the swipe down, Android's back. One `dismiss()` path, as in Customers' sheets (`FilterSheet.js:76–90`, `useBSMBackHandler`).
- **It always opens on what is applied**, never on an abandoned draft.
- **Reset** sets the draft to the default — **Today, 12 - 12 AM** — and does not close. The user still presses Apply. It is dimmed when the draft is already the default.
- **Default on a fresh screen:** Today, under the **shift time this phone applied last** (C‑4, answered) — 12 - 12 AM on a phone that has never applied one. The choice itself is never remembered: the screen always opens on Today.

## 3. The rules of the window — the part to verify most carefully

Everything below is **IST**, by fixed +05:30 arithmetic on epoch milliseconds — no `setHours`, no `Intl`, no moment — exactly as `range.js` does today, and proved the same way: the rules run again in child processes set to UTC, Los Angeles and Kiritimati.

### 3.1 The day start and the shift day

- The **day start** is one of two values: **12 AM** ("12 - 12 AM") or **6 AM** ("6 - 6 AM").
- A **shift day** runs from the day start to the next day start. Under 6–6, the shift day "19 Sep" is 19 Sep 6:00 AM → 20 Sep 6:00 AM.
- An instant belongs to the shift day whose start is at or before it. Under 6–6, 20 Sep 3:00 AM belongs to the shift day **19 Sep** (C‑2, answered: the running shift).

### 3.2 The four named choices

With `D0` = the start of the shift day that contains "now", and `M0` / `M1` = the day start on the 1st of that shift day's month / of the month after:

| Choice      | Window                       | In words                                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Today       | `D0` → `D0 + 1 day`          | the running shift day                                        |
| Yesterday   | `D0 − 1 day` → `D0`          | the one before                                               |
| Last 7 days | `D0 − 6 days` → `D0 + 1 day` | seven shift days **ending with today** (C‑3, answered: yes)  |
| This month  | `M0` → `M1`                  | the **whole calendar month**, in shift days (C‑10, answered) |

Worked, with now = **19 Sep 2026, 10:00 AM**:

| Choice      | 12 - 12 AM                                    | 6 - 6 AM                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Today       | 19 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM → 20 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM | 19 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM → 20 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM |
| Yesterday   | 18 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM → 19 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM | 18 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM → 19 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM |
| Last 7 days | 13 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM → 20 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM | 13 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM → 20 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM |
| This month  | 1 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM → 1 Oct 2026, 12:00 AM   | 1 Sep 2026, 6:00 AM → 1 Oct 2026, 6:00 AM   |

**Both shifts print the same dates; only the clock differs** — pressing a shift button never makes a window look a day longer.

And with now = **20 Sep 2026, 3:00 AM** under 6 - 6 AM: Today is still **19 Sep 6:00 AM → 20 Sep 6:00 AM** — the shift that is running. It becomes 20 Sep 6:00 AM → 21 Sep 6:00 AM at 6:00 AM sharp (C‑2, answered). By the same rule, at 1 Oct 3:00 AM under 6–6 "This month" is still September.

### 3.3 How an end is sent, and how it prints (C‑1 and C‑1b, answered)

**One rule, no exception: every end prints its own time, and that minute is not in the window.** The user, rounds 4–5: "we can show 12:00 am to 12:00 am for 12-12 and it would not involve end time 12:00 am ? similar to 6:00 am?" · "C-1 yes end shows own time."

The filter is **`From ≤ order time < the To shown`** — the user's rule of 2026-09-16 ("at 18:30 list all orders on or before 18:29:59.999"), unchanged. The server keeps its inclusive `$gte from, $lte to` (`dailySummary.js:332`) and the phone sends **the To shown less one millisecond**. **No API change.**

| The window's end                        | Prints                    | Sent as `to`            | Last order inside                                 |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Today, 12 - 12 AM                       | 20 Sep 2026, **12:00 AM** | 19 Sep 23:59:59.999 IST | 19 Sep 11:59:59 PM                                |
| Today, 6 - 6 AM                         | 20 Sep 2026, **6:00 AM**  | 20 Sep 05:59:59.999 IST | 20 Sep 5:59:59 AM                                 |
| Custom, To picked at 6:30 PM            | 6:30 PM                   | 18:29:59.999            | 6:29:59 PM                                        |
| Custom, To picked at 12:00 AM on 26 Sep | 26 Sep 2026, 12:00 AM     | 25 Sep 23:59:59.999     | the whole of the 25th                             |
| Custom, To picked at **11:59 PM**       | **11:59 PM**              | 23:58:59.999            | 11:58:59 PM — the day's last minute is **not** in |

- An order stamped exactly 20 Sep 12:00:00 AM is tomorrow's; one stamped exactly 6:00:00 AM belongs to the next shift, whose From includes it. The end is exclusive so that **two windows side by side never count an order twice and never drop one.**
- **11:59 PM is a time like any other** (C‑1b — the user: "custom time setting to 11:59 should show 11:59 itself. use 12-12 only when time preset selected"). Nothing is moved or rounded behind the user's back: what the field shows is what is used. The whole-day end, 12:00 AM of the next date, comes from the shift buttons (§3.5), not from a guess about what 11:59 PM was meant to be.
- The model stores the instants that are sent (`to` = the last millisecond inside) — the shape `defaultRange` and the server's `meta.range` already have — and every print of an end is `to + 1 ms` (`toBoundary`, kept from the parked picker with its tests). The server's echo of its default day (`…23:59:59.999`) therefore prints as the next 12:00 AM with no special case. `sentTo`, `clampPicked` and `isIstDayEnd` — the adapters for v1's buttons, which carried the old 11:59 PM exception — go away with the buttons.

### 3.4 Custom

- **It opens filled** with the window the draft already shows (choose Custom while on Yesterday and the four fields read `18 Sep, 12:00 AM` and `19 Sep, 12:00 AM`) — never empty, never the frame's sample dates.
- **Four fields**, each opening a picker for that one value: From date, From time, To date, To time — **a calendar for a date, a wheel for a time** (C‑7, answered; the platform detail is in §6a). A picked date keeps its end's time; a picked time keeps its end's date.
- **The whole of a day ends on the next date.** "Through the 25th" is To = **26 Sep, 12:00 AM** (6–6: 26 Sep, 6:00 AM). The shift buttons write it (§3.5), and the line under Custom says what the window really holds — "4 days selected" — so a To left on the 25th shows up as "3 days" before Apply, not after.
- **No future:** the From calendar stops at today's date; the To calendar stops at the date today's shift day ends on — tomorrow's, under both shifts. (v1 stops at "now", `DTBS.js:244`.)
- **From must come before To:** the agreed one-minute rule stays (`clampRange`, `range.js:158–168`): the end the user did **not** touch moves, in front of them, in the fields. No error text, no dead Apply.
- **"N days selected"** is the real length: whole days read "4 days selected"; anything else reads days and hours ("2 days 18 hrs selected"), under a day hours and minutes. Strings are functions in the table, as `selection.all(n, m)` is today.

### 3.5 The shift buttons and Custom — who wins

The user, round 5: **"use 12-12 only when time preset selected. if custom item changed then it should superimpose presets."** So:

1. **A shift button sets both clocks — and only when it is pressed.** The dates stay where they are.
2. **A time changed by hand wins over the button.** The hand-set time is shown and used as it is; the button goes unlit and the card's right-hand text prints the real times. Nothing snaps back to the shift.
3. **Pressing a shift button again wins over the hand-set times:** both clocks go back on that shift.
4. A **date** changed by hand does not touch the clocks, so the button stays lit.

| Before                                                        | Press 6 - 6 AM                                | Press 12 - 12 AM                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 22 Sep, 12:00 AM → 26 Sep, 12:00 AM · 4 days (12 - 12 AM lit) | 22 Sep, 6:00 AM → 26 Sep, 6:00 AM · 4 days    | back, exactly                                   |
| 22 Sep, 12:00 AM → 23 Sep, 12:00 AM · 1 day (12 - 12 AM lit)  | 22 Sep, 6:00 AM → 23 Sep, 6:00 AM · 1 day     | back, exactly                                   |
| 22 Sep, 3:15 PM → 25 Sep, 9:00 AM (set by hand — neither lit) | 22 Sep, 6:00 AM → 25 Sep, 6:00 AM · 3 days    | 22 Sep, 12:00 AM → 25 Sep, 12:00 AM · 3 days    |
| 22 Sep, 9:00 AM → 6:00 PM (one date, by hand — neither lit)   | 22 Sep, 6:00 AM → **23 Sep**, 6:00 AM · 1 day | 22 Sep, 12:00 AM → **23 Sep**, 12:00 AM · 1 day |

- The last row is the one guard: both clocks on one date would be an empty window, so the To date moves to the next day — that date's whole shift day.
- **Which button is lit** is read from the window, never stored beside it: the one **both** ends sit on; otherwise neither. Setting both times by hand to 6:00 AM lights 6 - 6 AM, because that is what the window is.
- The **last lit value is kept** as the day start: a named choice picked after a hand-made window uses it, the stepper keeps it, and it is what the phone remembers (C‑4).
- With a named choice selected, pressing a shift button re-works that choice (§3.2) and the summary follows.

### 3.6 Today stays today

- The screen's model keeps **the choice**, not only the instants: `{ choice, dayStart, from?, to? }` (`from` / `to` only for Custom).
- **Every refresh re-works a named choice against the clock** — the pull, the header's glyph, coming back to the screen. If a day start has passed since the last request, the window moves and the list is a new list; otherwise it is a plain refetch. A phone left on the screen overnight shows the new day on its next refresh. Custom never moves.
- **Today + 12 - 12 AM sends no range at all**, as now (`INITIAL_QUERY.range` is `undefined`): the server's clock decides the day and echoes it (`meta.range`, `rangeSource: "default"`). The first request stays `{ companyId, tab }`, so the captured fixtures and the tests that pin it stand. Every other window is worked out on the phone and sent.

### 3.7 The stepper (C‑8, answered: "introduce stepper")

`‹` moves the window one step into the past, `›` one step towards today. One pure rule, `stepWindow(window, direction, now)`:

1. **The size of a step is the window's own size.** A window that is whole calendar months (its From is a month's first day start and its end is a later month's) moves by **that many months**; every other window moves by **its own length** — a day for a day, seven days for Last 7 days, 2 days 18 hours for a hand-made window of that length. IST has no daylight saving, so a day is always 24 hours.
2. **A window that lands on a named choice becomes that choice** — and so "stays today" from then on (§3.6). Anything else becomes Custom with fixed ends, which never moves by itself. (A Custom window applied from the sheet is left as Custom even when it happens to equal Today — the user chose that radio.)
3. **`›` is dimmed once the window holds today's shift day.** Nothing is clipped to make a step fit — a clipped window would not step back to where it came from.
4. The day start does not change on a step.

Worked, with now = **Saturday 19 Sep 2026**, 12 - 12 AM:

| On the bar (dates only; both ends print the day start) | `‹` gives                    | `›` gives   |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------- |
| Today · 19 Sep → 20 Sep                                | Yesterday · 18 Sep → 19 Sep  | dimmed      |
| Yesterday · 18 Sep → 19 Sep                            | Thursday · 17 Sep → 18 Sep   | Today       |
| Thursday · 17 Sep → 18 Sep                             | Wednesday · 16 Sep → 17 Sep  | Yesterday   |
| Last 7 days · 13 Sep → 20 Sep                          | Custom · 6 Sep → 13 Sep      | dimmed      |
| Custom · 6 Sep → 13 Sep                                | Custom · 30 Aug → 6 Sep      | Last 7 days |
| This month · 1 Sep → 1 Oct                             | **Aug 2026** · 1 Aug → 1 Sep | dimmed      |
| Aug 2026 · 1 Aug → 1 Sep                               | Jul 2026 · 1 Jul → 1 Aug     | This month  |

Each step's From is the last window's To, to the millisecond — the exclusive end is what makes the windows tile.

So **"Last month" needs no row of its own** — it is one tap from This month.

- **A run of quick taps is one request.** The bar moves at once and the list shows its skeleton; the request goes **300 ms after the last tap** — the wait Customers' search already uses. Five taps back is one request, not five: the point on a low-end phone and a slow network. A step is otherwise an Apply: a new list, page 1, the top, nothing selected.
- Both steppers are 44 pt targets that do not scale with the text; a screen reader hears what the step is ("Previous day", "Next 7 days", "Previous month"; "Earlier" / "Later" for any other length), in both languages.
- The sheet opened after a step shows that window: Yesterday lit, or Custom open and filled.

## 4. Design corrections I suggest

Each stands unless you strike it.

1. **"Reset", not "Clear All"** — a date window cannot be cleared, only put back. It changes the draft only; Apply sends (§2.2).
2. **The summary and the screen's button are one component** (`RangeSummary`): full print in the sheet, compact print on the bar (§2.4), one formatter, one set of fit rules. In the sheet it is a live region, so a screen reader hears the window change.
3. **A better name for the card than "Preset Time".** v1 calls it "Set Time ( Shift-Wise )" and that is the meaning. Proposed: **"Shift time"** / **"शिफ़्ट का समय"**; the buttons keep v1's words, "6 - 6 AM" and "12 - 12 AM", which dealers already know.
4. **The card's right-hand text prints what the buttons give** — `12:00 AM – 12:00 AM` or `6:00 AM – 6:00 AM` — and, once a time is set by hand, the real times with neither button lit (§3.5).
5. **Choosing Custom scrolls its fields into view**, and the footer never scrolls: in frame 2 the last choice is already cut by the footer, and at a large text size much more would be.
6. **The `→` is read aloud as "to"**: the summary's accessibility label is a sentence ("Today, 19 September 2026, 12:00 AM to 20 September 2026, 12:00 AM"), the group is a `radiogroup`, each row a `radio` with its checked state, each shift button a button with its selected state.
7. **The sheet never sends while the user is choosing** — not on a row, not on a shift button, not while a time wheel turns. One request per Apply (§2.2).
8. **One sheet per screen.** The sheet is mounted once, beside the list; the chrome block and the panel hold only the bar. The quick return stops mounting a second picker (RT‑3 closes without taking the dates out of the overlay — Q‑1 needs no answer).
9. **Named `DateRangeSheet`, not `DateTimeRange`** — `DailySummary/__tests__/layout.test.js:349` reads raw source text for that word; the guard goes with the parked picker (§7), and the new name keeps the two apart until then.
10. **After a step the bar names the window** — a weekday, a month — instead of "Custom" (§2.4).
11. **Android's dialog buttons take our words.** The system dialogs follow the phone's language (§5), but their OK and Cancel can be ours (`positiveButton.label`, `negativeButton.label`), so at least those two follow the app's Hindi.
