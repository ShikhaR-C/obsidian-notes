# 00 — Course Map and Master Timeline

_Phase 0 · Orientation · Read this before Week 0. After this page you know exactly what to do in week 1, month 2, month 9 and month 24 — which decision, in which order, depending on which other decision, producing which template, taught in which lesson, and the observable test that says you may move on._

This is the **map**. The twenty lessons are the territory. Everything the course asks you to do is on this page as a dated row: the move, why it matters, who owns it, the artefact it leaves behind, the lesson that teaches it, and the test that says it is done. Nothing here is new material — if a row raises a question, the lesson in its **Lesson** column answers it, usually at more length than you want.

One structural difference from the sibling map in [[COO-Docs/00-course-map-and-timeline|COO Docs]] is worth naming at the top, because it changes how you read every table below. The COO's timeline is a **build order**: paper, then money, then tools, then a heartbeat, each one a prerequisite for the next in a fairly literal sense. The CEO's timeline is a **dependency graph with a few hard gates in it**. Most of what follows can be reordered; a handful of things genuinely cannot, and those are called out every time they appear. The hard gates are the interesting part of this page. The rest is a suggested order for work that will get reordered by reality anyway.

## 1. How to use this page

**Do the exercises in timeline order, not table-of-contents order.** The lessons are grouped by topic because that is how topics are learnt. A company is built in a different order, because a strategy written by an undefined seat gets re-litigated by whoever is unhappy with it ([[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]] §9), and because a trigger ladder built before you know your break-even count is a page of round numbers. You will therefore fill [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] twice — a rough first cut in week 4 because [[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments|C06]]'s reverse income statement needs a real monthly cash cost to run backwards from, and a proper one in week 5 — and you will not open [[19-planning-okrs-and-capital-allocation|lesson 19]] until month six, because there is nothing to allocate between until the kernel says what the constraint is.

Four rules govern the sequence, and every table below obeys them.

1. **Contracts before strategy.** The seat, the founders' agreement and the decision rights come first — not because they are more important than the strategy, but because an unsigned seat cannot hold a signed strategy. Week 0 is [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]], [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]] and [[C03-decision-rights-matrix|C03]]. Everything after them assumes they exist.
2. **Diagnosis before allocation.** You cannot decide what the next 200 founder-hours and the next ₹1,50,000 are for until [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]] names the constraint they are attacking. [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|Lesson 05]] precedes [[19-planning-okrs-and-capital-allocation|lesson 19]] by six months for exactly this reason, and lesson 19's orphan test is the mechanism that catches the objectives that skipped the step.
3. **Price before economics before the ladder.** [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] cannot be filled before [[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]] settles the metric and the tiers — there is no ARPA without a price. And [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]]'s 12/9/6/3 ladder is decoration before C12 produces a default-alive dealer count, because the rung at 6 months is supposed to name a lever, and you cannot name a lever without knowing what one dealer is worth. Sequence: **C11 → C12 → C13**, in that order, no exceptions.
4. **Written before said, said before delegated.** A decision that is not written down did not happen, and a decision you cannot state with its limit attached cannot be handed to anyone ([[16-decisions-delegation-and-not-being-the-bottleneck|lesson 16]] §4.3). This is the CEO's counterpart to the COO's _mechanisms, not intentions_, and it is why [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] opens in week 0 and never closes.

**How to read the columns.** _Why it matters_ is the failure the row prevents — read it when you are tempted to skip one. _Depends on_ names the row that must be true first; where it is blank, the row can be done any time. _Owner_ is the single accountable name; the CS, CA, lawyer and broker own their own filings and opinions, never your calendar. _Artefact_ names the toolkit file or the [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](toolkit/vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx) tab the work produces — the whole toolkit is indexed at [[CEO-Docs/toolkit/index|CEO Toolkit]]. _Exit criterion_ is the test: if you cannot demonstrate it to someone who was not in the room, the row is not done.

**On the month numbers.** Month 1 is your first month formally in the seat, not the company's first month — VSYST has existed since 2021 and much of Phase 2's corporate hygiene is an audit rather than a build. Weekday-tied work — the Monday numbers, the Friday money block — lands on the nearest one. Where a row waits on the CS, the CA, a lawyer or a counterparty, send it and book the follow-up the same day; their turnaround is not your delay to absorb silently.

**On the twenty-four months.** That is the honest pace for a seat held by a technical founder who also writes the production code, at a bootstrapped, pre-revenue company with three family directors and no employees. A full-time non-coding CEO compresses the windows; revenue compresses them further; a second engineer compresses Phase 4 dramatically. **The order does not change.** Skipping ahead is how founders end up with an OKR sheet and no signed founders' agreement.

### 1.1 What jumps the queue, and why

Four categories of work do not wait for your reading pace. They are not exceptions to the plan; they are the reason the plan has to be written down, so that when one of them interrupts you know exactly which row you were on. §10 carries the dates and the penalties.

1. **The founders' agreement, with vesting and the IP chain of title** ([[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|03]] §6, [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]]). The only document in the course that **cannot be retrofitted after a dispute starts**, and its IP clause covers the company's only asset. No ESOP may be granted until founder vesting is signed, and unpapered code assignment is the commonest Indian diligence finding. → **Week 0** for the checklist and the lawyer brief; **month 3** executed.
2. **The first board meeting and the board year** ([[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties|09]] §4, [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]). The gap between meetings is a statutory quantity, not a preference; a meeting postponed by three relatives on WhatsApp is still a Companies Act failure, and defective notice carries **₹25,000 on the officer in default** ([s.173(4)](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/)). → Four dates in three calendars as soon as the CS answers; pack #1 **seven days** before the first one.
3. **The bus-factor document** ([[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|20]] §8, [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession|C30]]). One sitting, no revenue, no hire, nobody's permission — and roughly half the rows come back blank, which **is** the finding. → **Month 1 or 2**, however early it feels. It is filed under Phase 4 because that is where it is _taught_, not when it is _needed_.
4. **Anything with a statutory date on it** ([[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties|09]] §6, [[C18-director-duties-and-governance-checklist|C18]]). DIR-3 KYC, AOC-4, MGT-7A, DPT-3, MSME-1, the AGM and the audited accounts arrive whether or not you have reached lesson 09. AOC-4 and MGT-7A run at **₹100/day with no cap** and feed the [s.164(2)](https://ca2013.com/164-disqualifications-for-appointment-of-director) three-year disqualification clock. → Per the calendar, **VERIFY LIVE with the CS/CA every April**.

Two more that are not statutory but behave like it at VSYST specifically, and both are on the disk in front of you rather than in the future: **the unmerged ledger correctness fix** — a fix that exists on a branch and is not deployed leaves the corrupting path running, the worst state available — and **the tenant-isolation gap** on the collection routes, which [[13-product-and-technology-leadership-for-a-technical-ceo|lesson 13]] §12 classifies as a director-level risk with a clock rather than a backlog row. Both belong in month 1, ahead of most of Phase 2.

**The phases overlap on purpose, and Phase 3 is the one that overlaps most.** Phase 2's board block is still running in month 3 while the values audit, the narrative and the weekly note have already started in month 2 — because a value written after the first hire is the first employee's inference, formed in week one from small evidence, and because the two live partner threads are already consuming senior hours today whether or not you have reached [[14-partnerships-ecosystem-and-founder-relationships|lesson 14]]. ### 2.1 The dependency graph, in one block

The four rules in §1 compress to this. Read it before the tables; it is the part of this page you will come back to.

```
        │        └────────────────► break-even dealer count ──► founder-weeks

        ├─ C08 positioning  ◄── needs C06's competitive alternatives
        │        └─► C10 script, demo run-of-show, objection bank
        ├─ C11 PRICE ──► C12 UNIT ECONOMICS ──► C13 LADDER   (never out of order)
        └─ C09 ICP + rubric ──► C10 pipeline ──► the loss log ──► back into C04

  C13 numbers + C18 filled from documents ──► C17 board pack #1 ──► minute book
  C04 + C13 + C25 no-list ─────────────────► C28 annual plan (block D)
  Everything above, once, dated ───────────► the six criteria in lesson 20 §10
```

Three notes on that block, because they are the places people get it wrong.

**C06 and C13 are mutually dependent, and the resolution is to do C13 twice.** [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|Lesson 05]] §10.4 asks you to run the reverse income statement backwards from your real monthly cash cost to a break-even dealer count. That cash cost lives in [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]]. So build a rough burn number in week 4 — one number, from the bank statements, no scenarios — finish C06 with it, and then build C13 properly in week 5 once C12 has told you what a dealer is worth. Do not wait for a perfect C13; a four-driver model you rebuild monthly beats a twenty-driver model you built once.

**C14–C16 are conditional, not scheduled.** The fundraise-readiness index, the investor narrative and the term-sheet decoder only get filled if the capital-decision box in [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|lesson 08]] §8.6 fires. The [[CEO-Docs/toolkit/index|toolkit index]] says the same thing. What is _not_ conditional is the hygiene underneath them — IP assignments closed, registers current, related-party arrangements papered — which happens in months 2–6 regardless, because assembling it is how you find your own problems.

**The loss log is the only arrow that runs backwards.** Every other dependency flows down the page. What every dealer conversation teaches you flows back up into [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]]'s assumption register, [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas|C08]]'s words, [[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]]'s objections and [[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]]'s no-list. That backwards arrow is the whole reason founder-led selling is a strategy instrument rather than a stopgap ([[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|lesson 07]] §1).

## 3. Week 0 — understand the seat

Week 0 produces no strategy, no revenue and no code. It produces **definition** — of the seat, of who decides what, of what happens if one of three relatives leaves — plus the habit that everything after it depends on. Skip it and every later row becomes a negotiation with someone who never agreed to the frame. Seven days, roughly two hours each, and only two of them need another person's diary.

- **Day 1 — the seat.** Read [[01-what-is-a-ceo|lesson 01]]. Write the seat in one paragraph: it must contain the four nouns — direction, capital, people, story — name the fifth job, and use _outside_ the way Drucker means it. Then the sentence you would say to a candidate who has never heard of VSYST. Then list the **five** things only this seat can do — not "should", _can_: if you stopped, would it simply not happen? — and five you are currently doing that failed the test. → **[[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]]** mission box, "Only me" and "Not mine after \_\_\_" blocks with a successor condition and a date per row. _Exit: read aloud to both co-directors, and you got a yes, a no or an edit — **not silence**._
- **Day 2 — the calendar.** Reconstruct last week hour by hour from the calendar, WhatsApp, the call log and git history — not from memory; memory flatters. Code it **twice**: once into the four jobs, once as W/Y/D for the three clocks. Most founder-CEOs at this stage find 90%+ on the week clock and zero on the decade clock. Book one weekly year-clock block and one decade-clock day this financial year, and treat both as unbookable. → **[[C27-ceo-weekly-template-and-calendar-audit|C27]]** entry #1 and a Month-3 target-calendar line in C01. _([[01-what-is-a-ceo|01]] §11.5, [[02-how-a-ceo-thinks|02]] §11.5.)_
- **Day 3 — the record.** Send the CS the three questions on what you are legally appointed as ([[01-what-is-a-ceo|01]] §4.4), and pull the MCA master data and the Articles into the binder while you wait. Then open **[[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]**: three retrospective entries — start with the per-GSTIN, web-only billing decision — and the next five decisions classified one-way or two-way with a cost to walk back and a time budget each. → a sent email, three journal entries, five One-Way-Door rows, five calendar blocks that match the budgets, and the same rows in the `Decision Journal` tab. _Every number in your legal note flagged **VERIFY LIVE** with the date you confirmed it._
- **Day 4 — the gaps.** Read [[02-how-a-ceo-thinks|lesson 02]] and [[04-how-to-become-a-ceo-in-2026|lesson 04]]. Score the fourteen competencies with **one line of evidence per row** — an artefact, an incident, a number, a person — and apply the rule: no evidence, subtract a point. Expect it to be ugly; 3–4 on the technical rows and 1–2 on capital, sales, governance and comms is the normal starting shape, and it only becomes a problem if it is still that shape in eight quarters. Then the avoidance audit: commits in the last thirty days, dealer conversations in the last thirty days, and what was on the calendar the day before each of your five largest coding sessions. → a fourteen-row audit in C01 with two named 90-day development goals, one rule a co-founder can hold you to, one C29 entry naming the pattern.
- **Day 5 — sign it.** Fill **C01** end to end and circulate it. Take at least one change from **each** co-director; if neither offers one, ask again in writing, separately. Sign it, and put it on the next board agenda so it gets minuted — in a founder-run company the board minute is the forcing function you have to manufacture. → a signed C01 and a C29 entry recording the signing.
- **Day 6 — the founders' agreement.** Read [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]]. All three founders, one sitting, sixty minutes, phones away. Work **[[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]]**: the equity split **with the dated paragraph explaining why**; identical vesting and cliff for all three; who determines "cause" — a **named** third party; the four leaver events including the "stops working but stays" test; IP assignment listing the actual assets (repos, designs, both store accounts, domains); ROFR, tag and drag with a note to mirror them into the Articles; and a non-compete drafted rather than copied. Mark every item needing a professional **VERIFY LIVE** with the name of who will check it. → a filled C02 and **a one-page brief telling the CS/lawyer exactly what to draft**. _A signed 80% document beats a perfect unsigned one — with two exceptions: **IP assignment and vesting must be right the first time**, because they are the two that cannot be retrofitted once a dispute starts._
- **Day 7 — who decides.** First cut of **[[C03-decision-rights-matrix|C03]]** on the rows that sit above [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]] — cap table, strategy kernel and annual plan, partnerships that bind the company, pricing architecture, related-party matters, director changes, debt and guarantees — each with a level, who is informed and where it is recorded; then check it against T22 and fix **every** contradiction. Then the stop-deciding list: every recurring operational decision that currently routes through you, with **who becomes the decider, the date it transfers and the limit it transfers with**. Start with vendors, tool spend and finance operations; leave product and key relationships for a later quarter. Then the disagreement protocol, with the outside tiebreaker **named — one human being** — and walked backwards through a real disagreement you have already had. Finish with the eight-quarter competence plan and two sent messages: a peer-group invite with the format stated, and a mentor request naming the first agenda. → **C03** v0, the stop-list verbatim in [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]] §B, an agreed protocol with a named tiebreaker in T01 §E, twenty-six calendar blocks, and one invite with another human on it.

The question behind day 7 is the one that predicts whether a founder-led seat survives: **"which operational decisions will you stop making?"** — asked in writing, answered verbatim, with a transfer date per decision. _"None yet"_, logged with a review date, is a valid and useful answer; an empty list you never wrote is a story you tell yourself.

**Do not do in Week 0:** do not write the strategy kernel — it is month 1, and it needs a signed seat under it; do not announce a title change outside the company; do not open a conversation with an investor; do not restructure equity before the lawyer has read C02; do not start the reading canon beyond the two books in Q1 of the eight-quarter plan; and do not build anything. Week 0 is agreement, and the credibility to change things is earned by first writing down how things actually work.

## 4. Months 1–3 — the outward job

Phase 2 answers the four questions nobody else in the company can answer — **where are we going, why will it hold, who pays, and what happens when the cash runs down** — and then puts a lawful record around all four. The order is **diagnosis → model → price → economics → ladder → customers → the board.** Two of those links are hard gates; the rest is a strong preference.

## 5. Months 2–9 — the inward job, interleaved

Phase 3 does not wait for Phase 2 to finish, and the tables below start in **month 2**, while the market map and the board block are still running. That is what the lessons actually say, for five reasons.

- **Values must be written before the first hire.** After means the first employee's inference — formed in week one, from small evidence — becomes the definition ([[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §11).
- **Every hiring mechanism is ten times cheaper to install before the first hire than after the third**, which is why almost all of [[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|lesson 10]] is preparation.
- **The weekly note and the stakeholder update cannot be retrofitted.** Their value is the streak; a series starting in month nine has no month-two column to be read against.
- **The two live partner threads are consuming senior hours today.** Leaving them unscored for six months is not neutrality, it is an overdraft on the CEO's week.
- **The product bets are what the kernel's fifth coherent action constrains.** Writing them in month eight means eight months of engineering allocated by whoever asked most recently.

### 8.1 Reading the ownership column

**Read the columns rather than the rows.** The COO's version of this table says that every ritual has one owner and reads or writes a named artefact. The CEO's version says something narrower: **every ritual the CEO owns produces either a decision or a page.** A CEO ritual that produces neither is a meeting he should be attending rather than running, and the honest response is to hand the pen back to the seat that owns it.

Three boundaries worth being explicit about, because they are where the two courses touch and where a founder wearing both hats will blur them first.

## 10. The statutory and cash dates that jump the queue

**This timeline is a default order, not a law.** It assumes a company where nothing is on fire, which will not describe your month two. Three things legitimately reorder it, and only three: **a statutory due date, a money-affecting incident in a live tenant, and a rung firing on the 12/9/6/3 ladder.** Everything else waits its turn. The plan's job is to make sure that when the interruption ends, you know exactly which row you were on.

The first is the one that will actually bite, and it is the one with the largest disproportion between cost and consequence anywhere in this course: **three continuous years of unfiled annual returns disqualifies all three directors for five years, across every company any of you might ever hold** ([s.164(2)](https://ca2013.com/164-disqualifications-for-appointment-of-director)).

| **3 months**    | **Survival.** You negotiate from weakness at every table you sit at             | One job only: cash. Everything discretionary stops, including roadmap work that does not produce a payment                                                 | Raising money at this point and calling it a strategy. Taking a customer you would otherwise refuse | Everyone affected, **before they find out another way** |

**The bands are not universal.** Adjust them to VSYST's actual cash-conversion lag — the time from "dealer says yes" to money in the account, which for a sales-assisted district motion may be six to ten weeks. **Your red line must sit at least one full cash-conversion cycle above zero**, or the lever you pull at the red line cannot land in time. That single sentence is the reason the line is at 6 rather than 4. And the ladder is a communication device as much as a finance one: the commonest way a bootstrapped family company dies is not the arithmetic, it is that one founder knew and the others found out late.

### 10.4 The self-imposed dates that behave like statutory ones

Three more, dated by you, that are worth the same protection because nobody outside the company will chase them: the **partnership kill criterion** and its calendar event ([[14-partnerships-ecosystem-and-founder-relationships|lesson 14]] §5.3) — extended **once, in writing, with a reason, never twice**; the **quarterly capital review** against the trigger box; and the **annual persist-or-pivot verdict**, at which PERSIST must be argued for rather than assumed. Put all three in the calendar the day you write them, because a date that lives only in a document is a date that arrives without anyone noticing.

## 11. What winning looks like

The course has one definition of done, it lives in [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|lesson 20]] §10, and it is six items. Every one is **checkable by a stranger** — someone walks in, asks to see six things, and either they exist or they do not. **None requires outside capital, a senior hire you cannot afford, an OMC deal, a market turn, or anything a competitor, a customer or a government has to do for you.** All six are within reach of three people in Raipur inside twenty-four months, on the company's own cash. That is the argument for this list over a more impressive one: it is a list you can actually be held to.

| #     | Criterion                                                                                                    | What a stranger asks to see                                                                                                                                                             | Status today                                     |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **1** | A written strategy that **survived a year and one honest revision**                                          | [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager\|C04]] in **two dated versions a year apart**, plus a record of what changed, what evidence changed it, and what was abandoned                          | No — v1 does not exist yet                       |
| **2** | Enough paying dealers to be **default-alive**                                                                | The `Runway & Scenarios` tab showing profitability on current revenue and current growth, **without raising anything**                                                                  | No — pre-revenue                                 |
| **3** | A board that meets on schedule with real minutes, and **one voice that is not family**                       | Four dated sets of minutes in twelve months, each recording something **decided** and at least one **recorded disagreement**, with one non-family person present                        | No — family-only board                           |
| **4** | A team where at least one person **makes decisions the CEO would have made differently — and is allowed to** | A named decision-log entry made by somebody else that the CEO **disagreed with and did not reverse**                                                                                    | No — no non-founder decision-makers exist        |
| **5** | A decision journal **scored honestly, including the bad entries**                                            | [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]] with **20+ entries, the oldest a year old, scored**, of which at least three are marked _wrong_ and one _right for the wrong reason_ | No — started, not yet a year old, not yet scored |
| **6** | A CEO who **took two weeks off** and came back to a company that had decided things without them             | The queue page, the sealed prediction, the debrief, **and at least one change to [[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]] that came out of it**                                              | No — the silent week has not been run            |

**Six noes, and that is the correct state for a pre-revenue company a few months old.** Writing them down as noes is more useful than any encouraging paraphrase, because a _no_ is a thing that can become a _yes_ on a date.

Three rules for scoring, once a year, on a fixed date, filed with the self-review. **Binary** — yes with the evidence named, or no; no partial credit, because partial credit is the mechanism by which founders talk themselves into having done something they have not done. **Name the evidence, not the effort** — "minutes dated 14 Apr, 12 Jul, 9 Oct, 15 Jan; dissent recorded 12 Jul; advisor present at all four", never "governance has really improved". And **read the six aloud to the other two directors**, with the evidence, in the same sitting as the self-review.

Then pick **one** _no_ and make it next year's. Not all of them. Criteria **1, 5 and 6 are available to VSYST this year, at three people, pre-revenue, for effectively no money** — which is precisely why they are where to start, and precisely why founders postpone them in favour of the ones that depend on revenue arriving.

Read the six together and they say one thing: **a company that thinks, decides, remembers and continues without its founder in the room.** [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|Lesson 20]] §10 carries the full treatment — the counterfeit that looks like each criterion, the nine things deliberately _not_ on the list and why each is excluded, and the closing argument about doing it on purpose while you are still there and still want the job. Do not take the summary above as a substitute for reading it; the counterfeits are the half that changes behaviour.

## 12. If you only have one day, one week, or one month

Three triage paths, for a reader who cannot do the whole course now. All three converge on the same four templates the [[CEO-Docs/toolkit/index|toolkit index]] names as the ones that matter if you only ever fill four: **[[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]]** (the seat exists), **[[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]]** (the direction is written), **[[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]]** (cash will not surprise you), and **[[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]** (you will get better at deciding). Everything else compounds off those.

### 12.1 One day — about five hours

The test of this day is that at the end of it there is **something signed, something sent, something computed and something started.**

| Time       | Do this                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Why this and not something else                                                                                                       | Leaves                                                |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **90 min** | Fill the **[[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan\|C01]]** mission box, the four-plus-one jobs, the five "only me" items and the holding-temporarily table with a successor condition per row. **Read it aloud to both co-directors and get a yes, a no or an edit — not silence** | Nothing else in the course works without it, and it is the only artefact whose absence makes every later conversation a renegotiation | A defined seat, witnessed                             |
| **45 min** | Say **cash, net burn and runway from memory**, write them down, then compute all three from the bank statements. **Note the gap**                                                                                                                                             | The gap is the exercise. A founder running the company on a stale runway number is the failure with no warning shot                   | Three true numbers, and a weekly reminder to re-check |
| **30 min** | Open **[[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]]**: three retrospective entries — start with the per-GSTIN web-only billing decision — and the next five decisions classified one-way or two-way with a time budget each                                             | The highest-return habit in the course and the only one that compounds. It costs nothing and needs nobody's permission                | An open journal and five matching calendar blocks     |
| **30 min** | **Send the crux question** by WhatsApp to both co-directors with a deadline of tonight: _what is the hardest part of making this business work — not the most urgent, the hardest._ No hints, no examples                                                                     | If the three answers differ you have found the argument every resource fight for the next year would otherwise have been a proxy for  | Three answers in flight                               |
| **30 min** | **File DPIIT recognition and Udyam.** Both free, both online                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | They take longer to be granted than you think, and DPIIT gates several later things                                                   | Two acknowledgement numbers                           |
| **20 min** | Send the CS the three questions on what you are legally appointed as                                                                                                                                                                                                          | The cheapest risk reduction available to you, and it runs on their clock, not yours                                                   | A sent email                                          |
| **20 min** | Put the **Friday money block** and **two field days** in next week's calendar, and send the two field-day invites to **named dealers**                                                                                                                                        | The two blocks with the weakest natural defence. Booked, they are the hardest in the week; unbooked, the softest                      | A week with two customer days in it                   |
| **15 min** | Merge and deploy the ledger correctness fix if it is still sitting on a branch                                                                                                                                                                                                | A correctness fix that exists and is not deployed leaves the corrupting path running                                                  | A deployed fix                                        |

### 12.2 One week — about fifteen hours across seven days

Everything in §12.1, plus the four things that are **cheapest today and impossible later**.

1. **The founders' agreement sitting** — all three, one hour, one sitting, phones away. Fill [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]] and send the lawyer a one-page brief. Non-negotiable: **the IP assignment and the vesting clauses must be right the first time**, because they are the two that cannot be retrofitted once a dispute starts. Between family founders a signature feels absurd; that is exactly why it is the highest-value hour in the week.
2. **Name the outside tiebreaker.** One human being, by name, in the founders' agreement, for deadlock. Fifteen minutes today, an impossible conversation in eighteen months. If it takes more than fifteen minutes, the disagreement you just uncovered is the most important thing you learned this week.
3. **[[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]], drafted and circulated.** Take [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]] §9.1's filled draft, change what you disagree with, fill every confidence percentage with the number you would actually bet at — and **do not sign it until both co-directors have disagreed with something in writing.**
4. **The board year, and the minute book.** Four dates in three calendars, a 7-day notice reminder on each, the CS asked which meeting regime applies, and page 1 of the minute book dated. Reconstruct nothing; start clean and note the start date honestly.

Plus two habits that only work if they start now: **the first weekly note**, same day and time from now on, with a non-empty `NOT DOING` section; and **the three numbers said out loud every Monday**.

What you have at the end of the week: a defined seat, a written direction two people have argued with, a founders' agreement in the lawyer's hands, a lawful board year, an open journal, and two habits with a streak of one.

### 12.3 One month — about forty hours

Everything above, plus weeks 1–4 of §7, plus six additions chosen because each is cheap, each has no prerequisite, and each becomes expensive to install later.

| Add                                                                                                                                                                     | Time   | Why now                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[[C05-choice-cascade-worksheet\|C05]] and [[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments\|C06]]**, with the break-even dealer count **on a physical wall** and in founder-weeks | 90 min | Two numbers three people can hold while driving: _are we a business?_ and _are we learning?_ The second is `K / (K+A+U)`, dated, in the `Strategy Kernel` tab                                                  |
| The **accidental-rewards audit** — rewarded, tolerated, punished (including silently), from named incidents in the last 90 days                                         | 40 min | No prerequisite, and it must be done **before** you read anyone's draft values or the draft contaminates it                                                                                                    |
| The **pre-mortem**, silent-first, all three, on paper                                                                                                                   | 75 min | It is _"today + 24 months and the company failed — why?"_. Ten minutes of writing alone is the whole mechanism, and it is the only reliable way to get the domain-expert director's real opinion on the record |
| The **escalation page**, written and pinned in WhatsApp                                                                                                                 | 40 min | The highest-return forty minutes in [[16-decisions-delegation-and-not-being-the-bottleneck\|lesson 16]]. Then ask both co-directors separately what would make them call you tonight, and compare              |
| **One decision handed over**, with all four parts and a limit written into [[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]]                                                          | 45 min | One, not two. Start with the least emotionally loaded — refunds and credit notes inside a fixed monthly ceiling — and then do not touch it for two cycles                                                      |
| The **bus-factor document**, first pass                                                                                                                                 | 45 min | Expect half the rows blank. **The blanks are the finding**, and each is a live single point of failure. Read it aloud to both co-directors and seal a copy with the CS                                         |

What you have at the end of the month: **the direction written and argued with, the cash mapped with pre-committed actions at four dated rungs, the ways the company dies ranked and owned, one decision that is no longer yours, and a written answer to what happens if you do not come back.**

That is not the course. It is the load-bearing tenth of it, and it is enough that the other nine-tenths have somewhere to attach.
