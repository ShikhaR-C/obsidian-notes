# C10 — Founder-Led Sales Pipeline, Script and Objection Bank

_Toolkit · fills exercises 11.2, 11.4 and 11.5 in [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|07 — Customers, Markets and Founder-Led Sales]] · Owner: CEO, personally, until both gates in lesson 07 §9 are passed · Cadence: touched the same evening as every field visit; reviewed for 20 minutes every Friday · Workbook tab: `Pipeline` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

This is the sheet the CEO carries into a district and fills in from the forecourt. It holds four things: **the eight stages a DZZLO deal actually passes through**, **the first-call script**, **the eight-minute demo run-of-show**, and **the objection bank** — the ten sentences a fuel dealer will say to you, what each one really means, the answer that works, and the piece of paper you leave behind.

It exists because founder-led selling is not a phase to survive; it is the mechanism by which the strategy is tested against a person who can say no. A hired rep cannot do it, because a rep can only sell what has already been learned — and nothing has been learned yet ([First Round Review — the founder-led growth playbook](https://review.firstround.com/founder-led-growth-playbook/); [SaaStr — should a founder handle sales themself?](https://www.saastr.com/should-a-startup-founder-handle-sales-when-first-getting-started/)). Paul Graham's version is the one to keep in your head on a bad Tuesday in a village 70 km from Raipur: the things that do not scale are the things that produce a company ([Paul Graham — Do Things that Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)).

Two disciplines are enforced here and everything else is commentary. The first is Neil Rackham's distinction, from observation of tens of thousands of real calls: every conversation ends in an **order**, an **advance** (a specific action with a date), a **continuation** (it will go on, nothing was agreed) or a **no-sale**, and founders systematically record continuations as progress ([Huthwaite International — SPIN Selling](https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-selling)). The second is Rob Fitzpatrick's: a real signal costs the customer something — time, reputation or money ([The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/); [Unusual Ventures — five takeaways](https://www.unusual.vc/rob-fitzpatricks-mom-test/)).

**Which produces the rule this template exists to enforce: a row with a blank Next step or a blank Next date is not an open deal. It is a dead deal you have not admitted to.** Mark it lost, write the reason in his words, and get the hour back.

**Ownership boundary, so the two courses do not fight.** [[11-revenue-operations-and-partnerships|COO lesson 11]] §2 owns the **system of record** — six stages in ERPNext, exit criteria, mandatory source and lost-reason fields, the weekly review. C10 is the **CEO's working sheet**: the words you say, the finer stage grain a founder needs while the motion is still being learned, and the objections you have personally heard. Column 2 of the stage table below maps every C10 stage onto a COO stage, and where they disagree, **ERPNext is right and this sheet is wrong**.

## When to use

- **First fill: the week you finish [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]]** — lesson 07 exercise 11.2 hands you six scored dealers; get to ten live rows before the following Friday.
- **The same evening as every field visit, call or WhatsApp reply.** Not "when I get time". A visit log written three days later is fiction.
- **Every Friday, 20 minutes**, against the COO's pipeline review: what moved, what has a blank next step, what gets killed.
- **After every ten conversations**, re-read the objection bank and add any genuinely new row. The day you go twenty conversations without adding a row is gate one in lesson 07 §9.1 — the motion has become teachable.
- **Feeds** [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] (close rate, founder days per deal → CAC) and [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] (new paying firms per month, the single most argued-about driver in the model).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **A stage is a fact, not a feeling.** Each stage below has an exit criterion that is a checkable event. "He seemed positive" moves nothing. This is the COO's law and it is not negotiable here.
2. **Every row has an owner, a next step and a next date, or it is not a row.** One name — whoever holds the next step. A next step is an action _you_ will take on a date, not a state you hope he reaches.
3. **Record the advance, not the mood.** Second meeting with a date; an introduction he makes himself; real data handed over; a deposit. "Send me the details" is not an advance and does not move a stage.
4. **Two-thirds of your hours belong above 70 on the ICP score.** The rubric is in [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]]. Founder-hours are the scarcest capital the company owns; a 45-score dealer costs the same hours as an 80 and closes at a fraction of the rate.
5. **No price on the first call, no PDF before a visit, and never "when suits you" — always two time choices.** Price stays quoted rather than published while the number is still being learned ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]]).
6. **Never answer an objection you have not first repeated back.** "Matlab aap ye keh rahe hain ki…" Half of what sounds like an objection is a question, and half of what sounds like a question is a polite no.
7. **Never invent an answer in the room.** Say you do not know, write it down, answer on WhatsApp inside 24 hours. In a district where everyone talks, that is the cheapest reputation you will ever buy.
8. **Log every loss with the reason in his own words.** Lost reasons are the cheapest market research available to you, and "went silent" is a reason — record it as one, do not leave the deal open to avoid writing it.
9. **Ten new names a week, with a pump and a route to the owner.** Sourcing is never the constraint; _qualified_ sourcing is. Warm and vernacular beats digital by an order of magnitude for this buyer — Indian B2B cold outreach converts around 2.23% ([Zeliq — B2B conversion rates by industry](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/b2b-conversion-rates-by-industry)) against ~3.4% average cold-email reply rates ([Instantly — 2026 cold email benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)), while an association office-bearer's introduction converts many times better ([ETV Bharat — AIPDA conclave](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/petrol-dealers-push-for-people-friendly-reforms-no-more-dirty-toilets-or-faulty-meters-enn25071806147), 2025).
10. **Count the cost of a meeting and put it in the sheet.** Indian field sales meetings run roughly ₹800–2,500 each ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cost-per-sales-meeting); [SalesHive](https://saleshive.com/glossary/cost-per-meeting/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** against your own fuel bills. That number is what makes cluster routing a strategy rather than a preference.

## Template

**A. Stage definitions — eight stages, each with an exit criterion**

| #   | C10 stage         | COO / ERPNext stage                 | Enters when                                                                 | Exits only when                                                                                                    |
| --- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **Identified**    | Lead                                | A named owner, a named pump, a phone number, a **source**, and an ICP score | A real two-way conversation exists. A broadcast message sent is not contact                                        |
| 2   | **Conversation**  | Contacted                           | He replied — call answered, voice note returned, or he talked at a visit    | **A demo is booked with a date**, with the owner present                                                           |
| 3   | **Demo**          | Demo                                | The eight minutes have been delivered to the decision-maker                 | An **advance** with a date: pilot, son/accountant meeting, top-10 list, or a named introduction                    |
| 4   | **Pilot agreed**  | Demo → Pilot boundary (Opportunity) | A pilot start date exists in writing, in his WhatsApp thread                | Tenant created, first real transaction entered, onboarding visit done                                              |
| 5   | **Pilot running** | Pilot                               | Real orders, rates or invoices are flowing on his data                      | Activation reached — ≥1 customer linked and ≥1 invoice inside 14 days                                              |
| 6   | **Verbal yes**    | Pilot (quote accepted, unpaid)      | He has said yes and the quote is accepted                                   | **Money received.** Nothing else exits this stage. This is the stage founders lie to themselves in                 |
| 7   | **Paid**          | Paid                                | Cash against a GST invoice                                                  | Handed to the success cadence at four weeks of steady use ([[10-customer-operations-support-and-success\|COO 10]]) |
| —   | **Lost**          | Lost (terminal)                     | A no, or a blank next date older than 30 days                               | Never. Reason recorded in his words                                                                                |

Note what stage 6 is for. A verbal yes with no money is the most dangerous row on this sheet, because it feels like a win and forecasts like one. **No advance, no paid** — that is the [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]]'s oldest rule and it is the reason "Active" belongs to the COO and not to you.

**B. The pipeline row — the `Pipeline` tab columns**

```
Dealer | Pump & village | Owner name | Phone | Source | ICP score | Stage
GSTINs | Est. ₹/mo | Owner (us) | NEXT STEP | NEXT DATE | Last touch
Advance won (what it cost him) | Founder hours to date | Lost reason
```

Two computed cells at the bottom of the tab, and they are the only two that matter: **close rate** (Paid ÷ deals that reached Conversation, over 20+ conversations) and **founder hours per closed deal**. Both feed CAC in [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]].

**C. The first call — six minutes, and you will want to make it twenty**

First contact is a 30–40 second WhatsApp voice note in Hindi, in your own voice, sent 10:30–12:00 or 16:00–18:00, never on the 15th or 30th. Then:

```
0:00  "Namaste [Name] ji, main [name], VSYST Technologies, Raipur se.
       [Referrer] ji ne number diya. Do minute hain?"
       -> Name, company, REFERRER, permission. Not the product.

0:30  "Abhi kitne udhaar customers hain, aur unka hisaab kahan rakhte hain?"
       -> Then STOP TALKING. Count to five after he finishes.

2:00  "Pichhli baar kisi customer ke saath rate ya amount ka jhagda
       kab hua tha?"
       -> A specific past event, never a hypothetical. Write down the
          name and the rupee figure he says.

3:30  ONE sentence, in the words he just used, plus one local name:
       "Hum wahi hisaab phone pe le aate hain -- [local dealer] ji ke
        yahan chal raha hai."
       -> No features. No "platform". No app store.

4:15  THE ASK IS A VISIT:
       "Main aapke pump pe aake das minute mein aapke apne ek customer
        ka ledger bana ke dikha doon? Mangalwar subah ya Guruwar shaam?"

5:15  Confirm, then write the date into the WhatsApp thread before you
       put the phone down.
```

Three branches worth rehearsing. _"Kitna hai?"_ → "Pehle dekh lijiye, phir batata hoon — abhi bataunga to aap galat cheez ke liye haan ya na bolenge." _"WhatsApp pe bhej dijiye"_ → row 5 of the objection bank; do not send. _"Manager se baat kar lijiye"_ → "Bilkul, unse bhi. Par das minute aapke saamne — wo aapka paisa hai."

**D. The demo — eight minutes, rehearsed against a timer**

Owners grant eight minutes, not thirty. Two screens land and everything else is decoration: **the ledger** and **the rate-confirmation window**. Lead with the ledger because it needs nobody's cooperation; the rate window comes second and lands harder, because it kills the specific dispute he described to you on the phone. **Before you open anything, ask for one real customer's name and last month's rough figure, and enter it live.**

| Min | On screen                                                             | What you say                                                                                                                                            | What you are reading                                                    |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| —   | Paper only                                                            | "Aapka sabse bada udhaar customer kaun hai? Kitna baaki hai abhi?" Write the ₹ figure between you                                                       | Whether he knows it without looking. If he doesn't, _that_ is the pitch |
| 0–1 | **His customer's ledger**, entered live                               | "Ye [customer] ka khaata. Ye balance, ye pichhla invoice, ye pichhla payment."                                                                          | Does the son lean in? Does the father pick up the phone?                |
| 1–3 | **Rate master + the 10 PM–6 AM confirmation window**                  | "Aaj raat rate daala. Unko notification. Unhone confirm kiya — timestamp ke saath. Subah 6 baje lock. Ab 'humne ye rate nahi maana tha' nahi ho sakta." | The anxiety-killer for the dispute he named on the call                 |
| 3–5 | **Dispatch + driver OTP**                                             | "Gaadi nikli, driver ko OTP, customer ne delivery pe OTP diya. Bina OTP delivery confirm nahi."                                                         | The theft or shortage story usually comes out here                      |
| 5–6 | **Invoice** — PRODUCT / CASH_REIMBURSE / GST, TCS flag                | Two sentences, no more: "Invoice apne aap. GST sahi. TCS ka flag apne aap."                                                                             | Whether the accountant is the blocker. He speaks here                   |
| 6–7 | **The statement PDF, forwarded to his own WhatsApp, in front of him** | "Ye main abhi aapke WhatsApp pe bhej raha hoon."                                                                                                        | The leave-behind. He forwards it — that is your second meeting          |
| 7–8 | Phone down                                                            | The ask, in order: pilot with a date → son/accountant meeting with a date → top-10 customer list → a named introduction → a recorded no                 | The advance                                                             |

**Never in a first demo:** the DIP module, the scope matrix, the API, the roadmap, or the word "platform". **Never demand an install** — demo on your device or the browser.

Hum uske pehle wala kaam karte hain — order, rate, dispatch, ledger. Data baad mein Tally mein hi jaata hai." Aap ya manager — do screen. Main pehle hafte roz aake baithunga." Then **name the person** at his pump who will operate it                                                                                                                                     | Printed onboarding checklist ([[10-customer-operations-support-and-success\|COO 10]] §7)                                                                                                                           |
| _"Purana data kaun daalega?"_                             | A boring, real, deal-killing blocker                                             | "Hum daalenge. Aap top 10 customers ki list aur balance de dijiye." Then actually do it, on-site                                                                                                                                                                                              | A blank one-page "top 10 customers + balance" sheet, left with him                                                                                                                                                 |

Two closing notes on the bank. Reciprocity and social proof are the two levers that actually move this buyer, and both are earned by doing something for him before you ask ([Cialdini's principles of persuasion](https://www.cognitigence.com/blog/cialdini-7-principles-of-persuasion)) — which is why the proof column is a list of things you _give_, not things you _claim_. And every row is an instance of the same underlying question: what job is he hiring the software to do, and what is he firing to make room for it ([HBR — Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done), 2016).

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All figures illustrative.** Week 6 in the Raipur district. Ten live rows; here are three.

| Dealer                     | ICP | Stage         | Next step                                       | Next date | Advance won                                                  |
| -------------------------- | --- | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Agrawal Fuels, Dhamtari Rd | 82  | Pilot running | Sit at pump 8 AM, enter 3 more credit customers | 04 Sep    | Gave top-10 list + last month's DSR page (time + risk)       |
| Sai Petroleum, Abhanpur    | 74  | Demo          | Meet the son + the CA, both present             | 06 Sep    | Forwarded the statement PDF to his own accountant unprompted |
| Mahaveer Diesel, Kurud     | 68  | **Lost**      | —                                               | —         | "Sirf cash customers hain" — disqualified on ICP row 1       |

The arithmetic that comes out of the tab after 22 conversations: 4 paid, close rate **18%**, founder hours per closed deal **31** — which is 3.9 founder-days, and at a ₹4,000 loaded day rate plus ₹3,500 travel, a CAC near **₹1,05,000**. That number goes straight into [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] and it is the number that says the _channel_ is wrong, not the price: the two deals sourced through the district association office-bearer took 9 and 11 hours; the two sourced by cold pump visits took 51. **The sheet did not tell the CEO to sell harder. It told him to stop driving.**

The uncomfortable row is Sai Petroleum. It sat at "Demo, next step blank" for 24 days while the CEO remembered it as "warm". Under rule 2 it should have been killed on day 30 — and the honest reading is that a fifth row, not shown, _was_ killed that Friday after 41 blank days, freeing six hours that produced the Agrawal pilot. Ninety-eight paying dealers is the default-alive count in C12 — the pass-two figure, once the association channel and the SMS and support fixes are in; the honest pass-one number is 124. There are more than a lakh retail outlets in India ([Energy Watch — India's petrol pump count crosses 1 lakh](https://www.energywatch.in/oil-and-gas/indias-petrol-pump-count-crosses-1-lakh-nearing-us-china-in-network-scale); [PPAC — retail outlets](https://ppac.gov.in/infrastructure/retail-outlets)). The constraint was never names. It was hours spent on rows that were already dead.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | Why it happens                               | The fix                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Blank next step, deal still "open"                 | Killing it means admitting the hour is gone  | Rule 2. A blank next date older than 30 days auto-moves to Lost on Friday                |
| Recording a continuation as an advance             | "It went well" feels like progress           | Rackham's four outcomes. Write which one it was, in the row                              |
| Verbal yes forecast as revenue                     | It is the nicest conversation in the job     | Stage 6 exits on **money**, nothing else                                                 |
| Sending the PDF because he asked                   | It feels responsive                          | It is a polite no with a delivery mechanism. Ask for the visit twice, then record the no |
| Quoting a price on the first call                  | He asked directly and silence is awkward     | Rehearse the deflection line. One nervous quote anchors a whole district                 |
| Demo runs 25 minutes                               | Everything in the product feels important    | Timer. Two screens. The rest is decoration                                               |
| Discounting the moment "mehanga" is said           | It is the fastest way to end the discomfort  | Price against the receivable. Discounts are a CAC problem, not a pricing problem         |
| Answering an objection you misheard                | You have heard it nine times before          | Repeat it back first, every time                                                         |
| Chasing 45-score dealers because they are friendly | Low-ICP dealers are often the most available | Two-thirds of hours above 70. Availability is not fit                                    |
| One CEO who can deliver the demo                   | It is faster to do it yourself               | Gate one in lesson 07 §9.1 needs two. Record the date the second person delivered it     |
| The sheet updated on Sunday from memory            | The evening is tiring                        | Same evening, in the car, five rows at a time                                            |
