# C24 — Crisis Comms and Incident Statement Kit

_Toolkit · fills §4.6 of [[12-communication-the-ceo-as-chief-storyteller|12 — Communication: The CEO as Chief Storyteller]] and the crisis half of [[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things|18 — Risk, Crisis and the Hard Things]] · Owner: the CEO speaks; nobody else does · Cadence: written once in calm, re-read every quarter, opened only in an event · Legal clocks tracked on the `Board Calendar` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Four pre-written first statements for the four crises this company can actually have. They are fill-in-the-blank on purpose. **The entire value of this document is that it was written on a calm Tuesday and not at 02:40 during the event**, because at 02:40 you will write something you cannot retract, and the one thing you cannot do in a crisis is un-say a sentence.

Split the ownership before anything happens. **[[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]] owns the machinery**: severity levels, the on-call rota, the timestamped log, containment, the blameless postmortem within five working days, and the routine support comms that go with a ticket ([Google SRE Book — Postmortem Culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/)). **This document owns what is said to humans** — the dealer whose money is wrong, the regulator, the partner, the team, and in the worst case the press. Lesson 18 §4.2 already states the rule: on a breach, the COO runs containment and the regulatory clocks, and **the CEO owns notification, the statement and the commercial consequences**. A six-hour reporting clock is a mechanism. What you tell forty dealers is not.

Two craft rules from the crisis-communication literature carry almost all of the weight, and both are counterintuitive under stress. **Speak inside the first hour, even with almost nothing to say** — the first response typically lands within sixty minutes and the gap gets filled with speculation if you are not in it ([Yes& CommCore — The First Hour of a Crisis](https://www.yesandcommcore.com/insights/the-first-hour-of-a-crisis-a-communication-checklist-for-leaders/); [PRSA — Crisis Communications Checklist](https://jobs.prsa.org/career-resources/finding-talent-10/crisis-communications-checklist-24-hour-response-protocol-405)). And **never speculate on cause, and never offer a reassurance you cannot prove.** A holding statement acknowledges, shows empathy, states one confirmed fact, commits to a next update at a named time, and stops ([Pagefield — holding statements](https://www.pagefield.com/news-insight/what-are-holding-statements-in-crisis-communications/)). "We believe this was limited to one tenant" said at hour one and contradicted at hour nine costs more than the incident did.

## When to use

- **The moment a SEV1 is declared** ([[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]]) — or the moment you are personally certain something is wrong and the severity call has not happened yet. The statement clock does not wait for the severity clock.
- **Any confirmed wrong stored financial figure in a live tenant**, whatever its rupee size. That is a company-level event at VSYST by written rule ([[13-product-and-technology-leadership-for-a-technical-ceo|lesson 13]] §7.3–7.4).
- **Any suspected unauthorised access to dealer data**, including a tenant seeing another tenant's data. Suspicion is enough to start the clocks; confirmation is not required first.
- **Before you answer a journalist, an OMC officer, a dealer association or an app-store reviewer** about anything unresolved. One sentence said unprepared becomes the quotation.
- **Not for a SEV3, a slow screen, or a single confused user.** Those are the COO's support path ([[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]]). Reaching for this kit for a routine ticket teaches the team that the kit means nothing.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One voice. The CEO.** At three founders the temptation is that whoever is free replies. Do not. Every external word in an incident comes from one named person, and the other two directors' job is to say "the CEO will respond by <time>". Write that name into the kit today.
2. **Fastest first, thinnest content.** The first statement's job is to exist, be accurate and set the next update time. It is allowed to contain almost nothing.
3. **State what you know, what you do not know, and when you will next speak.** Those three, always, in that order. The third is the one founders drop, and it is the one that stops the phone ringing.
4. **Never speculate on cause.** Not "a third-party library", not "a server issue", not "human error". You will be wrong roughly half the time and the correction is a second news event.
5. **Never quantify before you have counted.** "Some dealers" is honest at hour one. "Fewer than ten dealers" at hour one, revised to forty at hour nine, is the sentence that ends trust.
6. **Money errors get a named, corrected figure in the founder's own message — before the dealer finds it.** A dealer told by the founder, with the correction already in hand, usually trusts you more afterwards. A dealer who finds it himself never trusts a number you produce again ([[13-product-and-technology-leadership-for-a-technical-ceo|lesson 13]] §7.3).
7. **Do not apologise for the wrong thing.** Apologise for the impact on them, specifically and once. Do not apologise for "any inconvenience caused", which reads as a form letter, and do not perform contrition in place of a plan.
8. **Legal clocks run in parallel with human ones and neither waits for the other.** CERT-In's directions under §70B of the IT Act require mandatory reporting of specified cyber incidents **within six hours of becoming aware** ([CERT-In Directions, 28 April 2022](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf); practitioner reading at [Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/How-to-comply-with-CERT-Ins-new-six-hour-time-frame-to-report-cyber-incidents.pdf)) — **VERIFY LIVE** with counsel which incident types bind VSYST and what the current form requires.
9. **Nothing goes out that a director has not read.** One person writes, a second reads before send. The reader's only job is to find the sentence that will be quoted back at you.
10. **Log every statement, verbatim, with its timestamp**, in the same timeline the COO is keeping. The statement record is part of the postmortem and, in a regulated event, part of the file.

## The DPDP question — verify live, every time

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 puts the obligation squarely on the Data Fiduciary: §8(6) requires intimation of a personal data breach **to the Board and to each affected Data Principal**, in the manner prescribed ([DPDP Act 2023, official text — MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). The prescription arrived with the **Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025**, notified by MeitY vide G.S.R. 846(E) on **13 November 2025**, with a phased runway — most substantive obligations reportedly falling due around **13 May 2027** ([S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/); [Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025)). The reported shape is intimation to affected principals **without delay**, and a detailed report to the Board **within 72 hours**, with **no materiality threshold** — ten records and ten million records carry the same duty — and penalties in the Schedule running to the order of ₹200 crore for failure to notify ([Saikrishna & Associates — intimation of personal data breach](https://www.saikrishnaassociates.com/intimation-of-personal-data-breach-under-the-draft-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)).

**Every number, date and threshold in that paragraph is `VERIFY LIVE` with a data-protection lawyer at the moment of the event, not from this page.** The Rules are new, phased, and being read differently by different firms. Three questions decide your obligations and only counsel can answer them: (a) is the exposed data _personal data_ of an identifiable individual, or the dealer firm's commercial data — a fleet's outstanding balance may be both; (b) is VSYST a Data Fiduciary or a Data Processor for that particular field, and what does the dealer contract say; (c) does the CERT-In six-hour clock bite independently of DPDP. Get the answers written down **now**, while nothing is on fire, and keep counsel's name and mobile number in [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession|C30]].

## Template — the four first statements

Common skeleton, used by all four. Fill the blanks, delete nothing.

```
<Greeting, named person>. This is <CEO name> from VSYST.

WHAT WE KNOW      <one or two confirmed facts, including time and scope as
                  far as counted. no cause.>
WHAT IT MEANS     <what you should do, or need not do, right now.>
WHAT WE DON'T     <named unknowns. "we do not yet know X" is a strong sentence.>
WHAT WE'RE DOING  <action underway, present tense, owned by a name.>
NEXT UPDATE       <a clock time, not "shortly".>
Reach me directly on <number>.
```

| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Breach of dealer financial data**                    | CEO only. COO runs containment + clocks ([[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]])                                                                               | Internal: immediate. Affected dealers: within **2 hours** of confirming _any_ exposure. Regulators: on counsel's clock (CERT-In 6h; DPDP §8(6)) | That we have detected unauthorised access; the categories of data possibly involved; what we have already shut off; what the dealer should do today (change passwords, watch for calls impersonating us) | Cause. Attribution. "Only X dealers." "No financial data was accessed." Any claim you cannot prove in writing within the hour     | Hour 2, hour 12, then daily at a fixed time until the postmortem is published                                                   |
| **2** | **Ledger / billing error misstating a dealer's money** | CEO personally, to each affected dealer, **by phone then in writing**                                                                                    | Within **24 hours** of confirming the figure is wrong, and **before** the next statement or invoice goes out                                    | The exact wrong figure, the exact corrected figure, the period affected, whether anything was shown to _his_ customer, and that the correction is already made                                           | "A minor discrepancy." "Rounding." Blame on his staff, his data or his entry. Any softening of the rupee amount                   | Same day written summary with the corrected statement attached; day 7 with the fix deployed; day 30 with the class audit result |
| **3** | **Extended outage in the 22:00–06:00 rate window**     | CEO. The domain director calls the three largest dealers personally                                                                                      | **Within 30 minutes** of the window being unusable — this is the one surface where being late is worse than being wrong                         | That the window is down; the fallback in one line (confirm by WhatsApp/voice and we will backfill); the time of the next update                                                                          | "Should be back shortly." An ETA you have not verified. Any suggestion the dealer should just wait                                | Every 30 minutes until the window is usable again — on the clock you promised, even when there is nothing new; then a written note by 09:00 covering cause and backfill status, and a postmortem ([[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]]) within 5 days |
| **4** | **Founder or partner dispute becoming public**         | **One** director, agreed in advance and named in [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist\|C02]] — if the CEO is a party, the other named director | Only after counsel and a night's sleep. Speed is _not_ a virtue here                                                                            | As close to nothing as possible: that there is a disagreement between shareholders, that the company continues to operate and serve dealers normally, that a named person is available                   | Anything about the other party. Anything about money, equity or the family. "We are confident it will be resolved." Any adjective | Silence, unless a specific factual claim about the _company_ is made publicly. Then correct that one fact, once, and stop       |

**Statement 3 has a subtlety worth naming.** The 22:00–06:00 confirmation window is the only surface a dealer must touch every single day, and an outage inside it does not merely inconvenience him — it removes tomorrow morning's rate agreement, which is the thing that prevents a dispute four weeks later. So the outage message is not "we are down", it is **"here is how you still get a confirmed rate tonight, and we will backfill it into the record."** An outage with a working fallback is an inconvenience. The same outage with no fallback offered is a reason to keep using WhatsApp forever.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**Crisis 2, drawn from the real one.** In August 2026 a voucher received two status-update calls five minutes apart; the deployed code posted the amount on every call with no prior-status guard, and ₹50,000 of credit posted twice into a stored monthly bucket. The stored total read ₹6,70,000 against ₹6,20,000 of actual documents, and the heal routine had not run for that relationship in two months. **Nothing crashed. No alert fired.** It surfaced because a human looked at a panel ([[13-product-and-technology-leadership-for-a-technical-ceo|lesson 13]] §7.3). This is the class of failure that ends companies that hold money — Knight Capital lost about \$440 million in roughly 45 minutes on 1 August 2012 from a deployment fault ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Capital_Group)); TSB's 2018 migration cost a £48.65 million fine and its CEO's job ([Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSB_Bank_(United_Kingdom)>)) — **VERIFY LIVE** both figures if you quote them.

The message, sent by the CEO, on WhatsApp, after the phone call:

> _Ramesh bhai — Shikhar from VSYST. Calling to tell you something before you see it._
>
> _**What we know:** between 6 and 13 August, our system recorded one ₹50,000 payment from Sharma Transport twice. Your August ledger for them showed **₹6,70,000**. The correct figure is **₹6,20,000**. It affected this one relationship on your account. Two statements were generated in that period — I have checked, and neither was sent to Sharma Transport._
>
> _**What it means:** the corrected statement is attached. Nothing else on your account is affected. You do not need to do anything._
>
> _**What we don't know yet:** whether any other account has the same problem. I am checking every account today and will tell you the answer either way, even if the answer is that yours was the only one._
>
> _**What we're doing:** the code path that allowed the double entry was fixed and deployed on 22 August. I am personally auditing every place in the system that writes a balance for the same mistake._
>
> _**Next update:** by 6 p.m. tomorrow, with the result of the account check. My number is below — call me any time, including tonight._
>
> _This was our error, not yours, and I am sorry it reached your ledger._

Read what it does not do. It does not say "a minor discrepancy". It does not explain idempotency. It does not promise that no other account is affected, because at the time of writing that had not been counted. It names the wrong number and the right number in bold, which is the only part the dealer will read twice. And the apology is one sentence, at the end, about **his ledger** — not about "any inconvenience".

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | What it looks like                                              | Fix                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Waiting for full facts**                      | Nothing said for nine hours                                     | Rule 2. Hour one, thin and accurate, with a next-update time                        |
| **Speculating on cause**                        | "Looks like a server issue"                                     | Rule 4. You will be wrong, and the correction is a second event                     |
| **Reassuring beyond the evidence**              | "No financial data was accessed"                                | Rule 5. Say what you have counted, and say what you have not                        |
| **Softening a money error**                     | "A small discrepancy in your August figures"                    | Rule 6. The exact wrong figure and the exact right figure, in bold                  |
| **Three founders replying**                     | Two different scopes in one WhatsApp group                      | Rule 1. One voice, named in advance                                                 |
| **Writing the statement at 02:40**              | An unretractable sentence                                       | That is what this file is for. Fill it in calm                                      |
| **Treating the legal clock as the comms clock** | Waiting for counsel before telling a dealer his ledger is wrong | Parallel tracks. Counsel governs the regulator; you govern the dealer               |
| **A public reply in a founder dispute**         | A LinkedIn post explaining your side                            | Row 4. Correct a factual claim about the company, once, and stop                    |
| **No fallback offered in an outage**            | "We are working on it"                                          | Give him a way to get a confirmed rate tonight and promise the backfill             |
| **A statement nobody logged**                   | Nobody can reconstruct what was said when                       | Rule 10. Verbatim, timestamped, into the [[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]] timeline  |
| **Confusing the postmortem with the statement** | Publishing a root-cause analysis to dealers                     | [[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]] owns the technical record; this owns the human one |
