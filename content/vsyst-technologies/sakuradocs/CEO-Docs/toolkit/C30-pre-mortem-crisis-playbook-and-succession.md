# C30 — Pre-Mortem, Crisis Playbook and Succession Plan

_Toolkit · fills exercises 12.1 and 12.3 in [[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things|18 — Risk, Crisis and the Hard Things]] and 12.1 and 12.4 in [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|20 — The CEO's Own Operating System, Succession and What Winning Looks Like]] · Owner: the CEO — the pre-mortem is run with all three directors, each playbook has a single named owner, the succession document is read aloud to the board · Cadence: pre-mortem quarterly, 75 minutes; playbooks rehearsed twice a year; succession refreshed every six months on the self-review date · Workbook tab: `Risk & Pre-Mortem` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Three documents that share one property: **each one is worthless unless it was written before it was needed.** They are bound together here because they are the same act at three time horizons — _how could this end_ (24 months), _what do we do in the first hour_ (today), and _what happens if I am not here tomorrow_ (already live).

**(a) The pre-mortem** is Gary Klein's method, and it is the best risk technique available to a company with no risk department. The team assumes "that the project they are planning has just failed—as so many do—and then generate plausible reasons" for it ([Klein — _Performing a Project Premortem_, HBR, September 2007](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem); [gary-klein.com](https://www.gary-klein.com/premortem)). Two things make it work. **Prospective hindsight**: explaining a stipulated fact produces far more specific causes than speculating about possibilities, and it interrupts the planning fallacy ([Kahneman, _Thinking, Fast and Slow_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow)). And **it legitimises doubt** — Klein's rationale is that "too many people are reluctant to speak up about their reservations during the all-important planning phase". In a family founding team that second mechanism is the whole point: the domain-expert director may never say _"I think your pricing is wrong"_ to a CEO who is also his relative, but he will happily write _"we failed because dealers never agreed to pay per GSTIN"_ when the premise is that everyone already failed.

**(b) The crisis playbook index** exists because a crisis is a different animal from a hard thing. A hard thing has no correct answer and all the time in the world; a crisis usually has a knowable answer and no time at all. That is good news — it means a crisis **can** be pre-written, and a company of three that has written it down will out-perform a company of thirty that has not. The role split is Google's SRE practice, itself descended from the fire service's Incident Command System, on the stated rationale that "a clear separation of responsibilities allows individuals more autonomy than they might otherwise have" ([Google SRE — Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/)).

**(c) The bus-factor succession document** is not about death. It is about **any two-week unavailability you did not schedule** — an accident on a district highway, a hospitalisation, a family emergency in another state, a phone stolen with the authenticator app on it. A three-person company whose technical CEO holds the AWS root account, a DSC, a bank mandate, the IOCL relationship and the pricing rationale in his head has a [bus factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_factor) of one across several unrelated axes at once. One day and roughly ₹0 to fix, and almost nobody does it.

**Boundary, stated once.** The COO course owns operational risk — the register, insurance, the compliance calendar, incident postmortems ([[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|COO 13]], [[T15-risk-register|T15]], [[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]]). This template owns **existential** risk and the **CEO's half** of a crisis. One question divides them: _can a checklist, a drill, an alert or an SOP substantially reduce it?_ If yes it is operational and belongs to the COO. If reducing it needs a judgment about direction, capital, people or story that only the CEO can make, it belongs here.

## When to use

- **Quarterly, on the planning day**, all three directors, 75 minutes, paper only. Next quarter's session opens by reading last quarter's list — you will find causes that quietly became true and nobody noticed.
- **In a new hire's first month**, before they read yours. Someone six weeks in can see what three founders have stopped seeing.
- **After any near-miss or any [[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]] postmortem**, asking the handoff question: _could this recur bigger, and would the bigger version be existential?_
- **Before writing any crisis playbook** — the pre-mortem tells you which four or five events deserve one.
- **The succession section: today.** It has no trigger. It is already live, and the blanks on the first pass _are_ the finding.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Silent first, always.** Ten minutes of writing alone, on paper, before anyone speaks. No laptops and **no shared doc** — a shared doc is a conversation with a delay. This is the [Delphi method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi_method)'s core insight: anonymous independent responses first, precisely to stop authority, personality or reputation dominating.
2. **Stipulate failure, with a date, in one written sentence.** _"It is 1 September 2028. VSYST has shut down DZZLO OMS. The company failed."_ Not "struggled". **Failed.** The stipulation is what does the work.
3. **Read out round-robin, one cause each, no commentary and no defending** until everyone is empty — "but we've already handled that" is banned during the read-out. Then **merge only what is genuinely the same cause.** Resist merging "we ran out of cash" with "we never got dealers to pay": different mitigations, different owners, and merging them is how the actionable one disappears.
4. **Score independently, then argue only the gaps.** Each founder writes likelihood 1–5 and impact 1–5 without seeing the others'; all three columns go side by side. **The rows where you disagree by two or more points are the most valuable output of the meeting**, larger than the average — a gap means someone knows something the others do not, or is defending something. Twenty timeboxed minutes, and say out loud whose scores are better data on which rows.
5. **Impact is measured against survival, not rupees.** A 5 means _the company does not recover_; a 3 means _we lose a year_. The COO's register bands impact in rupees because operational risk is mostly financial; this is a different instrument.
6. **Top three by agreed score get a dated mitigation and one named owner, typed before anyone leaves the room.** Three, not ten — it is the number of mitigations a three-person company can actually run alongside its day job. "Improve onboarding" is an intention; "COO publishes the onboarding SOP and instruments time-to-first-invoice by 30 Nov 2026" is a mitigation. A blank owner or date is a red row.
7. **Every early-warning signal passes four tests**: observable (a number or an event, not a feeling), **already instrumented** somewhere you look weekly, leading rather than trailing, and carrying a named watcher plus a stated response. The signals that pass are almost always things you already measure — this exercise attaches meaning to existing telemetry, it does not create new telemetry.
8. **The CEO does not run the incident.** The CEO decides and communicates; the commander commands. If the CEO is the only person who can fix it — at VSYST today, often true — then the COO commands and the CEO is _only_ Operations for the duration, and someone else holds the phone. **Write this down now, while it is theoretical**, because at 11 PM on a rate-window failure nobody will negotiate it.
9. **Describe impact publicly; describe cause only after the postmortem.** Impact you can observe; cause you can only currently believe. An early wrong cause poisons the true explanation when it arrives — which is why crisis-communication practice puts "informing and adjusting information" ahead of any argument about blame ([Situational Crisis Communication Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_crisis_communication_theory); [Coombs & Holladay, _Management Communication Quarterly_ 16(2), 2002](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/089331802237233)).
10. **Rehearse, and record the date.** An unrehearsed playbook is a document, not a control. `Last rehearsed` starts at "Not rehearsed" and is the most honest cell on the sheet.
11. **Write the succession document as what is _true today_, not what should be true** — then **read it aloud to the other two directors.** A document nobody has read is not a control.

## Template (a) — the pre-mortem

Section A of the `Risk & Pre-Mortem` tab. Ten to fifteen rows. Columns, left to right: **# · cause of failure (stipulated, past tense) · L1 L2 L3 · I1 I2 I3 · agreed score (L×I, out of 25) · early-warning signal · mitigation (a dated action) · owner · by when · status.** Three independent score columns, one per founder — that is the point of the sheet. Only the top three rows carry mitigations.

```
THE 75-MINUTE PROTOCOL
  0-02   Read the stipulation aloud. One sentence, a real date, the word "failed".
  02-12  SILENCE. Each founder writes 8-12 causes ALONE, on paper.
  12-30  Round-robin read-out. One cause each, no commentary, no defending.
         Someone types them straight into the tab.
  30-35  Merge only genuinely identical causes. When in doubt, do not merge.
  35-45  Independent scoring. L 1-5 and I 1-5, no peeking. Three columns.
  45-65  ARGUE ONLY THE GAPS >= 2 points. Timeboxed. Name whose data is better
         on which rows, so deference lands where it belongs and nowhere else.
  65-75  Top three: dated mitigation + one named owner, TYPED BEFORE ANYONE LEAVES.
  Close  Diary the next session. Next quarter opens by reading this list.
```

**The handoff rule, both directions.** Every existential row asks _"is any part of this reducible by a mechanism?"_ — if yes, that part is handed to the COO's [[T15-risk-register|T15]] with a date. Every [[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]] postmortem asks _"could this recur bigger, and would the bigger version be existential?"_ — if yes, a row appears here. Review both registers in the same quarter, in adjacent sessions, so the handoffs happen while both people are in the room.

## Template (b) — the crisis playbook index

**First, name what qualifies.** A crisis needs at least two of three: **trust is at stake, not just uptime**; **a clock is running that you do not control**; **people outside the company will find out.** Write the four or five qualifying events into the index below. Naming them in advance stops a Tuesday-afternoon bug consuming a founder's week and — more important — stops the real one being under-called for six hours because nobody wanted to be dramatic.

**The roles, fixed in advance.**

| Role                   | Who at VSYST                                    | Owns                                                                        | Must not                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Incident Commander** | The COO, or the founder **not** fixing the code | The clock, the timeline document, who is doing what, escalate-or-stand-down | Touch the code. A commander with their hands in the system stops commanding within ten minutes      |
| **Operations**         | CEO/CTO and any engineer                        | Diagnosis and the fix. Nothing else                                         | Talk to customers mid-fix                                                                           |
| **Communications**     | **CEO**                                         | What is said, to whom, when, in what words                                  | Improvise. The words come from [[C24-crisis-comms-and-statement-kit\|C24]] with the facts filled in |
| **Customer contact**   | Domain-expert director                          | Being reachable by phone; capturing what dealers say                        | Explain cause. He relays status, he does not diagnose                                               |

**The index.** Section B of the `Risk & Pre-Mortem` tab, one row per playbook: **crisis · first hour: who does what · who speaks, and what the first statement says · notification obligations (VERIFY LIVE with counsel) · playbook owner · last rehearsed.** At least one row is also written out in full.

**The first hour, minute by minute.** The order matters more than the speed.

| Minute    | Action                                                                                                    | Why here and not later                                                                   |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **0–5**   | **Declare.** Say the word "incident" out loud, name the commander, open the document                      | Under-calling is the default failure. Declaring is cheap and stand-down is free          |
| **5–15**  | **Stop the bleeding.** Disable the failing path, roll back, revoke, go read-only. Do **not** diagnose yet | A shorter incident beats a better-understood one. Diagnosis has no deadline; damage does |
| **15–25** | **Blast radius.** How many tenants, which data, still happening, is money affected                        | Everything you say publicly depends on these four numbers                                |
| **25–40** | **First external message — even with nothing solved**                                                     | The message that costs nothing to send buys the next four hours                          |
| **40–55** | Diagnosis proper, Operations only, commander holding everything off them                                  | Protected focus is the commander's main product                                          |
| **55–60** | Update the document; confirm the next-update time; check whether a statutory clock has started            | The clock starts when you **notice**, not when you finish investigating                  |

```
THE FIRST EXTERNAL MESSAGE — four sentences, no fifth
  "We are aware of an issue affecting <what the customer sees> since
   approximately <time>. <What is and is not affected, in their terms>.
   Our team is working on it now. We will update you by <specific time>
   whether or not it is resolved."
  No cause. No fix estimate. No reassurance you cannot back.
  A NAMED NEXT-UPDATE TIME is the highest-value element in the message:
  it converts an anxious customer into a waiting one.

THE SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH — one append-only document, owned by the commander
  INCIDENT <date>-<n>  Severity: <1/2/3>  Commander: <name>  Status: ACTIVE/MITIGATED/CLOSED
  One-line description (facts only, no cause)
  Blast radius: tenants / months / money affected / still-live? Y-N
  Timeline (append only, IST, never edit a past line)
  Decisions taken (and by whom)
  External comms sent (time, channel, audience, EXACT text)
  Open questions          FACTS and HYPOTHESES in separate, labelled sections
  Next update due at: <time>   <- never blank while status is ACTIVE
```

**The cadence carries more weight than the fix.** Hourly while active, end-of-day while mitigated, once when closed, once again when the structural fix ships. A fast fix proves your systems are good; a met cadence proves _you_ are. If the time arrives and nothing has changed, send _"No change yet. Still working. Next update at \_\_\_."_ That message feels worthless to write and is the most valuable one you will send all night.

## Template (c) — the bus-factor succession document

Written in one sitting, stored in **two** places — the vault, and a sealed envelope with the Company Secretary — refreshed every six months on the CEO self-review date. **Expect half the rows to come back blank on the first pass. The blanks are the finding**; each becomes a task with an owner and a date.

**Section 1 — Statutory and corporate.** Every row is a **VERIFY LIVE with the CS** item.

| Item                      | Record                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Behind it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Minimum board**         | Can the company still act with one director unavailable? A private company needs **at least two directors**, and one director must have stayed in India **182 days or more** in the previous financial year | §149(1), §149(3) ([CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/149-company-to-have-board-of-directors/)). Three founder-directors = one director of headroom, no more                                                                                                        |
| **Quorum**                | What the Articles require, and whether two directors can validly pass a resolution without the third                                                                                                        | AoA + the Act. One sentence from the CS, in the document                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Resignation / vacancy** | Who files what and by when, including the **30-day** Registrar intimation in **DIR-12**                                                                                                                     | §168 ([CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/168-resignation-of-director/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/resignation-director-section-168-companies-act-2013.html))                                                                                        |
| **Share nomination**      | Whether each founder has filed **Form SH-13**. Without it the shares go through transmission and legal-heir proof                                                                                           | §72 + Rule 19 ([CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/power-to-nominate/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/transmission-shares-companies-act-2013-importance-nomination-comprehensive-guide.html)). **The highest-value, lowest-effort item here — one form** |
| **KMP**                   | Whether the company must appoint Key Managerial Personnel, and what changes if it does                                                                                                                      | §203; thresholds apply — re-verify at each capital or turnover milestone                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **A will**                | Each founder's personal will covering the shareholding                                                                                                                                                      | **VERIFY LIVE with a lawyer.** A nomination and a will interact and are not the same instrument                                                                                                                                                            |

**Section 2 — Access and instruments.** The unglamorous half, and the half that stops the company dead within seventy-two hours.

| Item                             | Failure mode                                                                                                                                                                                   | The document must say                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **DSC**                          | A Digital Signature Certificate is issued to a **person** and is non-transferable. If the only director with an active DSC is unavailable, statutory filings stop                              | **Every director holds a current, unexpired DSC.** Record issuer, holder, expiry, renewal date ([Controller of Certifying Authorities](https://cca.gov.in/)). Two hours and a few thousand rupees; almost no small company does it                                                                      |
| **Bank mandate and signatories** | A single-signatory mandate means salaries and vendor payments stop when that person does                                                                                                       | Record the current mandate exactly; then choose deliberately between joint signatories and per-transaction limits with two authorised signatories, and pass the resolution. **VERIFY LIVE with the bank** — a mandate change is a bank-form process, not just a resolution                              |
| **Root and owner accounts**      | AWS root, domain registrar, Google Workspace super-admin, GitHub org owner, MongoDB Atlas owner, Apple Developer and Play Console owners, Easebuzz merchant account, GST/MCA/income-tax logins | **Two humans hold each**, or one holds it and a second has documented emergency access. App-store account transfers are slow and identity-bound — check each store's process _before_ you need it                                                                                                       |
| **MFA / authenticator**          | The commonest real lockout is not death — it is one phone, lost, with every TOTP seed on it                                                                                                    | Backup codes printed and in the envelope; seeds recoverable by a second person                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Password manager**             | One vault, one master password, one head                                                                                                                                                       | Use the tool's emergency mechanism — Bitwarden's [Emergency Access](https://bitwarden.com/help/emergency-access/) grants a nominated person view or takeover after a set wait; 1Password's is the [Emergency Kit](https://support.1password.com/emergency-kit/). **VERIFY LIVE** which tier includes it |
| **Insurance**                    | No cover, or cover nobody knows exists                                                                                                                                                         | Term life per founder; separately, whether keyman insurance makes sense for the company (**VERIFY LIVE with the CA**). Policy numbers, insurer, who to call                                                                                                                                             |

**Section 3 — What is only in your head.** This is what separates a succession document from a password list.

```
THE RELATIONSHIP MAP  — one row per counterparty, and the rule that matters:
  A relationship with no second contact is a bus-factor item exactly like a root
  password. KEEP THE RELATIONSHIP, PUBLISH THE MAP. Founders get this backwards:
  they hand over the relationship (the partner notices and resents it) and keep
  the map in their head (which is the actual risk).

  Counterparty | Named human + number | History | What was promised VERBALLY |
  What they care about | SECOND CONTACT (named) | Who steps in tomorrow

ALSO IN THIS SECTION
  · Why the pricing is what it is; why particular clauses are in the dealer contract;
    why one district was chosen and another skipped
  · Which C29 decisions are still LIVE rather than closed
  · The decisions that are the CEO's alone, with the standing-authorities note
    attached — in an unplanned absence, that note IS the interim constitution
  · THE FIRST-72-HOURS RUNBOOK: who convenes, who tells the team, who tells the
    dealers and in what words, who tells IOCL, and WHO IS THE SINGLE ACTING
    DECISION-MAKER until the board meets.  Wording comes from C24.
```

**The other two horizons, briefly, because they are different problems.** **Role succession** hands over one function at a time, each triggered by a hire, on two rules: hand over _the decision plus the limit_, not the task; and **the artefact must exist before the person arrives** — you cannot hand over pricing to a hire if the reason for the pricing is unwritten. **Seat succession** is written _before there is any candidate_: the triggers, the six-step handover, and the written separation of the **three claims — ownership, board seat, executive job**. A family member may inherit shares without inheriting a directorship, and hold a directorship without an operating role; conflating them is the root of most family-business failure. PwC finds only about a fifth of Indian family businesses have a robust, documented, communicated succession plan, and senior-generation resistance to transitioning leadership running at roughly twice the global rate ([PwC India](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/services/private-and-entrepreneurial-services/family-businesses-the-evolving-bharat-story.pdf); [PwC's 12th Family Business Survey](https://www.pwc.in/services/entrepreneurial-and-private-business/pwcs-12th-family-business-survey.html)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Ram Charan's complaint is that succession is treated as an event rather than a process ([_Ending the CEO Succession Crisis_](https://hbr.org/2005/02/ending-the-ceo-succession-crisis), HBR 2005). **Say the sentence out loud once in a board meeting and minute it: "the CEO seat is not hereditary at this company."** If that is untrue, minute the true one instead. What destroys family firms is not either policy; it is the ambiguity.

**Read the ranking before you read the rows.** The top three are activation, selling and repeatability — not the breach, not the incumbent, not the OMC. It matches the outside evidence too: CB Insights found 70% of shut-down startups ran out of capital, but reads that as where the story ends rather than why — "poor product-market fit (43%), bad timing (29%), and unsustainable unit economics (19%)" are why the capital dried up ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/)). Row 1 is the symptom; rows 2, 4 and 10 are the disease.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | What it looks like                                                           | Fix                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Running the pre-mortem as a conversation**       | All three lists match                                                        | Rule 1. Silent, on paper, alone. If the lists match, the rule was not enforced                                                        |
| **Averaging the scores and moving on**             | The disagreements are smoothed away                                          | Rule 4. The ≥2-point gaps **are** the output                                                                                          |
| **A top-ten priority list**                        | Ten mitigations, none started                                                | Rule 6. Three, with dates and owners typed in the room                                                                                |
