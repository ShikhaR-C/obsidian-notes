# C07 — Moat and 7 Powers Audit

_Toolkit · fills the exercises in [[06-strategy-ii-moats-positioning-and-the-business-model|06 — Strategy II: Moats, Positioning and the Business Model]] · Owner: CEO; the domain-expert director argues every row · Cadence: filled once in Month 2, re-audited annually and whenever a trigger fires · No workbook tab of its own — each row's trigger metric becomes a watch-row on the `CEO Dashboard` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

This is the sheet that stops you from believing your own deck. Hamilton Helmer defines **Power** as "the set of conditions creating the potential for persistent differential returns", and insists that every Power has two halves: a **Benefit** (it improves your cash flow — higher price, lower cost, more volume) and a **Barrier** (a reason a competent, well-funded competitor _chooses not to_ arbitrage it away) ([7 Powers](https://7powers.com/); [7 Powers — synopsis](https://7powers.com/synopsis/); [Strategy Capital](https://strategycapital.com/about/)). Most things founders call moats have a Benefit and no Barrier. A cleaner codebase, a better UI, a head start, a founder who knows the trade — all Benefit, no Barrier. Buffett's older word for the same idea is the moat: the durable structural feature, not the current quality of management ([Berkshire Hathaway — shareholder letters](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html); [Economic moat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_moat)).

The seven Powers are **scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power** ([Commoncog — 7 Powers summary](https://commoncog.com/7-powers-summary/); [Sachin Rekhi — A Primer on Business Strategy from Helmer's 7 Powers](https://www.sachinrekhi.com/p/7-powers-hamilton-helmer); [Eagle Point Capital — 7 Powers: A Summary of Competitive Advantages](https://eaglepointcapital.substack.com/p/7-powers-a-summary-of-competitive)). They are not equally available at every stage. Helmer's **Power Progression** places counter-positioning and cornered resource at _origination_, scale and network economies at _take-off_, and branding and process power only at _stability_ ([StrategyPunk — 7 Powers framework template](https://www.strategypunk.com/7-powers-by-hamilton-helmer-a-strategic-framework-template/)). VSYST is at origination. So four "no"s are not a failure — they are a schedule.

Which is the point of the format. **For a pre-revenue three-person company, most honest answers are "no", and the audit is still worth two hours a year** — because the value is not the verdict column. It is the **trigger** column, which converts each "no" into an observable event that tells you to look at the row again, and the **months to break** column, which forces you to price your own advantage in the only currency that matters: how long a serious competitor would need to neutralise it.

## When to use

- **Month 2 of the CEO seat**, right after the market map ([[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments|C06]]) and before the strategy kernel is finalised in [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]].
- **Before writing any investor material.** Every "defensible" and "moat" in [[C15-investor-narrative-and-deck-outline|C15]] must trace to a row here that says _have it_. If it does not, delete the word.
- **Annually**, and immediately when a trigger fires — a competitor ships something, a partner signs, a metric crosses its line.
- **Before a large one-way-door bet** (a new module, a partnership, a district expansion): ask which row it moves and by how much. If it moves none, it is a feature, not a strategy ([[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]], [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Three verdicts only, and they are not synonyms.** `HAVE` = a competitor could see exactly what we do and still not copy it profitably. `COULD BUILD` = there is a named action, an owner and a date. `NO` = neither, plus the trigger that would change the answer. "We could build this" is never "we have this", and writing it as such is the single most common way this sheet gets falsified.
2. **A row is `HAVE` only if you can state the Barrier in one sentence** — the reason a competent, funded competitor _declines_ to copy it. No Barrier, no Power, whatever the Benefit ([7 Powers — synopsis](https://7powers.com/synopsis/)).
3. **Every row carries "months to break".** Assume a competent competitor with ₹5 crore and real intent. How many months until this advantage is gone? Under 6 months is not a moat; it is a lead. Write the number even when it embarrasses you.
4. **The trigger must be an observable event with a source**, not a feeling. "A competitor ships a multi-tenant two-sided cloud product on a subscription" is a trigger. "The market gets more competitive" is not. Each trigger that is a metric becomes a watch-row on the `CEO Dashboard`.
5. **A person is not a Power.** A founder's domain knowledge, a live relationship, an unsigned discussion — these are key-person dependencies and belong in the risk register, not here. They convert into Power only when written into the product's rules, the vault and a script someone else can run ([[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]]). This trap has its own paragraph below because founders fall into it more than all the others combined.
6. **Distinguish a network effect from a retention effect, using the NfX test:** does every new user make the product more valuable to every _existing_ user? ([NfX — The Network Effects Bible](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-bible)) If leaving merely becomes harder, that is a switching cost — real, valuable, and a different row.
7. **Switching costs must be measured, not asserted.** Use the standard typology — procedural (time and effort), financial (money lost) and relational (people and habits broken) ([Burnham, Frels & Mahajan — Consumer Switching Costs: A Typology, Antecedents, and Consequences](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1177/0092070302250897); [Cosmic Capital — why customers stay trapped](https://cosmiccapital.substack.com/p/7-powers-why-customers-stay-trapped); [Jeff Towson — why the future of switching costs is B2B](https://jefftowson.com/membership_content/why-the-future-of-switching-costs-is-b2b-winning-tech-strategy-daily-article/)) — and put a number on each: months of ledger history, linked customer firms, people retrained.
8. **Do not audit code.** Code was never the Barrier, and it gets weaker as software gets cheaper to write; proprietary data, network effects and process power get _stronger_ ([a16z's SaaS moat scorecard, summarised](https://developmentcorporate.com/saas/a16z-the-saas-moat-scorecard/)). For a founder-CEO who ships code, that is the uncomfortable line to internalise.
9. **Sign and date it, and keep the old version.** Next year's most useful output is the diff.

## Template

Verdict codes and the discipline behind them:

```
HAVE         a competitor can see it, understand it, and still not copy it profitably.
             Requires a one-sentence BARRIER. No barrier -> not HAVE.
COULD BUILD  named action + named owner + date. No owner -> it is a NO.
NO           plus the TRIGGER: the observable event that re-opens this row.

MONTHS TO BREAK = time for a competent competitor with real money and real intent
                  to neutralise this advantage.  < 6 months = a lead, not a moat.
STAGE           = Helmer's Power Progression: origination / take-off / stability.
```

| Power                   | What it would mean in our market | Verdict (`HAVE` / `COULD BUILD` / `NO` + trigger) | The Barrier, in one sentence | Action that would build it (owner, date) | Months to break | Stage       |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| **Scale economies**     |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | take-off    |
| **Network economies**   |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | take-off    |
| **Counter-positioning** |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | origination |
| **Switching costs**     |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | take-off    |
| **Branding**            |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | stability   |
| **Cornered resource**   |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | origination |
| **Process power**       |                                  |                                                   |                              |                                          |                 | stability   |

## Worked example — VSYST, audited honestly (illustrative)

Five of seven are `NO` or partial. That is the correct answer for a company that is months old, and writing it down is worth more than any of the optimistic alternatives. This table is the reusable form of the audit in [[06-strategy-ii-moats-positioning-and-the-business-model|lesson 06]] §2, with the two columns that lesson does not carry.

| Power                   | Verdict                                                                                                                                                                  | Barrier (if `HAVE`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Action that would build it                                                                                                                                                         | Months to break                                                                        | Trigger to re-audit                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Scale economies**     | **NO nationally · partial locally.** R&D amortisation is real but trivial at this market size; field and support cost per tenant genuinely falls with _district density_ | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Go deep in a district before going wide; instrument cost-to-serve **per district**, not in aggregate (CEO + COO, this quarter)                                                     | n/a until it exists                                                                    | A district crosses ~15 paying tenants **and** cost-to-serve per tenant there drops ≥30% vs single-tenant districts                           |
| **Network economies**   | **PARTIAL — local, saturating, two-sided.** Dealer→dealer: none, possibly negative. Dealer→customer firm: real. Across districts: zero                                   | Inside a saturated district, a transporter with three suppliers on one screen will ask the fourth to join                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Saturate districts; make re-using an already-onboarded customer firm across dealers a one-tap invite (CEO, Q+1)                                                                    | 18–24 months **per district**, and a competitor must repeat it district by district    | Median time-to-activate for new dealers **falls** as district penetration rises. Flat = retention, not network                               |
| **Counter-positioning** | **HAVE — the strongest Power actually available today**                                                                                                                  | An incumbent on licence + AMC + local-reseller economics cannot ship a subscription, mobile-first, two-sided, dealer-priced product without cannibalising the reseller margin its whole business runs on ([Jon Kol — counter-positioning](https://jonkol.medium.com/counter-positioning-how-upstarts-tackle-incumbents-3b5e06c5a449); [The School of Knowledge — counter-positioning](https://www.theschoolofknowledge.net/p/the-7-types-of-business-power-counter-positioning)) | Stay subscription, mobile-first, two-sided and dealer-priced. Do not add a perpetual licence, ever (CEO — a [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]] one-way door)       | 24–36 months, and only if the incumbent accepts the cannibalisation                    | An incumbent (a Tally partner, a regional pump-billing vendor, or an OMC) ships a genuine multi-tenant two-sided cloud subscription          |
| **Switching costs**     | **HAVE, and growing monthly** — procedural, financial and relational at once                                                                                             | Leaving means re-keying opening balances for every credit customer, retraining people the dealer **does not employ** (his customers' accountants, his drivers), and abandoning the audit trail linking voucher to invoice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Ledger depth, linked customer firms, compliance history, integrations — and _measure_ all four per tenant (CEO, instrumented this quarter)                                         | Not breakable by a competitor at all; only by us, through an outage or a data incident | Median months-of-ledger-history per active tenant passes 12; median linked customers per tenant passes 25                                    |
| **Branding**            | **NO.** There is no brand; there is a product and three founders                                                                                                         | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Five-plus years, zero data incidents, association endorsement, visible peer adoption. Not a Month-2 project                                                                        | n/a                                                                                    | Unprompted referrals exceed 30% of new closed deals                                                                                          |
| **Cornered resource**   | **NO — and this is the row we most want to get wrong.** See below                                                                                                        | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | A signed exclusive with an OMC or a dealer association; a proprietary data licence; domain knowledge codified into product rules and the vault (CEO + domain director, continuous) | n/a                                                                                    | A **signed**, exclusive, multi-year agreement exists — a signature, not a discussion ([[C26-partnership-evaluation-and-mou-checklist\|C26]]) |
| **Process power**       | **NO.** Essentially never available to a company that is months old                                                                                                      | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Years of accumulated organisational practice — the [[COO-Docs/index\|COO course]]'s entire subject                                                                                 | n/a                                                                                    | Do not re-open this row before ₹10 Cr ARR                                                                                                    |

**The cornered-resource trap, named.** VSYST has a domain-expert director with deep fuel-distribution knowledge and live IOCL conversations. It is tempting to write that in the cornered-resource row. Do not. A cornered resource is something **the company** holds on terms a competitor cannot obtain. A person who can resign, fall ill, or disagree with the other two directors is a key-person dependency — a line in the risk register, not a line in the moat audit. The conversion is specific and doable: domain knowledge becomes a company asset the moment it is written into the product's rules (the 10 PM–6 AM rate window, the TCS split, the three invoice types already are exactly this), into the vault, and into a sales script someone else can run. Until then it is risk, not Power. The same logic applies to IOCL: a live discussion is a relationship; an exclusive signed agreement is a resource.

**What the audit says to do.** Two `HAVE`s, both available at origination exactly as the Power Progression predicts, and both pointing at the same action: **stay structurally uncopyable, and go deep in a district rather than wide across a state.** That is not a moat yet. It is a schedule with two rows already started — which, for a pre-revenue company, is the correct honest position, and a far stronger one than a deck claiming five moats it cannot name a Barrier for.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | What it looks like                                         | Fix                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Benefit without Barrier**                  | "Our product is better / cleaner / faster" in a moat row   | State the Barrier in one sentence or downgrade the row                                             |
| **`COULD BUILD` written as `HAVE`**          | An aspiration in the verdict column                        | `COULD BUILD` requires a named owner and a date; without one it is `NO`                            |
| **A person in the cornered-resource row**    | The domain director's knowledge counted as a company asset | Risk register until it is in the product, the vault and the script                                 |
| **Retention effect called a network effect** | "Eighty linked customers means network effects"            | Apply the NfX test; eighty linked customers is a switching cost                                    |
| **Auditing the code**                        | "Our architecture is the moat"                             | Code is a rate advantage; data, network and process are the Barriers                               |
| **A trigger that is a mood**                 | "Re-audit if competition heats up"                         | Observable event, with a source and a threshold                                                    |
| **No months-to-break number**                | Every row asserted as durable                              | Force the number. Under 6 months is a lead, not a moat                                             |
| **Auditing to feel good**                    | Five `HAVE`s at month four                                 | Most answers should be `NO` at origination. Four `NO`s is a schedule, not a verdict on the company |
