# C01 — CEO Charter and 90-Day Plan

_Toolkit · fills exercises 11.1 and 11.2 in [[04-how-to-become-a-ceo-in-2026|04 — How to Become a CEO in 2026]] and exercise 10.1 in [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|03 — The CEO's Core Value and the Founder Contracts]]; lessons [[01-what-is-a-ceo|01]]–[[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|03]] all end here · Owner: the CEO writes it, the other two directors sign it, the board minutes it · Cadence: Week 0; re-read at day 90; refreshed annually · Workbook tab: the scorecard rows live on `CEO Dashboard` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two documents that only work together. The **charter** says what the seat is: its mission, what it owns, what it explicitly does _not_ own, the decisions it will stop making, how it is measured, and who covers it if you are hit by a bus. The **90-day plan** says what the seat will do first and what will exist at the end of it. Without the charter the plan is busywork. Without the plan the charter is a job title.

This is the direct counterpart of the COO's [[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan|T30]], and the two are designed to be read side by side. T30 defines the seat that owns the operating system; C01 defines the seat that owns direction, capital, people, story and governance. **Both get signed, and the same three people sign both** — which is the point. A charter you write for yourself and file is a diary; a charter countersigned by the other two directors and minuted at a board meeting is the thing that makes the seat exist.

The mission line borrows its spine from Fred Wilson's reduction of the job to three things: set the vision and strategy and communicate it, recruit and retain the very best talent, and make sure there is always enough cash in the bank ([AVC — What A CEO Does](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/)). At an Indian Private Limited company there is a fourth that cannot be delegated to anyone, ever: the **director's statutory duty** to act in good faith, with due and reasonable care, skill and diligence, exercising independent judgement, and to avoid situations of conflicting interest ([§166, Companies Act 2013](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** the current text and penalties with your CS, because the section has been amended.

The 90-day half exists because a transition is its own kind of work with its own failure mode: leaders in transition reflexively reuse the skills that worked before, and that is the mistake ([Michael D. Watkins, _Picking the Right Transition Strategy_, HBR, Jan 2009](https://hbr.org/2009/01/picking-the-right-transition-strategy)). A technical founder taking the CEO title will default to shipping code. The plan makes that visible before it costs a quarter.

## When to use

- **Week 0 of the course**, before anything else. It is the first artefact the CEO seat produces, and everything from [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]] onward assumes it exists. A strategy written by an undefined seat gets re-litigated by whoever is unhappy with it.
- **The day the title is decided among founders** — including the day you decide it is you and nobody says anything. That silence is not agreement; it is deferral.
- **When a CEO is hired or succeeded**, as the document they are handed on day one and rewrite in week one.
- **At day 90**, read against reality: what shipped, what slipped, what the seat actually turned out to own.
- **Annually, and any time the remit moves** — a COO seat activating, first outside capital, a partnership that changes the company's shape, or a co-founder going part-time.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Write the mission as one sentence you could fail at.** "Lead the company" is not failable. The house version for VSYST: _point the company at a market it can actually win, keep it funded and legal, and make sure the people and the story are good enough that the direction survives contact with reality._ If nobody could look at that in a year and say "no, you didn't", rewrite it.
2. **The "does not own" list is the more valuable half.** Every hour you spend on the operating system is an hour the direction does not get. Write, by name, what belongs to the COO seat ([[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan|T30]], [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]]), to the domain-expert director, and to the external CA and CS. Failure mode #1 in [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]] §4 — the CEO who stays an engineer — is prevented here or not at all.
3. **Ask, and write down, which decisions you will stop making.** The executive-search diagnostic that separates a real second-in-command from a decorated one is exactly this question, asked of the founder ([Alder Koten — why COOs fail in founder-led companies](https://charliesolorzano.me/2026/03/17/coo-founder-led-company-failure/)). Each answer becomes a dated row in [[C03-decision-rights-matrix|C03]] or [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]]. If the honest answer today is "none", write _that_ with a review date. An empty list on paper is a fact you can act on.
4. **The measurement block is seven real rows, not adjectives.** Take them verbatim from [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]] §3.2 and put them on the `CEO Dashboard` tab. Set targets at the start of a quarter; do not move them mid-quarter without a logged decision in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]. The standard board-evaluation format — five areas, 6–10 KPIs, a mid-year check-in so the annual review holds no surprises ([Ideals — CEO performance evaluation](https://idealsboard.com/blog/board-management/ceo-performance-evaluation-guide/)) — assumes an independent compensation committee. You do not have one. These seven rows are the version that survives without one.
5. **Name a backup for the seat, today, in writing.** Not a succession plan — one line: who signs, who talks to the bank, who talks to IOCL, and who has the credentials, if you are unavailable for four weeks. Indian family businesses are measurably worst at exactly this: **36% have no clear succession plan against 28% globally, and 21% have delayed succession outright against 10%** ([Forbes India on PwC's 12th Family Business Survey, Feb 2026](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/indian-family-businesses-high-on-ambition-low-on-governance/2991239/1); [PwC India](https://www.pwc.in/services/entrepreneurial-and-private-business/pwcs-12th-family-business-survey.html)) — **VERIFY LIVE** the figures against the current survey. The full version is [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession|C30]]; the one line goes here now.
6. **Every 90-day window ends in an artefact and an exit criterion, never a mood.** "Understand the market" is not a milestone. "[[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments|C06]] filled with 12 districts counted and three segments sized" is.
7. **The standard arc is listen → analyse → implement**: days 1–30 map what is and is not happening; days 31–60 analyse the metrics, the money and the people; days 61–90 present the view and begin implementing ([Forbes Coaches Council — a new executive's 90-day plan](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbescoachescouncil/2020/12/14/are-you-a-new-coo-start-out-strong-with-this-90-day-plan/)). Watkins's point is that the arc must match the _situation_ — a start-up is not a turnaround and is not a realignment ([HBR, 2009](https://hbr.org/2009/01/picking-the-right-transition-strategy)). VSYST is a start-up: nothing to fix, everything to build.
8. **Compress only if you have earned the right to.** A founder already in the seat does not need thirty days of listening to a company of three people. See the compressed version below. An outsider CEO must not compress it.
9. **Write your coding rule into the charter, where a co-founder can see it.** A technical CEO cannot "stop coding" — at VSYST there is one engineer and it is you. The workable rule has two clauses: _code inside a declared block, never in a gap_, and _never in the hour after an avoided conversation._ Porter and Nohria's finding that large-company CEOs spend **36% of their time reactively** is a floor, not a benchmark, for a founder who also ships ([HBR, 2018](https://hbr.org/2018/07/how-ceos-manage-time)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Your calendar audit in [[C27-ceo-weekly-template-and-calendar-audit|C27]] is what tells you whether the rule held.
10. **Get it signed and minuted.** Circulate to both co-directors, take at least one change from each, then sign and minute it using [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]. §173 sets the board-meeting cadence you are minuting it into — first meeting within 30 days of incorporation, then a minimum of four a year with no more than 120 days between two, with a relaxation for a **small company** ([§173](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CS whether VSYST qualifies as a small company, since the thresholds have been amended more than once.

## Template (a) — the charter, one page

```
CEO CHARTER — <company>              written <date> · review <date + 90d>
Signed: CEO ____________  Director 2 ____________  Director 3 ____________
Minuted at board meeting on: ____________   Minute ref: ____________

MISSION (one sentence, failable)
  ...

I OWN (outcome, not activity — one line each, each with a number attached)
  1. Direction   ...        2. Capital   ...
  3. People      ...        4. Story     ...
  5. Governance  ...        6. Final judgement on ...

I DO NOT OWN (say it plainly; name who does)
  - The operating system: cadence, SOPs, scorecard, compliance calendar,
    vendor register, hiring process, DoA below board level  -> COO seat (T30/T01)
  - Dealer-domain judgement and OMC field relationships     -> domain director
  - Statutory filing mechanics and accounts                 -> CA / CS (external)

DECISIONS I WILL STOP MAKING (from the week-one conversation)
  <decision>  ->  <new decider>  ·  limit <...>  ·  transfers on <date>
  (each one also becomes a row in C03 or T22, and a C29 entry with a day-90 review)

DECISIONS THAT ARE NEVER DELEGATED (whatever the amount)
  Statutory filings · anything signed for the company · the cap table ·
  a partnership that binds the company · the strategy kernel · related-party matters

HOW I AM MEASURED (the seven rows; targets on the CEO Dashboard tab)
  1 Revenue & paying GSTINs   2 Cash & runway months   3 Strategy-bet evidence
  4 Talent made/kept/regretted 5 CEO customer conversations 6 Governance zero-misses
  7 The honest self-measure: <chosen before you know the answer>

MY CODING RULE          <two clauses, from rule 9>
RICH OR KING            <one sentence: what you are optimising for, and what you would trade>
BACKUP FOR THIS SEAT    <name> — signs, banks, holds credentials, calls the CA
OUTSIDE-DIRECTOR TRIGGER  <written trigger, per lesson 03 §3.4>
ESCALATION TO ME        cash, a customer, a filing, a person, the product's
                        reputation — same day, whatever the hour
```

## Template (b) — the 30/60/90 plan

One table, three windows, one artefact list and one exit criterion per window. Keep it to a page; put every dated item on the `Board Calendar` tab.

| Window         | Mood | What you do | Artefacts produced | Exit criterion |
| -------------- | ---- | ----------- | ------------------ | -------------- |
| **Days 1–30**  | ...  | ...         | ...                | ...            |
| **Days 31–60** | ...  | ...         | ...                | ...            |
| **Days 61–90** | ...  | ...         | ...                | ...            |

At day 90, write half a page against it: what shipped, what slipped and why, what the seat actually turned out to own, and the three things days 91–180 must produce. That half page is the first entry in your own record — and it is the format [[C23-stakeholder-and-investor-update|C23]] reuses every month for the rest of the company's life.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**The compressed version, because the CEO is already in the seat.** A founder formalising the title does not spend thirty days learning a three-person company. What a founder-CEO actually lacks is not information — it is _decided_ direction, _written_ governance, and any external accountability at all. So days 1–30 produce artefacts, not notes, and the listening is aimed outward at dealers rather than inward at staff. Every number below is illustrative and belongs to VSYST's own first quarter.

The number that says it worked is not how many templates are filled. It is **row 7** — the self-measure you chose in week one while you were still honest. For VSYST's technical CEO the strongest candidate is the calendar one: hours last month on work only you could do, as a fraction of hours worked. Measure it in week 1 and again in week 13.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | What it looks like                                                             | Fix                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A charter with no "does not own" list**   | Two pages of ownership, nothing given away. The seat is a title                | Rule 2. If the list is empty you have not delegated anything, and [[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan\|T30]] is fiction                                                                               |
| **Signing your own charter alone**          | It lives in your folder. Nobody else has read it                               | Rule 10. Three signatures and a minute ref, or it did not happen                                                                                                                                    |
| **Mood milestones**                         | "Days 31–60: get closer to customers"                                          | Rule 6. Name the artefact and the exit criterion, or delete the row                                                                                                                                 |
| **Copying an outsider's 90 days**           | Thirty days of "listening" inside a company of three                           | Use the compressed version. Aim the listening at dealers, not at staff you already know                                                                                                             |
| **Measuring yourself on the COO's numbers** | Uptime and response time on the CEO Dashboard                                  | Those belong to [[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]]. Putting them here undermines the COO seat and drains your attention into the week ([[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts\|lesson 03]] §3.3) |
| **Deleting row 7**                          | The six comfortable rows survive; the embarrassing one is dropped in month two | Every other row can be gamed by a well-intentioned CEO. Row 7 is the one you cannot game, because you chose it before you knew the answer                                                           |
| **No backup named**                         | "We'll deal with it if it happens"                                             | One line, today. It costs five minutes now and is impossible to write during the event it exists for                                                                                                |
| **A charter that has never changed**        | Same file, twelve months, no edits                                             | A document nobody amends is a document nobody uses. The day-90 and annual reviews are part of the artefact, not admin                                                                               |
