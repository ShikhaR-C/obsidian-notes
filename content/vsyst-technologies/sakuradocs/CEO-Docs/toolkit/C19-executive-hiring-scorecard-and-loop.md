# C19 — Executive Hiring Scorecard and Interview Loop

_Toolkit · fills the executive-hiring work in [[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|10 — Building the Team: Hiring, Equity and Firing]] §5 · Owner: the CEO, personally — the loop can be delegated, the scorecard and the decision cannot · Cadence: once per executive search, rewritten from scratch for each role, never recycled · Workbook: no tab of its own; the offer number comes off `Comp Bands` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx) via [[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design|C20]]._

## Purpose

**This extends the COO's [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process|T10]] upward; it does not replace it.** T10 owns the company's hiring process — requisition, job posting, the standard loop, the take-home, offer mechanics, onboarding, the employment-law machinery. Every hire at VSYST runs through T10, including the ones on this page.

C19 is the executive layer that sits on top, and it exists because senior hires fail differently. A support associate who is a poor fit costs you three months and is visible in three weeks. A first leader who is a poor fit costs you a year, takes the team's trust with them, and is invisible for two quarters because everyone assumes the founders knew what they were doing. At a three-person, pre-revenue company the second failure is close to fatal.

The method is Geoff Smart and Randy Street's — the **scorecard** (mission, outcomes, competencies), a structured loop, and reference checks that actually get truth, drawn from ghSMART's assessment practice ([_Who: The A Method for Hiring_ — excerpt](https://geoffsmart.com/books/who-the-a-method-for-hiring/who-the-book-excerpt/); [Welcome to the Jungle — the scorecard's three parts](https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en/articles/who-the-a-method-for-hiring-by-geoff-smart-and-randy-street); [Umbrex — ghSMART profile](https://umbrex.com/resources/profiles-of-the-top-consulting-firms/overview-profile-and-history-of-ghsmart-consulting/)). The reference half is Brad Smart's topgrading, in particular **TORC — the Threat of Reference Check**, where the _candidate_ arranges the calls with their former bosses ([Brad Smart — the TORC technique](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/torc-technique-powerful-free-hiring-method-brad-smart); [Topgrading — how to get value from your reference checks](https://blog.topgrading.com/new-blog/resources/blog/how-to-get-value-from-your-reference-checks)).

Why structure rather than instinct: structured interviews — job-related questions asked of every candidate and scored against a rubric — are the strongest of the commonly used selection methods. Schmidt and Hunter's much-cited 1998 meta-analysis put structured interviews at **.51 validity against .38 for unstructured** ([summary PDF](https://firstpersonnel.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Summary-Schmidt-Hunter-1998.pdf)); Sackett and colleagues' 2023 re-analysis revised the numbers downward across the board but left **structured interviews at the top of the list, around .42** ([SIOP — _Is cognitive ability the best predictor of job performance? New research says it's time to think again_](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance-new-research-says-its-time-to-think-again/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** before quoting either figure to anyone, the corrections are contested. Google's public guide reaches the same practical conclusion: same questions, same rubric, every candidate ([Google re:Work — a guide to structured interviewing](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices)).

## When to use

- **Before VSYST's first leadership hire** — the person who will own a function rather than a task list. The honest position today: VSYST is three founder-directors, pre-revenue, and **the trigger for a genuine exec hire has probably not fired yet.** Use C19 the first time you are hiring someone whose work you cannot check line by line.
- **For every hire from the first one onward whose reference calls you would personally make** — which is the practical definition of "executive" at this size.
- **When you are hiring for a job you have never done yourself** — the specific case Ben Horowitz wrote the standard essay about ([a16z — _Hiring Executives: If You've Never Done the Job, How Do You Hire Somebody Good?_](https://a16z.com/hiring-executives-if-youve-never-done-the-job-how-do-you-hire-somebody-good/)).
- **When an existing role is being upgraded**, so the scorecard is written against next year's job and not last year's.
- **Never as a template to fill in after you have met someone you like.** The scorecard is written before the first conversation or it is a rationalisation.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Write the scorecard before you speak to a single candidate**, and get both co-directors to sign it. A scorecard written after you meet a candidate you like describes the candidate.
2. **Outcomes carry numbers and dates. Always.** "Grow the district" is not an outcome. "Twelve paying GSTINs in Durg and Rajnandgaon by 31 March, with monthly active dealers above 70%" is. Three to six outcomes, ranked. If you cannot write a number, you do not yet know what you want — and that, not the interview, is the thing to fix first.
3. **Do not educate yourself only by interviewing candidates.** Horowitz's warning: when you have never done the job, using interviews as your only information source is dangerous, and the strongest pull is toward hiring on **look and feel** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/hiring-executives-if-youve-never-done-the-job-how-do-you-hire-somebody-good/)). Before the loop opens, spend four hours with two people who have actually done this job elsewhere, and let them tell you what good looks like ([a16z — _The First Principles of Executive Hiring_](https://a16z.com/the-first-principles-of-executive-hiring/)).
4. **Competencies are scored, and at least one of them is a value.** Take the values-based competency and its question from the mechanism table in [[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §4.1 and [[C21-values-and-operating-principles-worksheet|C21]]. A culture that is not in the hiring rubric is not being selected for.
5. **Same questions, same order, same rubric, every candidate** ([Google re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices)). Score independently before anyone discusses anyone. Written scores in a shared doc _before_ the debrief, or the loudest voice sets the room.
6. **Borrow the bar-raiser idea, at your scale.** Amazon puts a trained interviewer from outside the hiring team into the loop with the authority to say no, to keep the bar independent of the hiring manager's urgency ([About Amazon — what is a Bar Raiser](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-bar-raiser); [Amazon leadership principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles)). VSYST cannot run that programme; VSYST **can** put one outsider in every exec loop — an advisor, a friendly dealer for a field role, an engineer you respect for a technical one — with a written veto that you honour.
7. **The candidate arranges the reference calls.** Say it in the first conversation: _"at the end of this process, you will arrange calls for me with your last three managers."_ That sentence does more filtering than any interview question, which is the entire point of TORC ([Brad Smart](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/torc-technique-powerful-free-hiring-method-brad-smart); [Red Seal — topgrading reference checking](https://redsealrecruiting.com/what-is-topgrading-reference-checking/)).
8. **Also take one reference nobody gave you.** A back-channel — someone you find yourself who worked with the candidate — is the only reference with no obligation to be kind. Never contact a current employer without permission; that is a livelihood, and doing it anyway tells the candidate exactly what kind of company you are.
9. **The CEO decides alone.** Many people gather evidence; consensus on executives drifts toward the candidate with the fewest weaknesses rather than the greatest strength ([a16z](https://a16z.com/hiring-executives-if-youve-never-done-the-job-how-do-you-hire-somebody-good/)). Take the veto in rule 6 seriously, then decide, and write the decision and its reasoning in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] so the review at month six has something to read.
10. **A "yes, but…" is a no.** Every founder who has fired an exec can name the moment in the loop when they already knew.

## Template (a) — the executive scorecard

```
SCORECARD — <role title>                         written <date> · signed <3 directors>
Reports to: <person>       Loop owner: <person>     Decision: CEO
Band and level (from C20): <band>            Equity guideline (C20): <range>

MISSION — one sentence. Why this seat exists, in the language of the business.
  "..."

OUTCOMES — 3–6, ranked, each with a number and a date. Rank 1 is the reason you are hiring.
  1. ...............................................  by <date>
  2. ...............................................  by <date>
  3. ...............................................  by <date>

COMPETENCIES — 6–10, each scored 1–5 against a written rubric.
  ROLE-SPECIFIC        <4–6, drawn from the outcomes above>
  VALUES-BASED         <at least 1, from C21's mechanism table>
  STAGE-SPECIFIC       Does the work themselves · Operates with no support function ·
                       Tolerates ambiguity and a small budget · Comfortable in a
                       dealer's office, not only in a deck

WHAT THIS SEAT IS NOT      <the adjacent job people will assume it is>
FIRST 90 DAYS              <what must exist by day 90 — artefacts, not moods>
DEAL-BREAKERS              <written before the loop opens>
SCORECARD SIGNED BY        CEO ______  Director 2 ______  Director 3 ______
```

## Template (b) — the loop

Five stages. Nobody advances without a written score. The whole loop should fit inside three weeks; a slow loop loses the strong candidates and keeps the available ones.

| #   | Stage                       | Length                                              | Run by                                         | What it is for                                                                                                                                     | Advance rule                                                      |
| --- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Screen**                  | 25 min, phone                                       | CEO                                            | Career goals, what they are good at, what they are not good at, who their last three bosses were and how each will rate them. Short and structured | Any deal-breaker, or vagueness about the last three bosses → stop |
| 2   | **Chronological interview** | 90–150 min, in person or video                      | CEO + one co-director                          | Template (c). The core instrument                                                                                                                  | Score ≥ 3 on every competency and ≥ 4 on the top two              |
| 3   | **Work sample**             | Real, bounded, paid if it takes more than two hours | Loop owner                                     | A district plan, a dealer call you listen to, a live support scenario, a code review — the actual work, not a puzzle                               | Written score against the rubric                                  |
| 4   | **Focused interviews (2)**  | 45 min each                                         | One co-director + **the outsider from rule 6** | One per top outcome, deep. The outsider has the written veto                                                                                       | Independent written scores before the debrief                     |
| 5   | **References (3 + 1)**      | 30 min each                                         | **CEO makes these calls**                      | Template (d). Three arranged by the candidate, one back-channel you found                                                                          | Any "would not rehire", or two hesitations, → stop                |

**Debrief rule:** everyone submits scores in writing first; then the loop owner reads them out; then people talk. The CEO speaks last and decides ([[02-how-a-ceo-thinks|lesson 02]] §9).

## Template (c) — the structured chronological interview

Walk the whole career, oldest job to newest, same five questions per job, in the same order. It is long on purpose: patterns show up across jobs that no single job reveals.

```
For EACH role, in chronological order:
  1. What were you hired to do?
  2. What did you accomplish that you are proud of?          [ask for the number]
  3. What were the low points — what went badly?
  4. Who did you work with?
     · What was your boss's name, and how do you spell it?
     · What will <name> tell me were your strengths and your areas for improvement?
     · How would <name> rate your performance out of 10, and why not higher?
  5. Why did you leave?

Then, once, at the end:
  6. What are your career goals for the next five years?
  7. What are you really good at professionally?  (push for 8–12, with examples)
  8. What are you not good at, or not interested in?  (push past the first two)
  9. Walk me through a week in your current job, hour by hour.
 10. If I gave you outcome #1 from this scorecard and no team, what would you do in month one?
```

**Three techniques that do most of the work.** _Ask "how would they rate you, and why not higher?"_ — naming the person before asking the question is what makes the answer honest, because the candidate knows you will be speaking to them. _Push three times on weaknesses_; the first answer is rehearsed, the second is a humblebrag, the third is usually real. And **use the pause.** After an incomplete answer, say nothing for five seconds. Almost everyone fills the silence, and what they add is the part they were deciding whether to tell you.

## Template (d) — the reference call that gets truth

Say this at stage 1: _"At the end of the process, I'll ask you to arrange calls with your last three managers."_ Then, on the call:

```
FRAME (say it out loud, first)
  "I'm not checking a box. <Candidate> is a serious finalist and I want to hire them
   well — which means I need to know how to manage them, not just whether to."

1.  In what context did you work with <name>, and for how long?
2.  What were they hired to do, and what did they actually deliver?    [listen for numbers]
3.  What are their two greatest strengths?
4.  What are their two biggest areas for improvement?      [everyone has two. wait]
5.  How would you rate their overall performance out of 10?
6.  ...and why not a 10?                                   [THE question. then PAUSE]
7.  What kind of manager gets the best out of them? What kind fails with them?
8.  How did they handle a moment when they were clearly wrong?
9.  Who else should I speak to about them?                 [a free back-channel]
10. WOULD YOU HIRE THEM AGAIN, IN THE RIGHT ROLE, TODAY?   [then STOP TALKING]

CLOSE: "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?"
```

**How to read it.** Question 10 is the whole call — an enthusiastic "absolutely, tomorrow" and a warm-but-qualified "in the right role, yes" are different answers, and the second one is a finding, not a courtesy. Question 6 is where the real information lives: nobody says 10, and the gap they describe is the gap you will manage. **The pause after 6 and after 10 is not a technique to be clever with; it is the instrument.** Count to five. Most people cannot leave a silence sitting on a reference call, and what arrives in second six is the sentence you called for.

Write the reference notes into the same document as the interview scores, verbatim where it matters. Six months later, when something is going sideways, the honest answer is usually already in there.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**Role: Head of Dealer Success, Chhattisgarh — VSYST's first non-founder leadership hire. All numbers illustrative.**

| Block                        | Filled                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mission**                  | Make a DZZLO district un-churnable: every paying dealer using the product weekly without a founder in the room                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Outcome 1**                | 12 paying GSTINs live in Durg + Rajnandgaon by 31 March, each having issued ≥ 20 invoices through DZZLO in their last full month                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Outcome 2**                | Weekly-active dealers ≥ 70% of paying tenants for three consecutive months                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Outcome 3**                | Onboarding time from contract to first invoice under 10 working days, with a written SOP handed to [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process\|T10]]'s next hire                                                                                                                                     |
| **Outcome 4**                | Every churn or near-churn written up in the dealer's own words within 48 hours                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Competencies**             | Fuel-distribution literacy (can read a dealer's ledger) · Field persistence · Teaches non-technical users patiently · Writes clearly in Hindi and English · **Values: "the dealer's ledger is sacred"** — scenario question · **Values: "say the number"** — brings figures, marks estimates |
| **Stage-specific**           | Will sit in a pump office for a whole day. Has no assistant, no CRM budget, no marketing support                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **What this seat is not**    | A sales manager with a target and a team. There is no team for at least a year                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Deal-breakers**            | Cannot travel districts; will not do the work personally; has never spoken to a customer who was angry                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **The outsider in the loop** | An advisor from the fuel trade, with a written veto — and, per [[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties\|lesson 09]] §7.5, **not** anyone in a position to award VSYST business                                                                                                        |
| **References**               | Three arranged by the candidate; one back-channel found through a dealer association contact. **No call to the current employer without written permission**                                                                                                                                 |
| **The band**                 | Set from [[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design\|C20]] before the loop opens, not after meeting the candidate. If a relative is a candidate for this seat, the scrutiny goes **up** — same band, extra scorecard, same outsider veto                                                       |

**And the honest note that belongs at the top of the file:** if VSYST cannot yet fund this seat for eighteen months out of [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]], the trigger has not fired and the answer is not "hire cheaper" — it is "not yet". Write the scorecard anyway. It is the clearest description of the job you will do yourself until then.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                             | What it looks like                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Fix                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The big-company operator who cannot do the work** | Impressive title from a company with a recruiting team, an analyst, a CRM and a brand. At VSYST they need a laptop, a bus ticket and their own phone. Six months in, nothing has shipped because nothing they knew how to do was to _do it themselves_ | The stage-specific competency block, and one work sample that is the actual job. Ask: "walk me through the last thing you built with no team." Vagueness here is the answer |
| **Hiring your exact opposite**                      | A technical founder falls for the polished commercial operator, or vice versa, because the gap feels like magic. Complementarity is real; it is not a substitute for evidence                                                                          | Score the scorecard, not the relief. The COO course makes the same point about the CEO–COO pairing ([[01-what-is-a-coo\|COO 01]] §2) — complement, but verify               |
| **Look and feel**                                   | "Great presence, everyone liked them"                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Rule 3. Named by Horowitz as the top criterion in most executive searches, and the reason most of them fail                                                                 |
| **Interviewing to learn the job**                   | Candidate three teaches you what the role is; candidate one is re-called                                                                                                                                                                               | Rule 3. Four hours with two people who have done the job, before the loop opens                                                                                             |
| **The scorecard written afterwards**                | It matches the finalist suspiciously well                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Rule 1. Signed by three directors before the first conversation                                                                                                             |
| **Consensus hiring**                                | Everyone must be comfortable; the least objectionable candidate wins                                                                                                                                                                                   | Rule 9. Outsider veto respected, then the CEO decides alone                                                                                                                 |
| **Reference calls as a formality**                  | Two friendly references, ten minutes each, done after the offer                                                                                                                                                                                        | Rule 7 and Template (d). Three the candidate arranges plus one you find, **before** the offer, made by the CEO                                                              |
| **Skipping the back-channel**                       | Only the references you were given                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Rule 8. And never the current employer without permission                                                                                                                   |
| **Ignoring the "yes, but"**                         | "Strong on everything except the one thing the job is"                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Rule 10. That sentence is the post-mortem, written in advance                                                                                                               |
| **Rebuilding the COO's process here**               | A second requisition form, a second offer template                                                                                                                                                                                                     | This layer is the scorecard, the loop, the interview and the references. Everything else stays in [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process\|T10]]                                 |
