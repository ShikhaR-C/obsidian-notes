# C26 — Partnership Evaluation and MoU Checklist

_Toolkit · fills §5 of [[14-partnerships-ecosystem-and-founder-relationships|14 — Partnerships, Ecosystem and the Relationships Only a Founder Can Hold]] · Owner: the CEO scores it; the domain-expert director sanity-checks the OMC and trade rows; the CS and a lawyer own Part B before anything is signed · Cadence: Part A before the second meeting, always; Part B before any document is initialled; both re-read at every renewal · No workbook tab — signature and renewal dates go on `Board Calendar` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Partnerships are the most flattering way for a small company to waste a year. A meeting with an oil marketing company feels like progress in a way that a Tuesday spent onboarding a dealer in Durg does not, and it produces no revenue, no learning and no artefact. **The default assumption for any partnership proposal at VSYST's stage is no**, and Part A exists to make you argue your way past that default with a score rather than with enthusiasm.

The base rates are unkind. The failure rate for corporate alliances sits consistently between **60% and 70%** ([Hughes & Weiss — _Simple Rules for Making Alliances Work_, HBR, Nov 2007](https://hbr.org/2007/11/simple-rules-for-making-alliances-work)), and McKinsey's work across 500+ companies has found large fractions of alliance portfolios underperforming at any given moment ([McKinsey — Measuring alliance performance](https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/measuring-alliance-performance)) — **VERIFY LIVE** both figures if you intend to quote them. Hughes and Weiss's useful finding is _why_: alliances fail less on the business case and more on how the parties actually work together day to day, which is exactly the part nobody negotiates.

Part B is the commercial-terms checklist, and it exists because a founder-CEO of an Indian Private Limited company signs documents that bind the company and cannot un-sign them. Note first that an **MoU is not automatically non-binding**. Indian courts look at substance: if it satisfies the ingredients of a contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, it is enforceable regardless of what it is titled, and doctrines like promissory estoppel can bite on parts of it ([iPleaders — enforceability of an MoU](https://blog.ipleaders.in/legal-importance-memorandum-understanding-enforceability/); [Indian Contract Act, 1872](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Contract_Act,_1872)). "It is only an MoU" is not a legal position. **VERIFY LIVE** with a commercial lawyer before you rely on that sentence in any negotiation.

VSYST has two live relationships that this template is built for: **IOCL** discussions and **Easebuzz** payment-gateway onboarding (`<vault>/correspondence/`). They are different animals — one is a would-be channel with enormous asymmetric power, the other is a vendor wearing partner language — and Part A separates them in about twenty minutes.

## When to use

- **Before the second meeting. Always.** The first meeting is free and you should take it. The second one costs a day of preparation, a day of travel and a week of hope, and it should not happen unscored.
- **The moment somebody says "let's explore synergies."** Convert, don't decline: ask for the one sentence that says what each side gives and gets. If the answer comes back as "explore", send Part A's first three questions by email and see what returns ([[15-the-ceo-operating-cadence-and-calendar|lesson 15]] §on inbound).
- **Before any document is initialled** — MoU, LOI, NDA with commercial terms buried in it, a pilot agreement, a reseller schedule. Part B, with a lawyer.
- **At every renewal, term extension or scope change.** An auto-renewing exclusivity nobody re-read is how a small company loses a territory it never sold.
- **Not for a customer contract.** A dealer buying a subscription is a sale, not a partnership, and dressing it as one is how bespoke builds get promised.

## Part A — the scored screen

Score each row **0–5**. Anything under **21 of 35 does not get a second meeting.** Anything scoring 0 on rows 1, 2 or 7 is a no at any total — those three are gates, not weights.

| #     | Question                                                                                                            | What a 5 looks like                                                      | What a 0 looks like                                                                               | Score   |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| **1** | **Does it serve the strategy kernel?** ([[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager\|C04]])                                     | It directly attacks adoption friction, trust, or the district motion     | It is a new market, a new buyer or a new product wearing a partnership label                      | /5      |
| **2** | **Is the exchange symmetric?** Write both sides in one sentence each: _we give *** / we get ***_                    | Both sentences are concrete and neither is "credibility"                 | One side is a number and the other is a feeling                                                   | /5      |
| **3** | **Engineering cost, in founder-weeks** — including integration, certification and every future version of their API | Under 2 weeks, or zero code                                              | An integration whose maintenance never ends and whose schedule is theirs                          | /5      |
| **4** | **Support cost — the recurring human work it creates, and who does it**                                             | No new recurring work, or work a dealer does himself                     | A queue with no owner, which means the CEO ([[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list\|C25]] category 4) | /5      |
| **5** | **The exclusivity ask.** What are they asking us not to do, for how long, and where?                                | None asked, or narrow, short and reciprocal                              | Exclusive, all-India, perpetual or auto-renewing, one-way                                         | /5      |
| **6** | **The exit.** How do we leave, at what notice, and what do we lose on the way out?                                  | 30–90 days' notice, our data comes with us, dealers stay ours            | Their consent required to exit, or our dealer relationships are contractually theirs              | /5      |
| **7** | **The smallest test.** What is the cheapest thing that proves this works?                                           | One district, one quarter, five dealers, no exclusivity, no code         | "A national rollout after signing"                                                                | /5      |
| —     | **KILL CRITERION** — the fact that ends this, written **before** we start                                           | "If no dealer is referred within 90 days of go-live, we stop and say so" | Blank, or "if it isn't working"                                                                   | —       |
| —     | **TOTAL**                                                                                                           |                                                                          |                                                                                                   | **/35** |

**The kill criterion is the field that makes this document worth filling.** It is the same discipline as a bet's falsification condition ([[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]]), for the same reason: without one, a partnership can never be lost, so it can never be ended, so it consumes founder attention indefinitely while everyone waits politely for the other side to move. Write it before the second meeting, put its check date on the `Board Calendar`, and tell the other side what it is — a partner who reacts badly to "here is when we would stop" has told you something useful for free.

Two more discipline notes. **Score rows 3 and 4 with the engineer in the room, and that engineer is you** — a founder-CEO systematically under-estimates integration cost because he is scoring the version in his head, not the version with their sandbox, their certification cycle and their 2029 API deprecation. And **write row 2's two sentences before the meeting, not after**; if "we get" is _credibility_, _validation_ or _exposure_, you have a marketing expense, not a partnership, and you should price it as one.

## Part B — the commercial-terms checklist

Nine terms. Get a lawyer for all nine; get one _specifically_ for exclusivity, IP and data.

| Term                      | What to fix in writing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | The trap                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Revenue share**         | The percentage, of _what_ base (gross or net of GST), who invoices the dealer, who bears the payment-gateway cost, and when it is paid                                                                                                                                                  | A share of "revenue" undefined against GST and TCS. Define the base with the CA before the number                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Exclusivity**           | Scope, field of use, territory, duration, and whether it is reciprocal. Tie it to a **performance minimum** — exclusivity that survives non-performance is a hostage situation                                                                                                          | Vertical exclusive-distribution arrangements sit under §3(4) of the Competition Act, 2002 and are judged by the rule of reason on appreciable adverse effect ([Indian Kanoon — §3](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1153878/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/antitrust-eu-competition/977026/exclusivity-clauses-in-commercial-agreements-issues-under-indian-competition-law); [Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/vertical-agreements-in-indian-competition-law/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **Territory**             | Named districts or states, not "India". Say what happens in a district they do not work                                                                                                                                                                                                 | "All-India" given away for a pilot in one state                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Term**                  | A fixed initial term, and renewal by **express written agreement**, never automatic                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Evergreen auto-renewal with a 90-day objection window nobody diarises                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Termination**           | For convenience with notice, for cause with cure period, and on insolvency. Plus **survival**: what continues after termination and for how long                                                                                                                                        | Termination only for cause, which in practice means never                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **IP**                    | Each side keeps what it brought. Anything jointly developed is licensed, not assigned. **Never assign the core product**                                                                                                                                                                | "Work made for hire" or "all deliverables" language swallowing DZZLO                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Data ownership + DPDP** | Who is Data Fiduciary and who is Processor, per data category; purpose limitation; sub-processing; breach-notification obligations flowing both ways; deletion on exit ([DPDP Act, 2023 — MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)) | A partner claiming rights over dealer transaction data. That data is the moat and the liability at once ([[C24-crisis-comms-and-statement-kit\|C24]])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **SLAs**                  | Uptime, response and resolution times **you can actually meet at three people**, with a defined maintenance window that protects 22:00–06:00, and credits capped as a percentage of fees                                                                                                | Signing a 99.9% enterprise SLA with penalties, with no on-call rota funded ([[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Liability + indemnity** | A cap — commonly fees paid in the preceding 12 months — with narrow, negotiated carve-outs, and indemnities that are **mutual** ([ContractNerds](https://contractnerds.com/negotiating-saas-agreements/); [Galkin Law](https://galkinlaw.com/limitation-of-liability-for-saas/))        | A generous indemnity given away under a cap you cannot survive. A great indemnity under a low cap is worthless; a high cap with no indemnity is irrelevant                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

### The never-sign list

Four clauses that a three-person bootstrapped company does not sign, at any price, in any document, however preliminary. Print this and keep it in the meeting.

1. **Unlimited or uncapped liability.** A company with no revenue and no cyber cover cannot carry unbounded exposure. If the counterparty insists, the honest answer is that VSYST is too small for this deal today.
2. **Uncapped indemnity**, especially for their acts, their downstream customers, or data they control. Indemnities are mutual or they are not signed. (The one industry-standard exception people will cite is uncapped **IP-infringement** indemnity — treat it as a live negotiation with counsel, not as a default you concede.)
3. **Perpetual, irrevocable or auto-renewing exclusivity.** Any exclusivity must be time-boxed, territory-boxed, tied to a performance minimum, and terminable on failure to hit it.
4. **IP assignment of the core product**, in any form — assignment, joint ownership, a source-code escrow with a release trigger the partner controls, or a licence broad enough to let them build a substitute. DZZLO is the company. Assign it and there is nothing left to run.

Add a fifth in practice: **no MoU containing a binding commercial term you have not had a lawyer read**, however non-binding the covering language claims to be.

### What a founder may sign, and what needs the board

| Instrument                                                                                             | Who signs                                                                               | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mutual NDA, no commercial terms                                                                        | CEO alone                                                                               | Routine; still read the term and the carve-outs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| A no-cost, non-exclusive pilot in one district, ≤ 90 days, terminable at 30 days' notice               | CEO alone, then **reported** at the next board meeting                                  | Inside the delegated authority the [[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]] matrix should already grant                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Anything with **exclusivity**, a revenue share, an IP term, a data-processing term, or a liability cap | **Board resolution first**, then signature                                              | It binds the company beyond the CEO's remit. §179 vests the Board's powers and requires several of them to be exercised by resolution at a meeting ([§179(3), Companies Act 2013](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/138085142/); [MCA — Companies Act, 2013](https://www.mca.gov.in/Ministry/pdf/CompaniesAct2013.pdf)) — **VERIFY LIVE** the exact list with your CS |
| Anything where a director or a relative has an interest                                                | Board resolution **plus** disclosure of interest; the interested director does not vote | §166 requires independent judgement and avoidance of conflicting interest ([§166](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors/)); §184 governs disclosure — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CS. At a family-founded company this row is not theoretical                                                                                                                  |
| Anything mortgaging, charging or disposing of the undertaking, or borrowing beyond limits              | Board **and** shareholder approval                                                      | §180 territory. Ask the CS before the meeting, not after                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

The house rule that prevents most damage: **the CEO may sign anything he could unwind in 30 days at zero cost. Everything else gets minuted first** ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]).

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**A distribution partnership with an oil marketing company** — scored as it would realistically look today. Illustrative.

| #   | Row                 | Score       | Note                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --- | ------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Serves the kernel?  | **4**       | Borrowed trust is explicitly one of the kernel's three channels; an OMC endorsement is the strongest available                                                                                        |
| 2   | Symmetric exchange? | **2**       | _We give:_ integration work, compliance posture, a named account contact. _We get:_ introductions — with no volume commitment. Honest, and asymmetric                                                 |
| 3   | Engineering cost    | **2**       | Realistically 6–10 founder-weeks once certification and their release calendar are counted, and the schedule is theirs                                                                                |
| 4   | Support cost        | **2**       | Any dealer arriving via an OMC arrives expecting OMC-grade support we cannot staff yet                                                                                                                |
| 5   | Exclusivity ask     | **3**       | None asked yet — which usually means it arrives in the second draft. Pre-decide the answer now                                                                                                        |
| 6   | Exit                | **4**       | Clean today, because nothing is signed. Would fall to 1 if dealer relationships were routed through them                                                                                              |
| 7   | Smallest test       | **4**       | One district, five dealers they introduce, 90 days, no exclusivity, no integration                                                                                                                    |
| —   | **Total**           | **21 / 35** | Exactly at the threshold — proceed **only** as the row-7 test, never as the row-2 partnership                                                                                                         |
| —   | **Kill criterion**  |             | **"If fewer than 3 dealers are introduced and onboarded within 90 days of the pilot letter, we stop, we say so in writing, and we do not build the integration."** Check date on the `Board Calendar` |

The score is doing exactly what it should: a genuinely valuable relationship that is **not yet a partnership**. The correct move is the cheapest version — a district pilot with no code and no exclusivity — and the correct thing to protect is row 6, because the day dealer relationships run through the partner rather than through VSYST, the exit price becomes the company.

**Contrast: Easebuzz.** Score it and it comes out low on rows 1 and 2 — and that is fine, because it is **a vendor, not a partner**. Payment gateways are procured, compared on rates, settlement cycles and chargeback terms, and registered in the COO's [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]. Running Part A on it takes ten minutes and its whole value is the ten minutes it saves you from treating a supplier relationship as a strategic one.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | What it looks like                            | Fix                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Partnership as procrastination**            | Six months of meetings, no dealers onboarded  | Default is no. Score it, and compare the founder-weeks against a quarter of selling            |
| **"We get credibility"**                      | Row 2's second sentence is a feeling          | Price it as a marketing expense or walk. Credibility is not consideration                      |
| **No kill criterion**                         | Everyone waits politely for two years         | Write it before meeting two, date it, and tell the other side                                  |
| **Underestimating integration**               | "It's just an API"                            | Count certification, their release calendar, and every future version. Then double it          |
| **Signing exclusivity for a pilot**           | All-India exclusive for five introductions    | Never-sign #3. Time-boxed, territory-boxed, performance-tied, or nothing                       |
| **"It's only an MoU"**                        | Commercial terms initialled without counsel   | Substance beats the title under the Contract Act. Lawyer first                                 |
| **An SLA you cannot staff**                   | 99.9% with penalties, no on-call rota         | Sign what you can meet at three people, with a maintenance window protecting 22:00–06:00       |
| **Data terms left vague**                     | "Both parties may use the data"               | Fiduciary/Processor named per category, purpose limited, deleted on exit                       |
| **A founder signing what needs a resolution** | Exclusivity signed on a handshake             | The 30-day-unwind rule. Otherwise minute it first ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes\|C17]]) |
| **A conflicted director not disclosing**      | A relative's firm becomes the channel partner | §166/§184. Disclose, do not vote, minute it — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CS                      |
| **No renewal diary**                          | Exclusivity auto-renews in year three         | Renewal dates on the `Board Calendar` the day of signature                                     |
| **Treating a vendor as a partner**            | A gateway relationship gets founder attention | Vendors go in [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register\|T14]] and get compared on price                  |
