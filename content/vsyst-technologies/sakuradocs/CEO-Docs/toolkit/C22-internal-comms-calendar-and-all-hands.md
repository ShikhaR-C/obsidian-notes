# C22 — Internal Comms Calendar and All-Hands Template

_Toolkit · fills §4 of [[12-communication-the-ceo-as-chief-storyteller|12 — Communication: The CEO as Chief Storyteller]] and §6 of [[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|11 — Culture and Values as a CEO Instrument]] · Owner: the CEO — all four artefacts here are written by the CEO personally and none of them are delegable · Cadence: weekly note every week, all-hands opening monthly, strategy update quarterly, bad news the same day · No workbook tab — the dates live on the `Board Calendar` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Four artefacts and a calendar. The **weekly written note**, the **five minutes that open the monthly all-hands**, the **quarterly strategy update**, and the **shape of bad news**. That is the CEO's entire internal communication surface. Everything else — the ops meeting, the 1:1 template, the scorecard, the support queue, the sprint — belongs to the COO course, and a CEO who writes about those is doing the COO's job badly while not doing his own ([[12-communication-the-ceo-as-chief-storyteller|lesson 12]] §1.3).

**Say the boundary out loud, because it is the thing that goes wrong.** [[T24-all-hands-agenda|T24]] owns the all-hands: the slot, the forty-five minutes, the facilitation, the wins block, the Q&A discipline, the rule that nobody hears news about their own work in that room for the first time. This template owns **the first five minutes and the last thirty seconds** — the direction, and the one number. If you find yourself writing an agenda here, stop and open T24. If you find T24 telling you what the company's direction is, that is a bug in T24.

The format is built on one finding that founders reliably disbelieve: repetition is not nagging. Neeley and Leonardi shadowed thirteen managers across six companies for more than 250 hours, logging every communication, and found **one in every seven communications was deliberately redundant with a previous one sent over a different medium — and the managers who were redundant that way moved their projects faster and more smoothly** ([HBR — _Effective Managers Say the Same Thing Twice (or More)_](https://hbr.org/2011/05/defend-your-research-effective-managers-say-the-same-thing-twice-or-more), 2011; the longer write-up at [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/its-not-nagging-why-persistent-redundant-communication-works)). The written note and the spoken opening are the same message on two media. That is the design, not a duplication to be optimised away.

The second foundation is that writing outlives rooms. "Speaking only helps who's in the room, writing helps everyone. This includes people who couldn't make it, or future employees who join years from now" ([37signals — How We Communicate](https://37signals.com/how-we-communicate)). At three people the "couldn't make it" audience is empty and the "future employees" audience is the entire point.

## When to use

- **From week one of the CEO seat existing** — even at three co-located directors, and even though it feels absurd. A transparency ritual started at four people reads as how the company works; the same ritual retrofitted at fifteen reads as a response to a problem.
- **The month the first person outside the three directors joins** — that is when the all-hands becomes a real meeting rather than the ops meeting wearing a hat ([[T24-all-hands-agenda|T24]]).
- **Before each quarterly planning half-day**, so the plan that follows is downstream of a stated diagnosis rather than of momentum ([[C28-annual-plan-and-ceo-okr-sheet|C28]]).
- **The hour you learn anything bad** — runway shortened, a dealer churned, a release broke money, a person is leaving, a partnership died. Template (d), same day.
- **Not** for status, blockers, ticket queues or sprint scope. Those are [[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]] and [[T28-weekly-business-review|T28]].

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Fix the four dates before you write a word of content.** A weekly artefact without a fixed slot becomes fortnightly, then quarterly, then a story you tell about a habit you used to have. Put all four on the `Board Calendar` tab and treat them like a statutory filing.
2. **Write the note even in a week where nothing happened.** Four lines saying so is information; silence is a signal the reader will fill in with something worse.
3. **Numbers come from the scorecard, never from memory.** If a number is worth stating weekly it is a row in the COO's [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]. Two sources of numbers is worse than one source and much worse than none.
4. **The `NOT DOING` block is compulsory.** It is the block that carries strategy, because strategy is refusal ([[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]]), and it is the block that stops a co-director quietly building the thing you decided against. Each line should trace to a row in [[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]].
5. **One channel, and it is not chat.** "Writing solidifies, chat dissolves" ([37signals](https://37signals.com/how-we-communicate)). The note goes into the vault; a **link** goes into WhatsApp. Never the note itself into WhatsApp, where it is scrolled past by 9 a.m. and unfindable by Thursday.
6. **Sentence one of the all-hands opening should barely change month to month.** The team's read on whether the CEO knows what they are doing is formed almost entirely by whether the direction sentence is stable. If it moves monthly it is not a direction ([[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §6.1).
7. **Open on a person and close on a number.** The customer story is ninety seconds about **one named dealer in one named district and what changed for him** — not a metric, not a segment, not "dealers are telling us". The close is **one** number, the same number every month, said last so it is the thing people carry out of the room.
8. **Include one thing that went badly, before anyone asks.** A CEO who only opens with good news trains a company to disbelieve the opening — and to route problems around him, which is how a problem becomes a crisis ([[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things|lesson 18]]).
9. **Praise in writing, correct in person.** Nothing that affects a person's own role, pay or standing reaches them for the first time in a group setting. That is the no-surprises rule and it runs in both directions ([[12-communication-the-ceo-as-chief-storyteller|lesson 12]] §4.4–4.5).
10. **Recognise a behaviour, not an outcome.** "Ten dealers signed" teaches people to want outcomes, which they already do. "She drove to Durg on a Saturday to watch the OTP flow fail in the dealer's hands" teaches them what to copy.
11. **Go first on your own mistakes.** Psychological safety is produced by observed consequences, not by stating a policy ([Psychological safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_safety); Edmondson, 1999). The cheapest mechanism a founder-CEO has is to make his own contribution to a failure the first line of the post-mortem ([[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]] is the template; the CEO owns whether the room is safe enough to fill it in honestly — [Google SRE Book, Postmortem Culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/)).

## Template (b) — the weekly note

```
VSYST — week of <date>                                    from <CEO>

ONE THING      the single most important thing this week, and why it is that
               and not the other candidate

WHAT CHANGED   outside: a dealer conversation, an OMC signal, a competitor, a
               rule change.  inside: a decision made, or a decision reversed

THE NUMBERS    3-5 lines straight from T05. one line of commentary only on
               anything off-track. no adjectives

DECIDED        what was decided and, one line each, why. anything one-way-door
               links to its C29 entry

NOT DOING      what we chose not to do, and what would make us reconsider

I GOT WRONG    one line. every week. this is the line that does the work

ASK            what I need from each of you, by name, with a date
```

## Template (c) — the all-hands run-of-show

Forty-five minutes at full size; twenty is the honest version at four people. The CEO owns rows 1 and 7. **T24 owns rows 2–6** — do not rewrite them here.

| #     | Block                    | Min | Who             | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----- | ------------------------ | --- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Customer story**       | 3   | **CEO**         | One named dealer, one district, one thing that changed for him this month. A person, not a metric. Then: the direction sentence, unchanged; what changed outside; what that means for the next four weeks; **one thing that went badly** |
| 2     | The numbers              | 8   | COO             | The scorecard, unedited — the same sheet the directors read on Monday ([[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]])                                                                                                                                       |
| 3     | Where the quarter stands | 8   | COO             | Rocks / OKRs, on-track and not, with the misses named ([[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review\|T19]])                                                                                                                                           |
| 4     | Wins and recognition     | 5   | COO             | Behaviours named, not outcomes. Include a well-run failure                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 5     | What we said no to       | 3   | CEO             | Two or three rows off [[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list\|C25]], with the reason. A no-list said out loud is the strongest evidence that a strategy exists                                                                               |
| 6     | Ask me anything          | 12  | COO facilitates | Questions taken in advance and live. "I don't know, I'll answer by Tuesday" is a complete answer — then answer by Tuesday                                                                                                                |
| **7** | **The one number**       | 1   | **CEO**         | Close on it. Same number every month. Say it, say what it needs to be, stop talking                                                                                                                                                      |

## Template (d) — bad news, internally

```
1. FAST          Same day. Before the rumour, before the dinner table.
2. FIRST-PERSON  "I decided." "I was wrong about." Never "mistakes were made."
3. THE FACTS     What happened, in numbers, unsoftened. What you don't yet know,
                 and the date you will know it.
4. THE COST      What it means for us. Runway, plan, this quarter's priority.
                 Say the uncomfortable number out loud.
5. THE PLAN      What we do next, who owns each part, by when. A partial plan
                 beats "we're figuring it out."
6. THE ASK       What you need from them — and what you are NOT asking for.
7. THE DOOR      "Ask me anything now, or come to me alone after." Then be
                 genuinely available for two hours.
```

The three ways to get it wrong are **slow, third-person, or without a plan**, and speed is the largest single variable in how it lands ([a16z — _The Struggle_](https://a16z.com/the-struggle/); the companion argument that there is usually no clever manoeuvre is [a16z — _Lead Bullets_](https://a16z.com/lead-bullets/)). At a **family-founded** company there is a specific hazard: bad news has an informal channel that outruns every formal one, and a director's spouse can know before the Support/Ops Associate does. Nothing closes that gap entirely. Speed narrows it — say it the same day, to everyone, in the same words, so the informal channel carries a rumour that has already been confirmed.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**Friday note, week of 2026-08-21.** All figures illustrative.

```
VSYST — week of 21 Aug 2026                               from Shikhar

ONE THING    Getting the ledger-rebuild fix merged and deployed. Not the
             onboarding import. A correctness fix sitting unmerged for nine
             days means the corrupting code is still the code that runs.

WHAT CHANGED outside: Bhilai dealer asked, unprompted, whether we can prove a
             balance to his CA — third dealer in six weeks to ask about proof
             rather than features.  inside: reversed last week's call to ship
             the analytics screen this month.

NUMBERS      Paying GSTINs 0 (pilots 6) · Cash ₹— · Runway — months ·
             Nightly rate confirmations 71% (target 80) · Pilots transacting
             at day 14: 3 of 6.

DECIDED      Ledger correctness takes the next bet slot. Why: three of six
             pilots have raised proof unprompted; bet 3's falsification
             condition has not triggered. Analytics deferred.

NOT DOING    Custom invoice layouts (C25, 2 askers, trigger: 5).
             ISO 27001 fieldwork (no deal requires it yet).

I GOT WRONG  I let a correctness fix sit on a branch for nine days because it
             was "basically done". It was not deployed, so it was not done.

ASK          Papa — two Durg dealer intros by Wed 27th.
             Mummy — the August compliance dates confirmed with the CA by Fri.
```

**The all-hands opening, five minutes, September.** _"Ramesh in Bhilai called on Tuesday. His fleet customer disputed a ₹4 lakh balance from June. He opened DZZLO on his phone in front of the customer, showed him the confirmed rate from 12 June at 22:41 and the OTP-verified delivery, and the argument ended in ninety seconds. He told me it was the first time in twenty-two years he has won that argument without shouting. That is the company._ **_Where we are going, unchanged: we are building the shared ledger between a fuel dealer and his credit customers, one district at a time._** _What changed this month: three separate dealers asked us to prove a number to their CA. That is the buying signal we did not expect and it is why correctness takes the next build slot instead of analytics. What went badly: I sat on a correctness fix for nine days. That will not happen again, and the rule that prevents it is now written down."_

**The close, thirty seconds.** _"One number. **Pilots transacting on day fourteen: three of six.** It needs to be six of six before we sell a single new district. Everything we do this month is that number."_

**Why that number and not revenue.** Revenue is zero and will be zero for a while; a number that cannot move is not a rallying number, it is a demoralising one. Day-14 activation is the weak link in the kernel's chain, it moves weekly, and every person in the room can affect it ([[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]] §2.2). **Change the one number only at a quarter boundary, and say out loud that you are changing it and why** — a number that quietly swaps when it stops flattering you is worse than no number at all.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | What it looks like                                       | Fix                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rebuilding T24 here**                       | A CEO-authored 45-minute agenda competing with the COO's | Rule: you own five minutes and thirty seconds. [[T24-all-hands-agenda\|T24]] owns the rest                                                                       |
| **The note drifting to status**               | Three paragraphs of sprint progress                      | Status is [[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda\|T03]]. If you deleted the status, is anything left? If not, you had nothing to say — write the four lines that say so |
| **Skipping the week nothing happened**        | Two notes in August, none in September                   | Rule 2. The gap is the message                                                                                                                                   |
| **A direction sentence that changes monthly** | Different opening line every all-hands                   | Rule 6. If it genuinely changed, that is a [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]] entry and you say so explicitly                                    |
| **Opening on a metric**                       | "Activation was 47% this month"                          | Open on Ramesh in Bhilai. The number closes; a person opens                                                                                                      |
| **Good-news-only openings**                   | Three months of wins, then a shock                       | Rule 8. One bad thing, said before anyone asks, every time                                                                                                       |
| **Deleting `I GOT WRONG` in a good week**     | It vanishes the month you most need it                   | It is a habit or it is nothing. In a genuinely clean week write the smallest true thing                                                                          |
| **Two sources of numbers**                    | The note says 71%, the scorecard says 68%                | Rule 3. One source — [[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]] — always                                                                                                         |
| **Bad news delivered third-person**           | "It was decided that…"                                   | Template (d) step 2. First person or do not send it                                                                                                              |
| **Correcting someone in the all-hands**       | A named miss discussed in the room                       | Praise in writing, correct in person. That conversation is a [[T04-one-on-one-template\|T04]]                                                                    |
| **The note in WhatsApp**                      | Nine screens of text at 4:30 p.m. Friday                 | Rule 5. Vault page, WhatsApp link                                                                                                                                |
