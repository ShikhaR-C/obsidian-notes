# C21 — Values and Operating-Principles Worksheet

_Toolkit · fills exercises 12.2 and 12.3 in [[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|11 — Culture and Values as a CEO Instrument]] §3–§5 · Owner: the CEO drafts and writes first but shares **last**; all three directors argue; the **board adopts and minutes** it · Cadence: adopted once over three weeks, reviewed at twelve months — not before and not never · Workbook: no tab of its own. The counter-signal list becomes a quarterly checklist; the values competency row goes into [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process|COO T10]] and [[C19-executive-hiring-scorecard-and-loop|C19]]._

## Purpose

One page that converts a values discussion into an instrument. It exists to stop the thing that happens in every founding team that has ever tried this: ninety minutes in a room produces "Integrity. Excellence. Customer focus." and everyone leaves feeling good, and nothing about how the company behaves has changed.

The governing test is Patrick Lencioni's, and this worksheet is built entirely around it. His 2002 essay opens with four words — "Communication. Respect. Integrity. Excellence." — and then names whose values they were: Enron's ([_Make Your Values Mean Something_](https://hbr.org/2002/07/make-your-values-mean-something), HBR, July 2002). The list is not wrong; it is **unfalsifiable**. Nobody has ever been fired for insufficient Excellence, because nobody can say what would count as a violation. So:

> **A value that nothing could violate is not a value. It is a mood.**

Every row of this worksheet therefore has to name **the behaviour it forbids** and **the trade-off it accepts**. A value is a constraint you agree to in advance, before you know what it will cost. If it never costs anything, it is not doing any work.

The second half of the worksheet is the half most companies skip. A value with no mechanism is a preference. Ben Horowitz's frame — culture is a set of actions, "who you are is what you do", designed or inherited but never absent ([a16z](https://a16z.com/books/what-you-do-is-who-you-are/)) — turns each value into a to-do list: a hiring question, a review criterion, a ritual, a public reward, a consequence, a budget line. Netflix's stated values are not its instrument; the **keeper test** is, and it is a mechanism, not a sentiment ([jobs.netflix.com/culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture); the original deck stated the price plainly — "adequate performance gets a generous severance package" — [Culture: Freedom & Responsibility](https://www.slideshare.net/reed2001/culture-1798664); [Patty McCord, _How Netflix Reinvented HR_](https://hbr.org/2014/01/how-netflix-reinvented-hr), HBR 2014). Amazon's principles work because they are written as behaviours in tension and used verbatim in interview loops and promotion documents ([Amazon Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles)); Stripe's read as an operating manual rather than a virtue list ([Stripe — culture](https://stripe.com/en-in/jobs/culture)). And the last column — the **counter-signal** — is the honest one: the specific observable thing that would prove to any employee that the value is fake.

## When to use

- **Before VSYST's first non-founder hire.** Values written after there are employees are read as a message about somebody. Written before, they are read as the terms of the job.
- **Whenever the values-based competency row in a hiring scorecard has to be written** ([[C19-executive-hiring-scorecard-and-loop|C19]] rule 4, [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process|COO T10]]). A value that is not in the hiring rubric is not being selected for.
- **At the twelve-month review**, with one question at the top: _which value has been enforced at a cost, and when?_
- **After a counter-signal actually occurs** — that is a review trigger, not a bad day.
- **Never as a launch artefact.** If the first time anyone hears these words is a poster or a careers page, you have built the thing this worksheet exists to prevent.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Audit before you draft.** Answer the three questions in [[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §1.2 for the last 90 days, with named incidents: what got **rewarded**, what got **tolerated**, what got **punished** (including silently). Those three lists _are_ your current values. Tolerance does most of the work — it happens forty times a week and is invisible — and Horowitz's line is the operational version: "if you see something off-culture and ignore it, you've created a new culture" ([a16z](https://a16z.com/books/what-you-do-is-who-you-are/)).
2. **Four to six, no more.** Fewer than four says nothing; more than six cannot be recited, which means they cannot be used at 4 AM, which is the only time they matter.
3. **Every value passes three tests in writing before it goes near a wall** (Template b). No exceptions, including for the one everybody loves.
4. **Write the counter-signal column first.** It is the only way to get an honest table. If you cannot name the observable event that would prove the value fake, you have written a slogan.
5. **Label the type, Lencioni-style — core / aspirational / permission-to-play / accidental — and publish the label.** Publishing an aspiration as a fact is how a team learns that the values page is fiction ([Lencioni](https://hbr.org/2002/07/make-your-values-mean-something); [Edgar Schein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Schein)). "We are not this yet, and here is how you will know when we are" is a stronger sentence than any adjective.
6. **A value needs at least three of the six mechanism slots filled** — Selection, Evaluation, Ritual, Public reward, Consequence, Allocation ([[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §4). Fewer than three and it is cut or downgraded to aspirational. **Allocation is the slot founders never fill and the one nobody can argue with**: the budget and the CEO's calendar are the values statement nobody edits for effect.
7. **The consequence slot must say what a violation costs, up to and including exit.** Without it the value is advisory. This is the slot founders skip, and its absence is visible to employees within a month.
8. **Do not import a mechanism you cannot back.** Netflix's keeper test is paired with very high pay, real severance and enough scale that one manager's judgement is checked by others. A keeper test run by a founder-CEO with no board, no HR function and family on the team is not a talent-density mechanism; it is an unappealable dismissal power with a slogan attached. The same warning applies to behavioural principles wired into a performance system: the _New York Times_ described Amazon's as producing a bruising workplace, with a peer-feedback tool usable to undermine colleagues ([Kantor & Streitfeld, _Inside Amazon_](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/08/16/technology/inside-amazon-wrestling-big-ideas-in-a-bruising-workplace.html), 15 Aug 2015; Amazon disputed the characterisation). **Import the honesty about cost, not the machinery.**
9. **Where a value and an incentive disagree, the incentive wins.** BYJU'S published a mission about learning outcomes while running a sales system regulators described as using "aggressive sales practices and deceptive marketing strategies" ([BYJU'S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byju%27s)). Nothing in the stated mission caused that; the compensation scheme did, and the mission provided cover. Before adopting a value, check what your own pay and praise actually reward.
10. **Values are adopted by the board and minuted.** They constrain hiring, pay and dismissal, so this is a governance act, not a comms exercise ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]). Set the review date in the same minute.
11. **The first violation is the real adoption.** Until a value has been enforced once against somebody's convenience — and everyone saw it — it has been announced, not adopted.

## Template (a) — the value row

Five columns. Fill them right to left if you want the truth: counter-signal, then mechanism, then forbids, then demands, then the name.

| Column                                          | What goes in it                                                                                                                                                                 | The test it has to pass                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Value**                                       | A sentence, not a noun. Ugly and specific beats elegant and abstract                                                                                                            | Could a new hire repeat it on day three?                 |
| **The behaviour it demands**                    | What a person actually does, observably, in an ordinary week                                                                                                                    | Could you watch someone doing it?                        |
| **What it forbids**                             | At least one specific, plausible, **tempting** behaviour                                                                                                                        | Is it something someone here would genuinely want to do? |
| **The mechanism that enforces it**              | ≥3 of six slots: a **hiring question** · a **review criterion** · a **ritual** · a **public reward** · a **consequence, up to firing** · an **allocation** of money or calendar | Does it run whether or not anyone feels like it?         |
| **The counter-signal that would prove it fake** | The observable event that, if it happened and nobody flinched, would tell every employee the value is decorative                                                                | Is it a thing you could actually notice this quarter?    |

Two supporting columns on the same row: **the trade-off accepted** (\_"We accept **_ in order to protect _**"\_) and **the Lencioni type**.

## Template (b) — the three tests, plus Schein's fourth

Run these in writing, per candidate value, before anything is adopted.

```
1. FORBIDDEN-BEHAVIOUR TEST
   Name one specific, plausible, TEMPTING behaviour this value rules out.
   Cannot name one -> delete the value.

2. TRADE-OFF TEST
   "We accept ______________ in order to protect ______________."
   Every real value trades against something else you also want:
   speed, revenue, harmony, a hire you liked.

3. COSTLY-CASE TEST
   Describe ONE concrete situation in the next twelve months where honouring
   this value costs money, a customer, a deal or a person.
   Cannot imagine one -> it is permission-to-play, not core.

4. ASSUMPTION TEST  (Schein)
   Does this describe something you already do, or something you wish?
   Both are allowed. LABEL WHICH. A wish published as a fact is how a team
   learns the values page is fiction.

5. THE PERSONAL-COST ROUND  (three founders, out loud)
   Each director says one thing this value will cost THEM personally.
   Nobody can name one -> cut it on the spot.
```

## Template (c) — the argue-and-adopt process, three founders, three weeks

Do not adopt values in a meeting. Meetings produce the values of whoever speaks best — and in a family firm, seniority in the family and seniority in the company are not the same ranking, and everyone in the room knows it. Writing first is what makes step 3 survivable: it converts an argument about people into an argument about text, which is the cheapest [psychological safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_safety) any three-person company can buy.

| Step                            | When                          | Who                              | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                        | Artefact                                             |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Write independently**      | Week 1, before any discussion | All three, separately            | Each writes 3–6 values in the sharp form: forbids, costs, one costly case. **Any language, any format** — five bullets in Hindi in a WhatsApp note counts. The CEO writes first and shares **last** | Three private drafts                                 |
| **2. Merge on paper**           | Week 1, end                   | CEO                              | All three drafts in one document, unedited, side by side. Mark overlaps. **Do not resolve conflicts yet**                                                                                           | One merged sheet in the vault                        |
| **3. Argue the conflicts only** | Week 2, one 90-min session    | All three                        | Discuss only where the drafts disagree. Name the decider before each argument starts ([[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]]). The domain-expert director speaks first; the CEO speaks last            | A shortlist                                          |
| **4. The cost test, out loud**  | Week 2, same session          | All three                        | Template (b) step 5. A value nobody can name a personal cost for is cut on the spot                                                                                                                 | The cut list, and why                                |
| **5. Draft the mechanisms**     | Week 3                        | CEO drafts; the COO seat reviews | Each survivor gets ≥3 of the six slots plus its counter-signal. Fewer than three → cut or downgrade to aspirational                                                                                 | This worksheet, filled                               |
| **6. Adopt and minute**         | Week 3, board meeting         | Board                            | Approve the set, the mechanisms, the aspirational labels **and the review date**                                                                                                                    | A resolution; the versioned values page in the vault |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**A draft to argue with, not a set to adopt as written.** Each is grounded in something already true about this company. Full rows, in the sharp form.

| #      | Value                                            | Behaviour it demands                                                                                                                   | What it forbids                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Mechanism (≥3 slots)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Counter-signal that would prove it fake                                                                                                                                     | Trade-off accepted                                                                                                                                                                        | Type                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **V1** | **The district is the classroom**                | Everyone who builds, sells or supports has sat in a dealer's office in the last 90 days                                                | Building from a WhatsApp feature request without seeing the ledger behind it; deciding what dealers want in a room no dealer has sat in; treating support as an interruption to real work                                 | **Hiring question:** "tell me about a time you changed your mind because of something a customer said in person." **Ritual:** every meeting opens with 90 seconds on one named dealer. **Allocation:** a district-travel budget line that is never the first thing cut                   | A quarter passes with no founder in a dealer's office and nobody notices. Or travel is cut in a tight month while a tooling subscription survives                           | Build-days lost to travel; slower shipping; good ideas die because no dealer will spend twenty minutes on them                                                                            | **Aspirational** for two quarters, then core                                                                                                                                                              |
| **V2** | **Say the number**                               | Figures, not adjectives. Estimates marked as estimates. "I don't know" is complete and respectable when it carries an owner and a date | "Good traction", "growing well", "a few dealers" — internally or externally; seeded demo data shown without saying so; rounding up for a partner meeting                                                                  | **Review criterion:** "brings numbers, flags the ones that are estimates." **Ritual:** the all-hands scorecard _is_ the unedited directors' sheet ([[T05-kpi-scorecard\|COO T05]]). **Consequence:** a knowingly inflated external number is a documented, escalating matter             | A founder inflates a pilot count in front of a partner and the other two say nothing in the car afterwards. Or a number is quietly corrected between two decks with no note | Harder conversations. In an IOCL or investor room an honest small number sits beside a competitor's flattering large one                                                                  | **Core**                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **V3** | **If it isn't written, it didn't happen**        | A decision exists when it is in the vault with a decider, a date and the reasoning. Positions written **before** the discussion        | Decisions reached at dinner and executed on Monday with no record; "we discussed this already" as an argument; knowledge that lives only in a founder's head                                                              | **Ritual:** decision-log entry within 24 hours ([[T09-decision-log-and-adr\|COO T09]], [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]]). **Selection:** a written work-sample in every loop. **Public reward:** recognition for "wrote the SOP so the next person didn't have to ask" | A decision taken at dinner is executed on Monday with no entry. Or the person who writes things down is the person seen as behind on "real work"                            | Real hours — and, named honestly, it advantages the best writer, who here is the CEO. **Mitigation is part of the value:** any language, any format; the CEO writes first and shares last | **Aspirational → core** once 90 days of entries exist                                                                                                                                                     |
| **V4** | **A dealer's ledger is sacred**                  | Tenant data touched only for a named reason, by a named person, for a bounded time, logged                                             | Production queries to satisfy curiosity; a screenshot of a dealer's outstandings pasted into WhatsApp to make a point; "just this once" access during a crunch; using one dealer's data to help another without saying so | **Consequence:** a stated exit-level breach in the code of conduct ([[T27-policy-handbook-toc\|COO T27]]). **Ritual:** quarterly access review ([[07-tools-and-it-foundation\|COO 07]]). **Selection:** a scenario question in every technical interview                                 | A production query run out of curiosity becomes a funny story rather than an incident. Or an exception is made "because it's faster" during a crunch                        | Engineering time, slower debugging, and occasionally a slower answer to a dealer who wants one now                                                                                        | **Core**, and partly permission-to-play — it is also the law                                                                                                                                              |
| **V5** | **Ship to a district before you demo to a hall** | A feature is done when a dealer used it unaided on an ordinary Tuesday. Adoption in one district beats a launch anywhere               | Conference-driven roadmaps; announcing before adoption; counting a merged pull request as delivery; building for the demo path                                                                                            | **Definition of done** ([[12-product-and-engineering-operations\|COO 12]]). **The no-list** in [[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list\|C25]]. **Public reward** for whoever found the dealer who tested it                                                                                   | A launch post goes out for something no dealer has used unaided. Or a feature is built for a slide in a partner meeting                                                     | You look smaller than you are for longer; some marketing moments are skipped on purpose                                                                                                   | **Aspirational → core**                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **V6** | **The family gets the harder question**          | Where a decision involves a director's relative, scrutiny goes **up**, and the reasoning is written                                    | A relative appointed, paid, promoted or retained on a decision that would not survive being written down; a performance conversation postponed because of who the person is                                               | **Selection & evaluation:** same band, same charter, criteria written before the review ([[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design\|C20]]). **Ritual:** the register of interests; the s.188 resolution. **Consequence:** the written "would we hire this person from outside?" test      | A family employee's review is postponed twice and nobody names it. Or a pay decision for a relative is made at home and announced at work                                   | Real family friction — and it will feel unfair to the person on the receiving end. Say so out loud when adopting it                                                                       | **Core**, and the only one with a statutory shadow (**s.166** already forbids a director securing "undue gain" for a relative — [Companies Act 2013, s.166](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors/)) |

**Decide V6 deliberately.** Publishing it announces the problem to every future employee, which is either honest or alarming depending on how it is written. Not publishing it means the ground rules exist privately — still far better than nothing, and the house call if the three of you cannot agree. What is not acceptable is having neither.

**Two notes on the draft.** V2, V3 and V4 are things VSYST arguably already does, and that is a feature: Schein's model says the durable values are the assumptions already in operation, so writing down what you already do is how a founding team's habits survive hire number seven ([Schein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Schein)). And **one deliberate, costly, visible act is worth ten mechanisms at the start** — refusing a deal because the dealer wanted his competitor's data; telling IOCL a smaller pilot number than you wished you had. You get about one of these a quarter. Do not let one pass unnamed.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | What it looks like                                                                                        | Fix                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The unfalsifiable list**                      | Integrity. Excellence. Customer focus                                                                     | The core rule. Name the forbidden behaviour or delete the value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Drafting before auditing**                    | A clean new set that contradicts what the company visibly rewards                                         | Rule 1. The rewarded/tolerated/punished lists first, with named incidents                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **No counter-signal column**                    | Every value looks true because nothing could disprove it                                                  | Rule 4. Write that column first                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Aspiration published as fact**                | "We are radically transparent" on day one                                                                 | Rule 5. Label the type and say when you will know you have arrived                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **A value with one mechanism**                  | It appears in the all-hands and nowhere else                                                              | Rule 6. Three of six slots, or downgrade it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **No consequence slot**                         | Nobody can say what a violation costs                                                                     | Rule 7. Up to and including exit, in writing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Importing a mechanism you cannot back**       | A keeper test at a nine-person family firm                                                                | Rule 8. Import the honesty about cost, not the instrument                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Values that contradict the incentives**       | A "customer first" value and a bonus paid purely on signups                                               | Rule 9. When they disagree, the incentive wins every time                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Adopting them in one meeting**                | The values of whoever speaks best                                                                         | Template (c). Written independently first; the CEO shares last                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Too many**                                    | Nine values, nobody can name three                                                                        | Rule 2. Four to six                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Never enforced**                              | A year passes and no value has cost anyone anything                                                       | Rule 11. The review question is _which value has been enforced at a cost, and when?_                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Copying a company whose model can afford it** | "No metric-based growth targets", borrowed from a profitable bootstrapped giant, at a pre-revenue company | Steal the **form** — prohibitions on your own behaviour with the cost visible ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/about/philosophy/); [Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/aboutus.html)) — not the specific prohibitions                                                                                                                                    |
| **Forgetting that a CEO's aside is policy**     | Praising the person who answered a dealer's WhatsApp at 11:40 PM                                          | You have just set a norm about 11:40 PM, whatever you intended. India's public argument about 70- and 90-hour weeks turns on exactly this: employees read a founder's opinion as policy ([N. R. Narayana Murthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._R._Narayana_Murthy); [S. N. Subrahmanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._N._Subrahmanyan)) |
