# C27 — CEO Weekly Operating Template and Calendar Audit

_Toolkit · fills the calendar-audit and weekly-grid exercises in [[15-the-ceo-operating-cadence-and-calendar|15 — The CEO's Operating Cadence and Calendar]] · Owner: the CEO, alone — nobody audits your calendar for you · Cadence: the audit monthly (30 minutes); the weekly template every Friday (25 minutes) · Workbook tab: the audit totals go on `CEO Dashboard` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two instruments that only work as a pair. **Part A, the calendar audit**, looks backward: pull last month, code every hour against the four CEO jobs, total it, and read the totals next to your written strategy. **Part B, the weekly template**, looks forward: a grid of named, defended blocks, a single top goal, six review questions and an energy log.

The audit exists because a strategy document is a statement of intent and a calendar is a record of behaviour, and when they disagree the calendar is right. At a three-person pre-revenue company that is not a metaphor: the founders' hours _are_ the capital allocation — there is no marketing budget to misallocate and no headcount plan to get wrong. The largest study of CEO time, 1,114 diaries across six countries parsed by machine learning, found that time-use data alone sorts CEOs into "leaders" and "managers", that the classification predicts firm productivity, and that roughly **17% of CEOs are mismatched** to their firms ([Bandiera, Hansen, Prat and Sadun, NBER WP 23248, 2017](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23248)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. A stranger holding only your diary can tell what kind of CEO you are. You do not get to overrule that with a deck.

Drucker put the method sixty years earlier and it has not been improved on: effective executives **record their time, manage their time, then consolidate it** — in that order, because everyone's memory of where their hours went is systematically kind ([_The Effective Executive_, 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Effective_Executive)). Part A records. Part B consolidates. The **gap between the two readings — percentages on one side, the kernel on the other — is the cheapest strategy diagnostic that exists**: thirty minutes, no consultant, no data you do not already own, and it produces a sentence you cannot argue with.

## When to use

- **First run: this month, on last month**, before writing any new plan. A plan written by someone who has not measured last month is a wish.
- **Monthly thereafter**, on the day you re-base runway in [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] — hours and cash are the only two things VSYST spends.
- **Whenever the week has felt full and nothing moved** — that feeling is a hypothesis, and the audit tests it — and **before any argument about hiring**, because "we need someone" is a claim about where hours go.
- **At day 90 of [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]]**, re-run and compare against week 1 — this is the "row 7" self-measure most likely to be honest.
- **Weekly template: every Friday, for next week** — not Monday morning. A week planned on Monday has already been claimed by whoever messaged first.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Reconstruct, do not rely on the calendar app.** A founder-CEO's calendar is incomplete by construction — half the month was never an invite. Rebuild the month from five sources: the calendar, the call log, WhatsApp, git commit and PR timestamps (these give you build hours almost exactly), and the notes folder.
2. **Half-hour blocks, one letter each. No splitting.** An hour that plausibly serves two jobs is coded to **the one you would have defended if the other had been cancelled**; splitting is how an audit becomes a flattering story. Sam Corcos of Levels tracked every fifteen minutes for two years — 7,922 hours — which is admirable and is not what you should do ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/an-exact-breakdown-of-how-one-ceo-spent-his-first-two-years-of-company-building/)). Half-hour blocks, reconstructed monthly, get 90% of the signal at 5% of the cost.
3. **Code by purpose, not by content.** Ninety minutes at the same pump with the same dealer is **D** if you went to learn what he does at month-end and **K** if you went to close him. If you cannot say which, that is the finding: you took a meeting without a purpose.
4. **Count transit separately, never inside the meeting it enabled.** Two district days plus an overnight is an allocation decision disguised as travel.
5. **Audit the whole week, not office hours.** Porter and Nohria tracked CEOs 24/7 for thirteen weeks; the design is the lesson ([HBR, 2018](https://hbr.org/2018/07/how-ceos-manage-time)). A founder's week does not empty at 6 PM.
6. **Read the totals aloud, next to the kernel.** Open [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]], read the guiding policy and coherent actions aloud, then read the four percentages aloud. Write **one sentence** naming the difference: _"last month the company's strategy was X and the written strategy was Y."_
7. **One change, not five.** The audit's output is a single altered block in next week's grid. Five changes is a mood; one change is a mechanism that next month's audit can test.
8. **A block without a deliverable is not a block**, and **at least one full day a week has no meetings at all.** A single mid-afternoon meeting destroys a day's building by splitting it into two pieces too small to hold anything ([Paul Graham, _Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule_, 2009](https://paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)).
9. **The top goal comes before the working day.** Two hours daily, in the calendar, claimed before anyone else can claim it ([Matt Mochary, _The Great CEO Within_ — notes](https://blas.com/the-great-ceo-within/)). In a family business with an office attached to the family's pump, pre-dawn is the only slot with a lock on it.
10. **Log energy alongside hours, and never optimise either.** Energy "has four wellsprings — the body, emotions, mind, and spirit — and in each, it can be systematically expanded and renewed" ([Schwartz and McCarthy, HBR, Oct 2007](https://hbr.org/2007/10/manage-your-energy-not-your-time)); two founders with identical calendars can have completely different months. And the moment you code hours to make the ratio look better, the number has stopped measuring anything ([Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law)).

## Template — Part A: the calendar audit

**A1. The coding scheme.** Four jobs, five buckets for everything else. The four are Fred Wilson's three — set the vision and strategy and communicate it, recruit and retain the best talent, keep enough cash in the bank ([avc.com, 2010](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/)) — with communication split out, because choosing a direction and repeating it consume different hours and fail differently.

| Code    | Job        | Counts                                                                                                                           | Does **not** count                            |
| ------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **D**   | Direction  | Kernel work, the no-list, market reading, discovery interviews **to learn**, competitive teardown, the decision journal          | Building what you already decided to build    |
| **K**   | Capital    | Runway, burn, pricing, collections, the CA, banks, investors — and, bootstrapped, **closing revenue**                            | Building a feature a prospect asked for       |
| **P**   | People     | Recruiting, 1:1s, feedback, comp, the founder relationship itself                                                                | Doing a teammate's task because it was faster |
| **S**   | Story      | All-hands, [[C23-stakeholder-and-investor-update\|C23]], the pitch, positioning copy, an association address, a dealer broadcast | A one-to-one sales call — that is **K**       |
| **X-B** | Build      | Code, design, debugging, reviews, infra                                                                                          | —                                             |
| **X-R** | Run        | Support, ops, admin, invoices, vendors                                                                                           | —                                             |
| **X-C** | Compliance | CA/CS, filings, board, contracts                                                                                                 | —                                             |
| **X-T** | Transit    | Travel and waiting. Count it                                                                                                     | —                                             |
| **X-N** | Noise      | Reactive, interrupted, honestly wasted                                                                                           | —                                             |

**A2. The tally sheet.** One row per code; put the four job rows on `CEO Dashboard` so twelve months become a trend instead of twelve separate embarrassments.

| Code                        | Hours | % of total | Last month % | Direction of travel |
| --------------------------- | ----- | ---------- | ------------ | ------------------- |
| D · K · P · S               |       |            |              |                     |
| **Four-jobs subtotal**      |       |            |              |                     |
| X-B · X-R · X-C · X-T · X-N |       |            |              |                     |
| **Total logged**            |       | 100%       |              |                     |

**A3. The three findings, written in full sentences.**

```
1. THE GAP        "Last month the company's strategy was ______, and the
                   written strategy (C04, dated ____) was ______."
2. THE ZERO       Which of D / K / P / S was under 5%? ______
                   Two consecutive months under 5% is a governance issue,
                   not a scheduling one -> log it in C29.
3. THE ONE CHANGE  One block, moved or created, effective next Monday: ______
                   Tested by next month's audit. Not five changes. One.
```

## Template — Part B: the weekly operating grid

**B1. The proposed week for VSYST's CEO today** — three people, pre-revenue, still shipping code. Two domain facts fix the shape before any productivity principle: a pump owner is reachable only **roughly 11:00–16:00**, which collides head-on with your best deep-work hours (you must choose; this grid chooses the customer on two days and the build on three), and **pumps trade at the weekend**, so six days with one genuinely protected day off is the honest design.

|                 | Mon                       | Tue                                   | Wed                                 | Thu                         | Fri                               | Sat                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **06:30–08:30** | **Top goal (D)**          | **Top goal (D)**                      | **Top goal (D)**                    | **Top goal (D)**            | **Top goal (D)**                  | —                    |
| **08:30–09:00** | Check-in; scorecard read  | Check-in                              | Check-in                            | Check-in                    | Check-in                          | Check-in             |
| **09:00–11:00** | Build (X-B)               | Build (X-B)                           | **Maker's day — no meetings (X-B)** | Build (X-B)                 | **Money block (K)**               | Release window (X-B) |
| **11:00–16:00** | Build; support exceptions | **Field day 1 (K/D)**                 | Maker's day continues               | **Field day 2 (K/D)**       | **Strategy block (D)**            | Saturday demos (K)   |
| **16:00–17:00** | Unblock window (P/X-R)    | Unblock                               | Unblock                             | Unblock                     | Unblock                           | —                    |
| **17:00–19:00** | Run & admin (X-R)         | **People block — 2 founder 1:1s (P)** | Run & admin (X-R)                   | Field debrief; pipeline (K) | **Weekly review + write-ups (S)** | Week close           |
| **Sunday**      | \--                       | \--                                   | **Off. Whole day. Non-negotiable**  | \--                         | \--                               | \--                  |

**B2. The defended blocks and their deliverables.** Bold on the grid = defended. A defended block may be moved once, in advance, by you; it may not be cancelled, and nobody else may book over it.

| Block                                       | Job   | Must produce, or it did not happen                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Top goal, 2 h daily                         | D     | Words on a page or a decision written down                                                                                                       |
| **Thinking & writing** (Fri strategy block) | D     | A change to [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager\|C04]] — or an explicit _"no change, and here is why"_                                               |
| **Customer conversations** (Tue + Thu)      | K/D   | **Minimum five named dealers a week**; an agreed next step per dealer; loss reasons logged in [[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script\|C10]] |
| Maker's day (Wed)                           | X-B   | Shipped or reviewed code, end to end, uninterrupted                                                                                              |
| Money block (Fri AM)                        | K     | Runway re-based in [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner\|C13]]; collections chased; one decision                                               |
| People block (Tue PM)                       | P     | Two 1:1s with running notes; one piece of the domain director's knowledge written down                                                           |
| Unblock window, 1 h daily                   | P/X-R | Everyone's blockers cleared in one place instead of all day                                                                                      |

**B3. The weekly review — Friday, 25 minutes, six questions, written.**

```
WEEK ENDING ____              Top goal this week: ______________
1  Did the top goal move?  YES / NO.  What exists now that did not?
2  Customer conversations: ___ of 5 minimum.  Names: ______
3  Which defended block got eaten, and by what?
4  What decision am I sitting on?  -> C29 entry, or delegate it (C03)
5  What did I do this week that only I could have done?  What did I do
   that I should not have?    (col 2 = next week's delegation backlog)
6  ONE thing to change next week: ______
```

**B4. The energy log.** Five rows a week, thirty seconds each. Score the day 1–5 on energy at 18:00, name the block that cost the most and the one that gave the most back.

| Day | Sleep (h) | Energy at 18:00 (1–5) | Block that drained | Block that restored | Note |
| --- | --------- | --------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- | ---- |

Read it monthly beside the audit. The pattern that matters is not "Tuesdays are bad" — it is **which category of work is quietly expensive.** Most founders find transit and unstructured reactive time cost far more energy per hour than the work they were dreading.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**The audit — a plausible VSYST month.** 26 working days, one district trip, DZZLO live on both stores, no revenue, 230 logged hours.

| Code           | Hours | %   |     | Code          | Hours  | %       |
| -------------- | ----- | --- | --- | ------------- | ------ | ------- |
| X-B Build      | 96    | 42% |     | K Capital     | 22     | 10%     |
| X-R Run        | 34    | 15% |     | D Direction   | 9      | 4%      |
| X-T Transit    | 26    | 11% |     | P People      | 8      | 3%      |
| X-N Noise      | 18    | 8%  |     | S Story       | 5      | 2%      |
| X-C Compliance | 12    | 5%  |     | **Four jobs** | **44** | **19%** |

Now the diagnostic. The written kernel says: _Indian fuel-distribution dealers, sold district by district, priced per GSTIN, web-only billing, India-only, saturate one district before touching a second._ The finding, in the form that makes it arguable:

> **"Last month the company's strategy was 'finish the product'. The written strategy was 'saturate Raipur district'. Those are different strategies, and only one of them got funded."**

Seven per cent of the month in front of a paying prospect, forty-two in an editor. **The zero:** Story at 2% and People at 3%. **The one change:** Thursday 11:00–16:00 becomes a defended second field day, and nothing else moves this month.

Two more readings. **X-T at 11%**: one overnight and two district days consumed more hours than direction, people and story combined — batch the district into one week a month. **X-N at 8%**: eighteen hours of nothing, the cheapest eighteen hours anyone will ever find you.

**The honest benchmark, so you do not over-punish yourself.** Corcos's two-year self-audit gave strategy **5%** of his hours and information processing — mostly email — **27%**, his single largest category ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/an-exact-breakdown-of-how-one-ceo-spent-his-first-two-years-of-company-building/)); executives average roughly **23 hours a week in meetings, up from under 10 in the 1960s** ([Perlow, Hadley and Eun, HBR, 2017](https://hbr.org/2017/07/stop-the-meeting-madness)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Your 4% on Direction is not an anomaly, it is the default, and defaults are what mechanisms exist to beat. Building at 42% was not wrong; the finding is narrower and harder to escape: **at 42%, build was not a contribution to the strategy, it was the strategy, and nobody decided that.**

**Energy log, same month:** average 3.1; the two field days scored 4 despite being the longest days; the drain was consistently the reactive WhatsApp afternoon and the overnight travel. That argues for _more_ field days and batched travel — the opposite of what the hour count alone suggests, and the reason both instruments are filled together.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                          | What it looks like                                                                        | Fix                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Auditing the calendar app**                    | 40 logged hours in a 60-hour month                                                        | Rule 1. Rebuild from call log, WhatsApp and git timestamps                                         |
| **Splitting hours across codes**                 | "That meeting was half K, half D"                                                         | Rule 2. One letter. Code it to the half you would have defended                                    |
| **Coding by content, not purpose**               | Every dealer visit filed as K                                                             | Rule 3. If you cannot name the purpose, the meeting had none                                       |
| **Hiding transit**                               | A four-hour drive counted as "customer time"                                              | Rule 4. X-T is its own row and it is usually the surprise                                          |
| **Five changes, or blocks without deliverables** | A heroic new schedule that lasts nine days; a "strategy block" that produces a nice think | Rules 7 and 8. One change; name the artefact or delete the block                                   |
| **Planning the week on Monday**                  | It is already claimed by 09:30                                                            | Friday, for next week. Always                                                                      |
| **Auditing once**                                | One embarrassing spreadsheet, never repeated                                              | Twelve monthly rows is a trend; one is an anecdote. Put it on `CEO Dashboard`                      |
| **Optimising the number**                        | Coding generously so D looks respectable                                                  | Rule 12. Goodhart's law applies to your own diary first                                            |
| **Dropping the customer minimum when busy**      | Five dealers → two → zero, silently                                                       | The minimum is a dashboard row, not a good intention ([[17-the-numbers-a-ceo-watches\|lesson 17]]) |
