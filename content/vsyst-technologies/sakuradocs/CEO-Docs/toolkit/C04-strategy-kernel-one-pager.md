# C04 — Strategy Kernel One-Pager

_Toolkit · fills the kernel exercises in [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|05 — Strategy I: Diagnosis and the Strategy Kernel]] · Owner: the CEO holds the pen; both co-directors must disagree with a draft in writing before it is adopted · Cadence: written once in Month 1, reviewed quarterly, rewritten annually — and never quietly amended · Workbook tab: `Strategy Kernel` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

This is the one page the whole company can be checked against. Richard Rumelt's argument is that a strategy has a hard nugget at its centre — **the kernel** — with exactly three parts, and that everything else in a strategy document is elaboration: a **diagnosis** that names what is actually going on, a **guiding policy** that says how you will deal with it, and a set of **coherent actions** that carry the policy out and support one another ([_Good Strategy Bad Strategy_, Crown, 2011](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/91324/good-strategy-bad-strategy-by-richard-rumelt/9780307886231/)). Strategy is "a way through a difficulty, an approach to overcoming an obstacle, a response to a challenge" — and bad strategy is what you get when a leader covers a failure to name the difficulty with "the language of broad goals, ambition, vision, and values" ([Rumelt, _The perils of bad strategy_, McKinsey Quarterly, 2011](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-perils-of-bad-strategy)).

Which is why the format is one page and not twenty. A twenty-page strategy is almost always a diagnosis that was never found, padded out with objectives. If your kernel does not fit on one page, you have not finished thinking; you have started writing.

The second half of the sheet — the **assumption register** — exists because a kernel written by a pre-revenue company is not a set of facts. It is a set of bets, and the responsible thing is to say which ones, how shaky each is, and what would prove it wrong. That discipline is McGrath and MacMillan's discovery-driven planning: the central move is systematically surfacing "the dangerous implicit assumptions that would otherwise slip unnoticed and thus unchallenged into the plan", and then funding the next step against a test rather than against a forecast ([_Discovery-Driven Planning_, HBR, July–August 1995](https://hbr.org/1995/07/discovery-driven-planning); [HBR — A Refresher on Discovery-Driven Planning, 2017](https://hbr.org/2017/02/a-refresher-on-discovery-driven-planning); [Discovery-driven planning — overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery-driven_planning)). Their own examples of dangerous assumptions read like a founder's internal monologue: _customers will buy our product because we think it's a good product; customers run no risk in buying from us instead of their existing supplier; we will have no trouble attracting the right staff._

The register also converts the strategy argument from a contest of confidence into a research agenda. Roger Martin's reframe is the one to keep in your head: the useful question is not "is this true?" — which invites people to defend positions — but **"what would have to be true for this to be a good choice?"**, which invites them to name the shakiest condition and go and test it ([Roger Martin — What Would Have to be True?](https://rogermartin.medium.com/what-would-have-to-be-true-83dac5bd2189)). The cascade in [[C05-choice-cascade-worksheet|C05]] runs that question across five choices; C04 runs it across the kernel's three.

**Why this is the highest-leverage page in the toolkit.** Almost every other template downstream inherits from it. The moat audit ([[C07-moat-and-seven-powers-audit|C07]]) tests whether the guiding policy is defensible. The ICP ([[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]]) is the where-to-play choice made operational. The no-list ([[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]]) is the "not doing" line enforced weekly. The OKRs ([[C28-annual-plan-and-ceo-okr-sheet|C28]]) are only legitimate if each one traces back to a coherent action here. If this page is vague, all of those inherit the vagueness and nobody notices for a year.

## When to use

- **At the end of the one-week diagnosis sprint** in [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]] §3.7 — Friday afternoon, CEO holding the pen, all three directors in the room.
- **Before any OKR, roadmap or budget is written.** A plan is not a strategy, and a planning process left to itself gravitates to the comfortable boxes — budgets and capabilities — precisely because the uncomfortable ones force you to say where you will _not_ play ([Martin, _The Big Lie of Strategic Planning_, HBR, Jan–Feb 2014](https://hbr.org/2014/01/the-big-lie-of-strategic-planning)).
- **Quarterly, as a 45-minute review**, not a rewrite: which assumptions were tested, what did they say, has the crux moved, and is the "not doing" list still being honoured?
- **Whenever a decision is hard.** If a choice is genuinely difficult, it is usually because it sits outside the kernel and nobody has said so.
- **Whenever an assumption is falsified** — that is the actual trigger for a strategy change, and it should feel like an event.
- **Before any investor conversation.** Everything in [[C15-investor-narrative-and-deck-outline|C15]] should be this page, expanded.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **The diagnosis is one or two sentences, and it must be uncomfortable.** A diagnosis that flatters everything the company already does is almost certainly wrong, because if you were already aimed at the real obstacle you would not be stuck. If nobody in the room winces, keep going.
2. **Write the diagnosis before anyone can talk you out of it.** Each director writes theirs separately, in writing, then all three are opened together. Rumelt's crux question is the fastest instrument here: _what is the hardest part of making this business work — not the most urgent, the hardest?_ If the three answers differ, that disagreement is the most valuable finding of your quarter and every resource argument for the next year is really this argument in disguise ([_The Crux_, 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Rumelt)).
3. **Run the six tests before it leaves the room.** All six, honestly. They are in the Template below and they are the only quality gate this document has.
4. **The guiding policy must forbid something, in the same breath.** Write it as two clauses: _the approach_ and _which means we will not…_. A policy with no refusal is a preference. The test is simple — what does it rule out? If nothing, you have written an aspiration.
5. **The "not doing" list is compulsory, and at least one item on it must be something you actually want to do.** A no-list full of things nobody was going to do anyway is decoration. The whole point of strategy is choosing not to do good things, because a three-person company has one unit of attention and every yes spends it ([Porter — _What Is Strategy?_, HBR, 1996](https://hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy)).
6. **Actions must reinforce each other, not merely coexist.** The working test: **actions are coherent when removing one makes the others measurably harder.** Take each action out of the list in turn and say out loud what breaks. If nothing breaks, that action is a task, not a strategic action, and it belongs in the COO's quarterly plan ([[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review|T19]]), not here.
7. **Five actions maximum, and every one names an owner.** At three people, a sixth action means one of the five is not really happening.
8. **The objective must be proximate, not blue-sky.** "Become the category leader" is not something anyone can act on this week. "Twelve districts, a measured time-to-first-invoice, three filmable reference dealers, four gatekeepers" is close enough to be achievable and specific enough that failure is visible.
9. **Every line carries a confidence number and a review date.** Confidence is a percentage you would bet at. Writing 0.9 next to something you have never tested is how a company lies to itself in a spreadsheet.
10. **The assumption register must have at least two live tests with an owner and a date this quarter**, and they must be the _shakiest_ assumptions, not the easiest ones. The point of the register is to spend your scarce evidence-gathering on the beliefs that would cost you a year if wrong. Run a pre-mortem on the kernel once a year — assume it has failed, then generate the reasons; imagining an event has already occurred measurably improves the ability to name causes ([Klein — _Performing a Project Premortem_, HBR, Sept 2007](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)) — and feed what surfaces into the register and into [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession|C30]].
11. **A change to the diagnosis is a one-way door.** It is not an edit. It invalidates the guiding policy, the actions, the ICP, the roadmap and the plan built on top of it, and it costs the company a quarter of accumulated direction. So it gets the Type 1 treatment: a written entry in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] made _before_ the outcome is known, and a board decision under [[C03-decision-rights-matrix|C03]]. Reversible decisions should be made fast at about 70% of the information you would like; irreversible ones should not ([CNBC on Bezos's 2015 shareholder letter](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/11/19/jeff-bezos-simple-strategy-for-answering-amazons-hardest-questions--.html)). Changing the guiding policy or an action is an ordinary quarterly decision. Changing the diagnosis is not.
12. **Keep every superseded version.** Next year's most useful artefact is the diff, and the date a belief changed is often more informative than the belief.

## Template

**(a) The one page.** Print it. Put it where the three of you actually sit.

```
STRATEGY KERNEL — <company>            set <date>   ·   review <date + 90d>
Version <n>   ·   supersedes version <n-1> dated <date>   ·   held by: CEO

DIAGNOSIS  (one or two sentences. What is actually going on? What is the
            NATURE of the obstacle? Must pass all six tests below.)
  ...
  Confidence: __%      Evidence it rests on: ...
  What would prove this wrong: ...

GUIDING POLICY  (the overall approach, and what it forbids — both clauses)
  We will ...
  Which means we will NOT ...
  Confidence: __%

COHERENT ACTIONS  (max 5. Each with an owner. Each must fail the removal test:
                   take it out, and say what breaks.)
  1. <action>                      owner: ___   if removed, this breaks: ___
  2. ...
  3. ...
  4. ...
  5. ...

WE ARE DELIBERATELY NOT DOING  (compulsory. At least one item must be
                                something we genuinely want to do.)
  - ...   (because: ...)
  - ...   (because: ...)

PROXIMATE OBJECTIVE  (close enough to be achievable, specific enough that
                      failure is visible. Not a blue-sky goal.)
  By <date>: ...

THE CRUX THIS QUARTER  (the one part of the challenge that is both critical
                        and addressable. If it hasn't moved in four quarters,
                        you have not been attacking it.)
  ...

CHANGE RULE
  Diagnosis change  = one-way door. C29 entry written before the outcome
                      is known + board decision (C03). Never a quiet edit.
  Policy / action change = ordinary quarterly decision, minuted.
```

**(b) The six tests of a good diagnosis.** Run all six before the page leaves the room. A diagnosis that fails any one of them is not ready.

```
1. MECHANISM, NOT MOOD    Does it name how the world works, or how people feel?
                          "Dealers aren't tech-savvy" = mood.
                          "He cannot verify our claims without giving us his
                           ledger, and giving us his ledger IS the switching
                           cost" = mechanism.
2. FALSIFIABLE            Can you say what evidence would prove it wrong?
                          If not, it is a belief, not a diagnosis.
3. CONTESTABLE            Could a competent competitor read it, disagree, and
                          then rationally behave completely differently?
4. SIMPLE                 One sentence, maybe two. A page-long diagnosis is an
                          admission that you have not found it yet.
5. IMPLIES ACTION         Does something follow from it? If nothing follows,
                          it is an observation.
6. UNCOMFORTABLE          Does it flatter what we already do? Then it is
                          probably wrong. Somebody in the room should wince.
```

**(c) The assumption register.** One row per belief the kernel is standing on. Sorted by shakiness, not by comfort.

| #   | Assumption (stated so it could be false) | Which kernel line depends on it | Confidence | What would falsify it | The cheapest test | Owner | Test by | Status | If falsified, this changes |
| --- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------- | ----- | ------- | ------ | -------------------------- |

| 2   |                                          |                                 |            |                       |                   |       |         |        |                            |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

Every number below is illustrative and belongs to VSYST's own first quarter. The kernel text is the draft from [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel|lesson 05]] §2.3, written to be argued with rather than admired.

```
STRATEGY KERNEL — VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd.   set 25 Aug 2026 · review 25 Nov 2026
Version 1 · held by: CEO

DIAGNOSIS   Indian fuel dealers do not lack software; they lack a reason to change
  a paper-and-Tally process that has never visibly failed them — and the person who
  must decide is a 45–65-year-old owner with low software trust, a switching cost
  measured in ledger migration and staff retraining, and thin operating cash. So our
  binding constraint is not features and not price. It is adoption friction and trust,
  and both are earned at district level, one dealer at a time.
  Confidence: 60%
  What would prove this wrong: dealers who are shown the product convert at similar
  rates whether or not they were introduced by someone they trust; or price emerges
  as the top named reason for no across 20 recorded losses.

GUIDING POLICY
  We will win district clusters completely rather than India thinly — starting where
  a founder can drive to the dealer and back in a day — by making the first fourteen
  days of a dealer's life the product we optimise above everything else, and by
  borrowing trust from the three channels the owner already believes: the dealer next
  door, the association office-bearer, and his CA.
  Which means we will NOT sell outside the cluster even when an inbound lead arrives;
  will NOT build for prospects who have not completed onboarding; will NOT compete on
  price against ₹15,000 one-time desktop licences; and will NOT spend on brand
  marketing before referral density exists.
  Confidence: 60%

COHERENT ACTIONS
  1. Choose the cluster and publish the refusal (Raipur + Durg divisions, 12 districts)
     owner: CEO  ·  if removed: founder hours scatter across 300 km and actions 2–4
     lose the density that makes them affordable
  2. Make time-to-first-invoice the company's single obsessed-over number; founder-run
     onboarding including ledger migration
     owner: CEO  ·  if removed: action 3 produces reference dealers who are not really
     live, and action 4's credibility dies at the first hard question
  3. Manufacture three reference dealers deliberately — chosen for peer credibility,
     not revenue — and film each at his own pump in the local language
     owner: domain director  ·  if removed: action 4 has nothing to point at and
     becomes a cold pitch to a gatekeeper
  4. Buy trust wholesale: one association office-bearer, one OMC territory manager,
     two CA firms serving pumps — each given something valuable before anything is asked
     owner: domain director  ·  if removed: action 1's remaining prospects stay cold
  5. Ship only onboarding-blocking work; everything else goes to the no-list with the
     dealer's name against it
     owner: CEO  ·  if removed: action 2 starves and the founder-week refills with code

WE ARE DELIBERATELY NOT DOING
  - No lubricant-distributor or bulk-diesel feature work until the pump-dealer
    beachhead has paying density  (because: same schema, different vertical, and a
    live lube lead WILL arrive and WILL be easier to close — this is the item we
    actually want to do, which is why it is on the list)
  - No pan-India launch, no thirteenth district for two quarters
  - No free tier, no in-app purchase, no bespoke build for one dealer
  - No OMC-scale pursuit that costs more than one founder-quarter

PROXIMATE OBJECTIVE
  By 31 Mar 2027: 12 districts declared and refused outside; time-to-first-invoice
  measured and under a written target; 3 filmable reference dealers live; 4 gatekeeper
  relationships with something given first.

THE CRUX THIS QUARTER
  Can a founder take a dealer from signature to first CORRECT invoice, repeatedly,
  inside a small number of days? Everything above rests on this and nothing has
  measured it.
```

**The assumption register, sorted by shakiness.** Note that rows 1 and 2 are not about the customer at all — they are about whether VSYST can do what it says it will do. That is normal for a company this size and it is why the two live tests are aimed there.

**What the register is telling you to do this quarter.** Not to argue about the diagnosis. To go and measure onboarding three times with a stopwatch, and to ask fifteen dealers one question. Two tests, both cheap, both aimed at the beliefs that would cost a year. That is the entire output of the strategy process for a three-person company, and it is worth more than any deck.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                       | What it looks like                                                                                   | Fix                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A diagnosis that is a goal**                | "Our challenge is to grow revenue 3× and become the leader in fuel-distribution software"            | Rumelt's fluff and failure-to-face-the-challenge in one line. Name the obstacle, not the wish                                 |
| **A comfortable diagnosis**                   | It implies the company should keep doing exactly what it is doing                                    | Rule 1. Nobody winced, so you have not found it                                                                               |
| **A page-long diagnosis**                     | Three paragraphs of context before the sentence                                                      | Test 4. Length is the symptom; not knowing is the disease                                                                     |
| **A guiding policy that forbids nothing**     | "We will focus on customer delight and operational excellence"                                       | Rule 4. Write the "which means we will not" clause or delete the policy                                                       |
| **A no-list of things nobody wanted to do**   | "We will not build a social network"                                                                 | Rule 5. If the list costs you nothing, it is decoration                                                                       |
| **Actions that merely coexist**               | Five sensible initiatives sharing three pairs of hands and no theory                                 | Rule 6. Remove each in turn. If nothing breaks, it is a task, not a strategic action                                          |
| **Assumptions with no test, owner or date**   | A register full of 80% confidences and empty test columns                                            | Rule 10. Two live tests, aimed at the shakiest rows, or the register is a decoration too                                      |
| **Confidence numbers written after the fact** | Everything is 0.8                                                                                    | Bet-at-these-odds is the standard. If you would not, write the number you would                                               |
| **Quietly editing the diagnosis**             | The sentence changed in March and nobody logged it; the ICP and the roadmap silently no longer match | Rule 11. One-way door: C29 entry plus a board decision under [[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]]                              |
| **OKRs that do not trace back here**          | A quarterly plan with objectives no line of the kernel implies                                       | [[C28-annual-plan-and-ceo-okr-sheet\|C28]] rule: every OKR names the coherent action it serves, or it is not funded           |
| **A kernel written alone**                    | The CEO wrote it, circulated it, and got three thumbs-up emojis                                      | Both co-directors must disagree with a draft **in writing** before it is adopted. Agreement that costs nothing proves nothing |
