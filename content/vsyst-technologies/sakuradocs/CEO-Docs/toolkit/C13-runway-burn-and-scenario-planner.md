# C13 — Runway, Burn and Scenario Planner

_Toolkit · fills the runway, scenario and trigger-ladder exercises in [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|08 — Capital, Runway and Fundraising]] · Owner: CEO — Fred Wilson's third job is "make sure there is always enough cash in the bank" and it is the one job with no delegate · Cadence: re-read on the same day every month, and on any cash surprise the same day it happens · Workbook tab: `Runway & Scenarios` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two halves, and the second is the one that matters.

The first half is a **model**: three cases — bear, base, bull — driven off eight numbers, projected twenty-four months, producing one output the whole company can hold in its head: _months of runway_. The second half is a **trigger ladder**: exactly what the CEO is obliged to do at 12, 9, 6 and 3 months of runway and at cash-flow positive, who must be told, and by when. The model tells you where you are. The ladder removes your discretion about what to do when you get there.

That removal is the entire point. The three jobs of a CEO are vision, talent and cash ([AVC — What A CEO Does](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/), 2010), and the cash one fails in a specific, well-documented way: the founder who can see the cliff talks himself out of acting until the cliff is close enough that every remaining option is bad. Paul Graham's reduction is the honest frame — a company is **default alive** if its current growth and current expenses get it to profitability on the money it already has, and **default dead** if they do not, and most founders who believe the first are actually in the second ([Paul Graham — Default Alive or Default Dead?](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html), 2015). Nothing in this sheet is harder than that one test, and nothing is more routinely dodged.

**The rule that governs this template: the ladder is decided in the calm and executed without discretion.** You write the 6-month actions while standing on the 12-month rung, when they cost nothing to write and you are not frightened. When you reach the 6-month rung you execute what you wrote — you do not re-evaluate it, because the person doing the re-evaluating is the person the ladder exists to protect the company from. The corollary, which is a real clause: **the ladder may be amended only while you are standing on green.**

Ownership boundary, so the two courses do not fight. **The COO's [[T20-budget-vs-actual-and-cash-forecast|T20]] owns the weekly rolling 13-week cash view** — the operational instrument, receipts and payments, week by week. **C13 owns the scenario, the 24-month shape, and the pre-commitment.** T20 tells you whether you can pay next Thursday's vendors. C13 tells you what you are obliged to do about the fact that Thursdays keep coming.

## When to use

- **First fill: in the first 60 days of the CEO seat**, with the CA in the room, from the bank statement rather than from memory ([[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]], days 31–60). The exit criterion there is blunt: three directors quote the same runway number.
- **Monthly, same calendar day**, immediately after the bank statement lands. Ten minutes. Update cash, update trailing burn, read the rung.
- **Same day as any cash surprise** — a lost deal that was in the model, a vendor invoice nobody forecast, a gateway settlement that did not arrive, a statutory demand.
- **Before any hire, any tool with a monthly bill, any office, any commitment that recurs.** Recurring cost is the only thing in this model that reliably compounds against you.
- **Before deciding to raise anything** — [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]] and [[C15-investor-narrative-and-deck-outline|C15]] both assume this sheet exists and is honest, because "how long does this money last?" is the second question every investor asks.
- **Reads from** [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] (revenue and variable cost per firm) and [[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]] (list price, prepay discount).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One cash number, from the bank, and it is smaller than you think.** Bank balances plus fixed deposits you would actually break, today. Then **subtract the money that is not yours**: GST collected and not yet remitted, TDS deducted and not yet deposited, employee PF/ESI withheld, unsettled gateway float. Holding statutory money and counting it as runway is the single most common way an Indian founder overstates his position — and it is the money whose non-payment can reach past the company to the directors personally (see rule 9). Receivables are not cash. Promises are not cash.
2. **Runway = closing cash ÷ trailing three-month average net burn.** Trailing, not forecast. The forecast is the thing you are trying to keep honest; using it as the denominator makes the sheet circular and flattering.
3. **Track gross burn and net burn separately.** Net burn drives runway; **gross burn is what you can actually cut.** A company with ₹1.9L gross burn and ₹1.6L net burn does not have ₹1.6L of cutting available — it has ₹1.9L, minus the parts it must keep.
4. **Eight drivers, three columns, no more.** New paying firms per month, annual revenue per firm, monthly logo churn, fixed monthly cost, variable cost per firm, opening cash, month of first hire, loaded cost of that hire. If a case needs a ninth driver you have stopped deciding and started modelling.
5. **The bear case has _higher_ fixed costs than the base case, not lower.** This is counter-intuitive and it is the rule that makes bear cases useful. Things going badly costs money: emergency travel, a legal opinion, a rebuild, a customer who must be won back. A bear case built by shaving revenue and holding costs flat is a base case wearing a hat.
6. **Model a raise as an event with a lead time, not as a line item.** A seed process runs roughly three to six months from first meeting to money in the bank, longer at Series A ([Startup Fundraising — the interval between rounds](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/strategizing-fundraising-the-ideal-time-interval-between-funding-rounds)). The commonly cited discipline is to start _preparing_ under 12 months of runway and be _actively raising_ by 6–9, because founders who begin with runway behind them negotiate from a position and founders who begin without one negotiate from a deadline ([Finance Resolver — runway before a fundraise, data from 500+ startups](https://www.financeresolver.com/post/how-long-should-your-startup-runway-be-before-starting-a-fundraise-data-from-500-startups)) — **VERIFY LIVE**, these are practitioner figures, not audited data.
7. **Annual prepay is the cheapest capital you will ever be offered.** At a 16.7% discount ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]] rule 7) twelve months of revenue lands in month one at a cost of two months of it. There is no term sheet, no dilution, no board seat and no lawyer, and ~16.7% is the settled market framing rather than a concession you negotiated ([Monetizely — annual vs monthly pricing psychology](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/why-annual-vs-monthly-pricing-psychology-matters-for-saas-revenue-leaders)). Model prepay explicitly as a separate cash line — it changes the early months of the curve far more than any driver in rule 4, because it is the only lever that attacks the **cash-flow trough** every subscription business digs before its cohorts pay back ([For Entrepreneurs — SaaS Metrics 2.0](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/), 2013).
8. **Report the burn multiple once you have any revenue at all, and do not grade yourself on it yet.** Burn multiple = net burn ÷ net new ARR; Sacks's scale is 0.5× amazing, 1.0× great, 1.5× good, 2.0× suspect, 3.0×+ bad ([David Sacks — The Burn Multiple](https://sacks.substack.com/p/the-burn-multiple-51a7e43cb200); [Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/burn-multiple/); benchmark tables in [Equals](https://equals.com/guides/saas-metrics/burn-multiple/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**, current expectations have tightened well below the original scale. At three customers the ratio is arithmetic noise. Track its _direction_ across quarters; that is real. Its level is not.
9. **Know before you need to know it: unpaid statutory dues can become personal.** Under **§179 of the Income-tax Act, 1961**, where tax due from a _private_ company cannot be recovered, every person who was a director in the relevant year is jointly and severally liable unless he proves the non-recovery was not attributable to gross neglect, misfeasance or breach of duty; **§89 of the CGST Act, 2017** does the same for GST ([TaxGuru — private company director's liability](https://taxguru.in/company-law/private-company-directors-liability-companies-act-income-tax-gst.html); [TaxGuru — §179 and recent case law](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/liability-directors-private-company-section-179-income-tax-act.html)). Courts have required the department to first record satisfaction that recovery from the company is impossible, but that is a procedural protection, not a shield. **VERIFY LIVE** with the CA and CS. The operational consequence is rule 10.
10. **The cut sequence is written now and never reordered under pressure.** See the table below. Statutory deductions withheld from employees are never in the sequence at all.
11. **Ring-fence an exit reserve, and read every rung against runway _net_ of it.** Stopping a company costs real money, and the bill falls due at the moment the company has the least cash it has ever had — so the dignified ending is the option founders discover they can no longer buy. Split the floor into two named numbers: the **operating reserve** (a bad month is not an emergency) and the **exit reserve** (what it would cost to end the company properly, today). The exit reserve is professional fees for the closing accounts and the route — strike-off under [s.248 / Form STK-2](https://ca2013.com/248-power-of-registrar-to-remove-name-of-company-from-register-of-companies/), filed with MCA's C-PACE ([Compliance Calendar — C-PACE](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/c-pace-fast-track-company-strike-off)), or voluntary liquidation under [IBC s.59](https://ca2013.com/section-59-voluntary-liquidation-corporate-persons/), which costs an order of magnitude more because it needs a registered insolvency professional — **plus** the unexpired portion of every annual prepayment refunded, vendor and app-store settlements, notice and full-and-final settlement for anyone employed, the engineering to build and run per-tenant data exports and keep a read-only window open before verified deletion under [DPDP Act 2023 s.8](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/186118625/), and a **25% buffer** on the lot. Full breakdown and the line-by-line drivers: [[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things|lesson 18]] §10.6. **Every rupee of it is VERIFY LIVE with the CA and the CS** — fees, forms and timelines move, and the number is re-priced at the annual plan, after every hire, and on signing any annual contract or prepayment. Ring-fence it in language, not in a second bank account: three founders agreeing in writing that it is not spendable, and one dashboard line that shows it.
12. **Every rung names who must be told and by when.** A trigger with no audience is a note to yourself, and notes to yourself are what the ladder exists to replace.
13. **You move down a rung on the trailing number, immediately. You move back up only after two consecutive months above it.** Down fast, up slow. One good month is weather.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All figures illustrative and matched to the workbook's default drivers — VERIFY LIVE against the bank and the CA.** Opening cash **₹25,00,000**. Base case: 1.5 new paying firms/month, **₹28,065 annual revenue per firm** (₹1,799 per GSTIN × 1.3 GSTINs × 12 — [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] §A, which owns this number), 2% monthly logo churn, ₹1,65,000 fixed monthly cost, **₹1,125 variable per firm** ([[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] §B total), first hire in month 12 at ₹45,000 loaded. The bear and bull columns carry **₹21,588** and **₹32,382** of annual revenue per firm — the same ₹1,799 list price against 1.0 and 1.5 GSTINs per firm, because the scenario is about how many billing units a dealer brings, never about discounting the price.

Gross margin per firm per month is ₹2,339 − ₹1,125 = **₹1,214**. Churn of 2% against 1.5 new firms a month gives a **steady state of 75 paying firms** — and covering ₹1,65,000 of fixed cost needs **136**. So before the hire is even made, the base case is **default dead by sixty-one dealers**, and with the month-12 hire it needs **173** — more than twice the steady state its own sales driver produces. That single sentence is worth more than the rest of the projection, and it is not a sentence a better sales month fixes. Even C12's pass-two margin of ₹1,534 — rate confirmations moved to push, support minutes halved — only pulls the requirement to **108**, still thirty-three dealers above steady state. The gap closes on fixed cost, on margin, or on a sales rate well above 1.5 firms a month. It does not close on optimism.

The cash curve, base case: runway reads **15.3 months at month 0**, crosses **12 at month 4**, **9 at month 8** (cash ₹12.4L, trailing burn ₹1.53L), **6 at month 11** (cash ₹8.0L), and the month-12 hire drops it to **3.8 in a single step**. Critical at month 13. **Cash out in month 16.**

Now the part that makes the sheet worth building. The bear case (0.5 firms/month, ₹21,588 per firm, 4% churn, ₹1,90,000 fixed, no hire) runs out in **month 14**. The bull case (3 firms/month, ₹32,382 per firm, 1% churn, hire at month 8) runs out in **month 17**, and at month 24 it is still burning ₹1.09L/month with sixty-four paying firms on the books. Against a base case that dies in month 16, that is **three months of spread between the pessimistic and the optimistic sales story**. **Which means the sales drivers — the numbers the three directors would happily argue about for an evening — are not what decides this company's survival.** Fixed cost and the hire date are. The lower the revenue per firm, the truer this gets: at ₹1,799 a GSTIN, no sales number the company can actually hit moves the cash-out date more than a quarter, while ₹1,65,000 of fixed cost moves it every month. That is a finding you cannot get from a forecast; you only get it from three columns side by side.

Two levers move the curve, and it is worth knowing exactly how far. **Annual prepay**: at **₹23,378** collected on signing (₹28,065 less C11's 16.7% discount) instead of ₹2,339/month, 1.5 new firms a month brings in about **₹32,000/month** of extra early cash — roughly ₹2.2 lakh banked by month 15, and cash-out moves from month 16 to **month 17**. **Deferring the hire**: ₹45,000 × 13 months = ₹5.85 lakh in total, but only four of those months fall before the base case runs dry, so it also buys **one month**, to month 17. Do both and cash-out reaches **month 19**. Three months, bought with the two levers the company wholly controls, and **not enough**: at ₹1,799 the base case does not clear 24 months on prepay and hire-timing alone. Clearing it needs a driver this sheet does not own — fixed cost nearer ₹1,30,000 than ₹1,65,000 with both levers running, or the margin work in [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] pass two, or outside capital. Which is exactly why rule 6's lead time and the amber rung's non-dilutive list are not optional reading.

The ladder in practice. The amber rung fires in **month 8** — and month 8 is precisely when the company will _feel_ good: eleven paying dealers, revenue rising every month, the product working. That is the moment the pre-commitment earns its existence. In month 8 the CEO's obligation is not to feel good; it is to write the month-11 plan, freeze recurring cost above ₹10,000, and tell both co-directors in writing that week. Written in month 3 — the last month the sheet still reads green — those actions cost nothing. Improvised in month 12, when the hire has just taken runway to 3.8, they cost the company.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Why it happens                                                             | The fix                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Counting GST and TDS held as runway             | It is sitting in the same bank account                                     | Rule 1. Subtract it. It is someone else's money and its non-payment can reach you personally                                                                        |
| Runway computed on forecast burn                | The forecast is lower and it is right there                                | Trailing three-month average. Always                                                                                                                                |
| A bear case with the base case's costs          | Nobody enjoys writing the bad one                                          | Rule 5 — bear has _higher_ fixed cost                                                                                                                               |
| Re-evaluating at the red rung                   | You are closer now and have more information                               | You also have less courage and fewer options. Execute what green-you wrote                                                                                          |
| The ladder amended while amber                  | It suddenly looks too aggressive                                           | It may be amended only on green. Put that sentence in the file                                                                                                      |
| Nine drivers and a tab of assumptions           | Modelling feels like progress                                              | Eight drivers. If the answer needs more, it is not a decision, it is a hobby                                                                                        |
| Runway told to nobody                           | It is embarrassing while it is short                                       | Every rung names an audience and a deadline. That is what makes it a mechanism                                                                                      |
| Hiring at 9 months of runway                    | The hire will "pay for themselves"                                         | They will not, in time. A hire is 6–12 months of cash before any return, and it is the most expensive cell on the sheet                                             |
| "We'll raise if we need to"                     | It has always worked for someone else                                      | A raise is a 3–6 month project. Start preparing at 12, be raising by 9. See [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index\|C14]]                                    |
| Spending the exit reserve to buy one more month | It is sitting in the same account and one more month always feels decisive | It is the price of the endings that are not the worst one. Every rung reads runway **net** of it, and rule 11 says it may not be re-sized while you are below green |
| Cutting the CA/CS retainer at 3 months          | It looks discretionary                                                     | It is the cheapest line on the sheet and the one that keeps a cash problem from becoming a personal one                                                             |
| Rebuilding the model instead of updating it     | A fresh model has no bad news in it                                        | Ten minutes a month, same calendar day, same file, new row                                                                                                          |
