# C20 — Compensation Bands and ESOP Design Sheet

_Toolkit · fills exercise 13.3 in [[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|10 — Building the Team: Hiring, Equity and Firing]] §7–§9 · Owner: the CEO, with the CS on every resolution and the CA on every tax line · Cadence: bands re-benchmarked once a year on a fixed date; the pool authorised once; the sheet reopened before every offer and every grant · Workbook tabs: `Comp Bands` and `ESOP Pool` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two instruments on one sheet, both of which have to exist **before** a specific person is in front of you. Part A answers _what does this seat pay?_ Part B answers _what does this seat get granted, under what law, and what will it actually be worth?_ Their entire value is that they were written while you were indifferent. A band produced after you met a candidate you like is not a band; it is that candidate's salary with a table drawn around it.

Part A exists because there is no usable benchmark for "backend engineer, Raipur, vertical SaaS, four years". Published Indian salary data is metro-weighted and the tier-2 discount is reported anywhere between 15–25% and 35–50% depending on who you read ([Hyring](https://hyring.com/blog/software-engineer-salary-india/); [PeoplePilot](https://www.peoplepilot.io/blog/india-salary-benchmarking-tech-2026)) — a spread that wide means the data is thin, not that the truth is in the middle. The band comes instead from **three real conversations, not three websites** ([[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|lesson 10]] §7).

Part B exists because ESOPs in an Indian private company are a statutory instrument, not a gesture: issued under **s.62(1)(b)** of the Companies Act 2013 and governed by **Rule 12 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014** — two pages you should read yourself once ([Rule 12 — CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/rule-12-companies-share-capital-and-debentures-rules-2014/)). Assume at least one number below has moved since it was written: the Income-tax Act, 1961 was itself replaced by the Income-tax Act, 2025 with effect from 1 April 2026, so every section number cited anywhere — including here — needs re-checking ([CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/articles/esop-for-foreign-owned-indian-subsidiaries-tax-deferral-extended-to-60-months-in-2026-55829.asp)).

And the sentence the rest of Part B is built around, which most founders discover three years late: **an unexercised option in an unlisted Indian private company is worth exactly zero until a liquidity event occurs or the company buys the share back** ([[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|lesson 20]] §9.3). This template makes you write the liquidity sentence _before_ the grant, because the liquidity promise is the part founders most reliably oversell.

## When to use

- **Before VSYST's first offer to a non-founder** — the band for that seat is written and signed by all three directors before the first conversation, alongside the scorecard in [[C19-executive-hiring-scorecard-and-loop|C19]].
- **Before the first ESOP grant** — in fact before the board meeting that approves the scheme, because the pool size, the grant guideline and the post-termination window all belong in the scheme document the CS drafts.
- **At every offer thereafter**, including — especially — an offer to a director's relative, and **on the one fixed annual review date**, company-wide. Not when somebody asks.
- **When a founder's own salary is set or changed**, which is a board act with a resolution, not a payroll act ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]).
- **Never** opened for the first time mid-negotiation. A band invented under time pressure is a number you will be defending to everyone else you hire for the next three years.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Band, not number.** Three points — entry, mid, top-of-band — plus the written condition that moves someone from entry to mid (usually: independent delivery of their charter's outcomes for two consecutive quarters). Mid is the anchor; min and max are tolerance.
2. **Pay at the band, never above it to close a candidate.** The first exception you make _becomes_ the band. If the market has genuinely moved, move the whole band and tell the people already in it.
3. **Run the offer check in the sheet before the offer goes out.** The `Comp Bands` tab flags anything outside roughly 0.75×–1.3× of band mid. An out-of-band offer is _allowed_; an **undocumented** one is not. Two fields are mandatory: the reason, and the answer to _"would we pay this to someone already doing the same job?"_ If that answer is no, you do not have an exception, you have a problem.
4. **Fix the structure once, with the CA.** Basic at ≥50% of total remuneration under the Labour Codes' wages definition; budget **CTC ≈ gross + 13–16%** for PF, gratuity accrual, insurance and LWF once thresholds fire, so the headcount plan is not systematically 15% wrong ([[09-people-operations|COO 09]] §5 owns the machinery).
5. **Publish the bands, not the numbers.** The default — nobody knows, everybody guesses — is the worst of the three options. House call: **open bands, private individual pay.** It answers _"am I being cheated?"_ without answering _"what does he earn?"_, and it forces you to write bands worth defending.
6. **Pay the founders something real and book it.** A ₹0 founder salary hides the company's true cost structure and puts the decision-maker under personal financial stress. Indian founder-salary datasets cluster around a low-₹20-lakh median, but those samples are metro and funded and are the wrong comparator for Raipur ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/startup-ceo-salary-report/); [6figr](https://6figr.com/in/salary/founder--t); [startupcfo.ai](https://www.startupcfo.ai/insights/startup-founder-salary-report-2026)) — **VERIFY LIVE**, all secondary. Directors' remuneration needs a **board resolution**, the interested director's disclosure and minutes; s.197's 11%-of-net-profit cap applies to **public** companies, not private ones ([s.197](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/168868116/); [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/director-remuneration-private-limited-rules)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**.
7. **The family-fairness rule, in these words:** _a related employee is paid from the same band as anyone else in that seat, is evaluated against criteria written down **before** the review, and gets **more** scrutiny than anyone else, not less._ If a relative is genuinely worth more, they are in a different seat — write the different charter. If they will take less, that is a **shareholder** decision (a loan, a deferral, a distribution), minuted as such, not a salary decision. The moment your first outside hire works out that a founder's cousin earns 40% more for the same work, you have spent your culture ([[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|lesson 11]] §8).
8. **A relative-employee hire generates three pieces of paper an outside hire does not:** the **s.188(1)(f)** board resolution appointing a related party to an office or place of profit, with the interested director not voting; that director's disclosure of interest; and the line in the Directors' Report. Prior _shareholder_ approval joins above the **₹2.5 lakh** monthly threshold in **Rule 15(3)** ([s.188 — CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/); [Rule 15 — CAIRR](https://ca2013.com/rule-15-companies-meetings-of-board-and-its-powers-rules2014/); [Vakilsearch](https://vakilsearch.com/article/related-party-transactions-section-188/)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**. Twenty minutes of the CS's time; skipping it is how a routine hire becomes an audit qualification.
9. **Grant options, not shares.** Direct shares land a perquisite bill on the employee in the year of issue, create statutory shareholder rights immediately, and are painful to recover if the person leaves in month seven. Options are taxable at neither grant nor vesting and simply lapse ([[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|lesson 10]] §8.1). **Sweat equity under s.54 + Rule 8 is a different instrument** — for a specific past non-cash contribution, not an incentive plan ([CS Prashant Kumar](https://csatwork.in/difference-between-esop-sweat-equity-and-phantom-stock-india/)).
10. **Express every grant three ways — options, % of fully diluted equity, and rupees at a stated valuation** — and never price the company in a sentence an employee can quote back at you after resigning ([Qapita](https://www.qapita.com/in/blog/how-esops-are-powering-indias-startup-revolution); [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|lesson 20]] §9.4).

## Template — Part B: the ESOP design sheet

**Block A — the pool.** House call: **authorise 7%** and grant against actual hires only. Indian benchmarks run roughly 5–8% at pre-seed/bootstrapped, 10–12% at seed, 12–15% at Series A ([ealkay](https://esop.ealkay.com/Ideal-Esop-Pool-Size-for-Startups-in-India-2026-guide); [Naraway](https://naraway.com/Blogs/esop-guide-indian-startups-2026-complete.html); [PedalStart](https://www.pedalstart.com/blog/esop-for-startups)) — all secondary, **VERIFY LIVE**. A bootstrapped company has no forced top-up event, so the pool you create is the pool you have, and an oversized one pre-concedes the dilution argument in [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|lesson 08]] §12. Set `ESOP Pool!B6` to **7%**, not the sheet's 10% placeholder, and keep ≥40% unallocated for the next 18 months of hiring.

**Block B — the statutory sequence.** The CS drives this; you sign it.

| 4   | **MGT-14 within 30 days** where a special resolution is passed                                                         | Filing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | **VERIFY LIVE with the CS** on the private-company exemption ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=4c46cd9c-0afa-412e-8842-2417ed417172)) |

Three eligibility traps, all **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**: contractors and advisors are **not** eligible employees under Rule 12; **promoters, the promoter group and any director holding >10%** are excluded — except that the exclusion is switched **off for a DPIIT-recognised startup for ten years from incorporation** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/corporate-and-company-law/843806/amendment-to-the-share-capital-and-debenture-rules-new-regime-and-relaxations); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/esop-effective-tool-startup.html)); and any grant of **≥1% of issued capital** to one identified employee in a year needs its own resolution. Get the CS to write down, dated, VSYST's incorporation date, the ten-year expiry, and the promoter-group position for each director's relatives.

**Block E — the two valuations, and the two tax events.** The rows people conflate.

|                            | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                              | When                                                                                                                                          | Signed by                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **FMV for the perquisite** | **Rule 3(9)(ii)** of the Income-tax Rules (some sources cite Rule 3(8)); unlisted shares valued by a **SEBI-registered Category I merchant banker**, on the exercise date or not more than **180 days** before it | At **exercise**                                                                                                                               | Merchant banker ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/taxation/esop-taxation-in-india/); [EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/perquisite-tax-esops-india)) |
| **FMV under Rule 11UA**    | **Rule 11UA** — NAV or DCF                                                                                                                                                                                        | Share **issues and transfers** below FMV (s.56(2)(x)), FEMA pricing, and historically s.56(2)(viib) angel tax — **abolished from AY 2025-26** | Merchant banker or registered valuer ([Marcken](https://marckenconsulting.com/valuation-report-statutory-checklist-india/))                                      |

| Event           | Taxed on                                                          | Head                                                                                                              | Who deducts                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grant / vesting | Nothing                                                           | —                                                                                                                 | —                                                                                                                                        |
| **Exercise**    | **(FMV − strike) × shares** as a perquisite under **s.17(2)(vi)** | Salary, at slab                                                                                                   | **Employer TDS** — s.192 (ITA 1961) / s.392 (Income-tax Act 2025, from 1 Apr 2026), in the month of exercise                             |
| **Sale**        | **(Sale price − FMV at exercise) × shares**                       | Capital gains; unlisted shares are long-term after **24 months**, **LTCG 12.5% without indexation**, STCG at slab | Employee ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/taxation/esop-taxation-in-india/); [Shahi & Co](https://shahiandco.in/blog-esop-taxation.html)) |

**The deferral, and the condition almost everyone misses.** **s.192(1C)** lets an _eligible start-up_ defer the perquisite TDS to the earliest of the statutory window, the sale of the shares, or the employee **ceasing to be employed** — **48 months** from the end of the relevant assessment year for shares allotted before 1 April 2026, **60 months** under s.392(3) read with s.289(3) of the Income-tax Act 2025 for allotments on or after it ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/taxation/esop-scheme-design-in-indian-startup-tax/)). Eligibility needs **DPIIT recognition _and_ a valid s.80-IAC certificate from the Inter-Ministerial Board** — roughly 3,700 of about 1.97 lakh DPIIT-recognised startups hold one ([EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/perquisite-tax-deferral-startups); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/esop-tax-deferral-startup-employees-dpiit-section-80-iac); [Income Tax India](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/schedulde_esop)). It is a **deferral, not an exemption**, and resignation crystallises the bill. **Find out this month whether VSYST holds an 80-IAC/IMB certificate** — saying "there's a startup tax deferral" without one is a lie with a five-year fuse. **VERIFY LIVE with the CA on every row above.**

**Block F — liquidity, said plainly.** This is the block founders skip and employees remember.

| Route                                  | What it is really worth                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | House call                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Buyback under s.68**                 | For buybacks **on or after 1 October 2024** the **entire consideration is a deemed dividend in the shareholder's hands**, taxed at slab with TDS by the company, and the cost of acquisition becomes a capital loss rather than a set-off ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/taxation/buyback-tax-in-india/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/shareholders/1511692/the-new-tax-landscape-for-buy-backs-implications-of-the-finance-no-2-act-2024); [EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/buyback-shares-tax-india)) | **Largely dead as an ESOP liquidity route for an independent company.** Do not build the promise on it. If you keep a conditional window, write the tax consequence into the same paragraph — **VERIFY LIVE with the CA** |
| **Phantom stock / cash-settled SARs**  | A contractual bonus plan: board resolution and a written agreement, no shareholder approval, no MCA filing. A **real cash liability**; salary in the year of payout ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/finance/phantom-stock-in-india/))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | **The instrument that fits a company that may never exit** — and the only route open to advisors                                                                                                                          |
| **Profit share**                       | A stated % of PAT above a threshold, split by points, paid annually                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | **The most honest instrument for VSYST's likely future.** Cash, in a year the employee can spend it                                                                                                                       |
| **Strategic acquisition (share sale)** | Governed entirely by your change-of-control clause. Decide **single vs double trigger now** — after a term sheet arrives it is the buyer's clause                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Write it into the scheme this year                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Asset sale**                         | The business is sold, the shell survives holding the cash, and **options are options on the shell**. If the scheme is silent, holders can watch the company be sold and receive nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | **Write the asset-sale case into the scheme explicitly.** The most common nasty surprise in the subject                                                                                                                   |
| **Acqui-hire**                         | **Non-founder option holders usually get nothing.** The consideration is deliberately structured as compensation to the acquired employees, not as price for shares                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | **Never describe an acqui-hire as an outcome employees share in** ([[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession\|lesson 20]] §9.3)                                                                                    |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All numbers illustrative; every rate VERIFY LIVE with the CA.** Assume **10,00,000** fully diluted shares and a 7% pool = **70,000 options**.

**The offer.** Priya, Implementation & Support Associate #1, L3. Band mid ₹3,40,000; offer ₹3,60,000 — **inside band**, no exception needed. Grant: **6,000 options = 0.60%** of fully diluted equity, strike **₹10**, four-year vest, one-year cliff, monthly thereafter, **five-year post-termination exercise window**.

| Step                            | Arithmetic                                               | Result                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grant, year 0                   | 6,000 options @ ₹10                                      | No tax, no cash                                                              |
| Cliff, year 1                   | 1,500 vest                                               | No tax                                                                       |
| Year 4                          | 6,000 vested                                             | No tax                                                                       |
| She exercises all 6,000         | 6,000 × ₹10                                              | **₹60,000 out of her pocket**                                                |
| Merchant-banker FMV at exercise | ₹250/share                                               | Shares "worth" ₹15,00,000                                                    |
| **Perquisite**                  | (₹250 − ₹10) × 6,000                                     | **₹14,40,000 added to salary income**                                        |
| Tax on it                       | ~30% + 4% cess on the top slice                          | **≈ ₹4.5 lakh**, deducted by VSYST as TDS                                    |
| **Cash she must find**          | ₹60,000 + ₹4,50,000                                      | **≈ ₹5.1 lakh, in a year she earned ~₹4.2 lakh, for shares she cannot sell** |
| With the 80-IAC deferral        | Moves to the earliest of 60 months, sale, or her leaving | The ₹4.5 lakh waits — **unless she resigns**                                 |
| Sale, year 7, at ₹400           | (₹400 − ₹250) × 6,000 = ₹9,00,000, held > 24 months      | LTCG at 12.5% ≈ **₹1.13 lakh**                                               |

**Read the eighth row again.** A grant designed without thinking about the exercise event hands your best employee a ₹5 lakh bill. That is not generosity; it is a trap with your name on it.

**The six sentences said out loud at grant** ([[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|lesson 10]] §8.7): the number of options; 0.60% today, lower if we issue more; four years with nothing in year one; ₹60,000 to buy them, payable at exercise; the tax at exercise with the arithmetic shown and an honest statement of whether the deferral applies to us; five years to exercise after leaving. Then the sentence that matters: **"If this company is ever worth ₹50 crore, your 0.6% is about ₹30 lakh before tax. If nothing happens it is worth zero. There is no sale planned. Buyback is now taxed as a dividend in your hands, so that is not the plan either — what we will do instead is a written profit share from our first profitable year."** If you cannot say that out loud, do not grant options. Grant a bonus.

**And the family row.** The domain director's nephew joins as Field Executive #1: same band, same 0.20–0.40% guideline, same vest, same cliff — plus the s.188 resolution with the interested director not voting, the disclosure of interest, the Directors' Report line, review criteria written and dated **before** the review period, and the CS's note on the promoter-group position. Seven documents, one sitting ([[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing|lesson 10]] §9.6).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | What it looks like                                     | Fix                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Band written after the candidate**        | The table matches the finalist suspiciously well       | Rule 1. Signed by three directors before the loop opens                                                             |
| **One exception to close a hire**           | "Just this once, he's exceptional"                     | Rule 2. The exception becomes the band. Move the band or hold the line                                              |
| **Paying the family in the wrong currency** | "Take less salary, you already own part of this"       | Rule 7. Two hats, two instruments, two records ([[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing\|lesson 10]] §9.1)  |
| **Softer scrutiny for a relative**          | Vesting everyone knows will never be enforced          | Rule 7. Scrutiny goes **up**, criteria written before the review                                                    |
| **A 90-day post-termination window**        | Copied from an American template                       | Block D. Five years minimum. It costs nothing today and closes candidates                                           |
| **Selling the buyback**                     | "We'll buy your shares back, that's your liquidity"    | Block F. Post-1 Oct 2024 the whole consideration is a deemed dividend at slab. Lean on phantom/SAR and profit share |
| **Selling the acqui-hire**                  | "If we get acquired, everyone wins"                    | Block F. Non-founder holders usually get nothing, and an asset sale can strand the pool entirely                    |
| **Promising a deferral you cannot give**    | "There's a startup tax exemption"                      | Block E. It is a deferral, it needs an **80-IAC/IMB certificate**, and leaving crystallises it                      |
| **Pricing the company to an employee**      | "The pool is worth ₹4 crore"                           | Rule 10. Percentage, strike, vest, and the honest range — nothing else                                              |
