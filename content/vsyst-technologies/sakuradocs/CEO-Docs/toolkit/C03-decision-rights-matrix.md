# C03 — CEO ↔ COO ↔ Board Decision-Rights Matrix

_Toolkit · fills the decision-rights work in [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|03 — The CEO's Core Value and the Founder Contracts]] and [[16-decisions-delegation-and-not-being-the-bottleneck|16 — Decisions, Delegation and Not Being the Bottleneck]] · Owner: the CEO drafts it, all three directors argue every row, the board ratifies it by resolution · Cadence: first cut in Days 1–30, ratified by day 90, read quarterly, re-ratified annually or on any change of shareholding · No workbook tab of its own — the ratification date and the next review date go on the `Board Calendar` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

**This template extends the COO's [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]] upward, and it deliberately does not repeat a single one of T22's rows.** T22 answers "who may commit the company's money and people, up to what limit, below board level" — nine families of operating decision: spend, hire and fire, contracts, discounts, refunds, deploys, data access, comms, statutory mechanics. C03 starts exactly where T22's top line ends. It answers the question T22 cannot: **when does a decision stop belonging to a manager, stop belonging to the CEO, and start belonging to the board as a body or to the shareholders as owners — and when does the Companies Act 2013 make that a legal requirement rather than a preference?**

Two layers sit on top of one another here and founders routinely merge them, which is where the trouble starts. The **contractual layer** is what you and your co-founders agree between yourselves — reserved matters in the founders' agreement ([[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]]) and the rupee limits you set out of prudence. It is entirely yours to design. The **statutory layer** is what the Act requires whatever you agree: certain powers can only be exercised by the Board by a resolution passed at a properly convened meeting ([§179(3)(a)–(k)](https://ca2013.com/179-powers-of-board/)), certain things need the shareholders, and getting it wrong makes the transaction voidable or the director personally liable. A matrix that only covers the first layer looks tidy and fails an audit; one that only covers the second is a compliance checklist, not a way to run a company. C03 carries both, in the same row.

The column shape borrows from Bain's **RAPID** — recommend, input, agree, perform, decide — whose whole contribution is separating _deciding_ from _being consulted_ and giving the "agree" role a narrow, named veto rather than a general right to be unhappy ([Mindtools — Bain's RAPID framework](https://www.mindtools.com/av8ceid/bains-rapid-framework/); [Toolshero — RAPID decision-making model](https://www.toolshero.com/decision-making/rapid-decision-making-model/)). Collapsed for a three-person company it becomes four columns — decides, consulted, informed, and the limit — plus the two statutory ones.

The reason to build it at all is Bezos's distinction. Type 1 decisions are one-way doors: consequential, near-irreversible, and worth a heavy process. Type 2 decisions are two-way doors that should be made fast, by whoever is closest, at roughly 70% of the information you would like. The failure mode that kills speed is applying the Type 1 process to Type 2 decisions ([CNBC on Bezos's 2015 shareholder letter](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/11/19/jeff-bezos-simple-strategy-for-answering-amazons-hardest-questions--.html)). A founder-CEO with no matrix runs _everything_ as Type 1 by reflex, because everything feels consequential when you own it. The matrix is how you stop.

## When to use

- **Days 1–30 of the CEO seat**, immediately after [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]]'s "decisions I will stop making" list. Every line on that list becomes a row here with a date.
- **Before the founders' agreement is signed** — the reserved-matters clause in [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]] should be _drawn from_ this matrix, not invented separately, or the two will contradict each other on the day it matters.
- **The first time a director says "I thought we agreed I could do that"** — that is a missing row, not a personality problem.
- **Before any board meeting that will approve borrowing, a partnership, share issue, or anything involving a relative or a director's firm.**
- **Immediately on any change of shareholding** — a new shareholder, an ESOP grant, first outside capital. The shareholder column stops being theoretical the day someone outside the founding three holds shares.
- **Annually, with the CS**, because private-company exemptions are conditional and have been amended more than once.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One decider per row. Always a person or a body, never "the founders".** "The founders will decide" is the sentence that produces a three-week argument. If the honest answer is "all three must agree", write **unanimous board resolution** and accept that you have created a veto for each of them, deliberately.
2. **Separate `consulted` from `agree`.** Consulted means their view must be heard and recorded before the decision; it is not a veto. Only the statutory rows and the reserved matters in C02 carry a real veto. RAPID's discipline is that a vague middle produces decisions nobody owns ([Toolshero](https://www.toolshero.com/decision-making/rapid-decision-making-model/)).
3. **Never write an operational row here.** If it fits one of T22's nine families and sits below the board line, it belongs in T22. The test: _would a competent employee be expected to make this?_ If yes, it is T22's. Duplicating a row in both documents guarantees they drift and then contradict.
4. **Walk the agenda against §179(3) and Rule 8 before every board meeting.** Anything on that list — issuing securities, borrowing, investing funds, giving loans or guarantees, approving the Board's report, diversifying the business, a merger, taking over another company — must be a numbered resolution passed _at a meeting_, not a WhatsApp agreement. Rule 8 of the Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules 2014 adds appointing or removing KMP and appointing internal and secretarial auditors ([§179](https://ca2013.com/179-powers-of-board/)). **VERIFY LIVE** with the CS — Rule 8's list has been amended.
5. **Know which §180 rows are switched off for you, and know what switches them back on.** [§180(1)(a)–(d)](https://ca2013.com/180-restrictions-on-powers-of-board/) normally requires a shareholder **special resolution** to sell substantially the whole undertaking, to invest merger compensation, to borrow beyond paid-up capital + free reserves + securities premium, or to remit a debt due from a director. **Section 180 does not apply to private companies** under MCA notification [GSR 464(E) dated 5 June 2015](https://ca2013.com/notifications/exemptions-to-private-companies-05062015-gsr-464-e/) — **conditional**, since the 13 June 2017 amendment, on the company not having defaulted in filing its financial statements under §137 or its annual return under §92 ([taxguru — s.180 analysis](https://taxguru.in/company-law/section-180-companies-act-2013.html)). Read that as the trade it is: _your borrowing freedom is collateral for your filing discipline._ Miss a filing and the exemption falls away. And read your **Articles** — the AoA can impose the restriction back on you regardless. **VERIFY LIVE.**
6. **Related-party rows are written before the transaction, never after.** [§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/) requires prior Board consent by resolution at a meeting, and shareholder approval above the Rule 15(3) thresholds. At VSYST's scale the percentage thresholds are crossed by small rupee amounts, because they are percentages of a near-zero turnover and a small net worth. An unapproved arrangement is voidable at the Board's option and the interested director must indemnify the company for the loss. The house rule: **if the counterparty is a relative or a firm a director controls, it goes on the agenda before it goes into effect.**
7. **Use resolution by circulation sparingly, and never for a §179(3) matter.** [§175](https://ca2013.com/175-passing-of-resolution-by-circulation/) lets the board pass a circulated resolution by a majority of directors entitled to vote — but if **not less than one-third** of directors require it to be decided at a meeting, it must go to a meeting. On a three-person board that is one director. Any of you can force any circulated item into a real meeting. Treat that as a feature and say so in the matrix.
8. **Every row names where the decision is recorded.** Board minute, [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]], the register of contracts in which directors are interested (Form MBP-4, [§189](https://ca2013.com/189-register-of-contracts-or-arrangements-in-which-directors-are-interested/)), or the COO's [[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]]. An approval nobody can find in nine months is not a control.
9. **Mark each row Type 1 or Type 2 and let that set the process, not the rupee amount.** A ₹20,000 decision that is a one-way door (choosing a payment gateway everyone's mandates will be sitting on; naming the product) deserves more care than a ₹2,00,000 reversible one. Type 1 rows get a [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] entry with the reasoning written _before_ the outcome is known.
10. **Ratify it by board resolution and put the version, ratification date and review date at the top.** Until it is minuted it is a suggestion. Use [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]].

## Template

```
DECISION RIGHTS — BOARD AND ABOVE          <company>        version 1.0
Ratified by board resolution on: ____________   Minute ref: ____________
Next review: ____________       Shareholding as at this date: ____________
Layer note: operating authority below the board line lives in T22 and is NOT
            repeated here. If a competent employee would be expected to make
            it, it is a T22 row.
Circulation: any ONE director may require any circulated item to be decided
            at a meeting (s.175). No s.179(3) matter is ever circulated.
Type 1 rows (one-way doors) require a C29 entry written before the outcome.
```

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

Every rupee figure is a proposal for the board to ratify, not a fact, and every statutory column is **VERIFY LIVE** with the company secretary before you rely on it. "Board" means all three directors at a properly convened meeting under [§173](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/). "Shareholders" is the same three people wearing a different hat — which is exactly why the distinction gets skipped, and exactly why it must not be.

Three notes on reading it. **The borrowing row has no rupee floor on purpose** — a ₹3 lakh working-capital OD taken quietly is the single most common way a small private company acquires a personal guarantee nobody minuted. **The related-party row also has no floor**, for the reason in rule 6. And **the two hardest rows to hold are the strategy row and the partnership row**, because both feel like conversations rather than decisions right up to the moment they are irreversible.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                                                      | What it looks like                                                                | Fix                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Duplicating T22 rows here**                                                | Discount limits and vendor spend appear in both documents, with different numbers | Rule 3. One decision, one home. C03 starts where T22's top row stops                                       |
| **"The founders decide"**                                                    | A row with three names in the decider column                                      | Rule 1. Name a body and a resolution type, or name one person                                              |
| **Treating a board decision as a WhatsApp agreement**                        | A §179(3) matter agreed in a group chat and never minuted                         | Rule 4. Numbered resolution, at a meeting, in the minute book                                              |
| **Assuming the private-company exemptions are permanent**                    | "We're a Pvt Ltd, §180 doesn't apply to us"                                       | It is conditional on §137 and §92 filings and can be overridden by your AoA. Re-check annually with the CS |
| **A shareholder column that is ignored because it is the same three people** | Board minute used where a special resolution was required                         | The hats are legally different. An investor's diligence will find it, and unwinding it later is expensive  |
| **Related-party transactions written up afterwards**                         | The rent agreement is dated March; the board approval is dated August             | Rule 6. Prior consent, MBP-4 entry at once                                                                 |
| **No Type 1 / Type 2 marking**                                               | Every decision waits for the monthly board meeting                                | Rule 9. Reversible decisions do not need a board; make them at 70% information                             |
| **A matrix nobody ratified**                                                 | A good draft in the CEO's folder for eleven months                                | Rule 10. Board resolution and a minute ref, or it does not bind anyone                                     |
| **Limits set so low that everything crosses them**                           | Every ₹15,000 decision arrives at the board                                       | You have rebuilt the bottleneck with paperwork. Raise the lines as evidence accumulates                    |
