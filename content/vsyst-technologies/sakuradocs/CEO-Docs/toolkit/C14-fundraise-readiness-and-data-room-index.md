# C14 — Fundraise Readiness Checklist and Data-Room Index

_Toolkit · fills exercise 15.6 in [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|08 — Capital, Runway and Fundraising]] · Owner: CEO owns the readiness verdict and the red list; the CS owns folders 01–02, the CA owns 03–04 · Cadence: opened at the 12-month rung of [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]], then one folder per month, then walked end to end every quarter · Workbook tab: none — this is a folder tree in the vault plus a red list; the numbers it points at live in `Runway & Scenarios` and `Cap Table & Dilution` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two things, and the second is why it exists.

The first is a **readiness verdict**: a short list of gates that decide whether this company should be raising at all this quarter, answered before anybody builds a deck. The second is a **data-room index** — the folder tree an Indian seed diligence actually walks, built in the order a diligence lawyer opens it.

The reason to build the index is not that investors demand it early. They do not; nobody asks for a data room at a first meeting. **The reason is that assembling it is how you find your own problems, and finding one four months before a diligence request is the difference between a fixable item and a re-priced round.** Every founder who has been through diligence describes the same experience: the round was never lost on the thing they were worried about. It was lost on an inconsistency — a deck number that did not match the MIS, a customer count that did not match the signed contracts, a founder who said one thing and a co-founder another.

**Which produces the rule that governs this template: a data room is built before the raise, over months, one folder at a time — never in a panic week.** A folder tree assembled in seven nights is visible as such, because retroactive governance always is: minutes that were all signed on the same afternoon, an assignment deed dated the week the term sheet arrived, a register written in one hand. Diligence teams see that pattern several times a year. The unorganised data room is not merely slow; it costs you negotiating position, which is a polite way of saying it costs money ([datarooms.in — how Indian startups use virtual data rooms during due diligence](https://datarooms.in/blog/how-indian-startups-use-virtual-data-rooms-during-due-diligence/); [Digify — virtual data room setup for due diligence](https://digify.com/blog/virtual-data-room-setup-for-due-diligence-a-7-step-guide/)).

**And there is exactly one item on this list that is genuinely deal-breaking in India rather than merely embarrassing: IP assignment.** It gets its own section below, section C, because a line in a checklist is not enough weight for the thing most likely to kill your round ([[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|lesson 20]] §9.2).

## When to use

- **First open it at the 12-month rung** of the [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] ladder — the green rung, where preparing costs nothing. Not at 9, and certainly not at 6, where the same work competes with selling.
- **One folder per month, in the order below.** Corporate first, because it is the slowest to fix and the one the CS has to drive.
- **The whole tree walked green/amber/red every quarter**, at the same sitting as the board calendar review ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]).
- **Immediately, out of sequence, on three triggers:** any code written by a person who is not a founder-director; any equity or "we'll sort you out later" promise made to anyone; any transaction with the family fuel business.
- **Before the first investor meeting, not during the process.** Paul Graham's blunt framing applies to the whole of it: a founder who can credibly say "we're fine either way" gets different terms from one who cannot ([Paul Graham — How to Raise Money](https://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html), 2013).
- **Also useful when you are not raising at all.** Most of this tree is what [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession|lesson 20]] §9.2 calls the common core — required for a strategic sale, a bank line, a large enterprise customer's vendor onboarding, and for simply being a well-run Pvt Ltd.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One folder a month, dated as you go.** Every document carries the date it was actually executed, not the date you scanned it. Backdating anything in this tree is a fraud question, not a housekeeping one ([s.447](https://ca2013.com/447-punishment-for-fraud/)).
2. **Every folder has one named owner and a green/amber/red status.** Red means "a diligence team would raise this". Amber means "it exists but is untidy". Green means an outsider could read it without asking you a question.
3. **The red list is the artefact, not the tree.** A tree with no red items after the first walk means you did not look properly. Expect three to eight on the first pass.
4. **One version of every number.** Never maintain a deck number and a real number. The MIS, the model, the deck and the audited accounts must reconcile, and where they legitimately differ (accrual vs cash, GST-inclusive vs net) the reconciliation itself is a document in folder 03.
5. **Disclose early, in writing, starting with the thing you are least comfortable with.** A problem you name in week one becomes a term. The same problem found in week six is a re-price or a walk.
6. **Related-party dealings get papered before anyone asks.** For a family-founded company whose domain-expert director's family runs fuel businesses, every arrangement, rent cheque and payment to a relative is a diligence item and a statutory one ([§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/), [§189 register](https://ca2013.com/189-register-of-contracts-or-arrangements-in-which-directors-are-interested/)). It is also a permanent reserved matter after a round closes.
7. **Do not build the deck before the tree.** The narrative and deck ([[C15-investor-narrative-and-deck-outline|C15]]) take four weeks; the tree takes months. Sequencing them the other way is how founders arrive at a term sheet with an open IP question.
8. **Access is controlled and logged.** One link per fund, revocable, watermarked, with view logs. Do not email a zip. Do not put the tree in a public Drive folder. Bank statements, dealer contracts and employee data are in here, and the last of those brings [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) obligations with it — **VERIFY LIVE with counsel**.
9. **The CS and CA are cheap and this is what you are paying them for.** A single sitting with each, once a quarter, closes more red items than a month of your own evenings.
10. **Every statutory number, form, threshold and timeline in this template is VERIFY LIVE.** Indian filing regimes move every Budget, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbered a great deal from 1 April 2026.

## Template

**A. Readiness gates — answer these before building anything**

A raise is a 4–6 month project and the slowest available fix for a cash problem                               |

| 4   | **Unit economics you would defend to a hostile reader**          | CAC payback computed from real invoices ([[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator\|C12]])                                                                                                                                                      | LTV/CAC quoted at eight customers                                                                                             |
| 5   | **The three directors agree on the raise**                       | Written, signed, dated ([[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist\|C02]], [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]])                                                                                                                       | "We'll decide when we see a term sheet"                                                                                       |
| 6   | **Founder vesting is executed**                                  | Signed between the three of you, voluntarily, before any investor asks                                                                                                                                                                                   | Absent — a diligence finding, every time, and then it is a negotiation between family members with a stranger holding the pen |
| 7   | **IP is assigned to the company**                                | Section C, all rows closed                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Any row open. **This is the only red on this table that is a stop**                                                           |
| 8   | **Statutory filings current**                                    | ROC, GST, TDS, annual return under [§92](https://ca2013.com/annual-return/), minutes under [§118](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/) | Anything overdue. **C. IP assignment — the first-order item**

This is not a checklist line. It is the single most common way an Indian startup's round dies in diligence, and the reason is structural rather than careless: **Indian law does not transfer authorship on good intentions.**

Under the Copyright Act, 1957, the author of a work is its first owner. Section 17(c) makes the **employer** the first owner of a work made by an employee in the course of employment, absent agreement to the contrary — but a **freelancer, consultant or contractor is not an employee**, so the default flips and the contractor keeps the copyright unless there is a **written assignment** complying with sections 18 and 19 ([Khurana & Khurana — copyright ownership in employment and freelance contracts](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/copyright-ownership-in-employment-and-freelance-contracts-key-legal-insights); [Unimarks Legal — freelancers and commissioned works](https://unimarkslegal.com/copyright-ownership-in-freelancers-and-commissioned-works-challenges-and-solutions/); [Copyright Act, 1957 — WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/15814)). **VERIFY LIVE with counsel** before relying on a sentence of this.

Read that against how DZZLO was actually built and the exposure names itself: code written by a founder _before_ VSYST Technologies existed; a friend who fixed something over a weekend; a freelancer paid over UPI with no contract; a designer who made the logo; an intern. Each is an open question about who owns the company's only asset — and it is cheap to fix while everyone is friendly and can become impossible to fix later, because the person whose signature you need has since left, fallen out with you, or worked out that they have leverage.

| #   | Row                                                                              | Evidence that closes it                                                                                                                                                                    | Status |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| 1   | **Each founder-director**, for all pre-incorporation code, designs and content   | A dated assignment deed to VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd., naming the work and the period, executed and stamped                                                                              |        |
| 2   | **Every employee**, past and present                                             | Employment agreement containing an assignment clause **and** a confidentiality clause                                                                                                      |        |
| 3   | **Every contractor, freelancer, agency and intern who ever touched the product** | A written assignment under ss.18–19 — a paid invoice is not an assignment                                                                                                                  |        |
| 4   | **The DZZLO name and logo**                                                      | Trade-mark application or registration; assignment from whoever designed it                                                                                                                |        |
| 5   | **Domains, app-store accounts, cloud accounts, code repositories**               | Registered in the **company's** name, not a founder's personal one. This is the row people fail on their own                                                                               |        |
| 6   | **Third-party and open-source components**                                       | A licence inventory with each licence type; nothing copyleft where it would infect the product                                                                                             |        |
| 7   | **AI-generated code and assets**                                                 | A note of what tooling was used and on what terms. Ownership of AI-generated work is unsettled in India — **VERIFY LIVE with counsel**                                                     |        |
| 8   | **Data**                                                                         | Whose data is in the system, on what consent, and what you may lawfully do with it ([DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)) |        |

**Cost to close all eight today: an afternoon with the CS and a template deed.** Cost to close them during a term-sheet exclusivity period: the round.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All statuses illustrative; every one to be re-walked with the CS and CA.** First walk, at month 4 of a green rung, three hours with the folder tree open.

| Folder        | Status    | Finding                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01 Corporate  | **Amber** | Minutes exist for eight of eleven board meetings. Three are notes in a Google Doc. The CS can regularise the record going forward; the gap is disclosed, not invented |
| 02 Cap table  | **Red**   | No founder vesting. Three directors, 100% held, nothing vesting. This is a finding at every seed round in India                                                       |
| 03 Financials | Green     | Two audited years, MIS reconciles, model matches the bank                                                                                                             |
| 04 Tax        | **Red**   | Two payments to the family pump for fuel and one for office space, unpapered, unresolved in the register                                                              |
| 05 IP         | **Red**   | Rows 1, 3 and 5 open: pre-incorporation code by the technical CEO, one 2024 freelancer, and the Play Store account still on a personal Google ID                      |
| 06 Product    | Amber     | No written security posture; tenant isolation is real in code and undocumented                                                                                        |
| 07 Contracts  | **Red**   | Eleven dealers, four with signed order forms. The other seven agreed on WhatsApp                                                                                      |
| 08 People     | Green     | No employees. Written "nil undocumented equity promises", signed by all three                                                                                         |
| 09 Legal      | Green     | Nil litigation, stated in writing. IOCL and Easebuzz correspondence filed                                                                                             |

**Five reds, and the arithmetic of fixing them is the point.** Vesting: one sitting and a CS fee. Related-party papering: one CA sitting. IP rows 1 and 3: a template deed, a WhatsApp message to a freelancer who still likes you, and ₹0. IP row 5: an afternoon of account migration. Contracts: send the standard order form to seven dealers who are already paying you and are pleased with the product.

**Total: about ₹40,000 of professional fees and four founder-days, spread across three months, while nobody is under pressure.** The same five items, discovered in week five of a confirmatory diligence with a 45-day no-shop running, are: a freelancer who now knows he has leverage, seven dealers being asked to sign paper during a period when you cannot afford one of them to hesitate, and a family conversation about the pump conducted under a deadline. That is not a checklist exercise. That is the entire argument of this template.

The honest second finding is what is **not** red. VSYST has no employees, no debt, no litigation and no outside shareholders — which means the tree is as small as it will ever be, and the reds are as cheap as they will ever be. Both facts are about today's shape, not permanent features. Re-price the effort the day the first offer letter goes out.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                   | Why it happens                               | The fix                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Building the tree during the process      | Nobody asks for it before then               | Rule: months, not a week. Start at the 12-month rung                                                                                                      |
| Deck first, data room second              | The deck is more fun and feels like progress | The tree takes months; the deck takes four weeks. Sequence accordingly                                                                                    |
| IP assignment as one line in a checklist  | It looks like paperwork                      | Section C. It is the only stop-gate on the readiness table                                                                                                |
| A "deck number" and a "real number"       | The deck number is more encouraging          | One version of every number. Diligence is lost on inconsistency, not on weakness                                                                          |
| Minutes reconstructed in one afternoon    | The meetings did happen                      | Regularise going forward and disclose the gap. Retroactive governance is visible instantly                                                                |
| Related-party dealings left informal      | It is family, and it feels rude to paper it  | [§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/) requires it anyway, and it becomes a reserved matter after a round                             |
| Contracts that are WhatsApp confirmations | The dealer is happy and it felt unnecessary  | Get the order form signed while he is happy, not while you are raising                                                                                    |
| Accounts in a founder's personal name     | It was faster on day one                     | Migrate every domain, store and cloud account to the company. Row 5 of Section C                                                                          |
| A public Drive link as a data room        | Fastest thing that works                     | One revocable, logged, watermarked link per fund                                                                                                          |
| A first walk that produces no reds        | You looked for confirmation, not problems    | Have the CS or CA walk it instead. Expect three to eight                                                                                                  |
| Treating the tree as raise-only work      | It only matters if you raise                 | Most of it is [[20-the-ceo-own-operating-system-and-succession\|lesson 20]] §9.2's common core — needed for a sale, a bank line, or simply being well run |
