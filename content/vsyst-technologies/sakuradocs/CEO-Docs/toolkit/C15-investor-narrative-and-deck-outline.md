# C15 — Investor Narrative and Deck Outline

_Toolkit · fills the narrative-and-deck work in [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|08 — Capital, Runway and Fundraising]] §11.2–11.3, and feeds exercise 15.6 · Owner: CEO — the story is one of the three jobs and has no delegate · Cadence: narrative rewritten whenever the strategy changes; deck rewritten after every four investor meetings · Workbook tab: none — every number on the traction and model slides is read from `Unit Economics` and `Runway & Scenarios` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx), never typed in fresh._

## Purpose

A deck is not an argument. It is a set of headings over an argument that either exists or does not, and the fastest way to find out which is to try to write the argument as prose with no slides available. **This template therefore has two halves: the one-page written narrative that must exist first, and the slide-by-slide that carries it.**

The slide-by-slide is built around two columns that most deck templates omit and that are the entire value here: **what each slide must prove**, and **the most common lie told on it**. Every slide in a seed deck has a characteristic dishonesty — a shape of overstatement so standard that experienced investors read past the words looking for it. Naming it in advance is the cheapest editorial discipline available, because you can then simply not tell it, which is both more honest and more persuasive than the alternative.

The formats are the classic ones and they have not moved: [Sequoia's business-plan outline](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/) and [Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 rule](https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/) — ten slides, twenty minutes, thirty-point type. What has been added since is data on how decks are actually read, and it is sobering: DocSend's analysis puts average VC review time on a seed deck at **under four minutes**, with only a minority of decks read to the last slide and later pages averaging around **fifteen seconds each** ([DocSend — pre-seed pitch deck guide](https://www.docsend.com/blog/pre-seed-pitch-deck-guide/); [DocSend — Startup Index pitch deck metrics](https://www.docsend.com/pitch-deck-metrics/); [PitchGrade — what investors actually read](https://pitchgrade.com/blog/what-investors-read-pitch-deck-docsend-data)) — **VERIFY LIVE**, these are a vendor's own cohort and the figures drift.

**The consequence for how you build it is not "make it shorter". It is: put the argument on the first four slides, because those are the only ones you can be confident were read.**

Note what this template is not. It does not decide whether to raise — that is [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|lesson 08]] §8 and [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]]'s gate 1, and building a deck before that decision is a well-known way to feel productive while avoiding it. **Do not build a deck on spec.**

## When to use

- **After** the readiness gates in [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]] are green, in Phase 1 of the §11.1 timeline. Four to six weeks before the first meeting, not eight months.
- **Narrative first, always.** If the five paragraphs will not come, no deck rescues them.
- **Rewrite after every four meetings.** Three meetings in, the pattern in what people push on is telling you something about the business rather than about the pitch ([[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]).
- **Also outside a raise.** The same narrative is the spine of a partner conversation with an OMC, a large enterprise dealer group, a first senior hire, and the monthly [[C23-stakeholder-and-investor-update|C23]] update. Writing it is not wasted even if you never raise.
- **Reads from** [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]] (the kernel), [[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments|C06]] (sizing), [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas|C08]] (the words), [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator|C12]] and [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] (every number).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Write the five paragraphs before opening any design tool.** One page, prose, no bullets. If a stranger reading it does not want to meet you, the problem is not the font.
2. **One idea per slide, and the idea is the headline.** The headline is a sentence that makes a claim — "Fuel dealers reconcile a month late from a paper diary" — not a label like "Problem".
3. **Front-load.** The first page gets more attention than any other by a wide margin. Problem, why now, insight and product are slides 1–4 for a reason.
4. **Every number on a slide names its source and its date.** A sourced ₹300-crore TAM beats an unsourced ₹5,000-crore one, because the second tells a partner you did not do the work.
5. **A small honest number beats a large vague one.** Experienced investors discount vagueness far harder than they discount smallness. "Eleven paying dealers, four of them cold, 92% month-3 retention" is a stronger slide than "strong traction and rapid growth".
6. **Do not hide the ticket size.** ₹1,799 per GSTIN per month is what it is ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]]). Address the resulting arithmetic head-on with the expansion path rather than letting a partner discover it in the meeting after yours.
7. **Show the product on a phone.** DZZLO is mobile-first; a screen recording of a real dispatch with a driver OTP, filmed at a real pump, does more than three slides of architecture.
8. **Never a use-of-funds slide that is 60% salaries with no plan attached.** Every rupee ties to a milestone that changes the company's evidence base.
9. **Two decks, one truth.** The send deck must be readable with nobody in the room; the present deck must be unreadable without you. Same numbers, different density (section C).
10. **Everything on a slide must reconcile with the data room.** [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]] rule 4: one version of every number. A deck number that does not match the MIS is how rounds are lost ([[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|lesson 08]] §11.6).
11. **Say the unfair advantage out loud.** Investors pattern-match for founder–market fit and will not infer it. A domain-expert director with three decades in fuel distribution is the single most differentiating fact about this company and it must be on the screen, not in your head.

## Template

**A. The narrative — five paragraphs, on one page, before any slide**

| ¶   | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                    | The test it must pass                                                                                    |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **The change in the world.** Something is different now — GST and e-invoicing pushing paper businesses onto systems, a generational handover in dealer families, smartphones on every forecourt | An investor funds a wave, not a feature. If ¶1 is about your product, start again                        |
| 2   | **The specific broken thing.** Not "SMBs lack software" — _a dealer sets tomorrow's rate at 11 PM, dispatches on a paper slip at 5 AM, and reconciles a month later from a diary_               | A stranger can repeat it back after one reading                                                          |
| 3   | **Why now, and why you.** The unfair advantage, stated plainly                                                                                                                                  | Someone who has never met you can say what your team has that others do not                              |
| 4   | **The evidence.** What is actually true, stated without inflation                                                                                                                               | Every sentence has a number and every number has a source in the data room                               |
| 5   | **The wedge and the expansion.** Rate-and-dispatch is the wedge; the transaction is the platform                                                                                                | The expansion is one you actually intend. An expansion you do not intend is a lie with a three-year fuse |

**B. The slide-by-slide**

| #   | Slide                    | What it must prove                                                      | **The most common lie told on it**                                                             | The asset that beats the lie                                                                                                                                    |
| --- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Problem**              | That a specific, named person loses specific money today                | Describing a problem the founder finds interesting rather than one a customer pays to remove   | One dealer's real receivable figure, with his consent, and the month he lost it                                                                                 |
| 2   | **Why now**              | That this is possible or necessary in 2026 and was not in 2019          | "Digital transformation is accelerating" — a sentence true of every year since 1998            | Two dated regulatory or behavioural facts, cited                                                                                                                |
| 3   | **The insight**          | That you know something non-obvious about this market                   | Presenting a feature as an insight                                                             | The one sentence a competitor would disagree with                                                                                                               |
| 4   | **Product**              | That it exists and works, in the customer's hands                       | A polished mock of a screen that is not built                                                  | A 40-second screen recording from a real pump                                                                                                                   |
| 5   | **Traction**             | That someone who is not your friend paid you                            | Vanity denominators: downloads, signups, "users", free tenants counted as customers            | Paying tenants, cold-closed count, retention by cohort, from [[C12-unit-economics-and-business-model-calculator\|C12]]                                          |
| 6   | **Market**               | That the reachable market supports the outcome you claim                | Top-down TAM: "1% of a ₹5,000 crore market"                                                    | Bottom-up: outlets × reachable share × ARPA, sourced to [PPAC](https://ppac.gov.in/infrastructure/retail-outlets) ([[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments\|C06]]) |
| 7   | **Business model**       | That the unit economics work at your actual price                       | LTV on revenue; a churn assumption produced by dividing one by a hopeful number                | CAC payback in months from real invoices; the count of dealers to default alive                                                                                 |
| 8   | **Competition**          | That you know exactly who you displace and who you sit beside           | "We have no competitors" — which means either the market does not exist or you have not looked | A grid where a competitor genuinely wins a column. Position beside, not against ([April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/books))                           |
| 9   | **Team**                 | That this specific team is unusually likely to win this specific market | Padding with advisors and logos to disguise a thin bench                                       | Three sentences of real, checkable domain history; the gaps stated as gaps                                                                                      |
| 10  | **Ask and use of funds** | That the money buys a named change in the evidence base                 | A pie chart of salaries with no milestone attached                                             | Ask → what it proves → by when → what the company looks like at the end ([[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner\|C13]])                                         |

Three optional slides, used only where they are load-bearing: **why this becomes big** (the expansion argument — for a vertical SaaS with a modest ticket this is often slide 6.5 and is where a partner's real objection lives, see [a16z — fintech scales vertical SaaS](https://a16z.com/fintech-scales-vertical-saas/) and [Inventure — vertical fintech is the new vertical SaaS](https://blog.inventure.vc/vertical-fintech-is-the-new-vertical-saas-6b0c90992bf0)); **the wedge-to-platform map**; and **milestones to the next round**.

**C. Two decks, and the difference is not cosmetic**

|              | **The send deck**                                                                 | **The present deck**                                                                                      |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Read         | Alone, in four minutes, on a phone, by someone deciding whether to take a meeting | With you in the room, for twenty minutes, by someone deciding whether to invest                           |
| Slides       | 12–15, including the ones that answer obvious questions                           | 10, plus an appendix you never open unless asked                                                          |
| Density      | Self-explanatory. Headline sentence + one supporting graphic + the source line    | Sparse. A number and an image. **You** are the content                                                    |
| Notes        | The narrative is inside the slides                                                | The narrative is in your mouth                                                                            |
| Failure mode | Too sparse to survive without you → it gets forwarded and dies                    | Too dense to talk over → you read your own slides aloud, which is the worst twenty minutes in fundraising |
| Rule         | Never send the present deck. Never present the send deck                          |                                                                                                           |

**D. The appendix** — cohort retention, the full unit-economics workings, the runway model, the org and hiring plan, the competitive detail, the security and tenant-isolation note, the regulatory position. It exists so that the ten slides can stay at ten. **Its real job is to let you answer a hard question with a page instead of a promise.**

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All numbers illustrative and to be re-read from the workbook before use.**

**The narrative, compressed to its five first sentences:**

1. _India's fuel retail is being pushed onto systems from three sides at once — GST and e-invoicing, OMC digitisation, and a generational handover in which dealers' sons are running the pumps their fathers built on paper._
2. _A dealer sets tomorrow's rate at 11 PM, dispatches ten thousand litres on a paper slip at 5 AM, and finds out a month later that a transport company disputes the rate — and has no timestamped proof either way._
3. _We are three founder-directors in Raipur: a domain-expert director with three decades inside fuel distribution, a technical CEO who ships the code, and a live product in both app stores, sold pump by pump in one district._
4. _Eleven paying dealer companies, four of them closed cold, 92% retention at month three, CAC payback of 11.5 months computed from real invoices — and we are bootstrapped, so every one of those rupees is real._
5. _Rate-and-dispatch is the wedge because it is the moment money is decided; the ledger is the retention; and once the platform sits on the transaction, the financial products the dealer already needs — credit against receivables, collections — are the business._

**Two slides worked properly.**

_Slide 6, Market._ The lie available here is "India has over a lakh retail outlets ([Energy Watch](https://www.energywatch.in/oil-and-gas/indias-petrol-pump-count-crosses-1-lakh-nearing-us-china-in-network-scale)); at ₹1,799 per GSTIN that is a ₹2,000 crore market." Nobody believes it and it costs you credibility on the slide where you most need it. The honest version, which is stronger: _1,00,000+ outlets nationally ([PPAC](https://ppac.gov.in/infrastructure/retail-outlets), 2026); our ICP — DODO dealers with 5+ active B2B credit customers — is an estimated 18% of them; at 1.3 GSTINs and ₹1,799/month that is roughly ₹500 crore of annual revenue if we won everything, which we will not. Our five-year plan reaches 4,000 dealers._ Then show the district: Raipur and its neighbours, a countable number of pumps, and how many are ICP. **A market a partner can count is a market a partner can believe.**

_Slide 10, Ask and use of funds._ Not "₹3 crore: 60% salaries, 25% marketing, 15% ops." Instead: _₹3 crore buys 24 months. It proves one thing — that the district-by-district motion works when the founders are not the ones driving. Specifically: two sales hires and a support hire, four new districts, 150 paying dealers, CAC under ₹40,000 with a non-founder closing, and month-24 revenue at a run rate that makes the Series A a choice rather than a necessity._ Every rupee ties to a row in [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]].

**And the slide VSYST must not skip.** For a vertical SaaS at a ₹1,799 ticket, the real objection is never the product — it is whether this becomes big enough to return a fund ([[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|lesson 08]] §8.4; [Christoph Janz — five ways to build a \$100 million business](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/five-ways-to-build-a-100-million-business-82ac6ea8ffd9)). Put that argument on the screen yourself, with the arithmetic visible and the expansion you actually intend, or the partner will do it on the whiteboard after you leave and you will never hear the conclusion.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Why it happens                                          | The fix                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Slides before narrative                      | Slides feel like progress and prose feels like exposure | Five paragraphs, one page, first. Always                                                                                                           |
| Building a deck before deciding to raise     | It is the most enjoyable way to avoid the decision      | [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index\|C14]] gate 1. Do not build on spec                                                                  |
| Labels as headlines                          | "Problem", "Solution", "Market"                         | Every headline makes a claim in a sentence                                                                                                         |
| Top-down TAM                                 | It produces a bigger number in less time                | Bottom-up, sourced, with the reachable subset shown                                                                                                |
| "We have no competitors"                     | It feels like strength                                  | Name who you displace and who you sit beside. Let one column go to a rival                                                                         |
| Free tenants counted as customers            | The number is larger and the definition is fuzzy        | Paying tenants only. Define the word on the slide                                                                                                  |
| Hiding the ticket size                       | It invites the hard question                            | Put it up and answer the arithmetic yourself                                                                                                       |
| One deck used for both jobs                  | Two decks is more work                                  | Send deck and present deck. They fail in opposite directions                                                                                       |
| A deck number that does not match the MIS    | Two files, two evenings, one optimistic rounding        | Read every number from the workbook. One version of the truth                                                                                      |
| Sending the deck before the data room exists | The deck is ready first                                 | Tree first ([[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index\|C14]]); a fast "can we see the data room?" is a good sign you should be able to survive |
| Never rewriting it                           | It took three weeks and it is finished                  | Rewrite after every four meetings. The objections are the edit                                                                                     |
| Design treated as decoration                 | "The content is what matters"                           | Both are true. A deck that is hard to read in four minutes does not get four minutes ([DocSend](https://www.docsend.com/pitch-deck-metrics/))      |
