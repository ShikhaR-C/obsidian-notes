# C08 — Positioning and Messaging Canvas

_Toolkit · fills the exercises in [[06-strategy-ii-moats-positioning-and-the-business-model|06 — Strategy II: Moats, Positioning and the Business Model]] · Owner: CEO — positioning is not delegable to marketing, and VSYST has no marketing · Cadence: filled in Month 2, re-read after every ten customer conversations, formally re-run annually or when the ICP moves · No workbook tab — the canvas is this page; the verbatim quotes that feed it are collected in the interview notes filed with [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]]._

## Purpose

Positioning is the context you set so a buyer instantly understands what your product is, why it is different, and why they should care. It is not a tagline competition. Ries and Trout's original formulation is creating "a position in the prospect's mind" in an over-communicated society ([Al Ries — _Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind_](https://www.alries.com/positioning), 1981). April Dunford turned it into a method a founder can run in an afternoon, assembled from five components in a fixed order — **competitive alternatives, unique attributes, the value those attributes enable, the customers who care a lot, and the market category** — plus two steps after: layer on a trend, then capture it so it can be shared ([April Dunford — A Quickstart Guide to Positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning); [_Obviously Awesome_](https://www.aprildunford.com/books), 2019; [Heinz Marketing — the 10-step process](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/10-step-positioning-process-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-3/); a worked application at [Userlist](https://userlist.com/blog/positioning-overhaul/)).

The order matters because each component constrains the next, and the leverage is in the first: **your competitive alternatives are what the customer would do if you did not exist, which is almost never another software product.** For DZZLO it is paper, Tally, WhatsApp, Excel and the munim. Position against "other fuel software" and you are arguing about products your buyer has never evaluated — Dunford calls them phantom competitors.

The canvas then produces a **message hierarchy**: one sentence, one paragraph, one page, in the buyer's own words — a message house, one roof sentence over pillars over proof ([Umbrex — Message House](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/message-house-framework/); [Wynter — messaging hierarchy](https://wynter.com/post/messaging-hierarchy)). What keeps each level honest is Jobs-to-be-Done: the dealer is not buying software, he is hiring something to get a job done ([HBR — Marketing Malpractice](https://hbr.org/2005/12/marketing-malpractice-the-cause-and-the-cure), 2005). The words come out of interviews, not out of you ([_The Mom Test_](https://www.momtestbook.com/)).

## When to use

- **Month 2**, after [[C06-market-map-sizing-and-segments|C06]]'s alternatives table exists — the alternatives table _is_ component 1, so build it there and import it.
- **Before writing the website, the deck, the demo script or the WhatsApp one-pager.** Everything downstream inherits from here; anything written before this is rework.
- **After every ten customer conversations**, re-read the canvas against the verbatim quotes. If the buyer's words and your words have drifted apart, the buyer is right.
- **Immediately when the ICP moves.** Component 4 changes, so components 3 and 5 change, so the whole hierarchy changes.
- **Not for a category-creation exercise.** VSYST's job is to own a segment inside a category that already exists in the buyer's head, not to invent one ([B2BNN — the truth about self-described category creators](https://www.b2bnn.com/2019/02/the-truth-about-all-the-self-described-category-creators-in-tech/); [STFO — own a segment instead](https://www.stfo.io/articles/category-creation/)).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Fill the components in order — 1 to 6 — and never skip back to write the tagline first.** A tagline written before component 4 is a guess about who cares.
2. **Component 1 lists what he does _today_, not who else sells software.** Paper, the accountant, WhatsApp, Excel, and doing nothing. "Doing nothing" is a row; usually the biggest one.
3. **Never sell the unique attribute — sell the value it enables.** "The more unique a feature is, the less likely a potential customer is to understand why it's important" ([Lenny's Newsletter — A guide to advanced B2B positioning](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/a-guide-to-advanced-b2b-positioning)). The 10 PM–6 AM confirmation window is VSYST's most unique attribute and its least self-explanatory; the demo opens on a rate dispute, not a settings screen.
4. **Component 4 must exclude people.** "Who cares a lot" is only useful if it names someone who does not. If your answer is "all fuel dealers", you have not chosen ([[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]] owns the exclusion list).
5. **Choose the market frame that makes your strengths obvious and your weaknesses irrelevant** — then check the frame's price anchor before you commit to it. A frame whose anchor is ₹0 costs you the whole sales conversation.
6. **The trend is layered on last and carefully.** A trend makes the purchase urgent; it must not become the pitch. If the product only makes sense because of the trend, the positioning is borrowed.
7. **Write the buyer's vocabulary down verbatim, in his script.** Not a translation of yours. The words in the message hierarchy must be words you have heard, and you should be able to name the conversation each one came from.
8. **Record at least two rejected framings and why**, and put the rejection in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]. A rejection is a decision about what you will not be measured against; without the written reason, someone re-opens it every quarter.
9. **Different audiences get different words for the same product, and that is allowed.** The buyer-facing name must be a word the buyer already uses for his problem; the investor-facing name must be a word investors already use for a market. Pretending they are the same costs you one audience.
10. **The internal form wins.** When the external form and the internal form disagree, rewrite the external one.

## Template

```
POSITIONING CANVAS — <product> — filled <date> — next review <date>
Filled by: ______   Argued with: ______   Verbatim quotes drawn from ___ interviews

1. COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES  (what he does if we vanish — include "nothing")
     - ...
2. UNIQUE ATTRIBUTES         (only what the alternatives cannot do)
     - ...
3. VALUE THEY ENABLE         ("so that ______" for each attribute above)
     - ...
4. WHO CARES A LOT           (and, explicitly, WHO DOES NOT)
     cares: ...              does not: ...
5. MARKET FRAME              (the category we choose to be evaluated in)
     frame: ...              price anchor of that frame: ₹ ...
6. TREND TO RIDE             (why now — layered on last)
     - ...

REJECTED FRAMINGS   (at least two, with the reason and the date)
     - "..."  rejected because ...            logged in C29 on ____
```

**Message hierarchy** — write all three, then stop improvising.

| Level             | Length      | Where it is used                       | Test it must pass                                  |
| ----------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **One sentence**  | ≤ 40 words  | The doorway. Say it, then stop talking | The dealer's next word is a question, not "achha"  |
| **One paragraph** | ≤ 150 words | The reply to "achha, aur batao"        | Contains his four places and no architecture words |
| **One page**      | 6 blocks    | The WhatsApp-forwardable leave-behind  | Survives being read without you in the room        |

**Vocabulary table** — one row per word the buyer actually uses.

| The trade's word | Roman / English | What it refers to | What it maps to in the product |
| ---------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |

**Audience name table** — one row per audience, one name each.

| Audience | The name to use | Why |
| -------- | --------------- | --- |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative; the long form is [[06-strategy-ii-moats-positioning-and-the-business-model|lesson 06]] §4–§5)

| Component                | Filled for DZZLO OMS                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Alternatives**      | The paper Daily Sales Register; Tally for the books; WhatsApp for orders and rate messages; Excel for the credit book; the munim who reconciles it all; a regional Windows pump-billing binary in a minority of cases; and most often **doing nothing and absorbing the losses**                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **2. Unique attributes** | Per-customer daily rate master with a 10 PM–6 AM confirmation window and auto-lock; delivery bound to a driver OTP; three-tier invoicing (PRODUCT / CASH_REIMBURSE / GST) at the schema level; automatic TCS at ₹50L, split correctly between sale- and payment-crossing; a **shared, non-deletable ledger both sides see**; DIP readings and invoice-side stock in one ledger; litre-based volume discounts; India-only schema validated at the model layer ([[00_OVERVIEW\|Product Overview]]) |
| **3. Value they enable** | Rate disputes end, because the customer confirmed in writing before the truck left. Deliveries stop being deniable. The credit book is current daily, so recovery starts on day 35 not day 95. GST and TCS are right the first time instead of corrected in a notice. The dealer stops needing the munim's memory to know who owes what                                                                                                                                                          |
| **4. Who cares a lot**   | A dealer whose **credit business is material** — bulk diesel and lubes to fleets, factories, hospitals, contractors — with ~15+ active credit customers, ≥1 delivery vehicle, GST registration, and an owner or son who runs the business from a phone. **Who does not:** a pure retail forecourt with no credit book. He needs a POS; selling to him produces a churn statistic                                                                                                                 |
| **5. Market frame**      | **The credit-trade system of record for fuel distribution.** Deliberately not accounting software, deliberately not a ledger app. Complementary to Tally. Price anchor of this frame: a munim at ₹12,000–25,000/month, not ₹0                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **6. Trend to ride**     | The compliance ratchet — e-invoicing thresholds descending, ITC driven off GSTR-2B matching, TCS under 206C(1H) making per-customer year-to-date turnover a legal requirement, e-mandate/UPI Autopay making small recurring collection possible, cheap Android reaching the pump office. **VERIFY LIVE** every threshold with the CA against [CBIC-GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/) before it goes in writing                                                                                      |

**Vocabulary (what a fuel dealer in Raipur actually calls this):**

| Word    | Roman      | Means                                 | Maps to                                  |
| ------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| हिसाब   | _hisaab_   | The reckoning — who owes whom         | The shared ledger, `month_crdrs`         |
| उधारी   | _udhaari_  | Credit given to a customer            | `dealer_custs` limit and balance         |
| पर्ची   | _parchi_   | The delivery slip the driver carries  | The OTP-bound delivery and the invoice   |
| वसूली   | _vasooli_  | Recovery — chasing what is owed       | Aged receivables, reminders, collections |
| रेट     | _rate_     | Today's price for this customer       | `rate_msts` and the confirmation window  |
| शॉर्टेज | _shortage_ | Stock that should be there and is not | DIP, `meter_reads`, `decants`            |

His own phrase for the product is close to **"उधारी और बिल का ऐप"** — the app for credit and bills — or, code-switched the way people actually speak, _"party ka hisaab aur billing ka software."_ His son, who will run the demo with you, says **"credit customer wala app."** In English, to an OMC territory manager, the same product is a _dealer credit and delivery compliance system_; to an investor it is a _vertical OMS and system of record for Indian fuel distribution_. Note what none of the successful Indian SMB products use in their own headlines: an architecture word. [OkCredit](https://okcredit.in)'s _"Udhar likho. Yaad dilao. Wasool karo."_ is three verbs the buyer already does, and it is the sentence to be jealous of; [Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/) names the statutory job because that is what the buyer searches for; [Khatabook](https://khatabook.com/en/) puts Hinglish in the headline.

**One sentence:** _DZZLO is the app where a fuel dealer and his credit customers agree the rate, prove the delivery, raise the GST bill and see the same ledger — so nothing is settled on a chit and nothing waits for month-end._

**One paragraph:** _Today a dealer's credit business lives in four places: the rate is a WhatsApp message, the delivery is a paper parchi, the bill is in Tally and the outstanding is in an Excel sheet the munim keeps. Nothing agrees until month-end, and by then the disputed rate is three weeks old and the customer who owes ₹4 lakh has stopped answering. DZZLO puts both sides on one ledger. The customer confirms tomorrow's rate on his phone before 6 AM; the driver's delivery is verified with an OTP the customer types; the invoice is raised with GST and TCS already correct; the payment is matched to the invoice it settles. Your accountant keeps using Tally — DZZLO feeds it. The dealer pays a monthly subscription per GSTIN; his customers use it free._

**One page — six blocks, in this order:** (1) the line he already believes — _"Aapka sabse bada nuksaan chori nahi hai — hisaab hai"_; (2) the four-places problem, named as his four objects; (3) the loop in five steps with five screenshots; (4) what it costs him not to have it, in his numbers; (5) price ex-GST with the input-credit note, tiered in units he uses — outlets and credit customers; (6) proof at his scale — one named dealer in his district, in his language. Not on the page: the stack, the word "platform", the test count, or any claim containing "AI".

### Two rejected framings

**Rejected — "Tally for petrol pumps."** It tests well in a room, which is the danger. It puts DZZLO in the **accounting-software category**, where the buyer already has a solution he is not unhappy with and a CA professionally invested in it; where the comparison becomes feature-for-feature against a thirty-year-old product with a vastly broader surface; and where DZZLO is framed as a **replacement** when it is a **complement** — DZZLO does not keep the books and its export paths exist so [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com) can keep keeping them. A framing that invites "we already have Tally" as the first objection, when the honest answer is "yes, and you should keep it", has made the sale harder for no gain.

**Rejected — "a digital khata / ledger app for fuel dealers."** Worse, for a reason that has nothing to do with the product: Khatabook taught crores of Indian merchants that a ledger app is **free**, scaling across 4,000+ cities and monetising through lending rather than subscriptions ([KrASIA — Khatabook raises \$60M](https://kr-asia.com/indian-digital-ledger-startup-khatabook-raises-usd-60-million-from-b-capital-group)); OkCredit ran the same play ([TechCrunch — OkCredit Series B](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/12/okcredit-series-b/)). Entering a category whose price anchor is ₹0 is self-inflicted, in a market where ability-to-pay is already the binding constraint ([[company/10_AFFORDABILITY_PROBLEM|affordability problem]]). It also frames a two-sided product as single-player.

Both rejections are dated entries in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]. A third, "fleet fuel management platform", is parked rather than rejected — it re-centres the story on the transport firm, who does not pay today, but it is a real future frame if customer-side fleet plans ship ([[app-store-economics/08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy|subscription strategy]]).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                     | What it looks like                                                            | Fix                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Positioning against phantom competitors** | A comparison grid against fuel-software vendors the dealer has never heard of | Component 1 is paper, Tally, WhatsApp, Excel, nothing                                         |
| **Selling the unique attribute**            | Opening the demo on the rate-master settings screen                           | Open on the dispute the window prevents; the attribute comes second                           |
| **Choosing a frame with a ₹0 anchor**       | "Digital khata for fuel dealers"                                              | Check the frame's price anchor before committing to the frame                                 |
| **Skipping to the tagline**                 | A slogan workshop before component 4 exists                                   | Fill 1–6 in order; the tagline is an output, not an input                                     |
| **Translating instead of listening**        | "Reconciliation" in the one-paragraph version                                 | He says _hisaab_. Use his word and name the interview it came from                            |
| **Trying to create a category**             | A deck that invents a new market name                                         | Own a segment inside a category he already has                                                |
| **One name for every audience**             | "Order Management System" said to a dealer                                    | One name per audience row; OMS stays in the repo and the deck                                 |
| **No rejected framings on record**          | The same framing debate every quarter                                         | Two rejections, with reasons and dates, in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]] |
| **The trend as the pitch**                  | Leading with e-invoicing mandates                                             | The trend makes it urgent; the value makes it worth buying                                    |
