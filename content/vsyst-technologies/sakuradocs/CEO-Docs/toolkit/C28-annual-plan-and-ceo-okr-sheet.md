# C28 — Annual Plan and CEO-Level OKR Sheet

_Toolkit · fills the annual-plan and OKR exercises in [[19-planning-okrs-and-capital-allocation|19 — Planning, OKRs and Capital Allocation]] · Owner: the CEO drafts it; all three directors sign the not-doing list; the board minutes it · Cadence: written in the month before the financial year starts, scored quarterly, never rewritten mid-quarter · Workbook tab: `Annual Plan & OKRs` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

One page and one table. **The page is the annual plan**: the single bet the year is making, the one thing that must be true by 31 March, and the explicit list of what the company is _not_ doing. **The table is the CEO-level OKR sheet**: three to five objectives, each with two to four key results carrying an owner, a baseline, a target, a current value, a computed % done, and a **typed confidence**.

The two halves do different jobs and the plan is the harder one. Objectives and key results are a measurement instrument invented at Intel by Andy Grove as "iMBOs" and carried to Google by John Doerr in 1999 ([Objectives and key results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectives_and_key_results); [Grove, _High Output Management_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Output_Management); [What Matters — what is an OKR](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example)). An objective is what is to be achieved; key results measure _how you will know_ ([What Matters — what are OKRs](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/what-are-okrs)). What OKRs are not is a strategy. A set of well-formed key results attached to the wrong bet will be achieved, on time, to no effect — which is Rumelt's charge against most corporate planning, that a list of goals gets mistaken for a strategy ([Richard Rumelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Rumelt)). The one page above the table is where the bet lives, and if it is wrong the table cannot save you.

The **not-doing list is the load-bearing half of the page.** Jim Collins's argument is that the discipline to discard what does not fit is what separates the exceptional from the merely busy, and that a "stop doing" list is a mechanism for allocating the most precious resource there is — time ([Jim Collins — _Best New Year's Resolution? A "Stop Doing" List_](https://www.jimcollins.com/article_topics/articles/best-new-years.html)). For a company of three people with no outside capital, the not-doing list _is_ the capital-allocation decision. Everything on it is an hour that goes to the bet instead.

**Ownership boundary, stated once so nobody re-litigates it.** The COO course owns the quarterly process: [[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review|T19]] runs the quarterly planning day and the review, and [[T06-okr-planning-sheet|T06]] is the sheet the company's quarterly objectives live on. **C28 owns the CEO's annual bet** — the year's direction, the not-doing list, and the three-to-five objectives the CEO personally carries. The quarterly sheet inherits from this one. When the two disagree, this one is wrong, because the quarter is where reality is.

## When to use

- **First fill: the month before the financial year starts.** India's financial year runs **1 April to 31 March** ([fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year)) — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CA — so drafting happens in March and the plan is minuted at the Q4 board meeting alongside the accounts ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]).
- **Scored at each quarter end**, on the same day the COO runs [[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review|T19]], in an adjacent session so the annual bet and the quarterly plan are argued in the same room.
- **Confidence updated monthly**, in the Friday money-and-strategy block ([[C27-ceo-weekly-template-and-calendar-audit|C27]]). This is a two-minute job and it is the whole early-warning system.
- **Re-opened mid-year only by a logged exception** — never quietly. See rule 8.
- **Not for personal goals, not for the operating scorecard.** Uptime, ticket response and month-end close belong to the COO's [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]. Putting them here drains the CEO's attention into the week and undermines the COO seat.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Write the page before the table, and write the bet as something that could be wrong.** "Grow the business" is not a bet. _"District-level proof and dealer-to-dealer referral beat broad marketing for this buyer"_ is a bet, because a year from now somebody can say it was false.
2. **One thing that must be true by year end.** One. If you cannot compress the year to a single sentence with a number in it, you have not decided what the year is for.
3. **The not-doing list gets three to six entries and all three directors sign it.** Each entry names what is being given up and what would have to happen for it to come back on. An unsigned not-doing list is a preference; a signed one is a decision you can point at in August when somebody is enthusiastic.
4. **Three to five objectives, two to four key results each. Never more.** Google's own guidance is three to five objectives with about three key results each ([Google re:Work — Set goals with OKRs](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs)). Ten objectives is zero objectives.
5. **Every key result is a number with a baseline, a target and a source.** No baseline means no % done and no honest scoring. `% done = (current − baseline) ÷ (target − baseline)` — and for a _lower-is-better_ row, invert it, or it will read as progress while you go backwards.
6. **Do not cascade to individuals until there are individuals.** Cascading OKRs is a coordination technology for organisations large enough that people cannot see each other's work. VSYST has three people in one WhatsApp group. Assign each key result an **owner**, which is a person's name — but do not manufacture a personal OKR set per founder. The trigger to start cascading is the day a manager has direct reports whose work the CEO does not see weekly; write that trigger down now, in the plan, so the change happens deliberately.
7. **Confidence is typed by the owner, not computed by the sheet — that is the entire point.** Christina Wodtke's method is to set a key result you can hold at roughly **5 out of 10 confidence**: a 1 means never going to happen, a 10 means you sandbagged ([Wodtke — _The Art of the OKR_](https://eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr/); [Wodtke — _Harnessing Confidence Ratings for Effective OKRs_](https://eleganthack.com/harnessing-confidence-ratings-for-effective-okrs/)). Type it monthly, before you look at last month's number. **A falling confidence line is the earliest warning this plan has of anything** — it moves weeks before the % done column does, because a person knows a target is slipping long before the arithmetic shows it.
8. **Goals do not change mid-quarter. The plan may — with a logged exception.** The quarter's key results are frozen once set; you get one quarter of being wrong, which is cheap, and the discipline of finishing what you started, which is not. But an annual plan is a hypothesis and hypotheses fail publicly. If the bet is falsified in month five, you change it — by writing a [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] entry that states what changed, the evidence that forced it, persist-or-pivot, and the date, and by minuting it. The exception is a line in section C of the tab. **A plan that quietly becomes a different plan is the single most common way a founding team stops trusting the planning process.**
9. **At least one key result is a survival row, and it never gets deleted.** Runway at year end, or the count of paying firms that makes the company default alive ([Paul Graham — _Default Alive or Default Dead?_, 2015](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html)). CB Insights' read of 431 shut-down startups is that running out of capital is where the story ends, not why it ended — the telling causes are poor product-market fit, timing and unit economics ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/)). Put a row on each side of that sentence.
10. **Score 0–1, not pass/fail, and expect 0.6–0.7 on a good target.** Google grades key results on a 0–1.0 scale precisely so a stretch target scored at 0.7 reads as success, not failure ([re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs)). A team that scores 1.0 across the board set the bar too low.
11. **Never tie compensation to these rows.** The moment a number decides money, it stops measuring the thing and starts measuring the incentive ([Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law)). At three founder-directors this looks academic; write the rule now, before the first hire makes it urgent.

## Template — Part A: the one-page annual plan

```
ANNUAL PLAN — VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd.     FY ________  (1 Apr – 31 Mar)
Drafted ______   Signed: CEO ____  Dir 2 ____  Dir 3 ____   Minuted ______

THE BET (one sentence that could turn out to be false)
   ______________________________________________________________

THE ONE THING THAT MUST BE TRUE BY 31 MARCH (one sentence, with a number)
   ______________________________________________________________

WHY THIS YEAR AND NOT NEXT (two lines: what closes if we wait)
   ______________________________________________________________

WHAT WE ARE DELIBERATELY NOT DOING THIS YEAR
   1. ____________  · given up: ______  · comes back if: ______
   2. ____________  · given up: ______  · comes back if: ______
   3. ____________  · given up: ______  · comes back if: ______

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE BET (name the evidence, before you have it)
   ______________________________________________________________

CASCADE TRIGGER   Individual OKRs start when: ______________________
CAPITAL RULE      Every rupee and every founder-hour above ______ goes
                  to the bet, or it needs a C29 entry.
```

## Worked example — VSYST, FY 2026-27 (illustrative)

**The page.** _The bet:_ district-level proof and dealer-to-dealer referral beat broad marketing for this buyer. _The one thing that must be true by 31 March:_ **twenty paying GSTINs across three Chhattisgarh districts, renewing, with a written and tested sales script.** _Why this year:_ the OMC conversation and the payment-gateway onboarding both mature this year, and a company with zero paying dealers has nothing to say in either room.

_Not doing, signed by all three:_ **(1)** no pan-India push — given up: inbound from Gujarat and Maharashtra; comes back if a district reaches saturation before Q4. **(2)** no second product — given up: the fleet-side app three dealers asked for; comes back if ≥ 8 paying dealers name it as the reason they would expand. **(3)** no OMC pursuit beyond one founder-quarter — given up: optionality; comes back if a written empanelment path exists ([[C26-partnership-evaluation-and-mou-checklist|C26]]). **(4)** no outside capital unless the [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner|C13]] trigger fires.

**The table.** Baselines and targets illustrative; every figure **VERIFY LIVE** against the actual books.

| #      | Objective / Key result                                 | Owner | Base | Target | Current (Q2) | % done | Conf.      | Q     |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----- | ---- | ------ | ------------ | ------ | ---------- | ----- |
| **O1** | **Prove the district motion**                          |       |      |        |              |        |            | Q1–Q4 |
| KR     | Paying GSTINs _(billed and collected, not signed)_     | CEO   | 0    | 20     | 6            | 30%    | **0.45 ↓** | Q4    |
| KR     | Districts with ≥ 1 paying dealer                       | CEO   | 0    | 3      | 2            | 67%    | 0.65 →     | Q4    |
| KR     | Written script tested on ≥ 20 conversations            | CEO   | 0    | 20     | 17           | 85%    | 0.80 ↑     | Q2    |
| **O2** | **Make onboarding survive without a founder**          |       |      |        |              |        |            | Q2–Q4 |
| KR     | Median days signup → first invoice _(lower is better)_ | COO   | 21   | 7      | 15           | 43%    | 0.50 →     | Q3    |
| KR     | Dealers onboarded with no founder on site              | COO   | 0    | 3      | 0            | 0%     | **0.35 ↓** | Q4    |
| **O3** | **Stay default alive**                                 |       |      |        |              |        |            | Q1–Q4 |
| KR     | Runway at year end (months)                            | CEO   | 14   | 12     | 13           | —      | 0.60 →     | Q4    |
| KR     | Gross margin %                                         | CEO   | 55%  | 70%    | 61%          | 40%    | 0.55 →     | Q4    |
| **O4** | **Earn the right to be trusted with a dealer's money** |       |      |        |              |        |            | Q1–Q4 |
| KR     | Money-affecting incidents _(one is too many)_          | CEO   | 1    | 0      | 0            | 100%   | 0.60 →     | Q4    |
| KR     | Board meetings held on schedule with signed minutes    | CEO   | 0    | 4      | 2            | 50%    | 0.85 ↑     | Q4    |

**Read the confidence column before the % done column.** Two rows are falling: paying GSTINs (0.60 → 0.45) and founder-free onboarding (0.55 → 0.35). Six GSTINs at the halfway mark against a target of twenty is not obviously fatal on the arithmetic — but the owner's confidence says the run rate is not going to double, and the owner knows something the spreadsheet does not. That is the signal, in Q2, four months before the % done column would have told you the same thing in a tone you could no longer do anything about.

**And notice what the two falling rows have in common:** both are versions of _the company cannot yet acquire or activate a dealer without the CEO in the room._ That is not two problems, it is one, and it is the same finding the pre-mortem produced independently in [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession|C30]]. When two instruments built on different data agree, stop arguing and act.

**The Q2 review row, written honestly:** _What we said_ — 10 GSTINs by Q2. _What happened_ — 6, all in two districts, five of them closed personally by the CEO. _Score_ — 0.6. _Persist / adjust / kill_ — **persist on the bet, adjust the coherent action**: sell to an association meeting rather than pump by pump ([[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]]). _C29 ID_ — DJ-014. The bet is not falsified; the channel inside it is. **That distinction is what a plan is for**, and it is only available to a company that wrote the bet down separately from the goals.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                                  | What it looks like                                                      | Fix                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A plan that is only a table**                          | Twelve key results, no stated bet                                       | Rule 1. The page comes first; the table serves it                                                             |
| **No not-doing list**                                    | Everything is "a priority this year"                                    | Rule 3. Three to six entries, three signatures, each with a comeback condition                                |
| **Ten objectives**                                       | Every function represented, none advanced                               | Rule 4. Three to five. What is left out is the decision                                                       |
| **Key results with no baseline**                         | "Increase revenue significantly"                                        | Rule 5. Baseline, target, source, or the row is not a key result                                              |
| **Lower-is-better rows read as progress**                | Days-to-first-invoice rises and % done rises with it                    | Rule 5. Invert the formula and mark the row                                                                   |
| **Cascading to three people**                            | Each founder given a personal OKR set in month one                      | Rule 6. Owners yes, cascades no. Write the trigger instead                                                    |
| **Computing confidence from % done**                     | The two columns move together and say the same thing                    | Rule 7. Typed by the owner, monthly, before looking at the number. A computed confidence column is decoration |
| **Ignoring a falling confidence line**                   | "It's still early" for two consecutive months                           | Two consecutive falls is an agenda item, not a mood                                                           |
| **Quietly rewriting the plan**                           | September's plan does not match April's and nobody remembers the change | Rule 8. A C29 entry and a minute, or the change did not happen                                                |
| **Deleting the survival row when it gets uncomfortable** | Runway drops off the sheet in Q3                                        | Rule 9. It is the one row that never gets deleted                                                             |
| **Scoring 1.0 across the board**                         | Everything green, nothing learned                                       | Rule 10. 0.6–0.7 on a real target. All-green means the targets were too small                                 |
| **Duplicating the COO's scorecard**                      | Uptime and ticket SLA appear here                                       | [[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]] owns those. This sheet owns the year's bet                                         |
