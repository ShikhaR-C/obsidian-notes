# C02 — Founders' Agreement and Vesting Checklist

_Toolkit · fills exercise 10.2 in [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|03 — The CEO's Core Value and the Founder Contracts]] · Owner: the CEO drafts the brief, all three founders fill it in one sitting, the CS and a startup lawyer draft the instrument · Cadence: once, before you need it; re-opened at every cap-table event and reviewed annually · Workbook tab: the resulting shareholding goes on `Cap Table & Dilution` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

A founders' agreement is the cheapest document in a company's life and the one that prevents the most damage: a private contract between founders covering equity, roles, time, vesting, IP and exit, signed before it is needed, binding only the signatories, filed with nobody ([SimplyBiz — 15 clauses every founders' agreement should have](https://simplybiz.in/15-clauses-every-founders-agreement-should-have/)).

**This template is not the agreement.** It is the checklist you fill _before_ briefing a professional, so the CS and the lawyer draft your decisions instead of making them for you at hourly rates. Each row has four columns — the clause, why it exists, what happens without it, who drafts it. Fill the first three yourselves; the fourth says who to hand it to.

Two facts govern everything here. **First: American startup advice does not map onto an Indian Pvt Ltd.** Restricted stock with a repurchase right, 83(b) elections, a Delaware charter — none exist here. Where the principle is universal and the instrument is not, this checklist says so and flags **VERIFY LIVE**. **Second: a private contract binds who signed it; the Articles bind everyone.** Transfer restrictions living only in the agreement can be defeated by a shareholder who never signed, and where the two conflict the **AoA prevails** ([Sterling & Partners](https://sterlingpartners.law/shareholder-agreement-india-startup-sha-clauses/); [Vakilsearch](https://vakilsearch.com/article/drag-along-and-tag-along-rights/)). So: decide here, draft in the agreement, **mirror in the AoA** — one exercise, done by the CS.

## When to use

- **Now, at VSYST** — three family founder-directors, incorporated, no agreement. The highest-value hour on this toolkit list, and it gets harder every month it is deferred.
- **Before incorporation** for any future venture: written while everyone is optimistic and nothing is worth fighting over. That is exactly why it works.
- **Whenever the facts move**: a founder goes part-time, a fourth person joins, an ESOP pool is carved, an investor's SHA must be reconciled with this one.
- **Before diligence** by an investor, an OMC, a GSP or a bank — the absence of this document is itself a finding — and **annually**, alongside [[C18-director-duties-and-governance-checklist|C18]].

## How to fill (rules)

1. **All three founders, one sitting, sixty minutes, before anyone drafts anything.** Disagreements found in this hour are cheap; the same ones found in year four are litigation.
2. **Write down _why_ the split is what it is, and date it.** One paragraph naming each founder's contribution — capital, domain, code, relationships, full-time-ness. Hellmann and Wasserman found equal splitting associated with **lower pre-money valuations at first financing**, the effect stronger in teams that decided quickly, with roughly **10% of firm equity** at stake ([NBER WP 16922](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16922/w16922.pdf)); "quick handshake" splits have a shelf life ([_The Founder's Dilemmas_](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas)) — **VERIFY LIVE** before quoting those numbers.
3. **Vesting applies to everyone on identical terms, including the CEO.** Symmetry is what makes it agreeable. The argument for a family co-founder who hears "vesting" as "you don't trust me": it protects the **remaining** founders first — without it "one founder could just leave the company and keep his or her shares while the other founders are the ones working hard" ([WilmerHale Launch](https://launch.wilmerhale.com/explore/formation/founders/vesting-restrictions-on-shares-held-by-the-founders)).
4. **Two clauses must be right the first time: IP assignment and vesting.** Everything else can be amended; these two cannot be retrofitted once a dispute starts. A signed 80% document beats a perfect unsigned one — with that exception.
5. **Never copy a non-compete from a US template.** §27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 voids agreements restraining anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business, with one exception for the sale of goodwill ([full text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1431516/)).
6. **Name who determines "cause" — and make it someone who does not benefit.** At a three-person family board, a board-only determination is two relatives voting on a third. Courts defer more to a determination by an independent auditor or tribunal than by a board that benefits from the clawback ([Treelife — the BharatPe/Grover SHA saga](https://treelife.in/legal/bharatpe-ashneer-grover-sha-saga/)).
7. **Every professional-dependent row gets VERIFY LIVE and a name** — a person, not "the lawyer". The output is a filled checklist _plus_ a one-page brief listing exactly what you need drafted.
8. **Do not rely on a post-departure non-compete.** Rely on the stack that works in India: IP assignment, confidentiality, a narrow non-solicit, and reverse vesting with transfer restrictions.

### Vesting — and how it is actually structured in India

**The principle** is universal: founders earn equity by staying and working, and unearned shares go back on early departure. **The standard shape** in Indian practice is **three to five years** with a **one-year cliff** (nothing in year one, then typically **25–33%** at once), monthly or quarterly thereafter, with the caution that "any cliff longer than 1 year is aggressive" ([Treelife — founder vesting in a shareholders' agreement](https://treelife.in/legal/founder-vesting-in-a-shareholders-agreement/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**.

**The US instrument does not exist here.** The obvious Indian substitute, a company buy-back under **§68**, is a poor tool for routine vesting: capped at **25% or less of the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves**, fundable only from free reserves, securities premium or a fresh issue (never borrowings), and **no further offer within one year** of the previous one's closure ([§68](https://ca2013.com/power-of-company-to-purchase-its-own-securities/); [EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/buy-back-of-shares-companies-act)). A pre-revenue company has no free reserves — there is nothing to buy back _with_. And the tax treatment of buy-back proceeds in the shareholder's hands changed materially with the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/08/buy-back-of-shares-will-recent-changes-in-the-tax-laws-end-the-party/)) — **VERIFY LIVE with your CA.**

**So Indian practice uses contractual reverse vesting.** All shares are issued up front and subjected to a reverse-vesting schedule under which, on a defined leaver event, the unvested portion must be **transferred** — not bought back — "at a nominal price or at a predetermined percent of the fair market value" ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/founder-vesting-in-a-shareholders-agreement/)). The transferee is typically the continuing founders, a nominee, a trust, or the ESOP pool. That needs machinery — **all VERIFY LIVE, none of it DIY**:

- [ ] A **call option** for the company or continuing founders, exercisable on defined leaver events, with a stated price formula.
- [ ] **Transfer restrictions mirrored in the AoA**, not only in the agreement.
- [ ] Executed but **undated SH-4 transfer forms** and/or a power of attorney in escrow, so a refusal to sign cannot stall the transfer.
- [ ] **Specific performance** and a named, affordable arbitration seat.
- [ ] A **valuation basis** for the transfer price — ask the CA about below-FMV consequences _in both directions_ before writing "nominal price". BharatPe's dispute included a separate fight because transfers were "priced at face value rather than fair market value" ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/bharatpe-ashneer-grover-sha-saga/)).
- [ ] **Acceleration**: ask for **double-trigger** (change of control _plus_ termination without cause in a window) as the default.
- [ ] Instrument boundaries: **sweat equity under §54**, fresh issues and ESOPs under **§62 / §62(1)(b)** ([§54](https://ca2013.com/issue-of-sweat-equity-shares/); [EquityList — Section 62](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/section-62-companies-act)) — **VERIFY LIVE**.

### Non-compete under §27 — the row everyone gets wrong

> _"Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void."_ — [Indian Contract Act, 1872, §27](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1431516/)

| Restraint                                                             | Enforceable in India?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **During** engagement — exclusivity, no moonlighting for a competitor | **Generally yes** — a condition of exclusive service rather than a restraint of trade ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/enforceability-of-non-compete-clauses-in-india/))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **After** engagement ends                                             | **Largely void, however narrow or short.** _Superintendence Company of India v. Krishan Murgai_: "a service covenant extended beyond the termination of the service is void", rejecting the English reasonableness test ([Supreme Court, 9 May 1980](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1186410/)); reaffirmed in **Varun Tyagi v. Daffodil Software** (Delhi HC, 2025), striking a bundled non-compete/non-solicit that "extended beyond the term of employment" ([Mondaq, 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/india/employee-rights-labour-relations/1643996/section-27-of-the-indian-contract-act-varun-tyagi-judgment-and-delhi-high-courts-ruling-on-non-compete-clauses)) |
| **Founder restraint on a genuine sale of shares/goodwill**            | **Better prospects** under Exception 1, read strictly and tested for proportionality; a restraint suppressing competition while promoters keep managerial control remains void ([Bhatt & Joshi Associates, 2026](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/non-compete-clauses-in-india-2026-enforceability-in-employment-contracts-vs-shareholder-agreements/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

One caution on reach: in _Percept D'Mark (India) v. Zaheer Khan_ the Supreme Court struck a ROFR clause binding a person's **future personal-service contracts after the agreement expired** as "clearly hit by Section 27" ([Supreme Court, 22 March 2006](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/571375/)). That restrains a _person_, not a share transfer between shareholders — different ground, and the reason a lawyer drafts this rather than you copying one.

### Family-specific clauses

Four additions belonging in a short family charter beside the agreement. PwC's 12th Family Business Survey found Indian family businesses high on ambition and low on structure — **36% with no clear succession plan (28% globally), 52% reporting "old guard" resistance to leadership transition (29% globally), 30% with family-only boards** ([Forbes India, Feb 2026](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/indian-family-businesses-high-on-ambition-low-on-governance/2991239/1); [PwC India](https://www.pwc.in/services/entrepreneurial-and-private-business/pwcs-12th-family-business-survey.html)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Notably 91% say they have clearly defined family values. Values are not the gap. Documents are.

- [ ] **Who may hire a relative, and who may fire one** — written before any relative is hired.
- [ ] **Salary set by role band, not family seniority** — the band, not the number, goes in the charter.
- [ ] **Where business is discussed**: minuted meetings, not weddings. One agreed sentence is enough to invoke later.
- [ ] **A named outsider tiebreaker.** Fifteen minutes today; impossible in eighteen months.

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

Three family founder-directors, incorporated, bootstrapped, no agreement yet. **Every term below is illustrative — a starting position for the lawyer, not advice.**

| Item                | Illustrative VSYST position                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Split               | 33.3% each, with a dated rationale paragraph naming capital, fuel-distribution domain, the code, and the OMC relationships                                           |
| Vesting             | 4 years, 1-year cliff at 25%, monthly thereafter, **identical for all three including the CEO**, as contractual reverse vesting with a call option — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| Cause determination | The company's CA firm, named in the agreement — not the board                                                                                                        |
| IP schedule         | DZZLO API and app repos, Figma files, the Play Store and App Store developer accounts, the domains, the Quartz vault — **registrant check done and recorded**        |
| Non-compete         | Drafted narrowly, with a file note that it is unlikely to survive post-departure; protection carried by IP + confidentiality + non-solicit + reverse vesting         |
| Transfers           | ROFR → tag-along → drag-along at 75%, all mirrored in the AoA by the CS                                                                                              |
| Deadlock            | Named individual first, then mediation, then arbitration seated in Raipur                                                                                            |
| Time commitment     | ≥ 40 hours/week for the CEO and the domain director; the third director's other commitments listed by name                                                           |

**Output:** a filled C02, a one-page brief for the CS and lawyer listing exactly what to draft, and a [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] entry recording the equity decision and its rationale on the day it was made.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until it matters.** There is no version of this document that is easier to write later.
- **Copying a US template.** Restricted stock, 83(b), Delaware — none of it applies.
- **Drafting into the agreement and not the AoA.** Half your protections become unenforceable against the one person they were written for.
- **Letting the board determine cause.** Two relatives voting on a third is not a determination anyone will defer to.
- **Bundling non-solicit with non-compete.** _Varun Tyagi_ struck the pair down together.
- **Treating "nominal price" as free.** Ask the CA about below-FMV transfers in both directions first.
- **Skipping the "stops working but stays" clause** because it is awkward — the most likely event here, and the one with no natural trigger.
- **Signing without reading the IP schedule.** If the Play Store account is in a personal name, the schedule is a lie until it is transferred.
- **Believing an equal split removes the conversation.** It defers it. Rule 2 is what turns the default into a decision.
