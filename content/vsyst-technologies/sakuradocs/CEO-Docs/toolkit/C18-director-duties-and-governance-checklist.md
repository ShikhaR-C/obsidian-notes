# C18 — Director Duties and Governance Checklist

_Toolkit · fills the director-duty work in [[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties|09 — Board, Governance and the Director's Duties]] §3 and §6 · Owner: **each director, individually** — this is not a company checklist, it is a personal one, and each of the three directors keeps their own copy · Cadence: read at the first board meeting of every financial year; ticked quarterly; the filing block reviewed each April with the CS · Workbook tab: dates go on `Board Calendar` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

**The COO course owns the company's compliance calendar.** Dates, chasing the CS, evidence, the register — that machine lives in [[T16-compliance-calendar|COO T16]] and [[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|COO 13]], and nothing here replaces it.

**This checklist is the other thing: what a *director personally* owes, declares, signs and can be personally penalised for.** The distinction matters because the two are not the same liability. If the company files AOC-4 late, the company pays a fee; if the pattern continues, the *director* is disqualified from every board they sit on. If the company forgets DIR-3 KYC, nothing happens to the company; the director's DIN is deactivated and they cannot act as a director anywhere until it is fixed. You cannot delegate a personal disqualification to a CS, and no CS can sign your MBP-1 for you.

Part A is [§166](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors/) — seven sentences that are the most important page of Indian company law a founder-CEO will ever read, restated here as a **self-test** rather than a summary, because abstract duties change nothing and a question you must answer in writing changes behaviour. Part B is the recurring trail: the disclosures, registers, meetings and filings that prove the duties were performed. Lesson 09 §3 explains *why* each of these exists; this template is what you actually tick.

**Every statutory element on this page is VERIFY LIVE — confirm with the Company Secretary, and confirm the tax-side rows with the CA.** Penalties across the Act were recut by the Companies (Amendment) Acts of 2019 and 2020, the small-company thresholds moved with effect from 1 December 2025 ([Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/mca-notifies-revised-small-company-thresholds/)), and MCA due dates get extended mid-year ([Razorpay Rize — ROC compliance calendar](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/roc-compliance-calendar/)).

## When to use

- **At the first board meeting of every financial year**, as a standing agenda item: MBP-1 and DIR-8 tabled from all three directors, this checklist re-read, the year's dates confirmed with the CS ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]).
- **Quarterly**, as a five-minute tick before the board meeting.
- **The week any related-party arrangement is contemplated** — a relative's firm as a vendor, a family lease, a relative's employment, the family pump as a tenant. Before it takes effect, not after.
- **Before any diligence** — an investor, an OMC empanelment, a GSP agreement, a bank facility. All of them read filings status.
- **The day a new director is appointed**, as the document they are handed.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One copy per director, with their name on it.** A shared company file defeats the purpose. The duties are personal and so are the penalties.
2. **Answer Part A in writing, in your own words, once a year.** Not ticks — sentences. A duty you can only tick is a duty you have not thought about.
3. **Write your relatives out by name, not by category.** "Relative" is a defined term — under §2(77) read with Rule 4 of the Companies (Specification of Definitions Details) Rules 2014 it covers HUF members, spouse, father and step-father, mother and step-mother, son and step-son, son's wife, daughter, daughter's husband, brother and step-brother, sister and step-sister ([CAIRR — Rule 4](https://ca2013.com/rule-4-the-companies-specification-of-definitions-details-rules-2014/)) — and it feeds the "related party" definition in §2(76) ([CAIRR — definitions](https://ca2013.com/definitions/)). In a company founded by three relatives, that is most of your address book. **VERIFY LIVE the current clause list with the CS.**
4. **The house rule, and it is short: if the counterparty is on your relatives list or a firm they control, it goes on the board agenda before it goes into effect.** The paperwork cost of doing it correctly is one paragraph in the minutes.
5. **Never assume you are under a threshold.** [§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/) thresholds in Rule 15(3) are percentages of turnover and net worth — indicatively goods/materials above 25% of annual turnover, property above 10% of net worth, services above 10% of net worth, an office or place of profit above ₹2.5 lakh monthly remuneration ([CAIRR — §188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/)) — **VERIFY LIVE.** A pre-revenue company with a small net worth crosses percentage thresholds *faster*, not slower. A ₹4 lakh annual rent can be a large percentage of net worth.
6. **Money in and money out are different animals.** Director → company is a director loan: document it and make sure it is reported in DPT-3. Company → director is [§185](https://ca2013.com/185-loan-to-directors-etc/) territory and the starting position is prohibition; the private-company exemption depends on conditions that evaporate the day a body corporate joins your cap table. **Never let an untitled transfer be reclassified by the CA at year end** — that is where §185 problems are born.
7. **Keep your own DSC and file your own forms.** DSC tokens parked at the CS's office is a control failure that a director signs for. Non-negotiable.
8. **Filing hygiene buys you the reliefs you actually use.** The private-company modification that lets an interested director participate in a meeting after disclosing interest is available only to a private company **that has not defaulted in filing its financial statements or annual returns** ([CAIRR — §184](https://ca2013.com/184-disclosure-of-interest-by-director/)). Let the returns slip and you lose the relief at exactly the moment you need it.
9. **If any annual filing is overdue, treat it as this week's work, not next quarter's.** MCA has run a compounding/settlement window charging a fraction of the additional fee on overdue annual filings ([CompanyJi — CCFS](https://companyji.com/ccfs-scheme-2026-extended/)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS whether a window is open.**

## Part A — the seven §166 duties, as a personal self-test

Answer each in writing once a year. The middle column is the question; the right-hand column is the VSYST situation that would breach it, written so you recognise it when it happens rather than after.

| # | §166 duty, plain English | Ask yourself, in writing | A VSYST situation that breaches it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **166(1) — Act in accordance with the company's articles.** Your own AoA binds you; not having read it is not a defence | *When did I last read the AoA, and which of its limits could I breach without noticing?* Name the borrowing limit and the reserved matters from memory, then check | A term loan or overdraft taken because the bank was ready, above the AoA's borrowing limit, with the resolution "done later". The facility is drawn before the board has authorised it |
| **2** | **166(2) — Act in good faith to promote the objects of the company, for the benefit of members as a whole, and in the best interests of the company, its employees, shareholders, the community and the environment** | *Name one decision in the last quarter where the interest of the company and the interest of a founder or the family diverged. What did I do?* If you cannot name one, you are not looking | Pricing the family pump's DZZLO tenancy at zero forever because it would be awkward to invoice a relative — a decision that helps a shareholder's other business and costs the company both revenue and a reference customer it can talk about |
| **3** | **166(3) — Exercise duties with due and reasonable care, skill and diligence, and exercise independent judgement** | *Did I read the pack before the last board meeting? Did I ask one question the CEO did not want? Did I sign anything I had not read?* | Signing the annual financial statements because "the CA prepared it and the CEO said it's fine". On a three-relative board this is the default behaviour unless the mechanisms in [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes\|C17]] make it awkward |
| **4** | **166(4) — Do not involve yourself in a situation in which you may have a direct or indirect interest that conflicts, or *possibly may conflict*, with the company's interest** | *List every entity I or my relatives have an interest in that could transact with VSYST. Is the list on my MBP-1 and current?* The test is the position, not the profit | A relative's firm becomes the SMS/OTP gateway or the printing vendor, chosen without a comparison, at a price nobody benchmarked. No fraud, no theft, and a live conflict the moment the price is questioned in diligence |
| **5** | **166(5) — Do not achieve or attempt to achieve any undue gain or advantage to yourself, your relatives, partners or associates. If found guilty, you are liable to pay an amount equal to that gain to the company** | *Is any company money paying for something a founder or a relative personally consumes? Is any related-party price above market?* | A vehicle or a phone connection on the company's books used mainly for the family's other business; registered-office rent to a family entity set above market with no board approval. The repayment obligation attaches to the **director**, not the company |
| **6** | **166(6) — Do not assign your office. Any assignment is void** | *Have I ever let someone else attend, vote or sign in my place?* | "I'll be travelling for three months — my cousin will sit in the board meetings and sign for me." Void, and the meetings may fail quorum. An alternate director is a different, formal mechanism the CS must set up |
| **7** | **166(7) — Contravention carries a fine on the director personally, stated in the Act as not less than ₹1,00,000 extending to ₹5,00,000** — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**, the section has been amended | *Do I understand that this lands on me, not on the company, and that there is no small-company discount in this sub-section?* | — |

**One extra row that is not in §166 and belongs on a personal checklist anyway:** [§164](https://ca2013.com/164-disqualifications-for-appointment-of-director) disqualification. Under §164(2), continued failure to file financial statements or annual returns disqualifies a director for a period measured in years — and conviction of a related-party offence under §188 in the preceding five years is a separate disqualification. **VERIFY LIVE the current text and periods with the CS.** This is the row that turns a bookkeeping lapse into a personal bar from every board you sit on.

## Part B — the recurring governance trail

### B1. Disclosures and registers (the director's own signature)

| Item | What it is | When | Who does it | What happens if missed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MBP-1 — disclosure of interest** | Each director discloses their concern or interest in any company, body corporate, firm or association | At the **first board meeting they participate in**, at the **first board meeting of every financial year**, and at the **first board meeting after any change** in interests ([§184](https://ca2013.com/184-disclosure-of-interest-by-director/)) | **The director signs their own.** The CS files it in the binder and reflects it in the register | Contravention of §184 — **VERIFY LIVE**, the 2020 amendment converted several such provisions into monetary penalties |
| **Interest in a specific contract** | Disclosure before the item is put; at a private company the interested director **may participate after disclosing** (GSR 464(E), 5 June 2015), conditional on clean filings | Per contract | The director, on the record, in the minutes | Loss of the relief; the transaction's approval questioned |
| **DIR-8 — declaration of non-disqualification** | Each director informs the company that they are not disqualified under §164 | Before appointment or re-appointment; in practice tabled at the first board meeting of each FY ([Rule 14, Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules 2014](https://ca2013.com/rule-14-companies-appointment-and-qualification-of-directors-rules2014/)) | **The director signs their own** | The company's own DIR-9 obligation is triggered on a disqualification event — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **Register of contracts in which directors are interested — MBP-4** | Chronological register of §188 contracts and other interested arrangements, entered **at once**, authenticated | Continuously | CS maintains; **the CEO's non-delegable job is to notice the transaction and report it** | Penalty on a defaulting director, stated as ₹25,000 ([§189](https://ca2013.com/189-register-of-contracts-or-arrangements-in-which-directors-are-interested/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Note the carve-outs: aggregate value not exceeding ₹5 lakh in a year, and a 2%-or-less interest in a body corporate |
| **Related-party approvals** | Board consent **by resolution at a meeting** — never by circulation; shareholder approval above the Rule 15(3) limits; particulars disclosed in the Board's Report | Before the arrangement takes effect | Board; CS drafts | An unapproved arrangement is **voidable at the option of the Board** and the director concerned must indemnify the company for loss ([§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **Your own relatives list** | Names, not categories, plus every firm they control | Written once, reviewed annually | Each director | Nothing statutory — and it is the single thing that makes every row above workable |

### B2. Meetings and minutes

| Item | Rule | VSYST | Miss it and… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Board meeting frequency** | Default: at least four a year, not more than 120 days between two; a small company, start-up private company, OPC or dormant company may hold **one meeting in each half of a calendar year with a gap of not less than 90 days** ([§173](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/)) | VSYST is comfortably inside the small-company thresholds as revised effective 1 December 2025 ([Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/mca-notifies-revised-small-company-thresholds/)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS.** **Plan four anyway** | The relaxation is a floor, not a target; two meetings a year guarantees a status report instead of a decision forum |
| **Notice** | Not less than **seven days in writing** to every director at their registered address | Notice + agenda + pack as one email, seven clear days ahead | ₹25,000 on the officer in default for defective notice ([§173(4)](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **Quorum** | One-third of total strength or **two directors, whichever is higher** ([§174](https://ca2013.com/174-quorum-for-meetings-of-board/)) | Three directors → quorum is two. **Two of the three can validly transact everything.** Sit with that | An inquorate meeting decides nothing |
| **Resolutions by circulation** | Must be circulated to all directors; **noted at a subsequent board meeting**; any not-less-than-one-third of directors can require a meeting instead ([§175](https://ca2013.com/175-passing-of-resolution-by-circulation/)) | One director out of three can force any item into a meeting | Unnoted circulations are a standard diligence finding |
| **Minutes** | Prepared, signed and entered in the minute book within **30 days**; names of directors present and **names of dissenting directors** recorded; secretarial standards observed ([§118](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/); [SS-1, revised effective 1 April 2024](https://www.icsi.edu/ssb/home/)) | Draft in 15 days, comments in 7, entered in 30 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/section-118-of-companies-act-2013-minutes-board-meeting.html)) | Penalty on the company and on every officer in default; **tampering carries imprisonment** — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **AGM** | First AGM within 9 months of the first FY close; thereafter within 6 months of FY end and not more than 15 months apart ([§96](https://ca2013.com/annual-general-meeting/)) | CS convenes; directors attend and adopt accounts | Penalties, and the AOC-4/MGT-7A clocks start regardless |

### B3. The annual filings a director signs or tables

**The CS files these. The COO tracks them. The director's obligation is narrower and non-transferable: know what you personally sign, know the consequence of each miss, and be able to answer "are your filings current?" without calling anyone.** Dates below assume a 31 March year end and an AGM on 30 September — **every date, fee and penalty is VERIFY LIVE with the CS each April** ([ClearTax Advisors — MCA compliance for a private limited company](https://cleartaxadvisors.in/mca-compliance-for-private-limited-company-2026/)).

| Filing | What / when (typical) | Who files | What the director personally does | Miss it and… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Audited financial statements + Board's Report** | Board approves, ~Jul–Aug | CA audits; board approves | **Signs the financial statements and the Board's Report** ([§134](https://ca2013.com/134-financial-statement-boards-report-etc/)). Since 14 July 2025 the report carries POSH complaint counts and a Maternity Benefit Act statement regardless of company size ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-mandates-workplace-harassment-maternity-disclosures-in-board-reports-38025.html/)) | §134(8) penalties on the company and on the officer in default — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **ADT-1** — auditor appointment | Within **15 days** of the AGM | CS | — | Late fee; the auditor's standing questioned in diligence |
| **AOC-4** — financial statements | Within **30 days** of the AGM | CS | Signs with own DSC | **₹100/day, no cap**; feeds the §164(2) disqualification clock |
| **MGT-7A** — abridged annual return (small company) | Within **60 days** of the AGM | CS | For a small company the return may be signed by the CS or, where there is none, **by a director** ([§92](https://ca2013.com/annual-return/)) | **₹100/day, no cap**; same clock |
| **DIR-3 KYC (Web)** | Now once every three financial years, by **30 June**; forms merged into DIR-3 KYC Web effective 31 March 2026 ([CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/dir-3-kyc-web-2026-complete-guide); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/dir-3-kyc-new-rules/)) | **Each director individually** | **Files their own, with their own DSC** | **DIN deactivated** — you cannot act as a director of *any* company until reactivated, plus a reactivation fee. **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **DPT-3** — return of deposits and exempted deposits, **including director loans** | **30 June**; extended to 31 July for FY 2025-26 by MCA circular ([IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/dpt-3-filing/due-date-extended-fy-2025-26)) | CS / CA | **Confirms their own director-loan balance is right** before it is filed | Penalties under the deposit rules — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **MSME-1** — half-yearly return of dues to micro and small suppliers outstanding beyond 45 days | **30 April** and **31 October** | CS | Knows whether any vendor is unpaid past the limit | Indicatively ₹20,000 plus a daily amount up to a cap ([ClearTax Advisors](https://cleartaxadvisors.in/mca-compliance-for-private-limited-company-2026/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** |
| **MGT-14** | Within 30 days of certain resolutions — e.g. adopting an ESOP scheme | CS | — | Late fee; the resolution's validity questioned in diligence |
| **DIR-12 / INC-22** — director change, registered-office change | Within **30 days** of the event | CS | Signs their own consent form on appointment | Daily fee; stale MCA master data breaks bank mandates and empanelments |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**One director's filled sheet, first board meeting of the financial year. All entries illustrative.**

- **Part A, duty 4, written answer:** *"Entities my relatives control that could transact with VSYST: \<family petrol-pump firm\> (registered office premises; also a prospective DZZLO tenant); \<brother-in-law's printing firm\> (has quoted for invoice stationery); \<cousin's transport firm\> (a prospective B2B customer of the pump, riding free on the tenant — no VSYST contract). All three are on my MBP-1 dated \<date\>. The printing quote is **not** the lowest of three; I have asked the COO seat to take two more quotes before it goes on the board agenda."*
- **Part A, duty 2, written answer:** *"Divergence this quarter: the family pump wants a bespoke shift-handover screen. No other tenant has asked for it. I estimated six engineering weeks. I voted against, and asked for the dissent to be minuted so the reason survives."*
- **Relatives list:** written out by name — eleven people and four firms. Reviewed each April.
- **Ticked this quarter:** MBP-1 signed and tabled ✔ · DIR-8 signed and tabled ✔ · DIR-3 KYC — due 30 June, DSC valid to \<date\> ✔ · circulated resolution of \<date\> noted in the minutes ✔ · MBP-4 extract read and two entries added ✔
- **Open, with a date:** *the registered-office occupancy is undocumented. Action: leave-and-licence at a benchmarked rate, board resolution and MBP-4 entry at the next meeting, \<date\>. Owner: CEO with the CS.*
- **The honest line at the bottom:** *"Did I read the board pack before the last meeting? No — I read it in the meeting. That is a 166(3) problem and it is mine."*

That last line is the reason the checklist is answered in sentences rather than ticks.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Treating this as the company's compliance calendar** | Duplicating [[T16-compliance-calendar\|COO T16]] here | This is the **personal** sheet. The company's machine stays with the COO seat |
| **"The CS handles it"** | The director cannot say whether filings are current | Rules 7 and 9. You sign; you should be able to answer in one sentence |
| **Categories instead of names** | "Relatives" as a heading, no list | Rule 3. Names and firms, written out, reviewed annually |
| **Approving the related-party item after the fact** | Rent paid for two years, resolution passed on the CA's prompt at audit | Rule 4. On the agenda **before** it takes effect |
| **"We're too small to cross a threshold"** | A ₹4 lakh rent against a small net worth | Rule 5. Percentage thresholds are crossed faster by small companies |
| **Untitled transfers between founder and company** | The CA reclassifies at year end | Rule 6. Salary, reimbursement against a bill, or a documented director loan — nothing else |
| **DSC parked with the CS's office** | Convenience during filing season | Rule 7. Directors sign their own forms |
| **Ticking Part A** | Seven ticks in eleven seconds | Rule 2. Sentences, once a year, in your own words |
| **Letting an annual filing sit overdue** | "We'll clean it up before the raise" | Rule 8 and 9. It costs you the private-company reliefs and starts the §164(2) clock |
| **No dissent ever recorded** | Family board, false consensus | The mechanism is in [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes\|C17]]; the duty it discharges is 166(3) |
