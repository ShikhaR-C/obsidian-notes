# C09 — ICP and Customer-Discovery Interview Guide

_Toolkit · fills the ICP and interview exercises in [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|07 — Customers, Markets and Founder-Led Sales]] §§3–5 · Owner: CEO owns the ICP and runs the interviews personally; the domain-expert director scores prospects and takes the notes · Cadence: ICP written in Month 2 and re-scored against the win rate every quarter; interviews run continuously, minimum two a month, forever · Workbook tab: scores land in the `Pipeline` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx); the ICP itself lives here._

## Purpose

Two halves of one instrument. **Half (a)** decides _who is worth your hours._ An ICP is not a description of everyone who could use the product; it is a description of the customer who, if you had a hundred of them, would produce a company you actually want. Standard practice defines an ICP as a data-backed profile combining firmographics, technology in place, buying-role structure and geography ([ZoomInfo — what is an ideal customer profile](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/marketing/ideal-customer-profile)), and turns it into a numeric score per account so that "good fit" stops being an opinion ([Cleanlist — ICP scoring](https://www.cleanlist.ai/glossary/icp-scoring)). The half almost every ICP guide omits is the **disqualification list** — the attributes that should tank a score regardless of everything else. That omission is expensive: knowing who _not_ to sell to is worth as much operationally as knowing who to sell to, and disqualification rules are what convert a founder's scarce hours into a win rate instead of a calendar.

**Half (b)** decides _what you believe._ It is a switch-interview script, and it studies the past rather than the future. Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done framing is that customers "hire" a product to make progress in a circumstance, which is why the milkshake study moved nothing until the researchers stopped asking about the milkshake and started asking about the morning commute ([HBR — Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done", 2016](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done)). Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek turned that into an interviewing method by naming the four forces acting on any switch — **push** of the situation and **pull** of the new solution drive change; **anxiety** about the new and **habit** of the present block it — with the equation _push + pull > anxiety + habit_ ([JTBD.org — The Four Forces of Progress](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/); [Unpacking the Progress Making Forces Diagram](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/unpacking-the-progress-making-forces-diagram/); Moesta's book-length version is [_Demand-Side Sales 101_](https://www.demandsidesales.com/)).

The trap the script fixes is specific and universal: founders spend all their energy on **pull** — more features, a better demo, a lower price — because pull is the only force they control directly. In this segment **anxiety and habit are almost always the binding constraint**, and neither is affected by a feature. Anxiety is answered with proof and reversibility. Habit is answered by keeping the old thing alive for thirty days, not by mocking it.

Both halves belong to the CEO rather than to a marketing function, because the ICP is a strategic choice — it is [[C05-choice-cascade-worksheet|C05]]'s where-to-play row made operational — and because the pattern only becomes visible to the person who sat in all ten conversations. After five interviews the pattern is visible. After ten, the objection bank in [[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]] writes itself.

## When to use

- **Month 2 of the CEO seat**, after the kernel and the cascade and before founder-led selling starts in earnest. Selling before the ICP exists means learning the market one wasted week at a time.
- **Before every first visit** — score the prospect from what you already know, then correct the score afterwards. A score written after the meeting is contaminated by how nice he was.
- **Continuously, minimum two switch interviews a month.** These are not sales calls and must not become them.
- **Whenever the win rate by band stops separating** — if the ≥70 band does not close at several times the 45–69 band, the rubric is wrong and you rewrite the rubric, not the market.
- **After any run of three losses**, to check whether you have been selling outside the profile without noticing.
- **Before any pricing or packaging decision** ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]]) — price is only meaningful against a named customer.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Score before the meeting, correct after.** Two columns, both dated. The gap between them is your calibration, and it is the only way to find out that you systematically over-score people who give you tea.
2. **Weights come from the diagnosis, not from a template.** VSYST's rubric weights the credit book and reconciliation pain most heavily because [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]]'s diagnosis says the constraint is a _reason to change_. A company with a different diagnosis needs a different rubric.
3. **The disqualification list overrides the score.** A disqualifier is not minus-ten points; it is a stop. Score 82 with a disqualifier is a no, and the reason gets logged.
4. **Never run a switch interview with a live prospect, and never with a DZZLO customer.** Interview dealers who adopted or refused _any_ new business system in the last 6–12 months — a billing package, a Tally upgrade, an automation vendor, a UPI QR provider, CCTV, a fleet-card scheme, even a new accountant. You are studying the physics of switching in this population, not fishing for a lead. The moment he thinks you are selling, the data stops.
5. **Do not describe the product until the last five minutes**, and only if he asks. If you find yourself explaining, you have left the interview.
6. **Ask about specifics in the past, never generics about the future.** Rob Fitzpatrick's rule is that you should be able to ask your own mother about your business and still get useful data — which is only possible if the questions never mention your idea. Three rules: talk about their life not your idea; ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals; talk less and listen more. Three kinds of data to refuse: **compliments, fluff and ideas** ([The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/); [Unusual Ventures — 5 takeaways](https://www.unusual.vc/rob-fitzpatricks-mom-test/); [a practical walkthrough](https://www.koji.so/blog/mom-test-customer-interviews-2026)).
7. **Get the day and the month.** Vagueness is where reconstructed stories live. "Kaunsa mahina tha? Us din kya hua tha?" is the highest-yield follow-up in the script after silence.
8. **Write his exact words, not your paraphrase.** The verbatim is the product. A quote goes into [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas|C08]] and into the objection bank unedited; your summary of it goes nowhere.
9. **Two people run it — one asks, one writes and draws the timeline.** Ask permission to record. Hindi or Chhattisgarhi throughout, whatever your own preference.
10. **Every conversation ends in an advance or a recorded no.** Rackham's four call outcomes — order, **advance** (a specific action with a date), continuation (it will go on, nothing agreed), no-sale — exist because founders systematically record continuations as progress ([Huthwaite International — SPIN Selling](https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-selling)). A real signal costs the customer time, reputation or money. "Send me the details" costs nothing and is not an advance.
11. **Score politeness as zero.** See the section below. In this market you will be treated well by almost everyone; treat warmth as background radiation and measure something else.

## Template (a) — the ICP definition and scoring rubric

Write the ICP as one paragraph first. Every clause must be doing work; if you cannot say which rubric row a clause maps to, delete the clause.

```
ICP — <company>, first-customer profile          set <date> · review <date+90d>

A <firm type> in <geography>, doing <throughput>, with <the structural feature
the product's loop needs>, who has <the push event, within a time window>,
who already <the proof that the category is not new>, with <the person who
makes the product survive week two> in the business, whose <decider> will
personally <the commitment that proves it>, and ideally <the expansion clue>.

BANDS   >=70  pursue this week, personally
        45-69 nurture: one useful asset a month, revisit in 90 days
        <45   decline politely and log why
DISQUALIFIERS override the score entirely. A disqualifier is a stop, not a penalty.
```

Read row 7 carefully, because it is the row founders get backwards. A dealer on pure paper looks like the biggest opportunity and is usually the hardest sale: he has never bought software, has no budget line for it, and his habit force is at maximum. The dealer who already pays a Tally AMC _and still keeps a paper DSR and an Excel credit sheet_ has already lost that argument with himself. He just has no product that covers orders, rates and credit. That is your customer.

## Template (b) — the disqualification list

Decline these regardless of score. Each row carries the trigger that would change the answer, so that a no is a "not yet with a condition" rather than a door closed.

| Decline                                                                     | Why                                                                | Trigger that would change it                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cash-only pump, no credit customers                                         | The product's loop has nothing to attach to                        | A standalone module decision in [[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list\|C25]] — a product call, never a sales call |
| Owner will not meet; only the manager will                                  | Manager-led deals do not renew, because the manager did not buy    | The owner joins a meeting, with a date                                                                         |
| Wants it free "for feedback" but will not commit staff time or a start date | Free customers give free-customer-quality feedback                 | He accepts the paid-pilot terms                                                                                |
| Makes a bespoke integration a condition of signing                          | One dealer's roadmap is not a roadmap                              | Three ICP dealers ask for it unprompted                                                                        |
| Needs a language you cannot support today                                   | A support promise you cannot keep destroys references              | The language is on the roadmap with a date                                                                     |
| Outside the chosen district while the district is unsaturated               | The where-to-play refusal in [[C05-choice-cascade-worksheet\|C05]] | Quarterly planning expands the territory — a board-visible decision                                            |
| In an active payment dispute with his OMC, or in a licence proceeding       | You inherit the chaos and can lose the tenant mid-pilot            | Written resolution                                                                                             |
| Wants to resell or white-label before you have 20 direct customers          | You lose the customer relationship exactly when you need it most   | Never during the founder-led phase                                                                             |
| Defers everything to a hostile accountant                                   | A silent veto that surfaces in month three                         | The accountant is in the room and has seen the Tally-compatible output                                         |
| Asks you to under-invoice, split a bill, or hide a transaction              | Non-negotiable                                                     | Never ([[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties\|lesson 09]])                                            |

Have the refusal sentence ready so that "no" costs you nothing socially: _"Sir, sach batayein to abhi hamara system aapke jaise pump ke liye utna kaam nahi karega — aapke paas udhaar customer hi nahi hain. Main jhooth bolke aapka paisa nahi lena chahta. Chhe mahine baad wapas aaunga."_ In a district where everyone talks, that is one of the cheapest reputation purchases available to you.

## Template (c) — the switch-interview script

**Who:** a dealer who adopted **or refused** any new business system in the last 6–12 months. Not your customer. **Setup:** at his pump, 45–60 minutes, two of you, permission to record, in his language. The force each question probes is marked in the margin — that tag is what you sort the transcript by afterwards.

```
THE FIRST THOUGHT                                                    [ push ]
 1. Take me back. What was the first moment you thought "ab iss tarah nahi
    chalega, kuch badalna padega"? Which month was it? What had just
    happened that day?
 2. Before that day, what were you using? Walk me through one full day with
    it, from the morning shift to the night close.                  [ habit ]
 3. Had you thought about changing before that? What stopped you then?
                                                          [ anxiety + habit ]

THE GAP
 4. Between that first thought and actually doing something — how long was
    it? Weeks? Months? What happened in between?                     [ push ]
 5. Was there one specific thing that finally made you start looking — a
    date, an inspection, a customer, a number that got too big?      [ push ]

THE SEARCH
 6. Where did you look first? Who did you ask?                       [ pull ]
 7. What else did you consider? Include "carry on as before", "let the
    accountant handle it", "put my son on it" — those count.         [ pull ]
 8. Who came to see you? What did they show you, and in what order?   [ pull ]

THE FEARS
 9. What worried you? What was the worst thing you imagined going wrong?
                                                                  [ anxiety ]
10. Who else in the family or at the pump had to agree? What did they say —
    exactly, in their words?                                       [ anxiety ]
11. What did you have to give up, or stop doing, to make the change?[ habit ]

THE DECISION
12. What was the moment you decided? Where were you sitting, who was with
    you, what was said?
13. What did you pay? How did you decide that number was acceptable — what
    did you compare it against?                                      [ pull ]

AFTERWARDS
14. First week — what actually happened? What surprised you? What did your
    staff say?                                                      [ habit ]
15. Today: what do you use it for that you didn't expect? What have you
    stopped using it for? If it vanished tomorrow morning, what would
    you do?

    [If he REFUSED:] What would have had to be true for you to say yes?
                     And who did say yes, that you know of?
```

**Six rules for running it.** (1) The only follow-up you need most of the time is _"aur phir kya hua?"_ — then silence. (2) Ask for the day and the month. (3) If he starts giving you opinions or advice, return him to the timeline: _"us din wapas chalte hain."_ (4) Write his exact words. (5) Never correct him. (6) If you are explaining, stop. The structured version of this arc, with timings, is documented from Moesta's own practice ([June — how to run a JTBD interview like the co-creator of the framework](https://www.june.so/blog/how-to-run-a-jtbd-interview-like-the-co-creator-of-the-framework); [Moesta on what these interviews reveal](https://saasclub.io/podcast/jobs-to-be-done-bob-moesta-423/)).

**Never ask these**, and here is the replacement for each:

| Never ask                                 | Why it lies                                                                        | Ask instead                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _"Kya aapko ye idea accha lagta hai?"_    | Invites a compliment; he will say yes to be polite                                 | _"Pichhli baar rate ka jhagda kab hua tha? Kya hua tha?"_                                   |
| _"Kya aap aisa software kharidenge?"_     | Hypothetical — free to say yes                                                     | _"Abhi credit ka hisaab kahan rakhte hain? Kisne set kiya tha? Kitna dete hain uske liye?"_ |
| _"Kitna dene ko tayyar hain?"_            | He has no idea, and his guess anchors you wrongly                                  | _"Pichhle saal software ya accounting pe kitna kharch hua? Kis-kis ko?"_                    |
| _"Kya ye feature useful hoga?"_           | He will design your roadmap. The problem is signal; the suggested feature is noise | _"Aakhri baar aisi problem kab aayi? Us waqt aapne kya kiya?"_                              |
| _"Aapki sabse badi problem kya hai?"_     | Produces a rehearsed abstraction                                                   | _"Pichhle hafte sabse zyada time kis cheez mein gaya jo nahi jana chahiye tha?"_            |
| _"Main demo dikha doon?"_ (in minute two) | Ends discovery, starts a pitch                                                     | _"Do minute apni process dikha sakte hain? Jaise aap roz karte hain."_                      |

## Template (d) — detecting a polite "no"

In this culture a direct refusal to a guest is close to rude, and negative feedback is routinely delivered indirectly — the same variation across cultures in how directly criticism is given that Erin Meyer maps in _The Culture Map_ ([erinmeyer.com](https://erinmeyer.com/books/the-culture-map/)). A dealer will therefore almost never say no. He will say something warmer and less useful, and if you cannot read it, your pipeline fills with ghosts and your strategy gets confirmed by people who were being kind. **This is not a soft skill; it is data hygiene.**

| What he says                                                   | What it usually means                                | The probe that resolves it                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _"Haan haan, bahut accha hai. Zaroor."_                        | Politeness. No commitment has occurred               | _"Accha lagna alag baat hai — agla step kya rakhein? Main Mangalwar 11 baje aa jaun?"_        |
| _"Aap office aa jaiye kabhi."_                                 | A friendly dismissal with no date                    | _"Zaroor — Mangalwar ya Guruwar?"_ A refusal to pick a day **is** the answer                  |
| _"Abhi season chal raha hai, thoda baad mein."_                | Not enough push yet, or the wrong person             | _"Season kab tak? Us waqt tak ye problem badhegi ya kam hogi?"_                               |
| _"Bete/bhai se poochhna padega."_                              | Either real (the son decides) or an exit             | _"Bilkul — main unko bhi dikha doon? Aap dono ke saath 15 minute."_                           |
| _"Bhej dijiye WhatsApp pe, dekh lenge."_                       | Deferral. Sending a PDF ends the conversation        | _"Bhej deta hoon — par 10 minute mein aapke apne ek customer ka ledger bana ke dikhaun?"_     |
| _"Kitna hai? … theek hai, dekhte hain."_                       | Price was never the issue; he is closing the meeting | _"Paisa ek minute alag rakhte hain — agar ye free bhi hota, to kal se use karte? Kya rokta?"_ |
| Tea is ordered, the meeting extends warmly, nothing is decided | Hospitality, not interest                            | Ask for the advance before the second cup                                                     |
| He gives his number readily but never opens your WhatsApp      | You were a pleasant visitor, not a candidate         | Stop chasing. Log the loss reason honestly                                                    |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**A scored prospect.** Fictional dealer, plausible numbers, in the format the `Pipeline` tab expects.

| B2B credit customers   | 11 active accounts                                                                           | 2   | 20  | 40                 |
| Credit share of volume | ~35%                                                                                         | 2   | 15  | 30                 |
| Reconciliation pain    | One named incident: ₹2.4 lakh written off from a transporter in Nov; one rate dispute in Feb | 2   | 15  | 30                 |
| Owner's tech posture   | Son, 29, back from Bhilai, runs the office and does the GST uploads himself                  | 3   | 15  | 45                 |
| Monthly throughput     | Comfortably above the district median                                                        | 2   | 10  | 20                 |
| Distance from Raipur   | 48 km                                                                                        | 2   | 10  | 20                 |
| Existing software      | Tally AMC paid + paper DSR + an Excel credit sheet maintained by the son                     | 3   | 10  | 30                 |
| GSTINs                 | 2 (pump + a transport firm)                                                                  | 2   | 5   | 10                 |
| Referenceability       | Not an office-bearer, but two neighbouring dealers named him unprompted                      | 2   | 5   | 10                 |
| Owner reachable        | Warm intro from the district association secretary                                           | 2   | 5   | 10                 |
| **Total**              |                                                                                              |     |     | **245 / 300 → 82** |

Band **≥70 — pursue this week, personally.** No disqualifier present: the owner has agreed to sit in the demo, there is no OMC dispute, no bespoke-integration condition, no reseller ambition. Score written **before** the first visit; re-score after, and note the delta.

**A forces map from one switch interview** — the one-page output that goes in the vault after every interview. This one is from a dealer who bought an ANPR/automation system eight months ago, not from a DZZLO prospect.

| Force       | Verbatim (his words)                                                                                                                                                           | Strength 1–5 | What VSYST does about it                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Push**    | _"Pichhle saal do party ka paisa doob gaya. Tab laga ki register se kaam nahi chalega."_ Also: the OMC territory manager asked for figures he could not produce in the meeting | **5**        | Source on this event: a written-off receivable. Ask about it on the first call ([[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script\|C10]] §first call, minute 2:00–3:30)   |
| **Pull**    | _"Bete ne dikhaya, phone pe sab dikh raha tha."_ He was shown a screen by his own son, not by a vendor                                                                         | 3            | Open the demo on the **ledger** for one of his own named customers, entered live. Not the feature tour                                                              |
| **Anxiety** | _"Data kahan jayega?"_ · _"Staff seekh payega?"_ · _"Beech mein band ho gaya to?"_                                                                                             | **4**        | Build the proof assets: a one-page data-and-export note, a written exit clause, and the "attendant ko kuch nahi karna" answer with a named operator at his own pump |
| **Habit**   | The DSR written by hand for 22 years; the accountant's Tally; the diary in the drawer                                                                                          | **4**        | Let him keep all three for 30 days. Do not mock the diary. Feed Tally rather than replace it                                                                        |

**What the map says to do.** Push is high and pull is low — meaning this dealer was _ready_ and nobody gave him a reason. The equation says the switch happens when push + pull beat anxiety + habit; here anxiety and habit together (8) outweighed push and pull (8) until his son supplied the missing pull for free. The instruction for VSYST is not to build features. It is to **manufacture pull cheaply** — a screen with his own customer's balance on it, shown in his own forecourt — and to **spend the rest of the effort on anxiety**, which is answered with reversibility and proof, never with reassurance.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                             | What it looks like                                            | Fix                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **An ICP that describes everyone who could use it** | "Fuel dealers in India"                                       | Rule 2. Describe the hundred customers who would make a company you want                                           |
| **No disqualification list**                        | A rubric with only positive points                            | Half (b). A disqualifier is a stop, not a penalty. It is the half most ICP documents omit                          |
| **Scoring after the meeting**                       | Every prospect who gave you tea scores 75                     | Rule 1. Score before, correct after, and watch your own bias in the delta                                          |
| **Running the switch interview on a prospect**      | It turns into a demo by minute nine                           | Rule 4. Different people, different purpose. Never the same meeting                                                |
| **Pitching inside the interview**                   | "Actually, we do exactly that…"                               | Rule 5. The moment you explain, the data stops                                                                     |
| **Hypothetical questions**                          | "Would you buy something like this?"                          | Rule 6. Past specifics only. Fluff is any sentence in the future or conditional tense                              |
| **Collecting feature requests**                     | A list of things he said he wanted                            | The problem behind the request is the signal; the request itself is noise                                          |
| **Paraphrasing instead of quoting**                 | Your neat summary of what he meant                            | Rule 8. The verbatim is the product. It feeds [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas\|C08]] and the objection bank |
| **Counting warmth as signal**                       | Four warm meetings recorded as progress                       | Rule 11 and Template (d). Warmth is background radiation. Measure the advance                                      |
| **A rubric that never changes**                     | Same weights, four quarters, win rates not separating by band | Track win rate by band. If the bands do not separate, the rubric is wrong                                          |
| **Chasing the pure-paper dealer**                   | "He has nothing! Huge opportunity!"                           | Row 7. He has never bought software and his habit force is at maximum                                              |
