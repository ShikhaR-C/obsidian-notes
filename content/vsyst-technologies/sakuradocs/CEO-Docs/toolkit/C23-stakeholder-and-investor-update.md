# C23 — Monthly Stakeholder / Investor Update

_Toolkit · fills §4 of [[12-communication-the-ceo-as-chief-storyteller|12 — Communication: The CEO as Chief Storyteller]] and the readiness half of [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|08 — Capital, Runway and Fundraising]] · Owner: the CEO writes it in his own voice; nobody drafts it for him · Cadence: monthly, by the 10th, forever, starting the month the seat exists · No workbook tab — it reads `Runway & Scenarios` and `CEO Dashboard` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

One page a month, sent to people outside the room, saying what happened — in numbers, including the parts that went badly. VSYST is **bootstrapped**. There is no board demanding a deck, no investor asking for figures by the 10th, and no consequence at all for not writing it. **Write it anyway.** It costs about ninety minutes once the scorecard and the runway model exist, and it buys four things you cannot buy any other way.

**One: it forces you to explain a number in prose.** A number in a spreadsheet can be stared past for a quarter. A number in a sentence that has to make sense to a reader who was not there cannot. Half the value of this document is extracted before anybody reads it.

**Two: outside eyes.** Three co-founders in one office converge on the same blind spot within a month. A CA, a mentor and an industry advisor reading the same page will each see a different thing wrong with it, and at least one of them will be right.

**Three: it is a dated record of what you believed.** Twelve months later it is the only honest answer to "did we know?" — which matters in a family founding team, where "I told you so" is otherwise unfalsifiable and permanently available ([[02-how-a-ceo-thinks|lesson 02]]).

**Four, and this is the one founders discount: a year of honest monthly updates is the cheapest fundraising asset in existence.** You cannot manufacture it later. The day you raise — an angel, a strategic, the Startup India Seed Fund ([Startup India Seed Fund Scheme](https://seedfund.startupindia.gov.in/)) — twelve updates written _before anyone asked_ are a traction narrative with a timestamp on every claim. Diligence stops being an interrogation and becomes reading. The literature on this is unanimous and self-interested, so discount it accordingly, but the direction is not in dispute: founders who send consistent updates are materially more likely to raise follow-on capital ([Visible.vc — How To Write the Perfect Investor Update](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-write-the-perfect-investor-update/)) — **VERIFY LIVE** any specific multiple before you repeat it, because the underlying samples are small and self-selected.

**The COO's [[T23-board-and-investor-update|T23]] is the operational sibling of this document, and the split is exact.** T23 says which numbers go in the pack, which sheets they come from, and by which date the close has to land for the pack to exist. C23 says what the CEO _says_ about them, in whose voice, to whom, and with which ask attached. When a board exists, T23's pack becomes the appendix and C23 becomes its first page ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]). Do not write both. Write C23; let the COO's close feed it.

## When to use

- **Monthly, by a fixed date** — the 10th, because the close pack has landed by then. A monthly artefact without a date drifts to quarterly and then to never.
- **Even at zero revenue, zero investors and zero board.** Especially then. The month you have nothing to report is the month the document is doing the most work, because it makes "nothing" visible while it is still cheap.
- **Before every board meeting**, expanded and circulated one to two weeks ahead, never handed out in the room ([[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]; §173 sets the meeting cadence you are minuting it into — [§173, Companies Act 2013](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/), **VERIFY LIVE** the small-company relaxation with your CS).
- **At the start of the quarterly planning half-day.** Three updates read end to end is the most efficient quarter review available, and the misses column usually names next quarter's priority before anyone proposes one.
- **When a fundraise starts** — at which point you _assemble_, you do not write ([[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]], [[C15-investor-narrative-and-deck-outline|C15]]).

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One page. One screen.** An update nobody finishes is a file, not a communication. Roughly 400–600 words plus the metrics block.
2. **The headline is one sentence and it goes first.** If the reader stops after the first line, what is the single thing they must have? Write that. If you cannot compress the month into one sentence you have not understood the month.
3. **Cash and runway are the first two rows of the metrics block, every month, without exception.** Not because they are interesting but because "make sure there is always enough cash in the bank" is one of the three things the job actually consists of ([Fred Wilson — What A CEO Does](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/)), and running out of money remains one of the most-cited causes of startup death ([CB Insights — top reasons startups fail](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/)).
4. **Every metric shows last month beside it.** A number without its predecessor is decoration. A number with its predecessor is a trend, and a trend is the only thing a reader can act on.
5. **"What went wrong" is compulsory and it is never empty.** A month with no misses means you did not look, or you looked and edited. This is the section that makes the other sections believable — and it is the section a future investor reads first, precisely because most founders leave it out. If you genuinely cannot find one, write "I could not find one this month, which probably means I am not looking hard enough."
6. **Write in the first person and name yourself in the failures.** "I under-scoped the import work." Not "the import work took longer than expected." Passive voice in a miss is the tell that the miss is not fully owned.
7. **One ask, and it is specific enough to be actioned or refused.** "Intros to fleet operators in Durg" is an ask. "Any thoughts welcome" is not. If you have no ask this month, say "no ask this month" rather than inventing one — an invented ask trains readers to skim the section.
8. **Do not grade your own decisions by their outcomes.** A bet that lost is not automatically a bad decision, and a bet that won is not automatically a good one; judging decisions by results is the standard error ([Annie Duke — _Thinking in Bets_](https://www.annieduke.com/books/)). Report the outcome, then separately report whether the falsification condition triggered ([[C25-product-bets-and-the-no-list|C25]]).
9. **Never change a metric definition silently.** If "active dealer" changes meaning, say so in the update and restate last month on the new basis. One silently redefined metric costs you every metric.
10. **Same recipients, same format, same date, forever.** Consistency _is_ the asset. A beautiful update sent twice is worth less than a plain one sent twelve times ([Pitchwise — monthly investor update](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/how-to-write-monthly-investor-update)).
11. **Archive it in the vault, dated, and never edit a sent update.** If a figure was wrong, correct it in the next one under its own heading. The archive's value is that it was written before you knew how things turned out ([37signals — How We Communicate](https://37signals.com/how-we-communicate)).

## Template

```
VSYST TECHNOLOGIES — <Month Year> UPDATE                       sent <date>
To: <CA> · <CS> · <mentor> · <industry advisor> · <co-directors>

HEADLINE  ...one sentence. the thing they must have if they read no further...

── METRICS ─────────────────────────── this month ── last month ── Δ ──
Cash in bank (₹)
Net burn / month (₹)
Runway (months, at current burn)
Paying GSTINs
MRR (₹)
Net new paying GSTINs (added − churned)
Active pilots / tenants transacting
<the one operating metric that matters this quarter>
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT WENT WELL          2-3 bullets. facts with numbers, not adjectives.

WHAT WENT WRONG         COMPULSORY. 1-3 bullets. what happened, what it cost,
                        what I am doing, by when. first person.

BETS + FALSIFICATION    one line per live bet: is the check date passed, did
                        the falsification condition trigger, what did we do
                        about it.  (C25)

THE ASK                 one specific, refusable request. or "no ask this month".

NEXT MONTH              3 things that will be true by the next update, each
                        with a date. these get graded, by name, next month.

CORRECTIONS             anything I got wrong in a previous update.
```

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

All figures are **illustrative placeholders** for a pre-revenue bootstrapped company; replace them with the real close before this is sent to anybody.

```
VSYST TECHNOLOGIES — AUGUST 2026 UPDATE                    sent 10 Sept 2026
To: CA · CS · Mentor · Industry advisor · Both co-directors

HEADLINE  Six pilots live, still zero rupees of revenue — and the month's real
          news is that three dealers independently asked us to prove a balance
          to their accountant, which is a buying signal we had not designed for.

── METRICS ────────────────────── Aug 2026 ── Jul 2026 ──── Δ ──
Cash in bank (₹)                  <founder-funded>   —        —
Net burn / month (₹)              <fill from CA>     —        —
Runway (months)                   <fill>             —        —
Paying GSTINs                     0                  0        0
MRR (₹)                           0                  0        0
Net new paying GSTINs             0                  0        0
Pilots live                       6                  4       +2
Pilots transacting at day 14      3 of 6             2 of 4  +1
Nightly rate-confirmation rate    71%                64%     +7pt
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT WENT WELL
• Two new pilots in Durg, both introduced by an existing pilot dealer. First
  evidence that the district motion refers itself.
• Rate-confirmation completion 64% -> 71% after the one-tap confirm shipped.
  Target is 80%; this is the metric closest to the moat thesis.
• A dealer settled a ₹4L dispute in front of his customer using the confirmed
  rate and the OTP record. He has since introduced us to two others.

WHAT WENT WRONG
• I let a ledger-correctness fix sit unmerged for nine days. Our own written
  rule says nothing else ships past seven. The corrupting code was live for
  those nine days. Fix deployed 22 Aug; the rule is now a release-checklist
  item, not a memory.
• Three of six pilots have not issued a real invoice by day 14. Activation is
  the weakest link in the strategy and it did not move this month.
• Billing is still not built, which means revenue cannot exist yet regardless
  of how many pilots we add. That is my sequencing error, not a delay.

BETS + FALSIFICATION  (C25)
• Bet 1 (14-day activation): check date 30 Oct. Not triggered. 3/6 vs the 6/10
  threshold — on the wrong side but the sample is too small to call.
• Bet 2 (rate window as habit): 71% vs an 80% falsification floor at 4 weeks.
  Two weeks left. If it stalls at ~72% the bet is lost and we say so.
• Bet 3 (ledger correctness sells): 3 of the last 10 dealer conversations
  raised proof unprompted. Bet is holding.

THE ASK
• Mentor: one introduction to a fleet operator in Durg or Bhilai who buys
  diesel on credit — I want to hear the *customer* side of the ledger, and I
  have only heard the dealer side.
• CA: confirm by 20 Sept whether we cross any TCS-relevant threshold on our
  own turnover this FY, so I stop guessing.

NEXT MONTH  (graded here next update)
1. Billing v0 live so a dealer can be charged at all — by 30 Sept.
2. Pilots transacting at day 14 at 5 of 8 — by 10 Oct.
3. Bet 2 called, won or lost, in writing — by 30 Sept.

CORRECTIONS
July's update said 5 pilots live. Two of those had signed but never onboarded.
The honest July figure was 4. I have restated the table above on that basis.
```

Note the shape of it. The headline does not lead with the pilot count; it leads with the **surprise**, because the surprise is the only thing in the month a reader could not have predicted. The misses section names a rule the company broke — its own rule — which is the kind of admission that makes the wins credible. And the `CORRECTIONS` block, which costs nothing and is used once a year, is the single feature that converts an archive of updates from marketing into evidence.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                           | What it looks like                               | Fix                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Not writing it because there are no investors** | "We'll start when we raise"                      | The archive is the asset, and it cannot be backdated. Send it to the CA and a mentor                                      |
| **An empty or absent misses section**             | Twelve months of highlights                      | Rule 5. A month with no misses is a month with no looking                                                                 |
| **Passive voice in the misses**                   | "Timelines slipped"                              | Rule 6. Name yourself, or nobody believes the section                                                                     |
| **Metrics without last month**                    | A column of numbers                              | Rule 4. One number is decoration; two are a trend                                                                         |
| **A silently redefined metric**                   | "Active dealer" quietly becomes "signed dealer"  | Rule 9. Say it and restate the prior month, or lose every metric's credibility                                            |
| **A vague ask**                                   | "Let me know if you can help"                    | Rule 7. Specific enough to be refused, or say "no ask"                                                                    |
| **Growing to four pages**                         | Screenshots, roadmap, architecture               | One screen. The detail belongs in the COO's pack ([[T23-board-and-investor-update\|T23]])                                 |
| **Editing a sent update**                         | Fixing last month's number in place              | Rule 11. Correct it in the next one, under `CORRECTIONS`                                                                  |
| **Grading yourself by outcomes**                  | "Bet 2 won, so it was a good decision"           | Rule 8. Report the outcome and the falsification condition separately                                                     |
| **Skipping a bad month**                          | No update in the month everything went wrong     | The skipped month is the most informative one in the archive, and its absence is louder than its contents would have been |
| **Two documents**                                 | A CEO update and a COO update to the same people | [[T23-board-and-investor-update\|T23]] is the pack; C23 is the page in front of it                                        |
