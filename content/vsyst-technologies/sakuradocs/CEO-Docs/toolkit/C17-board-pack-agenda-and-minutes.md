# C17 — Board Pack, Agenda, Resolutions and Minutes

_Toolkit · fills the board-mechanics work in [[09-board-governance-and-the-directors-duties|09 — Board, Governance and the Director's Duties]] §4 and §5 · Owner: the CEO writes the pack and sets the agenda; the CS owns the minute book and the notice · Cadence: quarterly — pack out seven clear days before, minutes in the book within thirty days after · Workbook tab: `Board Calendar` in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Three artefacts that make a board meeting real instead of decorative: **the pack** (what the board reads before), **the agenda** (what the ninety minutes are spent on), and **the minutes** (the only surviving proof that anything was decided).

They are one template because they are one chain. A pack circulated late forces a meeting spent presenting rather than deciding. A meeting spent presenting produces minutes with nothing in them. Minutes with nothing in them mean that two years later — in diligence, in a dispute, in front of a lender — the company cannot show that its directors applied their minds to anything. The chain breaks at the first weak link, and at a small company the first weak link is almost always the pack going out the night before.

The governing rule comes from Fred Wilson and is the same rule every experienced board investor repeats: **circulate the material in advance and spend the meeting on discussion, not reporting** ([AVC — What A CEO Does](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/)). Mark Suster's version is more specific and more useful — get the financial pack out well in advance, because most founders send it one or two days before, and a board that reads on the fly debates on the fly ([Both Sides of the Table — Managing Your Startup Board](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/managing-your-startup-board-a-short-presentation-4d709772bc00); [Inc. — 6 things every business leader should do before a board meeting](https://www.inc.com/mark-suster/startup-board-meeting-prepare-plan-agenda.html)). The Holloway syllabus collects the rest of the canon if you want to read further ([Holloway — Startup Boards syllabus](https://www.holloway.com/s/syllabus-startup-boards)).

The statutory half is not optional and is not American. **Every statutory element in this template is VERIFY LIVE — confirm with the Company Secretary before you rely on it.** Sections and penalties in the Companies Act 2013 have been recut by the 2019 and 2020 amendment Acts, the small-company thresholds moved again with effect from December 2025 ([Fox Mandal — MCA notifies revised small company thresholds](https://foxmandal.in/News/mca-notifies-revised-small-company-thresholds/)), and the ICSI Secretarial Standards are revised periodically — SS-1 on board meetings and SS-2 on general meetings were both revised with effect from 1 April 2024 ([ICSI — Secretarial Standards Board](https://www.icsi.edu/ssb/home/); [SS-1 full text, PDF](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/publications/16022022_Secretarial_Standard_on_Meetings_of_the_Board_of_Directors_SS_1.pdf)).

## When to use

- **Before VSYST's next board meeting** — the first one run properly, with a pack, an agenda and minutes that a stranger could read. This is the day-61–90 artefact in [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]].
- **Every quarter thereafter.** Fill it once and it becomes copy-paste-plus-edit for the life of the company.
- **Before any meeting that will approve a related-party arrangement**, a borrowing, an appointment, a share issue or an ESOP scheme — the items where the minute _is_ the authority.
- **When an advisor or an outside director first attends**, because the pack is what makes their attendance worth anything.
- **Before diligence.** An investor's lawyer reads minute books. Assume it, and write for that reader.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Seven clear days, or it is not a pack.** [§173(3)](https://ca2013.com/173-meetings-of-board/) requires not less than seven days' notice in writing to every director; §173(4) puts a penalty of ₹25,000 on the officer in default for a defective notice — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS.** Send the notice, the agenda and the pack as one email on the same day. A WhatsApp message three days out is not notice, however well everyone gets on.
2. **Never present the pack.** If you find yourself reading it aloud, the pack failed. The chair's only question on the CEO's letter is _"what changed since you wrote it?"_
3. **The bad news goes in the first half of the CEO's letter.** A board that finds a problem in the appendix stops believing the letter. Reprint last quarter's promises verbatim at the top of the dashboard with a status against each.
4. **One strategic discussion per meeting. Exactly one.** Framed as a question, with your recommendation and the two alternatives you rejected. Everything else on the agenda is scaffolding for that twenty-five minutes.
5. **Pre-draft every decision as resolution language.** If an item authorises money, a signature, a person or a share, it is a numbered resolution, not a discussion note. Walk the list against the board's non-delegable powers in [§179(3)](https://ca2013.com/179-powers-of-board/) and its rules before the meeting.
6. **Do not use circulation for anything on that list.** [§175](https://ca2013.com/175-passing-of-resolution-by-circulation/) allows resolution by circulation, but any resolution so passed must be **noted at a subsequent board meeting and made part of those minutes** — and where not less than one-third of directors require it, the chairperson must put it to a meeting. On a three-director board one-third is one: **any single director can force a circulated item into a real meeting.** That is a feature; say so out loud once so everyone knows they have it.
7. **Record dissent by name.** [§118(4)](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/) requires the minutes to contain the names of directors present _and_ the names of directors dissenting from or not concurring with a resolution. In a family company this is the single most valuable line in the minute book, and it will be blank for years unless you build the habit deliberately.
8. **Minutes are a fair and correct summary, entered within thirty days.** Draft within fifteen days, comments back within seven, entered in the book within thirty is the working sequence under the secretarial standards ([TaxGuru — §118 minutes of board meeting](https://taxguru.in/company-law/section-118-of-companies-act-2013-minutes-board-meeting.html)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**, the standards are revised.
9. **Six things per item, or the minute is useless.** The item in one sentence a stranger understands; the papers placed before the board, named; the discussion in three to six lines _including the counter-argument_; the numbered resolution; any dissent by name; the action with an owner and a date.
10. **The CEO sends the CS a one-page decision note the same week.** Minutes written at year end from a template, for a whole year of meetings, are the standard small-company failure, and it happens because nobody sent the CS anything.

## Template (a) — the board pack

Six to ten pages, PDF, one file, sent with the notice seven clear days ahead. Same order every quarter — a pack redesigned to flatter is a marketing document.

```
BOARD PACK — VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd.       Meeting: <date>  ·  Circulated: <date>
Prepared by: <CEO>   Papers: 1–9 below   Read time: ~25 minutes

1. CEO'S LETTER (1–2 pages, prose, first person, no bullets)
   What I believe now  ·  What changed this quarter  ·  What I got wrong last quarter
   The one thing that worries me  ·  What I want from you today
   [bad news lives in the first half of this letter]

2. DASHBOARD (1 page)  — same metrics, same order, plan vs actual, trend
   LAST QUARTER'S PROMISES, REPRINTED VERBATIM, with status against each

3. CASH AND RUNWAY (1 page)  — bank balance · monthly burn · runway months
   13-week cash view  ·  the date at which a decision becomes forced   [C13]

4. STRATEGY-BET UPDATE (1–2 pages) — per active bet: hypothesis · evidence this
   quarter · continue / change / kill, and why                          [C04]

5. CUSTOMERS AND PIPELINE (½ page) — tenants live · pipeline · wins ·
   LOSSES IN THE DEALER'S OWN WORDS

6. PEOPLE (½ page) — headcount · joined / left · open roles · any performance
   issue the board should not learn about later

7. RISKS (½ page) — only what CHANGED: a risk that worsened, a trigger that
   fired, an incident, a compliance date at risk

8. DECISIONS REQUESTED (1 page) — numbered, each with:
   recommendation · alternatives rejected · pre-drafted resolution language

9. APPENDIX — CS filings-status page · MBP-1s · related-party register extract ·
   minutes of the previous meeting · any contract to be approved
```

## Template (b) — the agenda and run of show, 90 minutes

Circulate this _as the agenda_, with the notice. Hard stop at ninety minutes; a board meeting that runs long teaches everyone that the times are decorative.

| Min   | Item                              | Who                    | What must happen                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–5   | **Constitution**                  | Chair / CS             | Quorum confirmed; mode of attendance recorded per person (in person / video); notice confirmed; leave of absence noted; minutes of the previous meeting approved and signed |
| 5–10  | **Statutory block**               | CS                     | MBP-1 and DIR-8 if this is the first meeting of the FY; resolutions passed by circulation since the last meeting **noted**; filings status                                  |
| 10–20 | **CEO's letter, taken as read**   | CEO                    | One question only: what changed since you wrote it?                                                                                                                         |
| 20–35 | **The numbers**                   | CEO                    | Cash, runway, burn, the operating metrics, plan vs actual, and the single number you are most worried about                                                                 |
| 35–60 | **The one strategic discussion**  | CEO opens; all discuss | Framed as a question. Recommendation plus the two rejected alternatives. **This is the meeting**                                                                            |
| 60–75 | **Decisions requiring the board** | Chair puts each        | Each item read as pre-drafted resolution language, proposed, discussed, carried or not, dissent recorded                                                                    |
| 75–85 | **People and risk**               | CEO                    | Only what moved on the risk register; hires, departures, incidents, compliance                                                                                              |
| 85–90 | **Close**                         | Chair                  | Actions read back aloud with owners and dates; next meeting date confirmed; **"what did we not discuss that we should have?"**                                              |

## Template (c) — the minutes

```
MINUTES OF THE <n>th MEETING OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS OF
VSYST TECHNOLOGIES PRIVATE LIMITED
held on <day, date> at <time> at <registered office / by video conference>

PRESENT (record each, with mode of attendance)
  1. <name>, Director — in person — in the Chair
  2. <name>, Director — video conference
  3. <name>, Director — in person
  IN ATTENDANCE: <CS / advisor as invitee / CA>
  LEAVE OF ABSENCE: <name>, granted

1. CHAIRPERSON AND QUORUM
   <name> took the chair. The Chairperson confirmed that the requisite notice
   had been given and that a quorum was present throughout the meeting.

2. MINUTES OF THE PREVIOUS MEETING
   The minutes of the meeting held on <date> were noted and signed by the Chairperson.

3. RESOLUTIONS PASSED BY CIRCULATION
   The resolution(s) passed by circulation on <date> were noted and form part of
   these minutes.                                                   [s.175(2)]

4. <ITEM TITLE>
   4.1 The item, in one sentence a stranger understands.
   4.2 Papers placed before the Board: <name them and their dates>.
   4.3 The Board discussed <3–6 lines>, including the argument against, namely <...>.
   4.4 RESOLVED THAT ...                                            [resolution language]
   4.5 Mr/Ms <name> dissented / did not concur, for the reason that <...>.   [s.118(4)]
   4.6 Action: <owner> by <date>.

<repeat 4.x per item>

n. ANY OTHER BUSINESS, with the permission of the Chair
n+1. DATE OF NEXT MEETING: <date>
n+2. VOTE OF THANKS AND CONCLUSION — the meeting concluded at <time>.

                                          _________________________
                                          <name>, Chairperson
                                          Date of entry in the Minute Book: <date>
```

**Minute-book housekeeping the CS runs and the CEO should be able to check:** pages consecutively numbered; blank space scored off and initialled; the chairperson initials each page and signs the last; a **separate minute book for each kind of meeting**; the book kept at the registered office. Properly kept minutes are evidence of the proceedings recorded and the meeting is presumed duly held. The penalties bite in both directions — a default carries a penalty on the company and on every officer in default, and **tampering with minutes carries imprisonment** ([§118](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/)) — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS for current amounts.**

## The routine resolutions a small private company actually needs

Keep this list beside the agenda and tick what applies. **Each row's statutory basis and any threshold is VERIFY LIVE with the CS** — the CS drafts the language; the CEO's job is to notice that the item needs a resolution at all, before it takes effect.

| #   | Resolution                                                                                 | Typical trigger                                  | Note                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Approve minutes of the previous meeting                                                    | Every meeting                                    | First item, always                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 2   | Note MBP-1 disclosures of interest from every director                                     | First meeting of each FY, and on any change      | [§184](https://ca2013.com/184-disclosure-of-interest-by-director/); the forms go in the binder                                                                                                                                       |
| 3   | Note DIR-8 declarations of non-disqualification                                            | First meeting of each FY / before re-appointment | [Rule 14, Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules 2014](https://ca2013.com/rule-14-companies-appointment-and-qualification-of-directors-rules2014/)                                                             |
| 4   | Note resolutions passed by circulation                                                     | Every meeting where any were passed              | [§175(2)](https://ca2013.com/175-passing-of-resolution-by-circulation/)                                                                                                                                                              |
| 5   | Open / close / change signatories on a bank account; approve the banking mandate           | Any bank change                                  | Banks ask for a certified copy of this one constantly                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 6   | Approve borrowing, a loan facility, a director's loan to the company, or a guarantee       | Any borrowing                                    | Check the AoA limit _and_ the restrictions on the board's powers before you draw                                                                                                                                                     |
| 7   | Approve a related-party contract or arrangement                                            | Before it takes effect                           | [§188](https://ca2013.com/188-related-party-transactions/) — board consent must be **by a resolution at a meeting, not by circulation**. Family rent, a relative's vendor firm, a relative's employment, the family pump as a tenant |
| 8   | Approve the audited financial statements and the Board's Report                            | Annually, before the AGM                         | Directors sign these personally                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 9   | Recommend / not recommend a dividend; call the AGM and approve the notice                  | Annually                                         | CS drafts                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 10  | Appoint or re-appoint the statutory auditor and fix remuneration                           | On appointment / casual vacancy                  | Followed by the auditor-appointment filing                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| 11  | Appoint or resign a director; appoint an MD or whole-time director and fix remuneration    | On the event                                     | Directors' remuneration needs its own resolution — **VERIFY LIVE with the CS**                                                                                                                                                       |
| 12  | Approve the ESOP scheme and each grant                                                     | On adoption and each grant round                 | Board plus shareholder approval; see [[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design\|C20]]                                                                                                                                                 |
| 13  | Issue shares / approve a share transfer / note a share transmission                        | Any cap-table event                              | Mirrors [[C16-term-sheet-decoder-and-cap-table\|C16]]                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 14  | Authorise a named person to sign a specific contract, MoU, lease or government application | Per contract                                     | The one founders skip until a counterparty's lawyer asks for it — [[C26-partnership-evaluation-and-mou-checklist\|C26]]                                                                                                              |
| 15  | Change the registered office; adopt or amend a policy the board must own                   | On the event                                     | CS files the event form                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 16  | Adopt the values set, the delegation of authority and the CEO charter                      | Once, then on amendment                          | [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan\|C01]], [[C03-decision-rights-matrix\|C03]], [[C21-values-and-operating-principles-worksheet\|C21]]                                                                                                |

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

**All numbers below are illustrative.** Q2 board meeting, three founder-directors, one advisor attending as an invitee by video, CS in attendance.

**Pack, page 1 — the CEO's letter, opening two paragraphs:**

> _"Two things changed this quarter and one of them is bad. We added four paying GSTINs, which is one behind the six I promised you in April. The bad one: two of the eleven live tenants have not opened the app in six weeks, and when I called both, the reason was the same — the person we trained left the pump. We have a training-dependency problem, not a product problem, and I did not see it because I was watching signups instead of weekly active dealers._
>
> _What I want from you today is a decision on one thing: whether we spend the next quarter on a second district or on making one district un-churnable. My recommendation is the second, and I know it looks like standing still…"_

**Dashboard row, reprinted promises:** _April promise: six paying GSTINs by 31 July. Actual: four. April promise: Easebuzz live by 30 June. Actual: live 12 July, twelve days late. April promise: DPT-3 filed on time. Actual: filed, on time._

**Decisions requested, item 2, pre-drafted:**

> _"RESOLVED THAT the Board hereby approves the leave-and-licence arrangement dated \<date\> between the Company and \<family entity\> in respect of premises at \<address\> used as the Company's registered office, at a monthly licence fee of ₹\<amount\>, being an arrangement with a related party within the meaning of the Act, the particulars of which have been placed before the Board; and THAT the said particulars be entered in the register of contracts in which directors are interested and disclosed in the Board's Report as required."_ — with the comparable-rent note attached as a paper, and the interested director's disclosure recorded before the item is put.

**The minute that matters, item 4:** _"Mr \<domain director\> did not concur with the resolution to defer the second district, for the reason that the dealer association meeting in \<district\> in October is an opportunity that will not repeat this year."_ Twelve months later, when the answer is known, that one sentence is worth more than the rest of the minute book — and it is the mechanism that makes disagreement survivable in a family boardroom.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                           | What it looks like                               | Fix                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pack sent the night before**                    | "I'll walk you through it in the meeting"        | Rule 1. Seven clear days with the notice, or move the meeting                                      |
| **The meeting is a presentation**                 | Slides, a projector, one voice for forty minutes | Rule 2. Taken as read; the chair asks what changed                                                 |
| **Bad news in the appendix**                      | Churn shows up on page 9                         | Rule 3. First half of the letter, in your own words                                                |
| **Three strategic discussions**                   | Every big question gets ten shallow minutes      | Rule 4. Exactly one. Park the rest with a date                                                     |
| **Decisions discussed, not resolved**             | "We all agreed" appears in the minute            | Rule 5. Numbered resolution language, pre-drafted                                                  |
| **A board-reserved matter passed by circulation** | Convenience, at speed                            | Rule 6. Walk the agenda against the non-delegable list first                                       |
| **No dissent, ever, across years**                | Family board, false consensus                    | Rule 7. Ask each director directly and record the answer                                           |
| **Minutes written at year end**                   | The CS drafts twelve months from a template      | Rule 10. One-page decision note to the CS the same week                                            |
| **Theatre**                                       | Fifty slides borrowed from a Series B deck       | Six to ten pages. The pack exists to make _you_ think, and to record what you believed at the time |
| **The advisor invited with no question**          | "Any thoughts?"                                  | Send them the pack **and one specific question** in advance                                        |
