_Phase 2 · The Outward Job · Months 1–3. ## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

There is a village temple near Raipur with a donation box — a hundi — bolted to the floor. The box has three locks and three different keys, held by three different people, and none of the three can open it alone. When it is opened, all three must stand there, and a fourth person — the schoolmaster, who is not family to any of them — writes in a long register: the date, who was present, how much was inside, and where the money went. Everybody signs. The register is kept in the temple, not in anyone's house.

Ask the villagers why the register exists and they will not say "to catch a thief." They will say something better: **it exists so that nobody has to be suspected.** The three key-holders are trusted men. They are trusted precisely because the register makes trust unnecessary. When somebody's cousin says "I heard the mandir money went into the road contract," the schoolmaster opens the register, and the rumour dies in one minute instead of splitting the village for a decade.

Now notice what happens the year they get lazy. Two of the three open it because the third is at a wedding. Nobody writes it down. The money is spent honestly — on the road, exactly as everybody agreed. Two years later there is a dispute, and there is no page to point at. Everyone remembers a different number. The honest men have no defence, because the only thing that ever defended them was the register, and they skipped it the one time it was inconvenient.

A board is the three locks. Minutes are the register. The outside advisor is the schoolmaster. And **the whole apparatus exists to protect the honest founder, not to catch the dishonest one** — because in an Indian Private Limited company the law does not ask whether you meant well. It asks whether the meeting happened, whether the interest was disclosed, and whether somebody wrote it down within thirty days.

## 1. Why governance matters at three people

### 1.1 The "too small for a board" trap

Every founder of a three-person company has said some version of this: _"A board? We sit in the same room. We decide things over chai. Governance is what you do after Series A."_

It is the most reasonable-sounding wrong sentence in Indian startup life, and it contains three distinct errors.

**Error one: it confuses the board with the boardroom.** VSYST already has a board. It was created on the day of incorporation and it has three members, because a Private Limited company must have a board of at least two directors ([CAIRR — s.149](https://ca2013.com/149-company-to-have-board-of-directors/)). The choice in front of you is not _board or no board_. It is **a board that meets, minutes and decides, or a board that exists only on the MCA master data and signs whatever the CS puts in front of it in September.** The second kind is not the absence of governance. It is bad governance, with full legal consequences attached.

**Error two: it treats governance as reporting.** Reporting is the smallest and least valuable thing a board does. The valuable things are: it gives a founder-CEO a boss; it forces a decision to be spoken out loud, on a date, with a person on the other side of the table asking "why?"; and it converts your private opinions into a written record you can be held to. A three-person company has no natural forcing function. Nobody schedules the CEO. Nobody makes you say the number out loud. The board meeting is the only recurring appointment where **the company, not the customer and not the codebase, is the subject.**

**Error three: it assumes the cost of skipping is deferred.** It is not deferred; it is accruing. §164(2) and §248 do not care that you are small.

Then there are the two soft failures, which arrive earlier and hurt sooner for a company like VSYST:

- **Failed diligence in a raise.** Every seed round in India runs a legal and secretarial diligence. The diligence list is, almost verbatim, §6 of this lesson plus the minute book. Missing minutes, unfiled MGT-14s, an undocumented director loan, no MBP-1s on file, rent paid to a founder's family with no board resolution — none of these kill a round on their own; together they add six to ten weeks and give the investor a discount lever they did not have to earn. [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|Lesson 08]] and [[C14-fundraise-readiness-and-data-room-index|C14]] carry the data-room index; the point here is that **the data room is assembled in the four years before the raise, not in the four weeks of it.**
- **Failed empanelment with a large counterparty.** An OMC like IOCL, a bank, or a payment gateway does not run a startup-friendly process. Vendor empanelment asks for incorporation documents, the last three years of filed financials, a board resolution authorising the signatory, and increasingly a security posture. A company whose board has not met since 2023 cannot produce a current board resolution authorising a signatory without back-dating one — which is exactly the act that converts an administrative lapse into [s.118(12)](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/) tampering, punishable with imprisonment up to two years. **The reason to keep the minute book current is that the alternative, under pressure, is a crime.**

Two habits follow from this table:

1. **Before every board meeting, walk the agenda against §179(3) and Rule 8.** Anything on the list must be a numbered resolution in the minutes, not a discussion item. This takes four minutes and prevents the most common small-company defect: a decision that was made but never _resolved_.
2. **Read your AoA once, properly, with the CS on the call.** The articles are the company's own constitution and can be stricter than the Act — they may reserve matters to shareholders that the Act leaves to the board, set a higher quorum, or restrict share transfers. Most founders have never read their own AoA. It takes ninety minutes and it is the cheapest governance work available to you.

### 2.5 What to check in the MoA and AoA — a concrete list

- [ ] **Object clause (MoA):** does it actually cover a multi-tenant SaaS platform, subscription billing and payment facilitation? A narrow object clause drafted in 2021 for something else is a diligence flag and a real problem when a gateway or an OMC asks.
- [ ] **Registered office clause and current INC-22 position** — the office is inside the family petrol-pump premises, which makes the occupancy arrangement a related-party fact (§3.4).
- [ ] **Authorised share capital** — is there enough headroom for an ESOP pool and a seed round without an AoA amendment first? ([[C16-term-sheet-decoder-and-cap-table|C16]], [[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design|C20]].)
- [ ] **Board composition articles** — minimum and maximum directors, whether any shareholder has a right to nominate a director, whether directors retire by rotation.
- [ ] **Quorum for board and general meetings** — the AoA may set a higher bar than s.174.
- [ ] **Casting vote of the chairperson** — does the chair have one? In a three-person board this is the difference between deadlock and decision.
- [ ] **Share transfer restrictions** — pre-emption rights, right of first refusal, and how a founder exit would actually work.
- [ ] **Borrowing powers** — does the AoA restrict the board where the Act does not?
- [ ] **Indemnity of directors** — does the AoA permit the company to indemnify directors and buy insurance for them? This is the hook D&O cover hangs on (§10).

Take the filled list to the CS and ask one question: _"Which of these will an investor's lawyer ask us to change, and can we change it now instead of during a round?"_

## 3. The director's duties — §166 in plain English

[Section 166](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors) is seven sentences long. It is the most important page of Indian company law that a founder-CEO will ever read, and almost none of them have. Below is each duty in the Act's language, in plain English, with a **concrete VSYST situation that would breach it** — because abstract duties do not change behaviour and specific scenarios do.

### 3.1 The seven duties

| #   | §166                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Plain English                                                                                                            | A VSYST situation that breaches it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **166(1)** — a director shall act in accordance with the **articles** of the company.                                                                                                                                                                   | Your own AoA binds you. Ignorance of it is not a defence.                                                                | The AoA requires a board resolution for any borrowing above a stated limit. The CEO takes an unsecured working-capital loan on a personal guarantee, transfers it into the company as a director loan, and nobody resolves anything. Breach — plus a DPT-3 problem (§6).                                 |
| 2   | **166(2)** — act **in good faith to promote the objects of the company** for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in the best interests of the company, its employees, the shareholders, the community and for the protection of the environment. | Your duty runs to _the company_, not to the founder who recruited you, not to the family, not to your own shareholding.  | The domain-expert director pushes a bespoke feature that helps the family pump's own operations but has no other tenant demand and costs six engineering weeks. Voting for it because it helps the family — rather than because it is good for VSYST — is a 166(2) breach dressed as a roadmap decision. |
| 3   | **166(3)** — exercise duties with **due and reasonable care, skill and diligence** and exercise **independent judgment**.                                                                                                                               | Show up prepared. Read the pack. Ask the question. Do not rubber-stamp.                                                  | A director signs the annual financial statements without reading them because "the CA prepared it and the CEO said it's fine." ### 3.2 Disclosure of interest — §184 and Form MBP-1

[Section 184](https://ca2013.com/184-disclosure-of-interest-by-director/) is the mechanism that makes §166(4) operable. Every director must disclose their concern or interest in any company, body corporate, firm or other association of individuals — **in Form MBP-1** — at:

- the **first Board meeting in which they participate** as a director, and
- the **first Board meeting in every financial year**, and
- the **first Board meeting after any change** in those interests.

And where a director is concerned or interested in a particular contract or arrangement, §184(2) requires disclosure and says the director "shall not participate in such meeting."

**The private-company modification matters here and is widely misunderstood.** Under notification GSR 464(E) dated 5 June 2015, §184(2) applies to a private company with a modification: **the interested director may participate in the meeting after disclosing their interest** — available only to a private company that has not defaulted in filing its financial statements or annual returns (amendment dated 13 June 2017) ([CAIRR — s.184](https://ca2013.com/184-disclosure-of-interest-by-director/)). So at VSYST an interested director can stay in the room and vote — _if_ the disclosure is made and _if_ the filings are clean.

Read that as the trade it is: **the relief is conditional on your filing discipline.** Let the annual returns slip and you lose not just this exemption but the §180 exemption in §2.4 as well. Good filing hygiene is not just avoidance of penalties; it is the price of the private-company reliefs you actually use.

Penalty for contravention of §184: CAIRR records imprisonment up to one year or a fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both — **VERIFY LIVE**, since this is exactly the kind of provision the 2020 amendment converted into a monetary penalty.

**The mechanism, not the intention:** MBP-1 from all three directors goes on the agenda of the first board meeting of every financial year, as a standing item, with the signed forms filed in the binder ([[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]) and reflected in the register under §189. Put it in [[C18-director-duties-and-governance-checklist|C18]] as an annual checkbox with a date.

### 3.3 "Relative" is a defined term — and in a family company it is the whole ballgame

Under §2(77) read with Rule 4 of the Companies (Specification of Definitions Details) Rules 2014, the following are **relatives**: members of a Hindu Undivided Family; spouse; **father (including step-father); mother (including step-mother); son (including step-son); son's wife; daughter; daughter's husband; brother (including step-brother); sister (including step-sister)** ([CAIRR — Rule 4](https://ca2013.com/rule-4-the-companies-specification-of-definitions-details-rules-2014/)).

That list is the trigger for "related party" under §2(76), which includes a director, a director's relative, KMP and their relatives, and firms and companies in which a director or their relative is a partner, director or member ([CAIRR — s.2](https://ca2013.com/definitions/)). **VERIFY LIVE** the exact clause list with the CS; §2(76) has several limbs and a residual "any other person as may be prescribed."

Write the list out for VSYST specifically — names, not categories — and keep it in [[C18-director-duties-and-governance-checklist|C18]]. In a company founded by three relatives operating out of a family petrol pump, the related-party perimeter is not an abstraction; it is most of your address book.

### 3.4 Related-party transactions — §188, the section a family-founded company must master

(a) sale, purchase or supply of any goods or materials; (b) selling or otherwise disposing of, or buying, property of any kind; (c) leasing of property of any kind; (d) availing or rendering of any services; (e) appointment of any agent for purchase or sale of goods, materials, services or property; (f) such related party's appointment to any **office or place of profit** in the company, its subsidiary or associate; (g) underwriting the subscription of any securities or derivatives.

### 3.5 Loans to directors — §185

Bootstrapped Indian founders move money in both directions instinctively. One direction is fine and one is a prohibition.

- **Director → company** (a founder puts money in) is a **director loan**. It is not §185; it is a deposit question, and it is reported annually in **DPT-3** (§6). It needs documentation: a loan agreement or at least a board-noted declaration that the money is the director's own funds and not borrowed, interest terms, and repayment terms.
- **Company → director** (the company advances money to a director or to persons in whom a director is interested) is [s.185](https://ca2013.com/185-loan-to-directors-etc/), and the starting position is **prohibition**: no company shall directly or indirectly advance any loan, or give any guarantee or provide any security in connection with any loan taken by, a director or a person in whom the director is interested.

The routes and the reliefs:

| Route                                  | Condition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | VSYST reading                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| §185(2) conditional route              | A **special resolution** in general meeting with full disclosure, and the loan must be used by the borrower for its **principal business activities**.                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Available but heavy.                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| §185(3) exceptions                     | Loans to an MD or WTD as part of conditions of service extended to all employees or under an approved scheme; loans in the ordinary course of a lending business at a rate not less than the prescribed government-security yield; holding-to-wholly-owned-subsidiary loans and guarantees.                                                                        | A salary advance to a founder-MD under a policy that applies to all employees is a different animal from an ad-hoc transfer. Get the CS to structure it.                                                                                   |
| Private-company exemption (GSR 464(E)) | §185 does not apply to a private company that: (a) has **no body corporate as a shareholder**; (b) has borrowings from banks/FIs/body corporates **less than twice its paid-up capital or ₹50 crore, whichever is lower**; and (c) has **no subsisting default** in repayment of such borrowings ([CAIRR — s.185](https://ca2013.com/185-loan-to-directors-etc/)). | VSYST probably qualifies today. **VERIFY LIVE** — and note that the exemption evaporates the day an investment vehicle (a body corporate) joins the cap table, which is precisely when a founder is most likely to be moving money around. |
| §185(4) penalties                      | Company fine ₹5 lakh–₹25 lakh; officer in default imprisonment up to six months or fine ₹5 lakh–₹25 lakh; the recipient similarly exposed. **VERIFY LIVE.**                                                                                                                                                                                                        | The floor is ₹5 lakh. There is no small-company relief.                                                                                                                                                                                    |

**The practical rule for a bootstrapped founder:** money going in is documented as a loan and reported in DPT-3; money coming out is either salary (on payroll, with TDS), reimbursement (against a bill), or repayment of a documented director loan (against the loan account) — and it is **never** an untitled transfer that the CA reclassifies at year end. That reclassification is where §185 problems are born.

### 3.6 The register of contracts — §189 and Form MBP-4

[Section 189](https://ca2013.com/189-register-of-contracts-or-arrangements-in-which-directors-are-interested/) requires the company to keep a register — **Form MBP-4** — of contracts or arrangements in which directors are interested, including §188 transactions, with entries made **at once, in chronological order**, and authenticated by the company secretary or a person authorised by the Board.

Two carve-outs worth knowing: contracts for the sale, purchase or supply of goods or services where the value **does not exceed ₹5 lakh in the aggregate in any year** need not be entered; and a director's interest of **2% or less** of paid-up share capital in a body corporate is outside the disclosure net. Penalty for a director who fails to comply: **₹25,000** ([CAIRR — s.189](https://ca2013.com/189-register-of-contracts-or-arrangements-in-which-directors-are-interested/)). **VERIFY LIVE.**

The register is maintained by the CS in practice. The CEO's job is different and non-delegable: **notice the transaction when it happens.** The register cannot record what nobody reported. Which is why §11's starter kit opens a related-party register in the same week it schedules the board dates.

### 4.2 A board that meets twice a year is a board that fails you

The relaxation permits two meetings. **Plan four.** The reasons are not legal:

- Four meetings put a floor under the strategy review cadence in [[19-planning-okrs-and-capital-allocation|lesson 19]] and give every quarter's OKRs a public reckoning.
- The 120-day rule in the default regime is a good discipline even when you are exempt from it — it means no decision waits more than four months for a legitimate forum.
- Two meetings a year means each one has eight months of business to cover, which guarantees the meeting becomes a status report and never a decision forum.
- The moment an investor or an outside advisor joins, four is the expected number anyway. Building the habit while it is cheap is the whole point of doing this at three people.

Put the four dates in the calendar for the whole year every April, invite the CS to all four, and put them in the `Board Calendar` tab of [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](toolkit/vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx).

### 4.3 Minutes — the only proof a decision was made

Under [s.118](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/):

- Minutes must be prepared, signed and **entered in the minute book within 30 days** of the conclusion of the meeting, containing "a fair and correct summary of the proceedings."
- Board minutes must record the **names of the directors present** and the **names of directors dissenting from, or not concurring with, a resolution** (s.118(4)).
- Matters that are defamatory, irrelevant or immaterial, or detrimental to the interests of the company may be excluded at the chairperson's discretion (s.118(5)).
- Minutes kept in accordance with the section are **evidence of the proceedings recorded**, and a properly minuted meeting is presumed to have been duly held (s.118(7)–(8)).
- Companies must **observe the secretarial standards** on board and general meetings specified by ICSI and approved by the Central Government (s.118(10)). In practice this adds process: draft minutes circulated to all directors within **15 days** of the meeting, directors' comments within **7 days**, entry within 30 ([TaxGuru — Section 118 minutes of board meeting](https://taxguru.in/company-law/section-118-of-companies-act-2013-minutes-board-meeting.html)). **VERIFY LIVE with the CS** — the standards are revised periodically.
- Penalty: **₹25,000 on the company and ₹5,000 on every officer in default** (s.118(11)). **Tampering with minutes: imprisonment up to two years and a fine of ₹25,000 to ₹1,00,000** (s.118(12)).

The pages are consecutively numbered, blank pages are scored off and initialled, the chairperson initials each page and signs the last, and the minute book lives at the registered office. A **separate minute book** is kept for each kind of meeting.

**What to actually write.** The failure mode in small companies is not fabrication; it is minutes so thin that nobody two years later can tell what was decided. A usable board minute records six things per item:

1. The item, in one sentence a stranger understands.
2. The papers placed before the board (name them — "the CEO's letter dated 12 May and the runway model v3").
3. The discussion, in three to six lines, **including the counter-argument** — not who said it, but that it was made.
4. The resolution, in resolution language, numbered.
5. **Any dissent, by name.** This is s.118(4) and it is also the mechanism in §8.
6. The action: owner and date.

Two rules of thumb. **If a decision authorises money, a signature, a person or a share, it must be a numbered resolution, not a discussion note.** And **if you would be embarrassed for an investor's lawyer to read it in diligence, that is a signal about the decision, not about the minute** — the fix is upstream.

## 5. The board pack

A board pack is **the document the board reads before the meeting so the meeting can be a conversation.** That single sentence contains the only rule that matters: **it is circulated in advance, never presented during.** Fred Wilson's practice is the standard — send the material ahead, and spend the meeting on discussion rather than reporting ([AVC — What A CEO Does](https://avc.com/2010/08/what-a-ceo-does/) and the AVC board-meeting canon). A pack read aloud is a pack that wasted three people's morning.

At three people the risk is not that the pack is too thin. It is **theatre**: fifty slides borrowed from a Series B deck, built to impress a board that consists of your brother and your cousin. Resist it. The pack exists to make _you_ think clearly, and to leave a record of what you believed at the time — which is exactly what [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]] is for.

**Contents — VSYST version, 6 to 10 pages, PDF, sent seven days ahead:**

| Section                       | What goes in it                                                                                                                              | Length    | Source                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. CEO's letter**           | Written in prose, first person, no bullets: what I believe, what changed, what I got wrong last quarter, what I want from you today.         | 1–2 pages | You. Nobody else drafts this.                                                                          |
| **2. Dashboard**              | The same metrics every quarter, same order, with plan vs actual and a trend. Never redesigned to flatter.                                    | 1 page    | [[17-the-numbers-a-ceo-watches\|lesson 17]]; `CEO Dashboard` tab                                       |
| **3. Cash and runway**        | Bank balance, monthly burn, months of runway, the 13-week view, and the date at which a decision becomes forced.                             | 1 page    | [[C13-runway-burn-and-scenario-planner\|C13]]; [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising\|lesson 08]]        |
| **4. Strategy-bet update**    | Each active bet from the kernel: the hypothesis, what evidence arrived this quarter, and whether you are continuing, changing or killing it. | 1–2 pages | [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager\|C04]]; [[05-strategy-i-diagnosis-and-the-strategy-kernel\|lesson 05]] |
| **5. Customers and pipeline** | Tenants live, pipeline, wins, and — more useful — **losses in the dealer's own words**.                                                      | ½ page    | [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales\|lesson 07]]                                              |
| **6. People**                 | Headcount, who joined or left, open roles, and any performance issue the board should not learn about later.                                 | ½ page    | [[10-building-the-team-hiring-equity-and-firing\|lesson 10]]                                           |
| **7. Risks**                  | Only what **changed**: a risk that got worse, a trigger that fired, an incident, a compliance date at risk.                                  | ½ page    | [[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance\|COO 13]] §4                                               |
| **8. Decisions requested**    | Numbered, each with a recommendation, the alternatives rejected, and pre-drafted resolution language.                                        | 1 page    | This lesson §4.4                                                                                       |
| **9. Appendix**               | Filings status from the CS, MBP-1s, related-party register extract, last minutes.                                                            | as needed | CS                                                                                                     |

Two disciplines make the pack honest:

- **The bad news goes in the CEO's letter, in the first half.** A board that finds a problem in the appendix stops trusting the letter. This is the "no surprises" rule from [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]] applied upward.
- **Last quarter's promises are reprinted verbatim** at the top of the dashboard page with a status against each. A board pack that never revisits its own commitments is a marketing document.

The template — agenda, pack outline, resolution language and a minutes skeleton — is [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]]. Fill it once and it becomes copy-paste-plus-edit forever.

## 7. Building a board before you have investors

You cannot appoint an independent director you cannot pay, and you should not hand a board seat to someone whose judgment you have not tested. What you can do — this year, at zero cash cost — is build an **advisory board**.

### 7.1 Advisor versus director — the distinction that must stay clean

|                       | **Advisor**                                               | **Director**                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legal status          | None under the Companies Act. A contractual relationship. | An officer of the company with a DIN, on MCA master data.                                                            |
| Duties                | Whatever the advisory agreement says.                     | The full [s.166](https://ca2013.com/166-duties-of-directors) set, plus s.184, s.188, s.189 and the rest.             |
| Liability             | Contractual.                                              | Personal, statutory, and includes "officer who is in default" exposure ([s.2(60)](https://ca2013.com/definitions/)). |
| Vote                  | None. Attends by invitation.                              | Votes; counts for quorum.                                                                                            |
| Can be removed        | Per the agreement — typically 30 days' notice.            | Only by the shareholders (s.169) or by resignation/vacation (s.168, s.167).                                          |
| Filing on appointment | None.                                                     | DIR-12 within 30 days; consent, DIN, DIR-8.                                                                          |
| Typical compensation  | Small equity grant, sometimes a fee.                      | Sitting fees and/or remuneration, governed by the Act.                                                               |

**The risk of calling someone a "director" who is not one is real and runs both ways.** Downward: an advisor whose card says "Director — Strategy," who signs an MoU, may bind the company through apparent authority, and a counterparty relying on the title has a case. Upward: a person who is not on the board but whose instructions the board habitually follows can be treated as a **promoter** under [s.2(69)(c)](https://ca2013.com/definitions/) ("in accordance with whose advice, directions or instructions the Board is accustomed to act") and can fall inside "officer who is in default" under the parallel limb of s.2(60) — liability without the seat.

House rule: **advisors are called advisors, in writing, on the website, on the card, and in the MoU signature block.** Nobody signs anything for VSYST who is not a director or a specifically authorised officer named in a board resolution.

### 7.2 Who to ask — three seats, deliberately different

| Seat                                      | Who                                                                                                                                                | What you get                                                                                                                                           | What they get                                                          | Where to find one for VSYST                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The fuel-industry veteran**             | A retired or semi-retired OMC executive, a large multi-outlet dealer from another state, or an ex-terminal/logistics manager                       | Reality-testing on the product loop, the OMC relationship, and what dealers will actually pay for; introductions that would take you two years to make | Relevance, a small stake, and the pleasure of being asked              | Dealer associations; the domain-expert director's network; the IOCL conversation itself — but never someone currently in a position to award you business (§7.5) |
| **The Indian SaaS operator**              | A founder or senior operator from an Indian SMB-SaaS company two to three stages ahead — ₹5–50 crore ARR, sold to small businesses, not enterprise | The pattern library you do not have: pricing, churn in SMB, sales-assisted GTM, when to hire the first AE, what breaks at 100 tenants                  | Deal flow, a small stake, and a live view of a sector they do not know | Indian SaaS communities (SaaSBoomi and similar); warm intros; the founders whose blog posts you already read                                                     |
| **The finance person who is not your CA** | An ex-CFO, a fractional CFO, or a finance leader from a product company                                                                            | Someone who reads the numbers adversarially, stress-tests the runway model, and asks the question the CA — who works for you — will not                | A small stake; a board they can influence                              | Your own network; the CA can _recommend_ one, and should not _be_ one                                                                                            |

**"Not your CA" is a design rule, not an insult.** Your CA and CS are paid vendors who file what you approve. An advisor's value is precisely that they are not on your payroll and can say the sentence a vendor will not.

### 7.3 What to offer — the Indian mechanics, honestly

The global default is the **FAST agreement** (Founder/Advisor Standard Template) from the Founder Institute, which is free and calibrated by stage and engagement: standard engagement (monthly meetings) **0.50% pre-seed / 0.25% seed / 0.10% Series A**; expert engagement (adds introductions and project work) **1.00% / 0.75% / 0.50%**; a **three-month cliff**, vesting over **two years**; and the sound recommendation that you work with someone for at least a month and about eight hours before proposing anything ([Founder Institute — FAST Agreement](https://fi.co/fast)).

Use the FAST numbers as an anchor. **But do not assume the instrument transplants**, because Indian equity mechanics are narrower than American ones:

| Instrument                                   | Who is eligible in India                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Practical view for VSYST                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **ESOP under s.62(1)(b) + Rule 12**          | Permanent employees, and directors (whole-time or not) — **excluding independent directors, promoters and promoter-group members, and directors holding more than 10% equity**. DPIIT-recognised startups get a **10-year** exemption from the promoter/director exclusions ([EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/esop-companies-act)). A private company may adopt a scheme by **ordinary** resolution; minimum **one-year** vesting. | **A pure advisor who is neither an employee nor a director is not obviously inside the Rule 12 "employee" definition.** Do not assume you can grant an advisor an ESOP. **VERIFY LIVE with the CS before promising anything.** |
| **Sweat equity shares (s.54)**               | Employees and directors, subject to rules on valuation, limits and lock-in                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | A formal route with real process. Ask the CS whether it fits an advisor. **VERIFY LIVE.**                                                                                                                                      |
| **Direct issue of a small number of shares** | Anyone, via the private-placement / preferential-allotment machinery                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Heavier process, and the shares are on the cap table permanently — vesting has to be created contractually.                                                                                                                    |
| **Cash retainer**                            | Anyone                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | The honest option when equity does not fit. A small monthly retainer or a per-meeting fee, with TDS.                                                                                                                           |
| **Appoint them as a non-executive director** | The advisor becomes a director                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Unlocks the ESOP/sweat-equity route — and hands them the entire §166 duty set and personal liability. Do not do this casually, and never do it just to solve a compensation problem.                                           |

**The tax question, stated plainly and then flagged.** For an employee, ESOP value is taxed as a **perquisite** at exercise (the difference between fair market value and exercise price), and again as **capital gains** on sale; eligible DPIIT-recognised startups have a TDS-deferral benefit. For a **non-employee advisor**, shares received for services are not employment perquisite — the amount is likely to be characterised as professional or business income, and separate provisions can apply where shares are received for inadequate consideration. **These are the two questions to put to the CA in writing before you make an offer: (1) what instrument can VSYST legally use for this person, and (2) who pays tax on what, and when.** Do not improvise. **VERIFY LIVE.**

### 7.4 The cadence that makes it real rather than decorative

An advisory board fails in one of two ways: nobody meets, or everybody meets and nothing changes. Both are prevented mechanically.

- **A one-page written agreement each.** Scope, expected time (e.g. one 60-minute call a month plus one board meeting a quarter), term (12 months, renewable), confidentiality, IP, the grant and its vesting with a cliff, no authority to bind the company, and a clean 30-day exit on either side.
- **A standing slot in the board meeting.** One advisor attends each quarterly board meeting as an invitee, with a named 15-minute slot on the agenda — not "any other business." Record their attendance as an invitee in the minutes.
- **A specific question, sent in advance.** "Here is the pack. The question I want you on is whether we should price per GSTIN or per outlet." Vague advisors give vague advice; the vagueness is usually the founder's fault.
- **A written follow-up within 48 hours.** What you heard, what you will do, what you rejected and why. This goes in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]].
- **A 12-month review.** Renew, re-scope or end it. An advisor whose grant keeps vesting while nothing happens is a tax on the cap table and a signal to everyone else that commitments here are decorative.

### 7.5 Conflicts — the row founders skip

Before you invite anyone: is this person currently in a position to award VSYST business, approve an empanelment, or influence a regulator or an OMC decision? If yes, an equity grant is not "aligning incentives" — it is a conflict, potentially a serious one, and it will surface in diligence or in a partner's own compliance review. **The advisor you want from the fuel industry is one who is out of the decision chain**, or the relationship is cash-only, disclosed, and cleared by the counterparty. [[14-partnerships-ecosystem-and-founder-relationships|Lesson 14]] and [[C26-partnership-evaluation-and-mou-checklist|C26]] carry the partner-side rules.

## 8. The independent voice a family founding team needs most

This is the most important section in the lesson, and it is the one with the least law in it. Nothing here is required by the Companies Act. All of it is required by arithmetic: **a board of three related directors will produce agreement whether or not agreement is warranted, and it will do so silently, without anyone lying, without anyone bullying, and without anyone noticing.**

Start from the honest premise. Family-founded businesses are not a fringe case in India; they are the economy. Family-owned enterprises are variously estimated to account for around **75–79% of India's GDP** and roughly **90% of listed companies** ([Deloitte India — family enterprises](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/india-s-family-enterprises-drive-economic-power.html); [PIB — Family Business Awards 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2080336); a McKinsey projection puts the figure at **80–85% by 2047**, [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/india-s-family-owned-biz-projected-to-drive-85-of-gdp-by-2047-mckinsey-124080101109_1.html) — **VERIFY LIVE**, these are survey figures with wide methodological variation). Family founding is normal, durable and often outperforms. What it does _not_ do by itself is generate disagreement, and disagreement is the raw material a board runs on.

### 8.1 How three related directors manufacture false consensus

Not one of the forces below requires bad faith. That is exactly why they are dangerous: every one of them looks like a virtue from the inside.

| Force                                           | What it looks like from inside                               | What it actually does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | The tell                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Role fusion**                                 | "We're family, we don't stand on ceremony."                  | The same person is simultaneously a brother, a shareholder and a director. An objection raised in the board seat is _received_ in the family seat. Renato Tagiuri and John Davis's three-circle model — family, ownership, business, developed at Harvard Business School in 1978 — exists precisely to name these as three different sets of interests that happen to sit in one body, and it maps the **seven** distinct positions the overlaps create ([John Davis — Three-Circle Model of the Family Business System](https://johndavis.com/three-circle-model-family-business-system/)). | Nobody can remember which hat they were wearing when a thing was agreed.                                                         |
| **The pre-decided decision**                    | "We already talked about this at home, let's not repeat it." | The board meeting becomes a ratification ceremony. The deliberation happened without a pack, without alternatives, without a record, and — crucially — without the one participant who might have disagreed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | The strategic discussion in the run-of-show (§4.4) finishes in four minutes.                                                     |
| **Dissent priced in family currency**           | "I didn't want to make it awkward."                          | In an ordinary board, opposing the CEO costs you ninety minutes of friction. Here it costs you the next family function. Because the price is paid outside the company, the company never sees the bill and never corrects.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | A director has strong private opinions that never appear in the room.                                                            |
| **Shared information**                          | "We all know the business."                                  | Nemeth's core finding is that dissent is valuable because it produces **divergent thinking** — it makes the group search more widely and consider information it would otherwise skip ([Charlan Nemeth, UC Berkeley](https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/faculty/charlan-jeanne-nemeth); _In Defense of Troublemakers_, Basic Books, 2018, [author's page](http://charlannemeth.com/in-defense-of-troublemakers/)). Three people in one office, one WhatsApp group and one customer set have almost nothing to diverge _from_.                                                                    | Everyone cites the same three dealers as evidence.                                                                               |
| **Silent monopolies of deference**              | "He's the technical one." / "He knows the trade."            | The technical CEO is unchallenged on product, the domain director unchallenged on fuel distribution. This reads as respect. It functions as two unaudited kingdoms — and the largest capital allocations at VSYST (engineering weeks, and which dealer segment to chase) sit squarely inside them.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | No one has ever asked the CEO to justify a build-vs-buy call, or the domain director to show the evidence behind a market claim. |
| **A legal permission mistaken for a good idea** | "The CS said it's fine for us to vote on it."                | The private-company modification to §184(2) means an interested director at VSYST may participate after disclosing (§3.2). That is a compliance relief. It is not a judgment relief.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | An interested director's own item passes 3–0 and the minutes record no discussion.                                               |

The compound effect has a name. Irving Janis called it **groupthink** — the deterioration of judgment that comes from a cohesive group's pressure toward unanimity, with symptoms including the illusion of unanimity, self-censorship and unquestioned belief in the group's own morality ([Janis, _Victims of Groupthink_, 1972 — summary, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/groupthink)). Janis studied crisis cabinets. The mechanism is identical at a table of three in Raipur, and the cohesion is stronger, because the group does not disband when the meeting ends.

And Alfred Sloan's response to it remains the most useful sentence any chairperson has ever spoken. Drucker records it in _Management: Tasks, Responsibilities, Practices_:

> _"Gentlemen, I take it we are all in complete agreement on the decision here."_ Everyone around the table nodded assent. _"Then,"_ continued Mr. Sloan, _"I propose we postpone further discussion of this matter until our next meeting, to give ourselves time to develop disagreement and perhaps gain understanding of what the decision is all about."_ ([Fortune — what every leader can learn from Alfred P. Sloan](https://fortune.com/2015/12/07/alfred-p-sloan-decision-making/))

Sloan treated unanimity as **missing information**, not as confirmation. That is the posture a family board has to install deliberately, because it will never arrive naturally.

### 8.2 The diagnostic — six questions, answered from the minute book, not from memory

Do this before you build anything. It takes twenty minutes and it is uncomfortable, which is the point.

| #   | Question                                                                                                                         | What a healthy answer looks like | What VSYST is likely to find                           |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **How many dissents or abstentions are recorded by name in the minute book since incorporation?**                                | At least a few, across years.    | Zero.                                                  |
| 2   | **In the last four decisions above ₹1 lakh, how many had a written alternative that was considered and rejected?**               | All four, in the minute.         | None — the alternatives existed in someone's head.     |
| 3   | **When did a director last change their position inside a meeting because of something said in it?**                             | You can name the meeting.        | You cannot.                                            |
| 4   | **When was the CEO's recommendation last rejected or materially amended by the board?**                                          | At least once a year.            | Never.                                                 |
| 5   | **Who, in the last twelve months, told you a plan of yours was wrong — outside your own head?**                                  | A named person, with a date.     | A customer, maybe. Nobody in the company.              |
| 6   | **If you removed the CEO from the room, would the other two directors reach a different conclusion on the current biggest bet?** | You genuinely do not know.       | You are certain they would not — which is the problem. |

**Read question 1 correctly.** A minute book with zero recorded dissent across every meeting since incorporation is not evidence of a board that agrees. It is evidence of a board that **does not decide** — because a body that never disagrees is not choosing between options, it is receiving them. And that record has a second life: in diligence, and in any later inquiry into a decision that went wrong, "the board considered and approved" carries weight in proportion to the evidence that the board actually _considered_.

### 8.3 Mechanism 1 — an outside advisor with a standing agenda slot, who speaks first

§7 told you who to recruit and how to pay them. This is the part that gives the arrangement teeth.

- **A named slot on the agenda, in the run-of-show, every quarter.** Not "any other business." The advisor's 15 minutes sit inside the one strategic discussion (§4.4, minutes 35–60), not after it.
- **The outsider speaks first on the strategic item.** This one rule does more work than everything else in this section. If the CEO frames the question and states a recommendation before the outsider speaks, the outsider is now responding to an anchor set by the most powerful person in the room. Reverse the order: the question is circulated in the pack, the advisor gives their reading cold, and only then does the CEO give theirs.
- **The advisor gets the full pack, same as directors, seven days ahead** — under NDA, with the confidentiality clause from their §7.4 agreement.
- **Their attendance is minuted properly.** SS-1 requires the minutes to record invitees, "the capacity in which an Invitee attends the Meeting and where applicable, the name of the entity such Invitee represents and the relation, if any, of that entity to the company" ([ICSI — SS-1, revised version effective 1 April 2024, para 7.2.1.2](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/SS-1_1_2024.pdf)). Do it properly and the advisor's presence is part of the record that the decision was informed.
- **Rotate which advisor attends**, so no single outsider becomes a fourth member of the same consensus. Three advisors, one per quarter, plus whoever is relevant to the year-end meeting.

**The failure mode to watch:** the advisor who is charming, agrees with you, and is invited back precisely because of that. If an advisor has never made you uncomfortable in four meetings, they are a supporter, not an advisor, and the seat is being wasted. Say so at the 12-month review.

### 8.4 Mechanism 2 — the rotating devil's advocate, and the honest caveat about it

The classic prescription, from Janis onward, is to assign someone the job of arguing against the proposal. Rotate it so it is a role, not a personality, and so no one director becomes "the negative one" — which in a family board is a reputation with consequences at home.

**But present the counter-evidence, because it is strong.** Charlan Nemeth's experimental work found that role-played devil's advocacy does **not** stimulate divergent thinking or improve decision quality the way **authentic dissent** does; people discount an argument they know is being performed ([Nemeth, Rogers and Brown, "Devil's advocate versus authentic dissent," _European Journal of Social Psychology_, 2001, described in Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/stop-playing-devils-advocate-and-other-advice-for-better-decision-making/); [EconTalk interview with Nemeth](https://www.econtalk.org/charlan-nemeth-on-in-defense-of-troublemakers/)). Her recommendation is to build dissent into the **process** rather than the theatre — she cites an investment firm that requires a written "contra-memo," worked on over several days, seriously arguing against a proposed investment.

**The house call for VSYST:** keep the rotation, but upgrade the artefact. The devil's advocate does not perform in the room; they **write, in advance, and circulate with the pack.**

| Form of manufactured dissent                         | Strength                                                                  | Weakness                                                                              | Fit at VSYST today                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **In-room devil's advocate**                         | Free; takes ten seconds to institute                                      | Nemeth: role-played dissent is discounted and does not produce divergent thinking     | Use only as a fallback when nothing was written                                                                                                                   |
| **Written contra-memo, circulated with the pack**    | Forces real search for evidence; survives the meeting; goes in the record | Costs the author two to three hours per quarter                                       | **The default. One director writes it, rotating each quarter.**                                                                                                   |
| **Outside advisor speaking first** (§8.3)            | Genuinely different information base; authentic                           | Only as good as the advisor, and only quarterly                                       | **Yes — pair it with the contra-memo**                                                                                                                            |
| **Pre-mortem** ("it is 2028 and this failed — why?") | Cheap, fast, surfaces risks the plan hides                                | Not a substitute for a real dissenter                                                 | Once a year in the annual planning meeting; owned by [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession\|C30]] and [[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things\|lesson 18]] |
| **Authentic dissent from a real director**           | The gold standard                                                         | Cannot be scheduled; only appears if the culture pays for it rather than punishing it | The goal all five mechanisms exist to make possible                                                                                                               |

The contra-memo is one page and answers four questions: **what would have to be true for this to be a mistake; what evidence would we expect to see if it already were; what is the best alternative we are not taking; and what does the person who loses from this decision say about it.** File it with the board pack. It goes in the appendix and it is named in the minutes as a paper placed before the board (§4.3).

### 8.5 Mechanism 3 — a written dissent record, which the law already gives you

You do not have to invent this one. It is sitting unused in your own statute book.

| Provision                                                                                                                                                            | What it says                                                                                                                                                                                     | How to use it deliberately                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [s.118(4)(b)](https://ca2013.com/minutes-of-proceedings-of-general-meeting-meeting-of-board-of-directors-and-other-meeting-and-resolutions-passed-by-postal-ballot/) | Board minutes must record "the names of the directors, if any, dissenting from, or not concurring with the resolution."                                                                          | The record already has a field for dissent. Zero entries is a choice, not a fact of life.                                                                                                                                   |
| [SS-1 para 7.2.2.1(m)](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/SS-1_1_2024.pdf)                                                                                        | Minutes shall contain "the fact of the dissent and the name of the Director who dissented from the Resolution **or abstained from voting** thereon."                                             | **Abstention is a first-class option.** A director who is not persuaded abstains and it is recorded. That is far easier for a brother to do than to vote against a brother — and it produces the same signal in the record. |
| [SS-1 para 7.2.2.1(k)](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/SS-1_1_2024.pdf)                                                                                        | Minutes shall contain "the views of the Directors … if specifically insisted upon by such Directors," subject to the Chairman's opinion that they are not defamatory, irrelevant or detrimental. | Any director may **insist** their reasoning goes in the book. Agree in advance, as a board, that the chair will always allow it — because the chair's decision on this is final (SS-1 para 7.3.2).                          |
| [SS-1 para 7.2.2.2](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/SS-1_1_2024.pdf)                                                                                           | Minutes shall "mention the brief background of all proposals and summarise the deliberations thereof. In case of major decisions, the rationale thereof shall also be mentioned."                | This is the hook for the house rule below.                                                                                                                                                                                  |

**Two house rules, adopted by resolution at your first proper board meeting so they bind future you:**

1. **Every material resolution's minute records the counter-argument, whether or not anyone dissented.** One sentence: _"The Board considered pricing per outlet rather than per GSTIN, and rejected it because outlet counts change monthly and would make invoicing unstable."_ Nobody had to dissent for the alternative to be recorded. This satisfies SS-1 7.2.2.2 and, far more importantly, it means that in two years you can reconstruct why — which is the whole purpose of [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]].
2. **A director who is unpersuaded abstains rather than nods.** And the abstention is minuted under 7.2.2.1(m), with a one-line reason under 7.2.2.1(k) if they want it.

**The self-interested reason to do this.** §166(3) requires a director to exercise "independent judgment." If a decision later goes wrong — a related-party arrangement is challenged, a borrowing sours, a filing failure is investigated — a director whose dissent or abstention is on the record is in a materially different position from one whose name sits under a unanimous resolution. **The dissent record protects the dissenter, and the counter-argument record protects everyone else** by showing the board actually deliberated. Both are free. Neither exists unless the chair asks for them out loud, every time.

### 8.6 Mechanism 4 — "Who would disagree with this, and what would they say?"

One question, asked before every material decision, out loud, by the chair, and answered by a specific person — not by the room.

The rule that makes it work is that **the answer must be an argument, not a name.** "Ramesh would disagree" is not an answer. "The strongest case against is that per-GSTIN pricing punishes the multi-GSTIN dealers who are our best accounts, and we have no evidence yet on how many of those exist" is an answer.

Use three variants, because they surface different things:

| Variant                | The question                                                                                                 | What it catches                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The absent expert**  | "If our SaaS advisor were sitting here, what is the first objection she would make?"                         | Pattern-library errors — pricing, churn, hiring sequence — that three people in one industry cannot see                                                         |
| **The dealer's chair** | "What does the Bilaspur dealer say about this on WhatsApp the day it ships?"                                 | Decisions that are correct internally and unusable externally. Ties to [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales\|lesson 07]]                                |
| **Two years later**    | "It is 2028, this decision was a mistake, and we are explaining it to an investor. What is the explanation?" | One-way doors dressed as reversible ones. Ties to [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log\|C29]] and [[C30-pre-mortem-crisis-playbook-and-succession\|C30]] |

Then apply Sloan's rule as a hard stop: **if a material item reaches unanimity with no counter-argument on the table, it is deferred to the next meeting**, or — where deferral is genuinely impossible — it passes with a written note in the minutes saying that no counter-argument was identified, which is itself an uncomfortable sentence to have to sign, and is meant to be.

There is a matching cultural half. Amazon's leadership principle "Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit" states that leaders "are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting … They do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly" ([Amazon — Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles)). The second half is the part a family board needs as much as the first: **disagreement ends at the resolution.** A director who dissents and then relitigates the decision in the corridor for six months has not been independent, they have been obstructive, and the family will learn to suppress dissent all over again. [[11-culture-and-values-as-a-ceo-instrument|Lesson 11]] and [[C21-values-and-operating-principles-worksheet|C21]] own the culture side of this; the board's version is one line in the minutes: **the decision was taken, and all directors committed to it.**

### 8.7 Mechanism 5 — separate the shareholder conversation from the family one

This is the hardest of the five and the one that prevents the most damage, because the conflicts that end family companies are almost never about strategy. They are about **money, roles and fairness** — and those are shareholder questions being argued in a family room, or family questions being argued in a board room, with no minutes and no rules in either case.

Run three separate conversations, deliberately, with different rules:

|                                      | **The board conversation**                                                                              | **The shareholder conversation**                                                                  | **The family conversation**                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Who is in the room**               | The three directors + invited advisor                                                                   | The shareholders (today, the same three — but as owners)                                          | Whoever the family decides, including spouses and the next generation                                        |
| **What it decides**                  | Strategy, budget, appointments, related-party approvals, borrowings — everything in §2.4's board column | Share issues, AoA changes, dividends, admitting an investor, founder exits, transfer restrictions | Nothing about the company                                                                                    |
| **Cadence**                          | Quarterly, minuted (§4)                                                                                 | At the AGM and whenever a resolution is needed                                                    | Quarterly or half-yearly, at home, deliberately not on a board day                                           |
| **What is written**                  | Minute book, s.118 + SS-1                                                                               | Minutes of general meetings; register of members                                                  | A short family charter or set of understandings — **not** a minute book, and with no company decisions in it |
| **What is explicitly out of bounds** | Who is respected, who works hardest, old grievances                                                     | Operating decisions                                                                               | Any decision on strategy, salary, hiring, equity or a related-party contract                                 |
| **Fit at VSYST today**               | Start now (§11)                                                                                         | Runs on the statutory calendar (§6)                                                               | Start now, and keep it genuinely separate — same three people, different room, different day                 |

Four rules make the separation real rather than nominal:

1. **Say which hat you are wearing, in words.** "I'm saying this as a shareholder, not as a director" is not corporate theatre; it is the cheapest available clarification, and after a month it stops feeling strange. The three-circle model is only useful if the circles are named out loud when they conflict.
2. **Money moves only in the formal rooms.** Founder salary, dividends, director loans, the family pump's occupancy of the registered office, a relative's employment, a relative's firm as vendor — all of it is decided on a board agenda with the §189 register open (§3.4, §3.6), never at dinner, and never "we'll sort it later." The register cannot record what nobody reported, and nobody reports what was agreed over food.
3. **Decide the deadlock rule while everyone still agrees.** Does the chair have a casting vote under your AoA (§2.5)? What happens if two directors want to sell the company and one does not? What are the exit terms if a founder leaves — buyback price, vesting, who keeps what? These belong in the founders' agreement and the AoA now, in a calm year, and they are the subject of [[C02-founders-agreement-and-vesting-checklist|C02]] and [[03-the-ceo-core-value-and-the-founder-contracts|lesson 03]].
4. **Remember whose piece of paper wins.** In Indian law the **articles** are the company's constitution, and the Supreme Court in _V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan_ (AIR 1992 SC 453) held that a restriction agreed between shareholders but not incorporated in the articles is not binding on the company ([case analysis — Agrud Partners](https://agrudpartners.com/case-analysis-v-b-rangaraj-v-v-b-gopalakrishnan/); discussion of the line of cases, [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/shareholders/1089446/enforcing-shareholders-agreements-in-india-a-legal-laggard)). Family understandings — however sincere, however old — are worth less than one clause in the AoA. **VERIFY LIVE with the company lawyer**; the case law has developed since 1992 and the treatment of private-company share-transfer restrictions has been argued both ways.

### 8.8 The independent-director question, answered honestly

Founders read about independent directors and assume they must appoint one. You must not, and you probably cannot afford to.

- **§149(4) applies to listed public companies** (at least one-third of the board independent). Rule 4 of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules 2014 extends a two-independent-director requirement to certain **unlisted public** companies — indicatively paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore, or turnover ≥ ₹100 crore, or aggregate outstanding loans/debentures/deposits > ₹50 crore ([CAIRR — s.149](https://ca2013.com/149-company-to-have-board-of-directors/)). **VERIFY LIVE.**
- **A private limited company is outside all of it.** VSYST has no obligation to appoint an independent director, and appointing one prematurely means handing full §166 liability, Schedule IV obligations and a DIN filing burden to a person you are paying nothing.
- **The honest substitute is §7's advisory board plus §8.3–8.7.** You are buying the _function_ — an unaligned voice with different information — without the _form_.
- **The trigger to revisit:** the day a term sheet lands (§9), the day the company converts to a public company or begins an IPO path, or the day a large counterparty's governance questionnaire asks. At that point a real independent director is recruited from a real pool — the IICA's **Independent Director's Databank**, which the Act's rules require independent directors to register with ([Independent Director's Databank, IICA](https://www.independentdirectorsdatabank.in/)). **VERIFY LIVE** the registration and proficiency-test requirements with the CS before anyone is appointed.

### 8.9 What "teeth" actually looks like — the one-year test

Twelve months after you install these mechanisms, you should be able to say all five of these sentences and point at the evidence:

1. **"Here is a minute, by date, recording a dissent or an abstention by name."**
2. **"Here are four contra-memos, one per quarter, each written by a different director."**
3. **"Here is a decision the board rejected or materially changed after an outside advisor spoke."**
4. **"Here is the counter-argument recorded in the minute for every resolution above ₹1 lakh."**
5. **"Here is the date of the last family conversation, and here is the date of the last board meeting, and they are different days."**

If you can say four of the five, the board is real. If you can say none of them after a year of meetings, you have rebuilt the same three-person consensus with better stationery — and the mechanisms failed not because they are wrong but because the chair stopped asking. **Every one of these depends on one person saying one uncomfortable sentence out loud, on schedule. That person is you.**

## 9. What changes when investors join the board

The day money arrives, the board stops being a room you convene and becomes a body you report to. Nothing in §1–§8 is wasted — but three things change at once, and founders who have not thought about them in advance negotiate them badly, in a week, while also trying to close a round.

### 9.1 Director or observer — and why the difference is not a formality

|                        | **Investor director**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | **Board observer**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Status                 | A director of VSYST, with a DIN, on MCA master data, filed by DIR-12                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | A contractual invitee. No statutory status at all                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Duties                 | The **full §166 set**, owed to _VSYST_ — not to the fund that nominated them                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Whatever the observer letter says. No fiduciary duty to the company                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Vote / quorum          | Votes; counts for quorum; can block                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Neither                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Information            | Board pack, papers, registers                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Board pack and financials, by contract — "information rights equivalent to a director without the associated fiduciary duties" ([Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance — The Board Observer](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/07/02/the-board-observer-considerations-and-limitations/)) |
| Can be excluded        | Only for interest under §184(2)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Yes — companies routinely reserve the right to exclude observers from conflicted, litigation or competitively sensitive items. **Negotiate that right in.**                                                                                                                                             |
| Indian mechanic        | Appointed under the AoA's nomination right; §161(3) nominee-director machinery is narrow and institution-specific ([CAIRR — s.161](https://ca2013.com/161-appointment-of-additional-director-alternate-director-and-nominee-director/)), so in practice the AoA is amended to create the seat | A letter. Nothing filed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Fit at VSYST today** | None yet. At a first institutional round expect **one** investor seat on a board of four                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Often the better ask from _your_ side for a small cheque: same information, no vote, no dilution of board control                                                                                                                                                                                       |

**The sentence to remember:** an investor director's legal duty runs to VSYST, not to their fund. That is protective in law and thin in practice — which is exactly why the reserved-matters list below exists.

### 9.2 Reserved matters — the veto list

A reserved-matters (or affirmative-vote) clause lists the decisions the company cannot take without the investor's consent, "irrespective of what the majority of shareholders or the board decides" ([Treelife — Reserved matters in an SHA](https://treelife.in/legal/reserved-matters-in-sha/)). The global reference set is the NVCA's model documents — the Certificate of Incorporation's protective provisions and the Investors' Rights Agreement ([NVCA — Model Legal Documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/)); Indian SHAs borrow the structure and add the Companies Act plumbing.

| Typical reserved matter                                                 | Why an investor wants it                            | What it costs a founder-CEO                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Amending the MoA or AoA; altering share capital; issuing new securities | Protects against dilution and constitutional change | Slows any ESOP-pool expansion or bridge round                                               |
| Approving the **annual budget and business plan**                       | The single most powerful item on the list           | Your operating plan now needs an outside yes. Plan the plan earlier                         |
| Borrowing beyond a stated limit; creating security                      | Protects the equity's position                      | Kills the informal director-loan habit (§3.5)                                               |
| **Related-party transactions** above a threshold                        | Directly aimed at family-founded companies          | Your §3.4 list becomes an investor-consent list. If it is already clean, this costs nothing |
| Changing the core business; M&A; selling material assets                | Protects the thesis they bought                     | Strategy pivots become negotiated, not announced                                            |
| Appointing or removing the CEO/KMP; changing founder compensation       | The accountability lever                            | See §9.5                                                                                    |
| Winding up, liquidation, dividend                                       | Standard                                            | Rarely bites early                                                                          |

Two Indian mechanics that decide whether any of this is real:

1. **The list must go into the AoA, not only the SHA.** A restriction agreed between shareholders but absent from the articles is not binding on the company (§8.7, _V.B. Rangaraj_ — [Agrud Partners](https://agrudpartners.com/case-analysis-v-b-rangaraj-v-v-b-gopalakrishnan/)). Investors' lawyers know this and will require an AoA amendment by special resolution. **This is why §2.5 tells you to read your articles now** — you will be amending them under time pressure otherwise.
2. **Private-company exemptions start falling away.** The §185 relief on loans to directors is conditioned on having **no body corporate as a shareholder** (§3.5) — the day a fund's vehicle appears on the cap table, it is gone. And the §184(2) and §180 modifications ride on unbroken filing discipline (§2.4, §3.2). **An investor round is the moment your governance shortcuts expire.** Fix them before, not during.

### 9.3 Information rights — what you will be signing up to produce

| Deliverable                                                         | Typical timing             | Who produces it at VSYST                             | Already exists?                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Monthly MIS — revenue, cash, burn, headcount, key operating metrics | 15–30 days after month end | COO, from the scorecard ([[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]]) | Mostly — the discipline, not the format, is the gap |
| Quarterly unaudited financials + board pack                         | With the board notice      | CEO + CA                                             | §5 is the pack. Build it now                        |
| Annual audited financials + Board's Report                          | Per §6's calendar          | CA + CS                                              | Statutory anyway                                    |
| Annual budget for approval                                          | Before the year starts     | CEO, from [[C28-annual-plan-and-ceo-okr-sheet\|C28]] | The forcing function most founders lack             |
| Cap table on demand                                                 | On request                 | CEO, `Cap Table & Dilution` tab                      | [[C16-term-sheet-decoder-and-cap-table\|C16]]       |
| Inspection / audit rights                                           | On notice                  | COO's binder ([[T02-company-binder-checklist\|T02]]) | This is the diligence of §1.3, forever              |

**The honest read:** almost every one of these is something a well-run company produces for itself. If producing them feels like a burden imposed by investors, the burden was always there and you were deferring it. Start the monthly MIS and the quarterly pack **now**, at three people, and the round adds a distribution list rather than a new machine.

### 9.4 The board's shift — from advisory to accountability

| Dimension               | Pre-investment board                         | Post-investment board                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purpose                 | Advice you may take or leave                 | Oversight you must answer to                                                                                           |
| The CEO's position      | Chairs, sets the agenda, controls the record | Still runs the meeting — but is now the person being evaluated in it                                                   |
| Last quarter's promises | A discipline you chose (§5)                  | An evaluation instrument                                                                                               |
| Bad news                | Awkward                                      | **Existential if it arrives late.** Late bad news is the single most common reason boards lose confidence in a founder |
| Composition maths       | 3 founders                                   | Often 2 founders + 1 investor + 1 independent — a board where founders can be outvoted long before they are out-owned  |
| What "no" means         | A conversation                               | A reserved matter                                                                                                      |

That composition row is the one to sit with. Noam Wasserman's data on technology start-ups found that **by the time ventures were three years old, 50% of founders were no longer CEO; by year four only 40% were still in the corner office; and fewer than 25% led their companies' IPOs** — and, pointedly for VSYST, that in a study of 450 privately held ventures "outside investors control the board more often where the CEO is a founder, where the CEO has a background in science or technology" ([Wasserman, "The Founder's Dilemma," _Harvard Business Review_, 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma)). A technical founder-CEO is precisely the profile investors are most likely to want to supervise. Knowing that in advance is not pessimism; it is how you negotiate board composition rather than accept it.

### 9.5 How a founder-CEO keeps a board that can fire them useful rather than performative

The instinct is to manage the board — to present, to reassure, to control the narrative. That instinct produces a performative board: polished, agreeable, and useless right up to the meeting where it removes you. The opposite discipline is cheap and it compounds.

| Practice                                    | What it is                                                                             | Why it works                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **No surprises, ever**                      | Anything material reaches board members **before** the pack, by phone, individually    | A board's confidence is built almost entirely on the accuracy of its expectations, not on the quality of the news                                       |
| **Bad news first, in the CEO's letter**     | §5's rule, now with consequences                                                       | A board that finds a problem in the appendix stops reading the letter and starts reading you                                                            |
| **Pre-wire every hard item**                | One-to-one calls in the week before the meeting; hear each objection privately first   | Meetings are for deciding, not discovering. Also surfaces the objection that nobody would voice in the room                                             |
| **Ask for something specific each meeting** | An intro, a hire, a customer, a judgment call                                          | A board asked for nothing gives advice; a board asked for something delivers work and becomes invested                                                  |
| **Reprint your own commitments**            | Last quarter's promises verbatim, with status                                          | You are the person keeping score of you. That is the strongest position available                                                                       |
| **Run an executive session**                | Fifteen minutes at the end without management present, then the chair debriefs the CEO | Normalise it _before_ it is needed. A board that has never met without you cannot tell you the truth, and the first time it does will be the wrong time |
| **Keep the outside advisor slot**           | §8.3 does not stop at Series A                                                         | The investor director is not an independent voice — they have their own book                                                                            |
| **Do not out-argue the room**               | Win on evidence or lose the item                                                       | A founder who wins every board argument has a board that has stopped arguing — §8 again, with more money at stake                                       |

**And the part nobody says out loud:** your protection is not control of the board. Control is temporary and dilutes on schedule. Your protection is **a two-year record of promises made in writing and met** — which is exactly what §4's minutes and §5's pack produce, whether or not an investor ever reads them.

### 9.6 What to do now, before any term sheet exists

1. **Read the AoA** for existing nomination rights, transfer restrictions and quorum (§2.5) — the amendment list an investor's lawyer will hand you is largely predictable.
2. **Clean the filings.** Every private-company exemption you rely on is conditioned on them (§3.2, §2.4).
3. **Close the related-party gaps** — rent, vendors, the family pump as tenant — with proper resolutions and register entries (§3.4). These will be a reserved matter in six months; make them boring first.
4. **Start the monthly MIS and the quarterly pack now** (§5, §9.3).
5. **Decide your board-composition red line in advance** and write it in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]: how many seats, who appoints the chair, and what you will not trade. [[08-capital-runway-and-fundraising|Lesson 08]] and [[C16-term-sheet-decoder-and-cap-table|C16]] own the negotiation itself.

## 10. Risk, insurance and personal exposure for a director

Founders think about risk as something that happens to the company. Half of the exposure in an Indian Private Limited company happens to **you** — the natural person, the bank account, the DIN. Limited liability limits your loss _as a shareholder_. It does very little for you _as a director_.

Three layers, and they do not substitute for each other:

| Layer                               | What it is                                                                                             | Who owns it                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Statutory personal liability** | Sections of the Companies Act, the tax laws and the IT Act that reach past the company to the director | **You.** Mostly uninsurable. Managed by doing the thing correctly, on time                                     |
| **2. Insurable liability**          | Claims that a policy will defend and pay — D&O, professional indemnity, cyber                          | CEO decides what to buy and when; **COO places it** ([[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance\|COO 13]] §6) |
| **3. Enterprise risk**              | The scored register of things that could go wrong, reviewed quarterly                                  | **COO** — [[T15-risk-register\|T15]]. The board sees only what _moved_ (§5)                                    |

### 10.1 Where personal exposure actually comes from

| Exposure                                                                   | Provision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Reaches you how                                                                                                                                    | Insurable?                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |

Read the two tax rows twice. **They are private-company-specific, they are not about fraud, and the burden of proof is on the director.** The escape clause — proving that non-recovery was not attributable to your gross neglect, misfeasance or breach of duty — is proved with exactly the artefacts this lesson has been building: minutes, a compliance calendar, evidence that the board reviewed dues, evidence that money was not moved out ahead of the department. **VERIFY LIVE with the CA** — both sections have live case law and the procedural safeguards have been tightened by High Courts.

### 10.2 D&O insurance — the hook, the trigger and the price

**The legal hook already exists in your Act.** §197(13) provides that where insurance is taken by a company on behalf of its MD, WTD, manager, CEO, CFO or company secretary indemnifying them against liability for negligence, default, misfeasance, breach of duty or breach of trust, **the premium is not treated as part of their remuneration** — with the sting in the proviso: if the person is **proved guilty**, the premium _is_ treated as remuneration ([CAIRR — s.197](https://ca2013.com/197-overall-maximum-managerial-remuneration-and-managerial-remuneration-in-case-of-absence-or-inadequacy-of-profits/)). That single sub-section tells you both that the company may buy it and what it will never cover.

**Check the AoA first.** Does it permit the company to indemnify directors and to buy insurance for them? That is the checkbox already sitting in §2.5, and it is the one clause you want fixed _before_ you need the policy.

| Trigger                                                              | Should VSYST buy D&O?                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Three founder-directors, pre-revenue, no employees, no outside money | **No.** Almost every realistic claim against you today is either uninsurable (tax, disqualification, fraud) or non-existent (no shareholder to sue you, no employee to claim) |
| **First outside director or independent director**                   | **Yes — before they join.** No competent outsider takes an Indian board seat without cover, and asking them to is the fastest way to lose the person you most need (§8)       |
| **First institutional round**                                        | **Yes.** Usually a closing condition; the NVCA's model set even includes a standalone Indemnification Agreement ([NVCA](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/))             |
| First employees at scale / first termination dispute                 | **Yes** — employment practices liability is the most common real-world D&O claim for a small company                                                                          |
| A large enterprise, OMC or bank counterparty asks                    | Often bundled into their vendor questionnaire alongside the items in [[certification-and-standards-roadmap\|Certification and Standards Roadmap]]                             |

**Price — and an honest warning about published numbers.** Indian sources disagree wildly. One broker's 2025 table puts a **₹1 crore** limit at roughly **₹20,000–30,000 a year**, **₹5 crore** at **₹40,000–50,000**, and **₹10 crore** at **₹70,000–1,00,000** ([Mitigata — D&O premium cost in India](https://mitigata.com/blog/dno-insurance-premium-cost/), Sept 2025). Others quote **₹2.5–5 lakh per US\$1 million of cover** for startups ([Pazcare — D&O for startups](https://www.pazcare.com/blog/d-o-insurance-for-startups-its-coverage-and-benefits)). Both cannot be right for the same risk. **VERIFY LIVE — get three broker quotes for VSYST's actual profile and ignore every published table, including these.** What you are really buying is the wording, not the number: check the **insured-vs-insured** exclusion, the **prior-acts** date, whether **defence costs** sit inside or outside the limit, and whether **Side A** cover responds when the company cannot indemnify.

### 10.3 Professional indemnity and cyber — the two that fit DZZLO's actual shape

| Cover                                       | What it responds to                                                                                                                           | Why it matters for DZZLO                                                                                                                                                                          | When it becomes worth buying                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Professional indemnity / technology E&O** | Claims that your software or service caused a customer financial loss — a wrong invoice, a mispriced rate, an outage during a dispatch window | DZZLO computes GST invoices, TCS past ₹50 lakh turnover, and voucher/reconciliation balances. **A defect here becomes a dealer's tax exposure, not just a bug**                                   | When the first contract with an indemnity or liability clause is signed — an enterprise dealer, an OMC-linked arrangement, or a gateway agreement         |
| **Cyber liability**                         | Breach response, forensics, notification costs, regulatory defence, third-party claims, business interruption, sometimes ransomware           | DZZLO holds dealer **financial** data: GSTINs, rate history, order values, invoices, payment and voucher records, driver OTPs, customer credit positions. Multi-tenant, on AWS, with a mobile app | When the first meaningful volume of dealer data is live — which for VSYST is **now**, not later. Get a quote and a wording even if you defer the purchase |

Both are placed by the COO alongside the rest of the programme ([[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|COO 13]] §6). The CEO's only job is deciding the trigger and reading the exclusions once.

### 10.4 DPDP Act 2023 — the accountability that lands on the company, and the duty that lands on you

DZZLO is a **Data Fiduciary** for its own users, employees and leads, and a **Data Processor** for the dealers' customers' data — the split established in [[certification-and-standards-roadmap|Certification and Standards Roadmap]] §4 and COO lesson 07. That is not a theoretical classification; it decides who carries which obligation in every enterprise contract you will sign.

| Obligation                                                           | Provision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | What it means at VSYST                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Reasonable security safeguards** to prevent a personal data breach | DPDP Act 2023, s.8(5)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Encryption, access control, logging, RBAC, retention limits — the same control set the certification roadmap calls the groundwork                                                                      |
| **Breach notification**                                              | s.8(6), operationalised by **Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules 2025**: intimate affected Data Principals **without delay** (nature, extent, timing, likely consequences, mitigation, what they should do, a named contact) _and_ intimate the Board immediately, with a fuller report **within 72 hours of becoming aware**, or such longer period as the Board allows ([DPDP Rules 2025 — Rule 7](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule7.html)) | You need an incident runbook **before** you need it. The COO's [[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]] is the post-event half; the notification clock is the pre-event half                                   |
| **Phased commencement**                                              | DPDP Rules 2025 notified **November 2025**; the substantive obligations bite after **18 months** ([AZB & Partners — phased rollout](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf))                                                                  | The vault's compliance calendar carries **13 May 2027** as the hard date ([[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance\|COO 13]] §2.3). Published sources differ by a day. **VERIFY LIVE**              |
| **Penalties**                                                        | The **Schedule** to the Act: up to **₹250 crore** for failure to take reasonable security safeguards; up to **₹200 crore** for failure to notify a breach; up to **₹150 crore** for a Significant Data Fiduciary's additional obligations; up to **₹50 crore** residual ([DPDP Act 2023 — the Schedule](https://www.dpdpa.com/theschedule.html))                                                                                        | Ceilings, not tariffs — s.33 requires the Data Protection Board to weigh nature, gravity, duration, repetition, gain or loss and mitigation. **VERIFY LIVE**; adjudication practice does not exist yet |

**Now the part founders get wrong in both directions.** The DPDP Act's penalties are **financial and land on the Data Fiduciary — the company** — adjudicated by the Data Protection Board with an appeal route; the Act contains no offences chapter creating personal criminal liability for directors ([DPDP Act structure — 44 sections, penalties at ss.33–34](https://www.dpdpa.com/theschedule.html)). **VERIFY LIVE with the company lawyer.** So: not personal, in the direct sense.

And yet it is entirely yours, for two reasons. First, at VSYST's size, a penalty on the company _is_ a penalty on you — there is no one else. Second, **ensuring the company has the safeguards is a §166(3) duty**: "due and reasonable care, skill and diligence." A board that never once minuted a discussion of data protection, in a company whose product holds dealers' financial records, has an evidence problem that is separate from, and worse than, the technical gap.

**And DPDP is not the only clock.** The CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 already apply, with **no size exemption**: mandatory reporting of listed cyber incidents — including data breach and data leak — to CERT-In **within 6 hours** of noticing them; ICT system logs enabled and maintained securely for a **rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction**; clocks synchronised to NIC/NPL NTP servers; and a designated point of contact ([CERT-In Directions under s.70B(6), 28 April 2022](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)). Six hours is faster than any insurer, any lawyer and any board can be convened — which is precisely why the runbook and the named point of contact exist in advance.

### 10.5 The house call

1. **Do not buy D&O this year** unless an outside director, an investor or a counterparty triggers it. Do **read the AoA indemnity clause** this month and fix it if it is missing (§2.5).
2. **Get a cyber quote and a wording now**, even if the purchase waits. The exercise itself tells you what a carrier thinks your controls are worth.
3. **Write the incident runbook before the incident** — CERT-In's 6 hours, DPDP Rule 7's without-delay-plus-72-hours, and who calls whom. It is one page. [[18-risk-crisis-and-the-hard-things|Lesson 18]] and [[C24-crisis-comms-and-statement-kit|C24]] own the communications half.
4. **List every personal guarantee any director has signed**, with amount and counterparty, and put it in the board pack appendix once a year.
5. **Put "data protection and security" on the board agenda once a year**, minuted, with the certification roadmap's trigger table as the paper. That minute is cheap now and valuable later.
6. **Score all of it in [[T15-risk-register|T15]]**, and let the board see only what changed (§5). The register is the COO's; the decision to accept, mitigate or insure each top risk is the board's.

### 11.4 What not to do this year

- **Do not appoint an independent director.** §149(4) does not apply to you, and you cannot pay one properly (§8.8).
- **Do not buy D&O** unless an outsider, an investor or a counterparty triggers it (§10.5).
- **Do not build a 40-slide board deck.** Six to ten pages, prose first, sent seven days ahead (§5).

## 12. Exercises

Each exercise produces a dated artefact. Do 12.1 first — every other exercise attaches to one of its four dates.

**12.1 — Produce a year of scheduled board dates (30 min, with both co-founder-directors and the CS on WhatsApp).** Take §11.2's grid and turn it into **four real dates** in the current financial year: day, time, place or video link, chair, and a 7-day notice reminder set on each. Confirm with the CS which regime applies (two meetings ≥90 days apart as a small company, or four with ≤120-day gaps) and **write the answer into the sheet with the date you asked** — that is your **VERIFY LIVE** record. Add the §6 statutory load to each row so the CS knows what is coming. **Artefact:** four immovable calendar entries in all three directors' calendars, plus a filled `Board Calendar` tab in [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](toolkit/vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx) with columns _date · chair · strategic question · statutory items · notice sent (Y/N)_. **Success test:** the next date is under 90 days away and nobody has to ask "when is the board meeting?" again.

**12.2 — Fill [[C18-director-duties-and-governance-checklist|C18]] completely, including the parts that embarrass you (45 min, alone, with MCA master data open).** Work the checklist row by row: DIN status and DIR-3 KYC date for each director; who is MD/WTD on paper (§2.3); the **named** §2(77) relatives list for all three directors (§3.3); MBP-1 and DIR-8 status; the §189 register's existence; every §3.4 related-party arrangement marked _papered / not papered_; the filings status from the CS; the AoA indemnity clause (§2.5); and **every personal guarantee any director has signed**, with amount and counterparty (§10.1). Mark each row **green / amber / red** and put a name and a date on every red. **Artefact:** a completed, dated C18 with a red list that becomes agenda item one at board meeting B1. **Success test:** at least three rows come back red or unknown. If everything is green on the first pass, you filled it from memory instead of from documents — do it again with the documents.

**12.3 — Build board pack #1 and run the meeting, using [[C17-board-pack-agenda-and-minutes|C17]] (2 hrs prep + 90 min meeting + 45 min after).** Write the pack to §5's nine-section contents — 6 to 10 pages, the CEO's letter in prose and first person, the bad news in its first half, and **at least two numbered decisions with pre-drafted resolution language** (start with the related-party regularisations from 12.2's red list). Send it **seven days ahead** with the formal notice. Run the meeting to §4.4's run-of-show with a timer, including the closing question _"what did we not discuss that we should have?"_. Then draft the minutes to §4.3's six-part structure and circulate within 15 days. **Artefact:** `board/YYYY-MM-DD-pack.pdf`, a signed attendance register, and minutes entered in the minute book within 30 days, containing at least one numbered resolution and one recorded counter-argument. **Success test:** a stranger reading only the pack could describe VSYST's strategy, cash position and biggest risk in fifteen minutes.

**12.4 — Run the false-consensus audit and install two mechanisms (30 min audit + one resolution).** Answer §8.2's six questions **from the minute book and the calendar, not from memory**, and write the six answers down. Then, at meeting B1, pass one resolution adopting the two house rules from §8.5: (a) every material resolution's minute records the counter-argument, whether or not anyone dissented; (b) an unpersuaded director abstains rather than nods, and the abstention is minuted under SS-1 7.2.2.1(m). Assign the **first contra-memo** (§8.4) — one page, four questions, written by a named director, circulated with pack #2. **Artefact:** the six audit answers filed in [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]], a minuted resolution adopting both house rules, and a named owner and date for contra-memo #1. **Success test:** contra-memo #1 changes at least one detail of the decision it argues against. If it changes nothing four quarters running, the memos are being written to be dismissed.

**12.5 — Shortlist three advisory-board candidates and send one message (30 min).** Fill §7.2's three seats with **real names** — the fuel-industry veteran, the Indian SMB-SaaS operator, the finance person who is not your CA — with, for each: how you reach them (warm intro path, not a cold email), what specific question you would put to them in the first call, and the §7.5 conflict answer written out. Rank them by _what VSYST is weakest at today_, not by who is most impressive. Then **send the first message today** — ask for one 30-minute call, not for a commitment, and name the question. **Artefact:** a three-row shortlist in the vault with intro path, first question and conflict check for each; one sent message with a date; and, once someone says yes, the one-page advisory agreement from §7.4 drafted before the second call. **Success test:** the first question you wrote is one that all three current directors genuinely cannot answer.
