# CEO Docs — Choose the Direction, Fund It, and Get People to Follow

> Audience: whoever holds the **Chief Executive Officer seat at VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd.** (Raipur) — today a technical founder-director who ships code, tomorrow a CEO who mostly doesn't | Where we are: **newly formed, bootstrapped, pre-revenue**; one product live on both app stores; no strategy written down, no board rhythm, no capital plan, no first paying district | Goal: understand the seat, make the four decisions only this seat can make, and run the company well enough that the direction is written, the cash never surprises you, and the company keeps working when you are wrong | Written & sanity-checked **2026-08-25** for an Indian Private Limited software company.

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

Picture a truck carrying fuel from a depot to a district, at night, on a road nobody has mapped.

Somebody has to keep the truck running — the tyres, the fuel, the driver's shift, the paperwork at the checkpost. That is the **COO**, and [[COO-Docs/index|there is a whole course about it]].

Somebody else has to answer a different set of questions. _Which district are we driving to, and why that one?_ _Do we have enough diesel to get there, or do we turn back now while we still can?_ _Who else is in this truck, and are they the right people?_ _And when the road forks and there is no map, who decides?_

That is the **CEO**. Not the person who drives hardest — the person who chooses the destination, makes sure the tank is never empty, picks who rides along, and tells everyone the same story about where they are going so they pull in one direction. When they choose wrong, the truck arrives somewhere useless with a perfectly maintained engine.

This course is about learning to answer those four questions on purpose instead of by accident.

## What This Folder Is

A **four-phase, hands-on course plus a toolkit** that takes a first-time founder-CEO with **no management or business background** from "what does a CEO actually do all day?" to "here is our written strategy, here is the capital plan, here are the people, and here is the record of every decision I made and why." It is written for **our actual company** (VSYST Pvt Ltd, maker of DZZLO OMS), **our actual stage** (formed, bootstrapped, pre-revenue, one district away from the first real customer), **our actual founding team** (three family founder-directors, a technical CEO, no CFO, no board beyond ourselves) and **our actual market** (Indian fuel distribution) — not a generic MBA syllabus and not a Silicon Valley playbook transplanted to Chhattisgarh.

Every lesson ends in exercises that produce something real: a written strategy kernel, a scored market map, a signed founders' agreement, a runway ladder with pre-committed triggers, a filled board pack, a decision journal you can be judged by. The [[CEO-Docs/toolkit/index|CEO Toolkit]] holds the 30 templates those exercises fill, and [vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx](toolkit/vsyst-ceo-workbook.xlsx) holds the spreadsheets behind them — strategy kernel, market sizing, unit economics, 24-month runway scenarios, cap table and dilution, ESOP pool, the CEO dashboard, annual OKRs, the decision journal, board calendar, pipeline, comp bands and the pre-mortem.

## The One Idea That Governs Everything

> **The COO's product is the operating system. The CEO's product is the company's direction, its capital, its people and its story — and the judgment that allocates all four.**

Fred Wilson's reduction is the spine of this course: a CEO does only three things — **set the vision and strategy and communicate it relentlessly; recruit and retain the very best talent; and make sure there is always enough cash in the bank.** Everything else that lands on your desk is either one of those three in disguise, or it belongs to somebody else.

An Indian Pvt Ltd adds a fourth that cannot be delegated at any price: **the director's legal duty.** You can hand the filings to the CS. You cannot hand over the signature, the fiduciary duty, or the personal liability.

The corollary is the rule that runs the whole course, and it is deliberately the mirror of the COO's:

```
Only you can decide it        → decide it, and write down why      (02, C29)
Someone else could decide it  → hand over the decision AND a limit (03, C03)
Nobody can decide it yet      → run the smallest test that could
                                prove the strategy wrong           (05, C04)
And in every case             → say it out loud, more times than
                                feels necessary                    (12, C22)
```

If the COO's discipline is _good intentions don't work, mechanisms do_, the CEO's is **a decision that isn't written down didn't happen, and a strategy nobody can repeat back to you isn't one.**

## How to Use This Course

- **Do the exercises, in order.** Reading about being a CEO is like reading about swimming — worse, actually, because the failure mode of a CEO who has only read is confident wrongness. Each exercise is small (15–60 minutes) and leaves an artefact: a filled template in this vault or a tab in the workbook. Keep filled copies in a `ceo-workbook/` folder next to this one; the toolkit files stay blank masters.
- **Follow the timeline, not the table of contents.** Lessons are grouped by topic; the [[CEO-Docs/00-course-map-and-timeline|timeline]] tells you what to actually do in week 1, month 2, quarter 3. Some things jump the queue — the founders' agreement and the first board meeting do not wait for you to finish reading.
- **Write the decision down before you know the outcome.** The [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|decision journal]] is the highest-return habit in this course, and the only one that compounds. Start it in week 0, even if the first entries are decisions you have already made.
- **You are allowed to disagree with this course.** Where the literature conflicts, the lesson gives both sides and then makes a house call for VSYST. If the house call is wrong for us, change it — and write down why in the file, so the next person inherits your reasoning and not just your conclusion.
- **The CA, CS and lawyer stay.** This course makes you literate and dangerous on company law, term sheets, ESOPs and employment — enough to _run_ them well, negotiate properly and catch mistakes, not to replace professionals. Every filing goes through the CS; every contract gets a legal read; every tax position goes to the CA.
- **The COO course is not optional reading.** The two seats are defined against each other. If you are currently wearing both hats — which at three people you are — read [[COO-Docs/01-what-is-a-coo|COO lesson 01]] and [[COO-Docs/03-the-coo-core-value-and-the-ceo-coo-contract|COO lesson 03]] alongside lesson 03 here.

> **A note on honesty (the vault rule).** Company-law provisions, tax thresholds, startup-scheme eligibility, ESOP taxation, funding-market norms, market-size statistics and tool prices **change every Budget and often mid-year.** Everything here was written to be right for an Indian Pvt Ltd as of **2026-08-25**, but wherever a _specific number, date, form, section, threshold, valuation or price_ matters to a real decision it is flagged **VERIFY LIVE** — confirm it with the CA, CS or lawyer, or the primary source, before you act. This course teaches the _judgment_ (how to frame a decision, what to trade off, what to write down), which barely changes; the _numbers_ you always verify.
