# 21 — Reference: Glossary, Reading List and Sources

_Reference · always open. Every term the twenty lessons use, the books in the order that actually helps, every source behind the claims — and the one table that lets you re-audit the whole course against reality once a year._

## 1. How to use this page

Four parts, four different jobs.

- **§2 — the glossary.** Alphabetical. One plain-English line per term, an Indian note where the Indian version differs from the imported one, and the lesson or template where the term does its real work. Read a lesson with this open in a second tab.
- **§3 — the reading path.** Not a list. A sequence, with what each book fixes and the quarter to read it in. It restates and extends [[04-how-to-become-a-ceo-in-2026|lesson 04]] §6, which is the canonical version — where they differ, 04 wins.
- **§4 — the sources, by domain.** Every reusable external page the twenty lessons cite, grouped under the eight domains this course actually runs on. If you remember a claim but not where it was made, find the domain, follow the link, then follow it back.
- **§5 — the standing VERIFY-LIVE list.** The most useful page in this file. Every `**VERIFY LIVE**` flag in the course, gathered into one table and **grouped by who confirms it** — so you can take one list to the CS, a second to the CA, a third to the lawyer, and a fourth to a vendor's own pricing page. Roughly 150 rows. Working through it once a year re-audits the entire course against reality.

Three rules that govern the whole page.

**Terms are defined as this course uses them** — for a three-person, family-founded, bootstrapped, pre-revenue Indian Private Limited selling vertical SaaS to fuel dealers, district by district, in rupees. The same words mean something slightly different at a 500-person company, and something materially different in a US venture context. Where the gap matters, the entry says so.

**Every India-specific statutory item carries VERIFY LIVE**, and the glossary deliberately tells you what the thing _is_ and who owns it, never what the current number is. Indian thresholds, rates, forms and due dates move at every Budget and often mid-year — and the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbers a great deal of what lesson 08 and lesson 10 cite. The number belongs in the professional's answer, dated, in your file. Not here.

**Nothing on this page is legal, tax or investment advice.** It is a map of what to ask, whom to ask, and where the course said it. The named professional's written answer, with the date you asked on it, is the artefact — that is the discipline the whole course is built on, and §5 is the list you build it from.

### 3.7 If you only get through five

One a month, in this order. Each changes exactly one artefact — schedule the change when you schedule the book, in [[C01-ceo-charter-and-90-day-plan|C01]].

1. **_Good Strategy Bad Strategy_** — because everything downstream is undefined until the diagnosis exists. Changes [[C04-strategy-kernel-one-pager|C04]].
2. **_The Mom Test_** — because a strategy built on flattering conversations is a strategy built on nothing, and this one is short. Changes [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]].
3. **_Obviously Awesome_** — because the technical founder's specific failure is assuming the product explains itself. Changes [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas|C08]].
4. **_Thinking in Bets_** — because the decision journal is the instrument by which a first-time CEO learns fastest, and it does not survive contact without this book's argument behind it. Changes [[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]].
5. **_The Hard Thing About Hard Things_** — because the quarter you most need it, you will not have time to start it. Changes nothing on paper, which is the point.

After those five, read by need: **Rumelt again** before the annual plan, **Helmer** once there are real customers to test a moat against, **Grove** before the first hire, **Lencioni** with the co-founders before the first real disagreement, and **the Bezos letters** twice a year forever, because they are short and the value is in the re-reading.

## 5. The standing VERIFY-LIVE list

This is the most useful page in the file, and the only one designed to be _worked_ rather than read.

Every `**VERIFY LIVE**` flag across the twenty lessons is gathered here — 183 items — and **grouped by who confirms it**, not by the lesson it came from. That grouping is the whole point. You cannot hand a lesson to your Company Secretary; you can hand her §5.2. The CA gets §5.3. The lawyers get §5.4, split by the kind of lawyer. §5.5 you check yourself against vendor pages in an afternoon. §5.6 nobody outside the company can confirm, because the answer is in your own database or in a district you have to drive to. §5.7 is the list of published statistics that are fine to think with and **not fine to quote in anything a stranger will read**.

**How to run the audit.** Once a year, in April — after the financial year closes and before the statutory calendar reloads — block two days. Day one: send §5.2 to the CS and §5.3 to the CA as written questions, and open a file. Day two: work §5.5 and §5.6 yourself. Write each answer back into the lesson or template it came from **with the date you asked and the name of who answered** — that dated answer, not the lesson, is what you may rely on. The lawyer lists in §5.4 are not annual; they are triggered, and the trigger column says by what. A course that has been through this once is current. A course that has not is a document about 2026.

**The honest framing.** Roughly a third of these will come back "no change". A third will come back with a number that moved a little. And a handful will come back with something that materially changes a decision you have already made — an ESOP promise, a runway trigger, a procurement claim, a pricing argument. Those are the reason the list exists.

### 5.8 The one-page version

If you do nothing else with §5 this year, do these six.
