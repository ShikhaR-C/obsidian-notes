# M04 — Positioning Statement and the Segment × Message × Channel Matrix

_Toolkit · fills the exercises in [[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch|04 — The Message: Positioning, Segment-Specific Pitches and Objections]] · Owner: CEO owns the positioning statement and the message house; the domain director owns every Hindi line and the pain column · Cadence: written Week 1, re-read before any asset is written, re-cut quarterly · Workbook tab: —_

## Purpose

**The positioning statement** decides which shelf DZZLO stands on. **The message house** compresses it into one promise and three pillars. **The matrix** answers the owner's question: *which business, from where, and what exactly do we say to it* (HC6). Fill them in that order.

Box one runs on April Dunford's five inputs — alternatives, unique attributes, value and proof, target-market characteristics, category **last** ([Heinz Marketing](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/five-components-of-effective-positioning-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-2/); [April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)); box two is roof, pillars, proof ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/message-house-framework/)).

## When to use

- **Week 1, once** ([[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch|lesson 04]], exercise 9.1) — two segments minimum, nine eventually.
- **Before any asset is written** — leaflet, landing page, store listing, broadcast ([[M11-content-and-whatsapp-calendar|M11]]), campaign brief ([[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem|M12]]): roof plus **exactly one** pillar.
- **Before a new segment or district** — a row you cannot fill is a segment you cannot sell.
- **Quarterly** — rule 6.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Fill in Dunford's order.** Alternatives first — parchi book, Excel, Tally, WhatsApp, the manager's memory — category **last**, or you land on the billing-software shelf in a price fight we lose.
2. **The competitor test.** A sentence a local billing vendor could paste on his site unchanged is a description, not a position; abstracted further you "sound exactly like every other product you compete with" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/a-guide-to-advanced-b2b-positioning)).
3. **Pain in their words; one line per row, under twelve words.** Only sentences you have heard a dealer say — "reconciliation inefficiency" is a sentence nobody said. Hindi gets used; English is the translation.
4. **No proof, no pillar.** Every proof cell names a screen, a document or a named person you can show on a phone **today** — never the zero-install WhatsApp flow ([[11_EDUCATION_GAP|education gap]] §7–8: **not built**).
5. **Customer-side rows say "via his dealer"** until that district has live dealers (HC2, [[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|lesson 02]]), and the last column names a job title — "the owner" is no answer where the munim holds a quiet veto ([[M02-icp-and-persona-field-cards|M02]]).
6. **Quarterly refresh — first Monday of the quarter, or after 20 demos.** Three founders, 45 minutes: rewrite pain lines a dealer contradicted, delete proof we can no longer show, re-verify every number (Raipur diesel **₹101.34/L, 27 Aug 2026** — [Shriram Finance](https://www.shriramfinance.in/diesel-price-in-raipur) — **VERIFY LIVE each time**). **Never change the roof mid-quarter.**

## Worked example — VSYST (illustrative)

> **For** petrol-pump dealers, bulk-diesel operators and lubricant distributors around Raipur selling on credit to 20–200 B2B customers, **who are dissatisfied with** a DSR + Tally + WhatsApp + Excel stack that reconciles only at month-end, **DZZLO is** the order-and-ledger app between the dealer and his credit customers, **that provides** one rate agreed *before* the sale, one order-to-invoice trail with driver OTP, and one shared ledger, **unlike** pump billing software, Tally or the parchi book — **because** DZZLO is the only one **the dealer's customer also opens.**

## Common mistakes

1. **A roof nobody can repeat** on a forecourt is copy, not a promise.
2. **Pain columns written at a desk, in English.**
3. **Proof that is a claim** — not "trusted by many pumps" but a named dealer you dial in front of him.
