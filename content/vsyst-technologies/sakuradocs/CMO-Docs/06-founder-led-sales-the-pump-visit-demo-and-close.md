# 06 — Founder-Led Sales: The Pump Visit, the Demo and the Close

_Phase 3 · Go to Market · Weeks 2–12. After this lesson you can walk into a pump you have never visited, get the owner talking in three minutes, demo on his own customers and his own rupee figures, put a dated 14-day trial on paper, follow up five times, ask for the money, and log it in a pipeline sheet that tells you how many of your 100 targets will be paying by week 12._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A tractor dealer in a mandi town does not sell from his showroom. He loads a tractor onto a trailer, drives to the farmer's field, and ploughs one furrow in **that farmer's own soil** while the farmer watches. Then he switches off the engine: keep it a fortnight, I will come back on the 14th.

He is not demonstrating a tractor. He is removing the last excuse — _"achha hoga, par mere khet mein chalega ki nahi?"_ — by demonstrating in the only place where the answer counts.

The pump is the field. His own credit customers and his own last rate dispute are the soil. The fortnight with a date on it is the close.

## 1. The pipeline, in one table

**Theory lives elsewhere.** Why a founder sells rather than a rep, ICP scoring, the four forces behind a switch, the objection bank and the eight-stage CEO sheet are in [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|CEO lesson 07]] and [[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]] (transporter-side view: [[09_Sales_Strategy|transporters 09]]). This lesson is what you do on Tuesday morning.

Sales stops at **Paid**; this course stops at **Referring**, because a dealer who paid and never got a customer onto the ledger is a refund with a delay (HC10).

**Mapping, so the courses do not fight.** Stages 1–2 are ERPNext's **Lead**; 3–5 its **Opportunity**; 6 its **Sales Invoice + Payment Entry**; 7–8 have no sales equivalent and live only on the marketing scorecard. ## 2. The pump visit

### 2.1 When, who, what you carry

| Decision             | The answer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **When**             | **11:30–13:00** and **15:30–17:00**. Rush is 08:00–11:00 and 17:00–21:00; a tanker decant pins the owner to the tank for 45–90 minutes. Avoid the 1st–5th (OMC settlement, salaries), the 15th, month-end and Mondays. **VERIFY LIVE with the domain director** |
| **Who**              | **Domain director leads; the CEO comes on the first ten visits.** The domain director opens because he speaks the trade; the CEO must hear the owner directly, because half of what is said is a product instruction that does not survive being retold in the car ([Paul Graham — Do Things that Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)) |
| **Why the engineer** | The owner's third sentence is one no non-engineer can answer honestly — _"purana data kaun daalega", "Tally mein jayega", "server kahan hai"_. An invented answer costs a district; and handover needs **two** people who can demo ([[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales\|CEO 07]] §9.1) |
| **How many**         | **Four pumps a day, clustered inside 25 km**, routed as a loop — see §7                                                                                                                                                                                             |

**What you carry:** a charged phone with the demo tenant **already seeded** (real pump names, today's rates, one real vehicle number), a power bank, offline screenshots for villages with no signal; a **one-page leave-behind**, Hindi one side, English the other; the **rupee-number sheet** — a blank "your top 10 credit customers and what each owes today" page you fill _with_ him and leave behind, reciprocity instrument and onboarding data in one; a printed `wa.me` **QR** to the domain director's WhatsApp Business number; two pens. **No laptop, no deck.**

### 2.2 The first three minutes

The words are in [[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch|lesson 04]] and [[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling|M05]]; carry it. The shape is fixed:

- **0:00–0:30 — name, company, referrer, permission.** _"Namaste [Name] ji, main [name], VSYST Technologies, Raipur se. [Referrer] ji ne bheja. Do minute hain?"_ A local name in the first sentence outperforms any claim you could make ([Cialdini](https://www.cognitigence.com/blog/cialdini-7-principles-of-persuasion)).
- **0:30–2:00 — one question, then silence.** _"Abhi kitne udhaar customers hain, aur unka hisaab kahan rakhte hain?"_ Count to five after he stops.
- **2:00–3:00 — one sentence back in his words**, one rupee number, one local pump name. No "platform", no features. Then the ask: **ten minutes, on your own customer's ledger, right now.**

### 2.3 The six listening questions

Ask about the past, never the future — his predictions about his own behaviour are worthless, his memories are not ([The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/); [HBR — Jobs to Be Done](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done), 2016).

1. _"Udhaar customers kitne hain, aur unka hisaab kahan rakhte hain?"_ — where the ledger lives today.
2. _"Pichhli baar rate ya amount ka jhagda kab hua tha? Kitne ka?"_ — a struggling moment with a date and a figure. **Write it down in front of him.**
3. _"Us din aapne kya kiya — kisko phone kiya, kya nikala?"_ — the workaround he already built. That is what you replace.
4. _"Mahine ke aakhir mein kitna baaki rehta hai, aur peechhe kaun padta hai?"_ — receivables, and who chases them.
5. _"Counter pe roz kaun baithta hai? Naam?"_ — the operator. Without this name the trial fails and you will not know why.
6. _"Agar kal se ye kaam badalna ho, sabse pehle kya band hona chahiye?"_ — what he will fire. The only forward-looking question, and it is about firing, not buying.

### 2.4 Reading the room, and when to stop

| Who                      | You spot him by                      | Really asking                  | Show **first**                                     | Never                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Father** (signs)       | Behind the desk; asks price early    | "Will this embarrass me?"      | His customer's **ledger**, then a dealer-next-door name | Settings, roadmap, the word "platform" |
| **Son** (influences)     | Picks up your phone unasked          | "Is it built properly, fast?"  | The **rate-confirmation window** and the timestamp     | Letting him install — demo on your device |
| **Munim** (vetoes)       | Standing, arms folded, near the door | "Does this replace me?"        | The **invoice** — GST, TCS flag, the export            | Positioning against him; get him in the room |

**Stop and leave** when a tanker turns in, when he takes a call and does not return within two minutes, or when he says _"WhatsApp pe bhej dijiye"_ twice. Say _"main disturb kar raha hoon — Mangalwar subah ya Guruwar shaam?"_ and go. **A visit that ends without a next date did not happen.**

## 3. The ten-minute demo on his own numbers

The run-of-show, with Hindi lines and persona variants, is [[M06-demo-flow-and-pilot-offer-sheet|M06]]:

> **paper (his ₹ figure) → tomorrow's rate for a real customer → the 10 PM–6 AM confirmation window → the order → dispatch with driver OTP → invoice with GST/TCS → the ledger, sent to his own WhatsApp.**

[[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]] times **eight minutes of screens**; M06 is those eight with the bookends timed — minute 0 is paper, minute 9 the ask.

**Never in a first demo:** settings, the superadmin console, the DIP module, the roadmap, the API, a price list. If he asks the price, deflect once — _"pehle dekh lijiye"_ — and quote at the close ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet|C11]]).

## 4. The pilot offer — 14 days, on paper

One page, both languages, signed in ballpoint at the counter ([[M06-demo-flow-and-pilot-offer-sheet|M06]]). Four things make it work:

1. **14 days, no card.** Short trials beat 30-day trials on conversion by roughly a fifth, because a month invites postponement ([Ordway Labs](https://ordwaylabs.com/blog/saas-free-trial-length-conversion/)). Fourteen days is also the [[08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy|subscription strategy]]'s policy — **VERIFY LIVE, owner sign-off pending.**
2. **"Hum khud aapke top teen udhaar customers ko is hafte onboard karenge."** We enter the balances, send the phone-OTP invites, sit at the counter for the first one. The highest-value sentence in the offer, because the customer is the **hard side** of this network ([[11_EDUCATION_GAP|the dealer-customer dependency gap]] §3) and the one stage he cannot do alone ([[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist|M08]]).
3. **A success criterion on paper before day 1**, ticked together on day 14: _top three balances correct without opening the diary; rates set on 12 of 14 nights; 10+ invoices raised._
4. **The day-14 meeting is booked on day 0.** Trials that end by drifting never convert.

**House-call tension, stated plainly.** [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|CEO lesson 07]] §7.2 says a pilot must **cost him something**, and a no-card trial costs nothing in rupees. It stands anyway for two reasons: there is **no billing code and no gateway in the product today** (Phase 2 of the [[08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy|subscription strategy]] is offline NEFT via superadmin), so a pilot fee is an invoice VSYST cannot cleanly raise; and the commitment is collected in another currency — the top-ten list, 45 minutes on day 0, the named operator, a diarised date, all of which cost him time and reputation, which is what the Mom Test asks for. **If the founders want the rupee version, add a ₹1,000 refundable deposit and log the decision.**

## 5. Follow-up, and the close

80% of non-routine sales need five or more follow-ups, and **92% of salespeople stop before the fifth ask** ([Cirrus Insight](https://www.cirrusinsight.com/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)). So the cadence is exactly five, on WhatsApp, from the domain director's business number — where 78% of Indian SMBs already are ([Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/whatsapp-statistics-india-2026); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/how-whatsapp-business-is-bringing-indias-smes-to-the-digital-fold/)).

| Touch      | Sent                       | Contains                                                                          | The ask                     |
| ---------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Day 0**  | Within 2 hours of leaving  | Thanks, **his own ₹ figure**, three lines of what he said, the day-14 date | Confirm the date            |
| **Day 2**  | Morning                    | A screenshot of **his** first customer's ledger, live in his tenant        | Set tonight's rate          |
| **Day 7**  | Midpoint                   | His week in numbers — rates set _n_/7 nights, invoices, balance visible    | Add customers four and five |
| **Day 13** | Evening                    | _"Kal hamari baithak hai"_ + the four success ticks, unfilled             | Confirm tomorrow            |
| **Day 14** | **In person**, not on chat | The ticks filled together, then the price                                  | **The money**               |

**The close.** Every company pays **per GSTIN**; staff, drivers and users are unlimited and free; **billing is web-only** — never in the app, never a link in the app. Tiers under sign-off: **₹599 / ₹1,799 / ₹4,999 per month plus Enterprise, ex-GST — VERIFY LIVE, owner sign-off pending; never quote the older ₹999–2,499 numbers.** Quote the tier that fits his transaction volume, price it against the receivable he named in question 2, and stop talking.

**"Discount de dijiye."** Never move the monthly number — a district learns your real price inside a week and every later dealer negotiates against it. Move something else: **annual prepay** (two months off, cash in advance — the only discount that buys VSYST anything), **founding-member status** (a numbered plaque for the forecourt; status is load-bearing here, [[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] §4.8), or **more of our labour** (five customers onboarded, not three). Discount authority sits with the CEO; log every exception ([[11-revenue-operations-and-partnerships|COO 11]] §7).

## 7. The arithmetic: 100 targets, twelve weeks

| Stage                    | Cold list (field visits) | Half-warm list (referral / association / OMC TM) |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Targets                  | 100                      | 100                                              |
| × contacted              | 65% → **65**             | 80% → **80**                                     |
| × visited                | 60% → **39**             | 70% → **56**                                     |
| × demoed                 | 65% → **25**             | 75% → **42**                                     |
| × trial started          | 45% → **11**             | 55% → **23**                                     |
| × **paying**             | 40% → **4–5**            | 45% → **10–11**                                  |
| × activated (both sides) | 65% → **3**              | 65% → **7**                                      |

**Can you physically do it?** Full-time outside reps average 5.1 visits a day ([RepMove](https://repmove.app/sales-efficiency-benchmarks-statistics/), 2025); founders who also ship code plan four. Two field days a week × four pumps × weeks 2–12 = **88 visit-slots**. About half are second visits and onboarding sits, leaving **~45 first visits** — enough for the cold column's 39, short for the warm column, which is a good problem and a hiring trigger (HC1).

**The punchline: the founding ten is a sourcing problem, not a selling problem.** The warm list yields twice the paying dealers from the same 100 names, because referral converts at 25–40% against field sales' 5–12% cold ([[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] §5). If week 6 finds you grinding at four paying, the fix is not more visits — it is [[05-channels-where-to-find-them-and-in-what-order|lesson 05]] and [[08-referrals-associations-and-partners-the-warm-engine|lesson 08]].

## 9. At VSYST — applying this now

- **Domain director** — books and runs **five pump visits** from the top of [[M03-target-list-builder|M03]], clustered, Tuesday and Thursday; fills [[M07-sales-pipeline-tracker|M07]] the same evening; owns the WhatsApp Business number every follow-up goes from.
- **CEO** — rides along on all five, silent for the first three minutes, answers only technical questions, seeds the demo tenant before Tuesday, runs one demo personally.
- **Third director** — prints 20 leave-behinds, 20 rupee-number sheets and the QR card; opens the `Pipeline` tab; puts **Monday 10:00, ten minutes** in all three calendars.
- **All three, Monday** — the review in [[M07-sales-pipeline-tracker|M07]]: what moved, what has a blank next step, what dies. The five counts go onto the marketing scorecard ([[11-metrics-budget-and-the-marketing-scorecard|lesson 11]], [[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]]), which feeds [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]].

**Owner decisions blocking the close:** the tier sheet, and whether the 14-day trial carries a ₹1,000 refundable deposit (§4).
