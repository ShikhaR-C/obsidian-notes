# CMO Docs — Marketing and Sales from Zero for DZZLO OMS

> Audience: whoever holds the **marketing-and-sales seat at VSYST Technologies Pvt. Ltd.** (Raipur) — today all three founder-directors, none of whom has ever done marketing | Where we are: **bootstrapped, pre-revenue, live on both app stores, no marketing person, no sales person, ₹30–50k/month of cash** | Goal: from zero to running **measured** campaigns — one district, named dealers, a CAC you can defend — and only then the large multi-district ones | Written & sanity-checked **2026-08-27** for a two-sided vertical SaaS sold to Indian fuel dealers.

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A man in Raipur buys a chhota haathi — a small goods tempo. He parks outside the furniture shop on Ring Road and the cement depot at Bhatagaon: whoever has just bought a sofa or ten bags of cement is the only person in the city who needs a tempo in the next ten minutes. **That choice — where to stand — is marketing.** So is the number painted on his tailgate, and getting the shop owner to say _"isko le jao, ye theek aadmi hai."_ **Sales** is the ninety seconds at the counter: _"Telibandha, ₹350, loading included, abhi chalte hain."_ Yes or no.

**Two people must stay happy, and only one pays him.** The household pays the ₹350; the shop hands him the work. Scratch one sofa and the household complains to the shop, and the shop stops saying his name. He is **sold by the shop and judged by the household** — DZZLO's exact shape, where the pump dealer owns the account and pays while his B2B customers use the same app free, and the product only does its best work when both are on it.

Each night he writes one line in a diary: which shop sent which trip. Three months on it reads _furniture 41, cement depot 12, painted tailgate 3_ — so he buys the furniture man tea on Fridays and stops repainting the tailgate. **That diary is marketing measurement**, and ours is eight numbers on a sheet.

## The Three Questions This Course Answers

**1. "Do we hire a marketing person, or do it ourselves?"**
The founders do it themselves for the first hundred dealers — no CMO, no agency; those hundred are R&D, not revenue, and recruiting users by hand gives the best feedback you will ever get ([Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)). The **first** hire is a Raipur field associate at ₹20–35k/month (**VERIFY LIVE**), only once all three triggers hold: ≥10 paying dealers, one channel that produced ≥3 repeatably, founders' selling past ~15 h/week.
→ [[01-what-marketing-and-sales-actually-are|Lesson 01]] states it; [[12-scaling-hiring-and-launching-the-big-campaign|lesson 12]] has the ladder above it; [[M01-marketing-charter-and-90-day-plan|M01]] records it with a review date.

**2. "How do we get customers when the product needs two kinds of user?"**
Sell to the dealer, seed the customer, count both. The dealer is the buyer **and** the only channel to his customers, so we never market to fleets or factories cold where no dealer is live — that pitch is delivered by the dealer, with our material. A dealer is won only at **≥1 customer firm linked and ≥1 invoice inside 14 days.**
→ [[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|Lesson 02]] explains why; [[07-onboarding-and-activation-getting-both-sides-live|lesson 07]] and [[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist|M08]] get both sides live.

**3. "How do we tell clearly which business, from where, so that they opt for our product?"**
With a matrix, not a slogan. Per segment — pump dealer, bulk-diesel operator, lubricant distributor, fleet, factory, contractor, hospital — their pain in their own words, the line we say back, the proof we show, the channel that reaches them, and **who must say yes**. Then you name the firms.
→ [[03-who-exactly-icp-personas-and-the-target-list|Lessons 03]] and [[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch|04]] build it; it lives in [[M02-icp-and-persona-field-cards|M02]], [[M03-target-list-builder|M03]] and [[M04-positioning-and-message-house|M04]].

## The One Idea That Governs Everything

> **Marketing is finding the people whose problem you solve, saying it in their words where they already are, and making it easy to say yes. Sales is the conversation that finishes it. For a two-sided product: sell to the dealer, seed the customer, and count both.**

The corollary is the rule that decides what you do next, in any week:

```
Nobody has bought it yet      -> go to the pump yourself, this week      (lesson 06)
One has bought it             -> get the proof and the referral           (lesson 08)
Ten have bought it            -> write the script down, measure the channel (lessons 10–11)
A hundred have bought it      -> hire, repeat the district, then go big   (lesson 12)
And in every case             -> both sides live, or it doesn't count     (lessons 02, 07)
```

## How to Use This Course

- **Do the exercises, in the week they belong to.** Reading about selling is like reading about swimming. Keep filled copies in a `cmo-workbook/` folder; the toolkit files stay blank masters.
- **Follow the timeline, not the table of contents.** [[CMO-Docs/00-course-map-and-timeline|00]] says what happens in Week 1, Week 6 and Month 9.
- **One owner per artefact, named.** Shared ownership is no ownership.
- **Nothing counts until both sides are live.** ≥1 customer firm linked and ≥1 invoice inside 14 days is the bar, and it moves for nobody.
- **The message stays a founder artefact.** Hand over visits, follow-ups and calendars — never what we say, or the OMC and association relationships.
- **If a template does not fit VSYST, change it**, and note the change in its file.

> **A note on honesty (the vault rule).** Salary bands, ad rates, WhatsApp pricing, association fees, pump counts and our own tier sheet **all move**, and several numbers here have never been tested at VSYST — there are no paying dealers yet. The **system** barely changes; the **numbers** you replace with your own the moment you have them.
