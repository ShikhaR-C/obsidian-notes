# 05 — Channels: Where to Find Them, in What Order

_Phase 3 · Go to Market · Weeks 2–12. After this lesson you can rank every channel open to DZZLO for a bootstrapped company in Raipur, write the first action for the top three, turn the live IOCL conversation into a repeatable introduction channel, run a two-week channel experiment that ends in a verdict, and say which channels you are not touching yet and what would change that._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

Kamla didi cooks tiffins in Shankar Nagar, Raipur. Twelve customers, and she wants fifty. She could slide 2,000 pamphlets into the morning newspapers. She could rent the hoarding near the bus stand. Or she could call her twelve customers and ask, _"aapke office mein aur kisi ko chahiye?"_

All three are channels. A **channel** is a route by which a stranger finds out you exist. The pamphlets cost ₹4,000 and bring two people. The hoarding costs ₹15,000 and brings nobody she can trace. The twelve calls cost an evening and bring nine.

Kamla didi's mistake would not be _choosing_ the hoarding — it would be choosing it **first**. With fifty happy customers and photos of full dabbas the hoarding might work; with twelve she has nothing to put on it. **Channels are not good or bad. They are early or late.** You earn the expensive ones by exhausting the cheap ones. At ₹30–50k a month, order is the whole game — house call **HC4**, the spine of this lesson.

## 1. Three words, then the rule

- **Channel** — a repeatable route to a stranger's attention. Not the message ([[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch|lesson 04]]) and not the conversation ([[06-founder-led-sales-the-pump-visit-demo-and-close|lesson 06]]) — just the road.
- **CAC** — customer acquisition cost: everything spent on a channel ÷ paying dealers it produced. ₹12,000 and 3 dealers = ₹4,000. Count founder time at ₹500/hour or the comparison lies.
- **Warm vs cold** — warm means someone the dealer trusts hands you over. Indian B2B cold conversion runs around **2.23%** ([Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/b2b-conversion-rates-by-industry)); a warm introduction into the same pump converts several times better. That gap _is_ the ranking.

**The rule:** run the channels below in order, one or two at a time, until each has produced dealers or been honestly killed. Never eight at 10% effort — three people cannot.

## 3. The IOCL conversation is a channel, not a meeting

VSYST holds the most valuable asset on that list and is treating it as a partnership discussion. Reread [[discussion-document|the DZZLO × IOCL discussion document]]: ~60% of pump sales are on credit but only ~20% flows through XTRAPOWER, and DZZLO can hand IOCL real-time customer-wise credit litres. Strong strategic case. **It is also a distribution channel — and that value arrives years before any integration does.** It becomes one through three artefacts:

| Ask | What you request | Why they say yes | What you bring |
| --- | ---------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| **TM introductions** | "Introduce us to five dealers with credit-recovery trouble." Five names and a forward — not a mandate | He is measured on off-take and dealer health; a dealer who recovers receivables faster lifts more product | Your own RO: MS 30→80 KL, HSD 125→400 KL |
| **A dealer meet slot** | 15 minutes at the next divisional dealer meet — the densest gathering of ICP owners there is | Costs nothing, fills an agenda, reads as dealer support | One slide, one phone demo, one testimonial |
| **An OMC training day** | Co-run a half-day on digital credit ledgers and TCS at the divisional office | Officers already run training days; content is the scarce input | A printed Hindi checklist carrying both logos |

Three rules keep it alive. **Never ask for exclusivity or an MoU first** — an MoU takes nine months and a legal review; five names take one message. **Pay through an agreement or not at all**: [[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] models ₹3–5k per TM introduction, but a PSU officer cannot take cash-in-hand and offering it ends the relationship permanently — whether any individual fee is payable at all is a **VERIFY LIVE** question for the CA and company secretary. Non-cash reciprocity — training, data, a joint case study — is always safe. **Keep the ask to one line** ([[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]] has it in Hindi and English). The same artefacts work on BPCL and HPCL later; IOCL is first only because Amey answers the phone.

## 5. Customer-side channels: mapped, and deliberately switched off

Transporter associations, the truck-stop and dhaba clusters along **NH-53** — Hajira to Paradip via Gujarat, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh and Odisha ([NH 53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_53_\(India\))) — and factory purchase departments in the Urla and Siltara belts are all reachable. [[08_Marketing_Strategy|The transporters marketing strategy]] maps them: transport-nagar walkabouts, Saturday night-stops at dhaba clusters on the Raipur and Nagpur bypasses, sponsorship at **AIMTC** (3,500+ federations, ~95 lakh truckers, since 1936 — [AIMTC](https://www.aimtc.org/about-us/)).

**None gets used cold. That is HC2 — arithmetic, not timidity.**

| Marketing to a transporter… | …no dealer live | …15 dealers live |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| What you can promise | "Install this, then ask your dealer to join" | "Three of your suppliers are on it" |
| What he gets today | An app with no supplier in it | Confirmed rates, one ledger, driver OTP |
| Outcome | A dead install, a burnt name | Adoption in one call |

The customer side has **no single-player value**: the rate-confirmation window, the shared ledger and driver OTP all need a dealer on the other end ([[11_EDUCATION_GAP|Education Gap]]). So the customer channel today is one thing — **the dealer, carrying our material**: the invite list, the Hindi one-pager, the forward that says _"aapka supplier ab DZZLO par hai"_ ([[07-onboarding-and-activation-getting-both-sides-live|lesson 07]]). One honest gap — the zero-install WhatsApp customer flow that would make this effortless is the intended direction, **not built yet**; today the customer installs the same app via phone-OTP invite. Plan for what exists. **Trigger to switch customer channels on:** ≥15 activated dealers in Raipur with overlapping customer lists; then the corridor campaign in [[10-campaigns-planning-running-and-measuring-one|lesson 10]] opens NH-53 properly.

## 6. One channel, two weeks, a number, a verdict

> **Pick one channel. Run it hard for two weeks. Count one number. Write kill, keep or scale.**

| Field | Rule | Example |
| ----- | ---- | ------- |
| **Channel** | Exactly one; two at once teach nothing about either | CA / tax consultant |
| **Hypothesis** | One falsifiable sentence | "A Raipur CA with pump clients introduces 3+ dealers for a 10% rev-share" |
| **Effort box** | Hours and rupees, fixed up front | 6 founder-hours, ₹2,000 |
| **The number** | One, a step you control — never revenue | Qualified pump meetings booked |
| **Kill line** | Written _before_ you start | <2 meetings in 14 days → kill |
| **Verdict** | Kill / keep / scale, with one sentence of why | — |

Log every experiment in the **`Channel Experiments`** tab of `toolkit/vsyst-cmo-workbook.xlsx` — one row: channel, hypothesis, start date, hours, spend, meetings, dealers won, CAC, verdict, owner. It is the only defence against what kills bootstrapped marketing: six half-channels for six months and no way to say which worked. CAC per channel flows into [[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]] and the weekly scorecard (**HC8**). Two weeks is deliberate — long enough for an association meeting or a CA callback, short enough that a bad channel costs a fortnight.

## 7. Not yet — and exactly what unlocks it

| Channel | Why not yet | Trigger | Who decides |
| ------- | ----------- | ------- | ----------- |
| **Paid search / Meta ads** | Ads send strangers to a page with no proof on it — ₹5–15k CAC to convert nobody | **≥50 paying dealers** AND a landing page with ≥3 named Raipur testimonials carrying rupee numbers | CEO + D3 |
| **PR / press** | Nothing to announce; a funding-less launch story gets no pickup | A real milestone: 100 dealers, a signed OMC agreement, or a funding round | CEO |
| **SEO blog / programmatic pages** | Compounds over 12–18 months; _relying_ on it now is fatal | 2 pages/month from Month 4, zero budget. Expect nothing before Month 12 | CEO |
| **Agency or freelancer** | Agencies scale a proven channel; an unproven one buys expensive guesses | One channel has produced ≥3 dealers repeatably and founders' selling time exceeds ~15 h/week — the first-field-hire test (**HC1**, [[12-scaling-hiring-and-launching-the-big-campaign\|lesson 12]]) | All three |
| **Conference booths** | ₹5–15L for a handful of early leads; a ₹40k Dealer Day beats it tenfold | Post-seed, or an association slot at cost | D3 |

## 9. At VSYST — applying this now

VSYST has one live pump (its own), one live OMC conversation, a live Play Store listing, three founders and no marketing person. Enough for four channels, not eight.

- **DD** — owns 1, 2, 6. Phones the live dealer for three referral names. Routes Monday's five pump visits from [[M03-target-list-builder|M03]]. Drafts the IOCL ask (§3), sends it to Amey by Friday.
- **D3** — owns 3, 4, 7, 8. Gets the district office-bearer's name and number from the live dealer, confirms the association's registered name, opens the `Channel Experiments` tab and files row 1.
- **CEO** — owns 9, 10. Rewrites the Play Store listing with the [[M04-positioning-and-message-house|M04]] one-liner and six Hindi screenshots; films one DIP clip at the company's own RO.
- **All three, Friday, 30 minutes** — fill [[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]] rows for the OMC TM, the association office-bearer and one CA. Agree the kill lines out loud.

**Explicitly not happening:** no ads, no agency, no conference, no transporter outreach, no LinkedIn, no PR. Each has a written trigger in §7; when one fires, the channel opens without another argument. Why-this-segment reasoning lives in the CEO course ([[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|CEO 07]], [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]]); CAC by channel lands in [[11-metrics-budget-and-the-marketing-scorecard|lesson 11]]; WhatsApp and content mechanics in [[09-content-whatsapp-and-the-minimum-digital-presence|lesson 09]].

## 10. Exercises

**10.1 — Pick three, write the first action (30 min).**
From §2 pick exactly three channels for Weeks 2–5. For each write into [[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]]: the channel, the named human it runs through (a person, not an organisation), the first action, owner initials, the date. Cannot name a human? The channel is not ready — pick another.
_Artefact: [[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]] rows 1–3 filled, with three named people and three dates._

**10.2 — Draft and send the IOCL/OMC ask (45 min).**
Using §3 and the templates in [[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]], write one WhatsApp message to Amey: one line on what DZZLO does for a dealer's credit book, one number from your own RO, and exactly one ask — five dealer names, or 15 minutes at the next dealer meet. Under 120 words, no attachment. Read it aloud; past 40 seconds, cut. All three directors approve, then send.
_Artefact: a sent WhatsApp message, pasted into [[M10-partner-and-channel-playbook|M10]] as the worked OMC row, dated._

**10.3 — Open one channel experiment (20 min).**
In the `Channel Experiments` tab of `vsyst-cmo-workbook.xlsx`, fill one row for the channel starting Monday: hypothesis, start and end dates exactly 14 days apart, effort box in hours and rupees, the single number you will count, the kill line, the owner. Leave the verdict cell empty. Put the end date in the calendar as a 15-minute meeting named "Verdict: \<channel\>".
_Artefact: one filled row in the `Channel Experiments` tab plus a calendar entry on the kill date._
