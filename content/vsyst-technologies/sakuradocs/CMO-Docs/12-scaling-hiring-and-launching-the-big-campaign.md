# 12 — Scaling: Hiring, Agencies, Brand and Launching the Big Campaign

_Phase 4 · Scale · Months 6–18. After this lesson you can write the entry test that lets VSYST leave the Raipur 100, draft the field-associate charter, scorecard and incentive plan, decide what to give an agency and what never to, say when brand starts earning its keep, and run a three-district launch as a dated T-60 / T-30 / T-0 plan with a budget and a go/no-go._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A tiffin service starts with one woman cooking in her own kitchen. She knows every customer — who hates lauki, who pays on the 5th, whose dabba must reach by 12:30. Then she gets 200 orders, hires two cooks, and discovers something uncomfortable: **she can only hand over what she wrote down.** The dal she cooked by feel comes out different every day.

Scaling marketing is the same. You are handing over a recipe, not adding effort. **You may only scale what you can measure and repeat.** If the pitch lives in the founder's head, three hires produce three pitches, and when it fails you will not know which one failed.

So: **write it down, prove someone who is not you can run it, then buy more of it.**

## 1. The entry test — earn the right to leave Raipur

HC3 says saturate one district first; HC9 says every rung has an entry test. This is the test for the rung after the Raipur 100. **All five must be true on a dated review, not "roughly true."** If one fails you are early, not slow — the fix is eight more weeks in Raipur.

| # | Gate | Bar — **VERIFY LIVE**, set at the review and then don't move | Lives in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Measured CAC by channel** | **3 channels**, each with **≥5 paying dealers** and a defensible rupee CAC on 90-day trailing data — from the tab, not memory | [[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator\|M13]] |
| 2 | **A script someone else closed with** | **≥3 closes** run from the written script by someone who is neither the CEO nor the domain director | [[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling\|M05]] |
| 3 | **Activation, both sides** | **≥60%** of new dealers activated in 14 days — ≥1 customer linked, ≥1 invoice raised (HC10; set it off your own first-50 cohort) | [[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist\|M08]] |
| 4 | **A referral engine** | **≥4 referrals/month received**, **≥25% of new dealers referral-attributed** — the month-12 target in the [[06_REFERRAL_PROGRAM\|Dealer Dost research]] | [[M09-referral-program-one-pager\|M09]] |
| 5 | **The money gate** | Committed revenue covers **12 months of the hire's loaded cost before he sells anything** — about **30–40 paying GSTINs** | [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales\|CEO 07]] §9.2 |

Gate 2 is the one founders fake. A script that only works in your mouth is not a script, it is a personality. A cleaner tell: **20 consecutive conversations with no new objection.**

## 2. The hiring ladder (HC1)

The house call restated: **founders do marketing and sales themselves for the first 100 dealers.** Do not skip rungs — each makes the next measurable.

| Rung | Role | Hire when (all triggers hold) | Comp — **VERIFY LIVE** | Owns | Never asked to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Field sales / marketing associate**, Raipur | ≥10 paying dealers, one channel produced ≥3 repeatably, founders' selling time >~15 h/week | **₹20–35k/month + incentive.** Raipur postings run ₹15–35k, Paytm's at ₹18–25k ([apna.co](https://apna.co/jobs/field_sales-jobs-in-raipur)); national average ~₹26.5k ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Salaries/field-sales-executive-salary-SRCH_KO0,21.htm)). Plus ₹6–10k travel | 25 pump visits/week off the M03 list, demos from the M05 script, onboarding both sides, the pipeline tracker | **Invent the message.** He runs the script; he does not rewrite it |
| **2** | **Associate #2 and #3**, one per district | #1 hit target two months running, the district has an association or OMC warm entry, the T-60 plan is written | Same band and plan | One district each, same playbook, same numbers | Cover two districts. One rep, one district, one number |
| **3** | **Marketing generalist / lead** | **₹50L–1 Cr ARR**, or post-seed | ~**₹8–15L CTC** — the Indian SaaS band for a first commercial hire ([Zohort](https://zohort.com/bdr-vs-sdr-vs-ae-saas-sales-hiring-guide/)) | Content, WhatsApp, partners, landing pages, the scorecard, briefs, freelancers | **Run field visits.** A lead at pumps is an associate on a lead's pay |
| **4** | **Head of sales _or_ head of marketing** | 3+ associates, 2+ districts, a lead in seat, founder span breaking | Post-seed exec band ([[C20-compensation-bands-and-esop-design\|C20]]) | Quota, territory, forecast — or the demand engine | — |
| **5** | **CMO** | Post-Series-A / **₹5 Cr+ ARR**, a team of 5–15 to lead | Exec band + equity | Brand, category definition, a multi-state demand engine, the marketing org | Be hired to *find* the engine |

**Hire two, not one — or fake it.** With one rep you cannot separate a rep problem from a process problem; hiring one "doesn't save you money or time" because you lose the A/B test ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/when-you-hire-your-first-sales-rep-just-make-sure-you-hire-two/), [and](https://www.saastr.com/dear-saastr-how-do-i-hire-a-great-first-1-2-sales-reps)). Two is unaffordable here: run **one associate plus the domain director, same script, in parallel.**

**Head of sales first.** The argument is blunt: "a good early-stage salesperson should be able to hunt for their own leads using their network or sheer hustle. On the flip side, a marketing person who can close deals doesn't really exist" ([Blue Seedling](https://www.blueseedling.com/blog/should-my-first-business-hire-be-marketing-or-sales/)). Marketing stays with the lead until Series A.

**Never hire a CMO to find the engine.** The failure mode: "hiring a brand-builder to run growth before the company has an engine for that leader to scale" ([HSG](https://www.hsgcap.com/article/startup-first-coo-cfo-cmo-hiring-timing/)). Even timed right the seat is unforgiving — 4.2-year average tenure, shortest in the C-suite ([Spencer Stuart via Behind the CMO](https://www.behindthecmo.com/resources/cmo-tenure-statistics/)).

### 2.1 Where to find them — rank by "has already been told no in a pump office"

| Pool | Why | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OMC field staff** — sales officers, TM assistants (IOCL/BPCL/HPCL Raipur) | Knows the pump, the dealer's day, the DSR, the vocabulary. Highest fit | Associations, the IOCL relationship ([[08-referrals-associations-and-partners-the-warm-engine\|lesson 08]]) |
| **FMCG / telecom distributor reps** (TSO, DSE) | Trained on beat plans, call targets, route discipline. The pool churns — 25–35% annual attrition in Indian FMCG frontline sales ([Taggd](https://taggd.in/blogs/fmcg-sales-attrition-growth-risk/)) | apna, WorkIndia, distributor referrals |
| **Ex-Khatabook / Vyapar / OkCredit / MyBillBook field teams** | Have sold *software* to a shopkeeper who did not want software | LinkedIn, apna, your own dealers |

### 2.2 The loop — the ride-along is the real test

Process mechanics belong to the COO ([[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process|T10]]); exec hires use [[C19-executive-hiring-scorecard-and-loop|C19]]. **The marketing addition is the work sample — a paid half-day ride-along.** Four pumps; the candidate runs the M05 opener at two while you watch. Score three things a CV cannot show: does he speak the owner's Hindi, does he stay in the room after the first "abhi nahi", does he write down what he heard.

### 2.3 Incentives — pay for activation, not signups

**Both sides live, or it doesn't count.** A signup bounty buys a folder of dead tenants. So variable pay lands on **activated** dealers — ≥1 customer linked and ≥1 invoice inside 14 days ([[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist|M08]] defines it, HC8 counts it). SaaS practice puts all-in comp near 20–25% of first-year deal value for early reps ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/a-framework-and-some-ideas-for-your-first-sales-comp-plan/)); at DZZLO ticket sizes that becomes a flat bounty per activation plus a district milestone ([[M14-marketing-hiring-scorecard-and-role-charters|M14]]). Keep it to **three lines a rep can compute in his head.**

### 2.4 Handing over the pump visit

The founder does not stop going to pumps; he stops going to *every* pump. Over ~30 days: founder runs it, associate watches → they run it together → associate runs it, founder watches silently → associate goes alone, founder reads the notes ([[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|CEO 07]] §9). **The gate is gate 2 of §1.** Until then you have a trainee, not a rep.

## 3. Agency vs freelancer vs in-house

The trap first, because it is expensive and politely sold: **agencies sell impressions, and nobody outside this building knows the dealer.** **Buy execution; never judgement.**

| Task | Cost band — **VERIFY LIVE**, three quotes | When it makes sense — and what stays in-house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Video editing** (Hindi demos, testimonials) | Freelancer: ₹1,000–5,000 per reel; ₹25k–1.5L/month for a 10–30 video retainer ([Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/freelance-video-editing-rates-india-vs-global-market)) | From video #3. **Shoot on a phone at the pump and write the script yourself** — the dealer's words are the asset; pay for the cut |
| **Landing page** | ₹20–60k at agency rate cards ([Gleam Studio](https://www.gleamstudio.design/blog-post/how-much-do-design-services-cost-in-india)); less from a solo freelancer | Once, for the template; district variants are copy edits. **Copy and CTA stay yours** ([[M11-content-and-whatsapp-calendar\|M11]]) |
| **Meta / Google ads** | Freelancer or small agency: ₹5–20k/month, or 10–15% of ad spend, *plus* the media ([The Conversions](https://theconversions.com/blog/digital-marketing-cost-india/)) | **Not yet.** HC4: no paid ads before ~50 dealers and a landing page with real proof. **Targeting and offer stay yours** |
| **PR** | Nobody. Retainers start ~₹50k/month | **Not yet.** A dealer does not read TechCrunch. Association newsletters and OMC circulars are DZZLO's PR, and founder-held |
| **Design** (brand kit, collateral) | Freelancer: ₹2–5k per social asset up to ₹1L+ for a design system ([Gleam Studio](https://www.gleamstudio.design/blog-post/how-much-do-design-services-cost-in-india)); a v1 kit sits far below | Once, at §4's trigger. **Brand decisions stay in-house; only files are bought** |

The rule that survives every quote: **if the deliverable requires knowing what a dealer fears, it stays in-house.** And WhatsApp is a conversation, not a posting schedule: it stays in the building.

## 4. Brand — when it starts mattering

Brand is not a logo; it is what a dealer believes before you speak. For the first 100 dealers that belief comes from proof and from who introduced you. Three things then flip the switch:

1. **An association endorses you** — the badge outperforms any campaign; that channel converts at 10–20% warm ([[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|owner acquisition]] §5).
2. **You enter a second district**, where nobody knows the founders' faces and the name travels alone.
3. **The name sits on the pump's invoice.** Every DZZLO invoice a dealer hands a transporter is a free brand impression at the moment of the transaction — the largest brand asset VSYST owns, and it costs nothing.

The minimum kit and no more: wordmark (English + Hindi), two colours, one font, a dealer leave-behind, a WhatsApp display card, an invoice footer, a stall backdrop. Architecture and taglines are decided — **DZZLO customer-facing, VSYST Technologies legal**, branded-house, "डीज़ल लो" as the idiom ([[06_Brand_Architecture|Brand Architecture]] §§1–2). Do not re-open it; commission the files.

**Trademark: file before the second district.** Government fee is **₹4,500 per class** e-filed for a DPIIT-recognised startup or Udyam-registered MSME, **₹9,000** otherwise, under the Trade Marks Rules 2017 — professional fees on top ([Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/trademark-registration-fees-india)). **VERIFY LIVE** with the IP lawyer: classes (9 and 42 minimum), whether the Udyam/DPIIT certificate is current, the Hindi wordmark. Already a P0 task in the [[12_Roadmap_Phases|roadmap]] (0.1.4).

## 5. Launching the big campaign — three districts in 90 days

HC3 as a plan: **a "huge campaign" is the district playbook run in N districts at once.** Nothing new is invented. Brief, budget and post-mortem live in [[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem|M12]]; this is the shape.

| Phase | What happens | Owner | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T-60** | Pick 3 districts by the [[M03-target-list-builder\|M03]] method (ICP pump count within 60 km, association strength, OMC territory overlap, one warm contact, travel time) · hire and ramp 3 associates, 30 days each with a founder · letters to associations and OMC territory managers · a proof pack: 3 named Raipur dealers, one with a rupee number | CEO + domain director | 3 districts named in writing · 3 offers signed · 3 association meetings booked |
| **T-30** | One **Dealer Day** booked per district — venue, a CA on stage, snacks; the ~₹40k workshop shape from [[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION\|owner acquisition]] §6 · a WhatsApp list and landing-page variant per district, from M03 · a 60-day referral bonus window | Associates + lead | Dates confirmed · ≥120 numbers per district · 3 pages live · bonus posted to the Dealer Dost group |
| **T-0 (launch week)** | Dealer Day in each district on consecutive days, founders at all three · associates run 25 visits/week · **daily 15-minute stand-up** · scorecard live | Everyone | ≥40 attendees per event · ≥10 demos per district · scorecard filled nightly |
| **T+30** | First gate per district: visits, demos, trials, **activated dealers**, CAC | Lead | **Go** ≥5 activated · **Watch** 3–4 · **Stop spending** <3 |
| **T+60** | Second gate · double down on the winning channel, cut the losing one | Lead | **Go** ≥10 activated · CAC ≤1.5× Raipur |
| **T+90** | Post-mortem per district in [[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem\|M12]] · scale the two that cleared, exit the third, redeploy its associate | CEO | Post-mortem written · a district-4 decision with a date |

**The one number: activated dealers per district — 15 each, 45 total, in 90 days.** Not leads, not signups, not reach.

**Budget shape (illustrative, every line VERIFY LIVE):** roughly **₹5.5–8 lakh over 90 days**, **~65–70% of it people** (3 associates × ₹28k + travel × 3 months); the rest events (3 × ₹40k), association co-marketing (₹25k–1L per chapter), print and video (₹40–60k), landing pages (~₹25k), referral bounties (~30 × ₹2,000). **This does not fit HC7's ₹30–50k/month bootstrap budget, and is not meant to.** HC7 caps *non-people* spend *while pre-revenue*; this launch is funded from revenue at ₹30L+ ARR or the seed round ([[12_Roadmap_Phases|roadmap]] Phase 1). If neither exists, run **one** district at a third of the cost.

**The rungs above.** *State* (~Months 12–18): a marketing lead in seat, Chhattisgarhi/Hindi creative as default rather than translation, the OMC **regional** office rather than territory managers, association work at state-federation level. *Multi-state* (Month 18+): the NH-53 corridor first, because transporters travel it and the customer side carries the brand across the border (HC2, [[05_Beachhead_and_Expansion|beachhead]]). Both gated by §1's five tests, re-run at scale.

## 6. What changes for the founders at each rung

| Rung | Field hours | Keep | Release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raipur 100 (now) | 15–25 h/week, all three | Everything | Nothing |
| First associate | 12–15 h/week | Message, pricing, OMC/association relationships, closes above standard quote | Routine first visits, follow-ups, onboarding calls |
| 3 associates, 3 districts | 6–8 h/week — Dealer Days, escalations | Message, partners, scorecard review, the go/no-go | Territory ownership, daily pipeline, demos |
| Marketing lead in seat | 3–4 h/week | Positioning sign-off, IOCL/association relationships, budget | Content, WhatsApp, campaigns, freelancers, the calendar |
| CMO | Board-level only | The category story, the biggest partnerships | The demand engine itself |

Two things founders **never** release: **the message** (HC5 — a strategy artefact, not copywriting) and **the OMC and association relationships** (relationships do not transfer with a title).

## 8. At VSYST — applying this now

VSYST is at the top row: three founders, pre-revenue, zero dealers on a paid plan. **Nothing in §§2–5 is a this-week action, and pretending otherwise is the most expensive mistake this lesson can cause.** This week's work is writing the tests down while you are still honest — you will not be, at dealer #9 with a candidate in front of you.

- **CEO** — writes the §1 entry test into [[M14-marketing-hiring-scorecard-and-role-charters|M14]] with real numbers and a review date, books the review as a recurring calendar entry, logs it ([[C29-decision-journal-and-one-way-door-log|C29]]).
- **Domain director** — starts the bench now: five names from OMC field staff, distributor reps and hardware engineers he knows. A bench built over six months costs nothing; one built in the week you need it costs a mis-hire.
- **Third director** — three quotes for brand kit v1; confirms with the CA whether Udyam/DPIIT status earns the ₹4,500-per-class rate; files classes 9 and 42.
- **Template this week:** [[M14-marketing-hiring-scorecard-and-role-charters|M14]] — and leave the JD unposted.
