# 10 — Campaigns: Planning, Running and Measuring One (then Ten)

_Phase 4 · Scale · Months 3–12. After this lesson you can write a one-page campaign brief with a single number it must move, run the "Raipur 100" campaign on ₹30–50k/month, A/B-test a pitch in the field without lying to yourself about the sample, write the post-mortem that produces a CAC per channel, and climb the ladder from one district to a multi-district launch._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A tiffin service in Raipur can do two things. The first is "marketing": print pamphlets when there is money, hand them out when there is time, hope. The second is a **campaign**: _"In thirty days I will get forty lunch subscriptions from the three office buildings on GE Road. Every weekday at 12:30 I stand at the gate with ten free sample dabbas. Budget ₹8,000. My number is forty."_

Both cost money. Only the second can be repeated, taught to someone else, or made bigger — because only it has an audience you can name, a number you can count, and a date on which you say whether it worked.

"Launching a huge campaign" is the same thing in ten office parks at once, by ten people, one sheet of paper each — after the first one told you what a subscription costs to win.

## 1. What a campaign is, and what "doing marketing" is

A **campaign** is a time-boxed push with **one audience, one message, one offer, one or two channels, a budget, and exactly one number it must move.** Cannot name all seven? Then it is activity, not a campaign.

The one-number rule is not style. Lean Analytics calls it the **One Metric That Matters** — pick it from the riskiest part of the business ([Holistics](https://www.holistics.io/blog/lean-analytics-part-1-an-introduction-to-analytical-thinking/)).

**The tell.** "Doing marketing" sounds like _"let's post more on WhatsApp"_: it runs forever, succeeds when the response feels good, and is owned by everyone. A campaign sounds like _"60 dealers on the Raipur 100 list get the shortage message by Friday"_: it ends on a date, succeeds at **10 activated dealers counted from the database**, and has one owner per budget line.

**The eight parts of a brief** ([[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem|M12]] is the page you fill):

| Part                      | Answers                                       | The trap                             |
|---|---|---|
| **Name and rung**         | Which rung of the ladder is this?              | No rung = a hobby                    |
| **Goal + the one number** | Which number moves, X to Y, by when?           | Two numbers = zero numbers           |
| **Audience (the list)**   | Which named businesses, how many, listed where? | "Pumps in Chhattisgarh" is not a list |
| **Message**               | The one line we say, in their words            | Feature lists; English only          |
| **Offer**                 | What they get if they say yes today            | "Try our app" is not an offer        |
| **Channels (1–2)**        | How we reach them, in what order               | Six channels, none worked properly   |
| **Budget + owners**       | ₹ per line, one name per line                  | Founder time unpriced → "looks free" |
| **Calendar + review**     | Which week does what; when we read the numbers | No end date → no post-mortem         |

## 2. The worked example — the "Raipur 100"

**The funnel it implies** — write these down _before_ Week 2, so Week 6 can name the broken step:

| Step                                 | Target (rate)         | Counted in                        |
|---|---|---|
| Named pumps on the list              | 100                   | `Target List` tab                 |
| Contacted (WhatsApp / phone / intro) | 100 (100%)            | `Pipeline` tab                    |
| Pump visits held                     | 40 (40%)              | `Pipeline` tab                    |
| Demos given (10-minute)              | 25 (62% of visits)    | [[M07-sales-pipeline-tracker\|M07]] |

Ten out of a hundred is a **10% contact-to-activation rate**. Sanity-check it: cold field visits convert at 5–12%, warm ones at 25%+, referrals at 25–40% ([[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] §5). A list worked through referral, the OMC and the association _should_ beat 10%; a cold-walked one will not. **VERIFY LIVE** — replace every rate with your own after Week 6.

## 3. The campaign ladder — how "huge" is actually reached

Every rung is the same brief with a bigger denominator. **You do not skip a rung**, and each entry test is measured, not felt. That is atomic-network logic: build one dense network before copying it, because a thin network everywhere dies everywhere ([Andrew Chen](https://www.coldstart.com/)).

| Rung                                        | Entry test (must be **measured**)                                                             | What changes                                                                                       | Duration · template |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — one dealer**                          | Product does the job for one pump end to end                                                     | Nothing. One founder, ₹0, one relationship                                                          | 2–4 weeks · [[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist\|M08]] |
| **2 — ten founding dealers** _(Raipur 100)_ | 1 dealer activated both sides, ≥1 invoice/week for 4 straight weeks                              | Named list of 100; ₹30–50k/month; referral + OMC + association + visits                             | 10 weeks · [[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem\|M12]] |
| **3 — 100 / Raipur district**               | ≥10 paying dealers; **one** channel produced ≥3 repeatably; CAC known ±30%; activation ≥60%      | First field associate (~₹20–35k/month, **VERIFY LIVE**); monthly Dealer Day; landing page with proof | 6–9 months · M12 + [[M14-marketing-hiring-scorecard-and-role-charters\|M14]] |
| **4 — NH-53 corridor (Raipur → Nagpur)**    | ~60% of ICP pumps within 60 km live; 90-day retention ≥85%; 2 reps run the script unassisted     | 2–3 reps; corridor logic (customers who travel the highway); association MoU; Hindi YouTube          | 6 months · M12 multi-district |
| **5 — Chhattisgarh state**                  | Two districts at rung-3 economics; the playbook exists as a document; support SLA holds at 3×    | State endorsement; a launch event per district; broadcast list; local press                          | 9–12 months · M12 multi-district |
| **6 — multi-state**                         | State CAC at target and payback inside the plan; funded or profitable                            | Marketing lead hired; paid ads switched on; agency for content                                       | 12+ months · M12 + M14 |

**What never changes at any rung:** the pitch script ([[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling|M05]]), the definition of an activated dealer, and the rule that both sides are counted. Those three make rung 5 a copy of rung 2 instead of a fresh experiment.

Jumbotail did it in kirana retail: dense first in Bengaluru with 30,000+ stores, then 13 cities ([Free Press Journal, 2020](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/jumbotail-expands-operations-to-13-cities-aims-to-reach-200000-kiranas-in-fy21)).

## 4. Running it: the weekly loop and honest field tests

- **Monday 09:30 — numbers (15 min).** The eight scorecard numbers ([[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]]) are already filled; you read them, you don't build them. Off-track rows get an owner and a date.
- **Tuesday–Friday — field.** Visits, demos, setups, follow-ups. Nothing is rescheduled for internal work.
- **Friday 18:00 — learnings (20 min).** What did dealers say that surprised us? Which objection appeared twice? What changes on Monday?

The discipline is Amazon's weekly business review: spend the time on controllable **inputs** — visits, demos, activations — not outcomes you can only watch ([Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/the-amazon-weekly-business-review/)). The 4DX rule applies: anyone should see in five seconds whether the campaign is winning ([Perdoo](https://www.perdoo.com/resources/online-guides/4dx)).

**Field A/B testing — and the honesty it needs.** Run two pitch openings, or two leave-behinds, on **alternate weeks** — not alternate visits, since one person cannot switch scripts mid-week without contaminating both. Log the version each pump got in `Channel Experiments`.

Then be honest about the sample. Forty visits gives twenty per arm: enough to see a **doubling**, nowhere near enough for a 15% lift. Picking a winner because "A looks better this week" is the documented error — peeking at a running test and stopping when it looks good turns a claimed 5% significance into a real 26% ([Evan Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html)).

- **Only test things you expect to double.** Two different pitches, not two adjectives.
- **Fix the sample before you start** — "four weeks, two arms, then we look once."
- **Declare a winner only when it wins three weeks running _and_ you can say why in one sentence.** No nameable reason means noise.

| Change freely (weekly)                                     | Never change mid-campaign            |
|---|---|
| Opening line, objection answers                            | The audience list definition         |
| Leave-behind, demo order, follow-up cadence                | The offer                            |
| Visit routing, time of day, which segment you prioritise   | The definition of "activated"        |
| Channel **mix** — move budget from weak to strong at Week 9 | **The one number**                   |

Changing anything on the right does not modify the campaign — it **ends** it and starts a new one. Write a new brief, and record in the post-mortem that the first was abandoned.

## 5. How big campaigns differ — the multi-district launch

Past rung 3, "huge" means **the same brief run in four districts at once.** What is added:

| Element      | One district                       | Multi-district launch                                                                        |
|---|---|---|
| People       | 3 founders                         | One hired field rep per district on the **proven** script; a founder as roving coach          |
| Authority    | One association secretary          | An **association endorsement letter** on letterhead, in every leave-behind                    |
| OMC          | One territory manager              | A **slot at the OMC dealer meet** per district — the highest-authority room available          |
| Events       | One Dealer Day                     | **One launch event per district**: same deck, a local dealer on stage                          |
| WhatsApp     | Manual openers                     | **Broadcast to the association list**, district-specific proof |
| Media        | None                               | Local Hindi press + a YouTube dealer testimonial per district                                  |
| Referral     | Always-on ₹2,000 bounty            | A **time-boxed bonus window** in the launch month ([[06_REFERRAL_PROGRAM\|Dealer Dost]]) |
| Landing page | Optional                           | Required — district-named proof: "17 pumps in Durg run on DZZLO"                               |

**Three coordination artefacts, no more.** (1) **One brief per district**, same template, one named owner. (2) **One scorecard**, a row per district plus a company total, filled by Monday 09:30 — its marketing rows feed the company scorecard ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]). (3) **A daily 15-minute stand-up** on WhatsApp video: yesterday's visits, today's plan, one blocker.

**The budget shape flips.** In one district, spend is fuel and printing. In four it is **mostly people and events** — reps, travel, venues — and still barely advertising. OkCredit reached 6.5M merchants in 24 months at a ratio Lightspeed put as _"for every paid acquisition, 2 organic merchants join"_ ([Lightspeed](https://lsvp.com/stories/empowering-indias-60m-offline-merchants-2/)). Association rooms stay the cheapest authority going — AIPDA, the largest national body for retail-outlet dealers, runs conclaves that put a Union minister in front of the membership ([The Tribune, 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/petroleum-dealers-must-become-active-partners-in-indias-energy-transition-hardeep-puri/)). Size districts from PPAC's state-wise file — **VERIFY LIVE** ([PPAC](https://ppac.gov.in/infrastructure/retail-outlets)).

**The four ways it fails:**

1. **Launching before CAC is known.** Four districts × unknown economics = four times the burn, no diagnosis. The rung-3 entry test exists for this.
2. **Message drift across reps.** By week three every rep has "improved" the pitch. Fix: the script is a document ([[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling|M05]]), the coach rides along weekly, and the Friday call reads one real opening line aloud.
3. **Activation not tracked.** Signups look wonderful in a launch month. If nobody counts invoices in 14 days, you find out in month four.
4. **Both sides not counted.** A dealer with no live customers is a delayed churn event. Count activated **customer firms per dealer** in every district row — [[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|lesson 02]], [[11_EDUCATION_GAP|the education gap]].

Which district when, and which gate unlocks the next, is [[12_Roadmap_Phases|the roadmap]]; transporter-side campaigns are in [[08_Marketing_Strategy|transporters 08]] §4.

## 6. The post-mortem — the only part that compounds

On the last day, in 45 minutes, the founders fill six boxes in [[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem|M12]]: **hypothesis · result · CAC by channel · keep · kill · next campaign.**

Two rules from engineering practice. **Blameless:** assume everyone acted sensibly on what they knew and hunt the systemic reason, not the person — otherwise nobody writes an honest one twice ([Google SRE](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/)). **Actionable:** every finding becomes a line in the next brief or gets deleted. A post-mortem with no consequence is a diary.

The output is not prose. It is a row per channel in `Channel Experiments` (spend, dealers activated, CAC, verdict) plus a dated entry test for the next rung — what makes campaign eleven cheaper than campaign one. It feeds [[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]].

## 7. At VSYST — applying this now

This week, before anything is spent:

- **Third director** — writes the Raipur 100 brief in [[M12-campaign-brief-budget-and-post-mortem|M12]] with seven budget lines in rupees, and books the two standing slots in all three calendars.
- **Domain director** — confirms the one number, signs off the funnel targets, books the IOCL and association meetings inside ten days.
- **CEO** — writes the query returning "activated dealers this week" and "customer firms per dealer" into the brief's Source column. A number that cannot be queried is an opinion.

The honest "not yet": **no paid ads, no PR agency, no brand campaign, no second district** until the rung-3 entry test passes with real numbers. The trigger is written down; don't argue it again.
