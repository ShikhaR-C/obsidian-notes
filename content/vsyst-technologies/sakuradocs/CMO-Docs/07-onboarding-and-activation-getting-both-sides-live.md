# 07 — Onboarding and Activation: Getting Both Sides Live

_Phase 3 · Go to Market · Weeks 2–12. After this lesson you can define activation for DZZLO in one testable sentence, run a dealer through a day-by-day 14-day onboarding, get his top three credit customers invited and confirming rates, train the three staff members who can quietly kill the account, and read the four early-warning numbers that say a new tenant is dying before it churns._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A kirana shop in Raipur gets a card machine. The salesman photographs the shopkeeper holding it, ticks "installed", and goes home. For a month everyone pays cash, the receipt roll stays wrapped, and at quarter-end the shop returns the machine. On the salesman's sheet it was a success.

Now imagine he had stayed forty more minutes: swiped his own card for a ten-rupee sale, taught the counter boy to say "card chalega, sir", and come back on day three to check the swipe count. That shop keeps the machine.

**Signing up is the photograph. Activation is the first real swipe, repeated until it is a habit.** DZZLO's version is harder: our machine needs two people — the dealer must send a rate, his customer must confirm it. Marketing's job ends not at "trial started" but when both sides have moved. That is **HC10: sales-assisted, product-retained**.

## 1. Activation, defined — and why "signed up" is a vanity number

"Dealers signed up" is the textbook vanity metric: impressive, only ever rises, tells you nothing actionable — Eric Ries's original list names registered users explicitly ([ProductPlan](https://www.productplan.com/glossary/vanity-metrics)). An **actionable** metric is one where you know what makes it move.

An **activation metric** is the milestone a new user must hit for you to believe he will still be here in six months. The test is arithmetic, not taste: users who hit it should retain **at least 2×** better than users who don't, and you must be able to influence it directly ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)). Growth practice splits the journey into three moments — **setup**, **aha**, **habit** — and warns that most companies stop measuring at setup, the card-machine mistake ([Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/guides/define-customer-activation-moments); [GrowthPigeon](https://growthpigeon.com/articles/setup-aha-habit-saas-activation-moments)). Activation is the join between marketing and everything after it ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/blog/pirate-metrics-framework)).

**The house definition (HC8):**

> A DZZLO tenant is **activated** when it has **≥1 customer firm linked and ≥1 invoice raised, within 14 days of install.**

| Moment | Dealer side | Customer side | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Setup** | Tenant, products, rates entered | Invite accepted via phone OTP | Necessary, not sufficient — never count it |
| **Aha** | First invoice out of DZZLO, not the bill book | First rate confirmed in the window | The pitch, delivered once ([[04-the-message-positioning-and-the-one-line-pitch\|lesson 04]]) |
| **Habit** | Invoices weekly; a voucher settled; a DIP logged | Confirms most nights unchased | The real churn predictor — read at day 30 and 90 |

Why 14 days: customers reaching first value inside 14 days retain far better than those taking 30+, and the average B2B SaaS activates only ~**37.5%** of signups (Userpilot 2026, 62 companies, via [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/saas-onboarding-best-practices-2025-guide-checklist)) — **VERIFY LIVE**. Indian SMB buyers disengage after the sales handoff unless someone stays in the loop ([CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/b2b-saas-customer-retention-playbook-india-2026)).

**The two-sided twist.** A dealer with invoices and zero linked customers is *half* activated — the dangerous half. He is paying for a nicer bill book, and the day a cheaper one appears he leaves. The value the customer stays for exists only once the other side shows up ([Andrew Chen — Cold Start](https://www.coldstart.com/)). Theory: [[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|lesson 02]]. This is the fortnight that makes it true.

## 2. The dealer's first 14 days

Deliberately **white-glove** — us inside the dealer's workflow, not a help link ([Dock](https://www.dock.us/library/white-glove-onboarding)). At three founders and ten dealers that is affordable, and the only way to learn what a self-serve flow must one day do.

**CEO** = technical founder, **DD** = domain director, **D3** = third director. Tick-boxes and full message text: [[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist|M08]].

| Day | What happens | Who | The WhatsApp that day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0** | On site, 90 min: install on owner's + munim's phones, tenant, GSTIN, products, opening balances, tonight's rates | DD on site, CEO on call; **munim watches** | "Aaj raat 8 baje aap khud pehla rate daaliye — main line par hoon" |
| **1** | Dealer names his **top three credit customers**; invite sent to #1 | Dealer names, DD sends, D3 logs in [[M07-sales-pipeline-tracker\|M07]] | His own invite message, drafted by us, **sent from his number** |
| **2** | First rate confirmation inside the 10 PM–6 AM window | Customer's order-placer; DD on standby 9:45–10:15 PM | To the customer: "aaj raat rate ka notification aayega — ek tap" |
| **3** | First real order → dispatch with **driver OTP** → **first invoice** | Dealer + driver; DD watches end to end | Screenshot back: "yeh aapka pehla DZZLO invoice hai" |
| **7** | First **voucher** against that invoice; ledger PDF to the customer | Munim enters it; DD checks it reconciles | Ledger PDF + "ab har mahine yeh khud ban jayega" |
| **10** | First **DIP reading** — dip, density, decant | Pump manager; CEO checks it landed | 3-minute Hindi video: dip entry on your own phone |
| **14** | **Review at the pump, 30 min:** activation sign-off, linked-customer count, referral ask, testimonial | DD leads; dealer + munim | Written summary: invoices raised, customers live, what's next |

Two rules make it work. **Day 0 ends with a rate in the system, or day 0 is not over** — no rate, no confirmation, no order, no invoice. **We type on day 0; the dealer types on day 3** — if our thumbs are still working in week two we have built a service business, not a product.

## 3. The customer-side invite — the crux

This is the part that fails. A dealer will invoice happily from DZZLO forever and never mention it to a customer, because introducing new software to the man who owes you ₹8 lakh feels risky. Make it a *favour to the customer*, and make it take ninety seconds.

**Three names, not thirty.** The biggest credit customers by outstanding — they have the rate disputes, so they gain most, and three is few enough that he will actually do it (HC2: the customer-side pitch is delivered **by the dealer, with our material**).

**He sends his own message, from his own number.** We draft; he forwards. Hindi first, English underneath for the second-generation transport-office staff:

> नमस्ते [नाम] जी। हम अब सारे credit customers का rate, order और ledger **DZZLO app** पर कर रहे हैं। कल का rate आपको रात को app में दिखेगा — एक tap में confirm कर दीजिए। पूरा ledger और हर invoice की PDF app में हमेशा मिलेगी, phone करके पूछना नहीं पड़ेगा। कोई charge नहीं है। मेरी team का आदमी कल आपके office आकर 15 मिनट में set कर देगा — बस आपका मोबाइल नंबर चाहिए।

> Namaste [Name]. We've moved all our credit customers' rates, orders and ledgers to the **DZZLO app**. You'll see tomorrow's rate at night — confirm with one tap. Your ledger and every invoice PDF stay in the app, so no more calling the office. Free for you. My colleague will set it up at your office tomorrow in 15 minutes — I just need your mobile number.

**Our follow-up call, within 24 hours.** Thirty seconds, not a sales call: *"Sir, [dealer] ji ne aapka number diya. Main kal 15 minute ke liye aa raha hoon — jo order karta hai aur jo accounts dekhta hai, dono ko bula lijiye."* Those two, not the owner.

**Install on site, with the right two people.** DZZLO onboards by **phone-OTP invite**, not email and password, and a customer firm has separate scopes for admin, order-placer and accountant ([[00_OVERVIEW|product overview]] §2). Install on the **order-placer's** phone (`COrder`) and the **accountant's** (`CAccount`); the owner can hold `CPrimary` and never open it. Installing only on the owner's phone is how this dies.

**Train the rate-confirmation habit in one message.** The daily rhythm the product hangs on, in one sentence:

> रोज़ रात 10 बजे notification आएगा। कल का rate दिखेगा — सही लगे तो एक tap **Confirm**. सुबह 6 बजे तक confirm नहीं किया तो वही rate अपने आप lock हो जाएगा और कल की billing उसी पर होगी। इसलिए रोज़ रात एक tap.

Frame the 6 AM lock honestly. **It is the customer's protection, not our rule**: the rate he confirmed is the rate on his invoice, timestamped — the end of "humne yeh rate maana hi nahin". Ignore it and the dealer's posted rate stands. Never let a dealer use the lock as a threat; the moment it feels like a trap, he stops opening the app.

**Give the customer his own reason: multi-dealer.** One customer firm links to **many dealers**, each with its own credit line (`dealer_custs`). Say it at the install: *"Aapke doosre supplier bhi DZZLO par aayenge — tab sabka ledger ek hi app mein."* That is the traveling-customer flywheel ([[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|lesson 02]]), the reason we saturate one district first (HC3), and why customer-side effort compounds when a dealer churns.

**When the customer refuses.** Some will, and pushing damages the dealer's relationship — the asset we are borrowing.

| Situation | What we do | What the dealer keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat no | Log the reason in M08 and stop. Dealer uses **dealer-on-behalf ordering** — he places the order | Invoicing, GST/TCS, credit ledger, driver OTP, DIP — full single-player value |
| "Send me the ledger, I won't install" | Send the **ledger PDF** on WhatsApp monthly, from the app | Same — plus the customer now reads a DZZLO document every month |
| Staff installed, owner never opens it | Fine. Count it — the order-placer is the user that matters | Confirmations and orders flow normally |
| Refused, then a rate dispute happens | **The re-ask moment.** Return within 48 hours with the timestamped confirmation record | The dispute becomes the demo |

Count honestly: zero linked customers means **dealer-side activated only** — amber, on the re-ask list.

## 4. The honest note: the zero-install flow is planned, not built

Our own research says the right customer-side architecture is **WhatsApp-first** — no install, no account, phone as identity, one-tap confirm links ([[11_EDUCATION_GAP|Education Gap]] §7–8). It is **not built**: today the customer installs the same app and joins by phone-OTP invite. Never pitch it as if it exists.

Meanwhile:

- **Treat the install as a field task we perform**, not a link we send. Fifteen minutes at the transport office beats any onboarding email — and WhatsApp is where these firms already live, ~77% of Indian SMEs on WhatsApp Business ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/how-whatsapp-business-is-bringing-indias-smes-to-the-digital-fold/)).
- **Keep the customer's job to one tap.** Every extra field costs a customer; low digital confidence among small-business staff is a constraint, not an excuse ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/hub/2022/12/improving-digital-literacy-in-india-a-review/)).
- **Log every refusal and its reason** in M08 — that list is the product spec for the WhatsApp flow.
- **Watch install conversion.** Education Gap §9 sets the kill-switch: under ~10% customer-side install conversion, the app path is wrong. Note that §10 calls onboarding over 15 minutes per dealer a red flag — that target assumes the unbuilt architecture. **Until it exists, the 14-day white-glove motion above is the house call**, and the gap is the honest cost of the missing feature.
- **Cost check for later:** Meta charges per message in India — utility ≈ ₹0.115, marketing ≈ ₹0.863, 24-hour service-window replies free, +18% GST ([Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india)) — **VERIFY LIVE**.

## 5. The three people who can silently kill the account

The dealer signs; his staff decide. None will say they are blocking it — the app just quietly stops being used. Vernacular, hands-on training is what carried Khatabook and OkCredit through millions of Indian merchants, and the more a user enters, the less likely he is to leave ([The Flyy](https://www.theflyy.com/blog/growth-hacking-the-rise-of-bookkeeping-fintech-apps)).

| Person | Why he blocks it | The 15-minute training | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Munim / accountant** | Fears the app exposes his errors or replaces him; the ledger has been *his* authority for 20 years | Show the ledger and the monthly credit/debit roll-up. Frame: "month-end ab 3 din ka nahin, 3 ghante ka." Give him his own login | He enters one voucher unaided |
| **Pump manager / shift in-charge** | DIP readings feel like surveillance | Dip, density and decant entry on **his own phone**. Frame: variance now has a record that protects him from blame | He logs one dip and one decant |
| **Driver** | One more thing before he can leave; may not read English | One screen, one OTP, nothing else. Frame: the OTP proves he delivered full quantity — his defence against "kam maal aaya" | He completes one OTP delivery |

Two rules: **train each one alone, at his station, on his own phone** — never a group lecture; and **get the owner to introduce the training**, because staff read his enthusiasm, not ours. Resistance here is fear of exposure and redundancy; participation beats a manual.

## 6. Support during the first 30 days

**One WhatsApp group per new dealer, for 30 days** — owner, munim, DD, CEO — named `DZZLO — [Pump name] — Setup`.

- **Why a group:** early questions carry context and the answers are worth the munim seeing. Multi-channel engagement also tracks with better Indian retention — 62% better across three or more channels ([CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/b2b-saas-customer-retention-playbook-india-2026)) — **VERIFY LIVE**, vendor-reported.
- **Every message still becomes a ticket.** Tickets, SLAs, triage and macros belong to the COO's [[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]] and are not duplicated here. Marketing runs the group; support runs the queue.
- **The group closes on day 30, deliberately**, naming the official support number. A group that never closes is a private support channel for one dealer, and does not survive ten of them.

## 7. The numbers, and the early warnings

Four numbers, weekly, on the marketing scorecard ([[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]]; the rows feed the company scorecard [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]). Full definitions: [[11-metrics-budget-and-the-marketing-scorecard|lesson 11]].

| Metric | Definition | Starting target (**VERIFY LIVE** after 10 tenants) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Activation rate** | Tenants meeting HC8 ÷ tenants installed, by 14-day cohort | ≥ 60% |
| **Customers linked per dealer** | Median linked firms at day 14 and day 30 | ≥ 1 at D14, ≥ 3 at D30 |
| **Rate-confirmation rate** | Confirmed in the window ÷ rates published, per dealer | ≥ 70% by D30 |
| **Invoices per active tenant per week** | Invoices raised ÷ active tenants | ≥ 10 by D30 |

Health signals are about drift, not collapse: watch the *velocity* of change against a tenant's own baseline, not a fixed threshold ([Accoil](https://www.accoil.com/blog/customer-health-score)). Outreach within 48 hours of a signal improves survival ([CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/b2b-saas-customer-retention-playbook-india-2026)).

| Early-warning signal | What it means | Response, within 48 hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No rate published 2 nights running | He is back on the phone | Call the **owner**, not the munim. Ask what broke |
| Zero customers linked by day 7 | He is embarrassed to ask, or forgot | Offer to make the call ourselves |
| Confirmation rate under 50% for a week | The order-placer changed, or push isn't arriving | Visit the customer's office; check permissions |
| Invoices flat while orders continue | Billing moved back to the bill book | Sit with the munim for one hour |
| The WhatsApp group silent for 5 days | The most reliable death signal | Show up at the pump unannounced |

## 8. The activation moment is the referral moment

Day 14 is the **highest-trust moment you will ever have with this dealer** — he has just seen his own invoices, his own ledger and his customer confirming a rate, and referral prompts work at the instant of felt value ([[06_REFERRAL_PROGRAM|Dealer Dost research]] §9). Hence the three agenda items: sign off activation, get two names of pump owners he knows, film a 60-second Hindi testimonial on the forecourt. Script, bounty and tracker: [[08-referrals-associations-and-partners-the-warm-engine|lesson 08]], [[M09-referral-program-one-pager|M09]]. Ask on day 14, or you are asking a stranger in month three.

## 9. At VSYST — applying this now

VSYST has a live product, tenants in production, no written onboarding motion. This week:

- **CEO (technical founder):** run the Mongo cohort query — per tenant, install date, first linked `dealer_custs` row, first `invs` row — and produce one number: activation rate for existing tenants. Put it on the scorecard even if it is ugly. Also pull customer-side install conversion, so the kill-switch in §4 has real data.
- **Domain director:** pick **one** live or pilot dealer and run the 14-day table from day 0, filling [[M08-onboarding-and-activation-checklist|M08]] as you go. Top three credit customers named on day 1, one installed by day 4.
- **Third director:** own two artefacts — the bilingual message set in M08, and the per-dealer WhatsApp group (create, name, close on day 30, every ticket to [[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]]).
- **All three, day 14:** the review at the pump — sign off activation, ask for two referral names, film the testimonial.

Budget impact is near zero — founder time, travel inside 60 km, one video, well inside the ₹30–50k/month envelope (HC7). Pricing questions raised during onboarding go to the [[08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy|subscription strategy]] and are answered **VERIFY LIVE, owner sign-off pending** — never improvised at the pump.
