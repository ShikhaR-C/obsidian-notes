# 01 — What Marketing and Sales Actually Are (and Who Should Do Them Here)

_Phase 1 · Understand · Week 0. After this lesson you can define marketing, sales, brand and growth without jargon, draw the DZZLO funnel from "never heard of you" to "brought you the next dealer", say what a CMO does and which parts VSYST needs now, defend the house answer on hiring a marketing person, and write the charter the rest of this course hangs on._

## Explain-it-like-I'm-5

A woman near the Urla industrial estate in Raipur runs a tiffin service from her kitchen. **Marketing** is everything she does before anyone talks to her: the painted board at the factory gate, the free sample dabba for the security guard, her number on every lid, and her habit of asking each new customer "aapko kisne bataya?" **Sales** is one thing: ten minutes with the plant's HR manager — "chalis dabba, ₹70 each, 12:45 tak pahunch jayega, pehla hafta free agar pasand na aaye" — where he says yes or no.

**Brand** is what the workers say when she is not in the room — "Sharma aunty ka dabba, kabhi late nahi" — and it is why she charges ₹70 while the man on the corner charges ₹60. **Growth** is the notebook where she wrote that of her last forty customers, thirty-one came through the guard and nine off the board — so this month she stops repainting the board and sends the guard two dabbas.

That is the whole discipline. This course is about doing it on purpose, in one district, with numbers.

## 1. Four words that get mixed up

The textbook definition is the American Marketing Association's — "the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value" ([AMA](https://www.ama.org/the-definition-of-marketing-what-is-marketing/), reapproved 2017). True, and useless on a Tuesday. Use these instead.

| Word          | Working definition, in one breath                                                                                     | At DZZLO today                                                                                    | Who owns it now                   |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Marketing** | Finding the people whose problem you solve, saying it in their words where they already are, making it easy to say yes | Named Raipur target list, the Hindi line about rate disputes, the WhatsApp opener, one demo video  | All three founders, Week 0        |
| **Sales**     | The one-to-one conversation that finishes it — one named person, one decision, one date                               | The pump visit: chai, ten-minute demo on your phone, "kal aapke top teen customers set kar dein?"  | Domain director leads; CEO demos  |
| **Brand**     | What people say when you are not in the room — and why you can charge more than the next guy                          | "DZZLO — jo bagal wale pump pe chalta hai; rate ka jhagda band"                                    | Nobody buys it; delivery earns it |
| **Growth**    | Measuring which effort produced customers, and doing more of that one                                                 | Monday scorecard: which channel produced this week's demos, at what cost                           | CEO, Monday, 20 minutes           |

The AMA calls a brand "a name, term, design, symbol, or any other feature that identifies one seller's goods or service as distinct from those of other sellers." A logo is a brand *marker*; the brand is the reputation behind it, and reputation is delivered, not designed. **DZZLO** is the product brand, **VSYST Technologies** the corporate one ([[06_Brand_Architecture|transporters 06]]).

Two traps, named before the techniques.

- **The pretty-things trap.** Treating marketing as logo, website, brochure — outputs, and the least important ones now. [[02_Marketing_Tutorial|The marketing tutorial]] §6 ranks "pretty website before distribution" pitfall number one; engineers fall into it hardest.
- **The pushy-thing trap.** Treating sales as manipulation, so something to avoid or outsource. Drucker: "the aim of marketing is to make selling superfluous… to know and understand the customer so well that the product or service fits him and sells itself" (*Management*, 1973 — [Process Excellence Network](https://www.processexcellencenetwork.com/innovation/articles/peter-drucker-on-sales-and-marketing)). You will never get all the way there with a 58-year-old pump owner, but every hour of good marketing shortens the conversation.

"Growth" needs its own line because the word is abused. Sean Ellis coined "growth hacking" in 2010 for someone "whose true north is growth", using data and experiments rather than advertising and PR ([GrowthHackers](https://growthhackers.com/growth-hacking/what-is-growth-hacking/)); its core artefact is one **North Star metric** ([Sean Ellis](https://medium.com/growthhackers/what-is-a-north-star-metric-b31a8512923f)) — ours is **activated dealers with at least one activated customer** (lesson [[11-metrics-budget-and-the-marketing-scorecard|11]]).

## 2. The funnel, in plain words

A **funnel** is the list of stages a stranger passes through on the way to becoming a customer, with a count at each stage. The theory — TOFU/MOFU/BOFU, AARRR, the flywheel critique — is in [[02_Marketing_Tutorial|the marketing tutorial]] §1.3–1.4. What matters here is our shape: **ours has two activation stages and nobody else's does**.

| Stage                         | Plain words                     | For a Raipur petrol-pump dealer                                                                              | We count                          | Lesson |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------ |
| **Awareness**                 | He has heard the name       | Saw it in the dealers' WhatsApp group; his OMC territory manager mentioned it; GE Road runs it | Pumps in the district who know us | 05, 09 |
| **Interest**                  | He has a reason to care     | You said "rate ka jhagda"; he named the transporter who refused to pay ₹40,000            | Visits that surfaced a real loss  | 06     |
| **Trial**                     | Using it, free, with help   | 14-day no-card trial (**VERIFY LIVE** — tier sheet pending sign-off); you set his rates up with him   | Trials started                    | 06, 07 |
| **Paying**                    | Money has moved             | He pays per GSTIN on the web, never in the app ([[08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy\|subscription strategy]]) | Paying dealers                    | 07, 11 |
| **Activated — dealer side**   | He uses it without you      | ≥1 customer linked and ≥1 invoice inside 14 days; rates set before 10 PM with no reminder             | Activated dealers                 | 07     |
| **Activated — customer side** | *His* customer uses it      | A transport fleet confirms the rate in the 10 PM–6 AM window and opens its ledger                     | Customers activated per dealer    | 02, 07 |
| **Referring**                 | He brings you the next one  | "Verma ji se baat karo, unka bhi yehi problem hai" — and sends the intro himself           | Referrals asked / received        | 08     |

**The bottom two rows are where Indian SME SaaS dies.** A dealer who pays and never links a customer is a refund waiting to happen: the rate window, order flow and shared ledger only work if the other side participates. "Sold" is not the milestone — "both sides live" is (lesson [[02-the-two-sided-problem-sell-to-the-dealer-seed-the-customer|02]]), so the scorecard counts activation twice. And **Interest is the stage founders skip**: the demo only lands after he has named what he lost money on (lesson [[06-founder-led-sales-the-pump-visit-demo-and-close|06]]; scripts in [[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling|M05]]).

## 3. What a CMO actually does all day — and what you need now

| A CMO's real job              | At a 30-person company                           | Needed at VSYST in the next 90 days?                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Owns a number**             | Sets and hits the quarterly new-customer target  | **Yes — founders own it.** On the scorecard this Monday ([[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator\|M13]]) |
| **Positioning and message**   | Decides what the company means; polices the words | **Yes, small.** One page in Week 1 ([[M04-positioning-and-message-house\|M04]]); theory is [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas\|C08]] |
| **Channel portfolio**         | Runs 3–6 channels, kills losers, moves budget    | **Partly.** Two channels, in the HC4 order (lesson [[05-channels-where-to-find-them-and-in-what-order\|05]]) |
| **A team and a budget**       | Hires and manages 4–15 people                    | **No.** No team; ₹30–50k/month total                                        |
| **Brand and creative**        | Identity, campaigns, agencies, sponsorships      | **Not yet.** Trigger in lesson [[12-scaling-hiring-and-launching-the-big-campaign\|12]]     |
| **Product marketing**         | Launches, pricing comms, competitive positioning | **Partly** — CEO owns it ([[C11-pricing-and-packaging-decision-sheet\|C11]]) |
| **Analytics and attribution** | Which rupee produced which customer              | **Yes — on a Google Sheet.** Eight numbers, twenty minutes a week                                           |
| **PR, events, comms**         | Press, conferences, awards                       | **No.** Exception: association meetings, a channel not PR (lesson [[08-referrals-associations-and-partners-the-warm-engine\|08]]) |

Strip that to the "yes" rows and the real job for ninety days is **one number, one message, two channels, one sheet** — six hours a week for two people. Not a full-time role, and certainly not one with "Chief" in the title.

## 4. The house answer: the founders do it (HC1)

*Should we hire someone for marketing first, or start ourselves?*

**House call: the three founders do marketing and sales themselves for the first 100 dealers. No CMO. No agency. Hire one field associate when three conditions hold — not before.** Four arguments, in order.

**1. The first hundred customers are R&D, not revenue.** Steve Blank's distinction says why: an existing company *executes* a known business model, a startup *searches* for one — and searching is a founder's job ([Search versus Execute](https://steveblank.com/2012/03/05/search-versus-execute/)). Paul Graham says it operationally: "the most common unscalable thing founders have to do at the start is to recruit users manually" — Airbnb went door to door in New York — and "the feedback you get from engaging directly with your earliest users will be the best you ever get" ([Do Things that Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)). Every objection at a pump gate this quarter is a product requirement, a pricing input and a line of copy. Hire that out and you have paid someone else to learn what you needed to know. [[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] §6 agrees: Days 15–45 is the R&D phase, founder at every pump.

**2. You cannot hand over a script you have not written.** [[09_PRODUCT_VS_SALES|Product vs Sales]] names the failure: you hire a rep "to get sales moving", the rep asks what the ICP is, what the script is, how to answer "abhi Tally se kaam chal raha hai" — you have no answers, and the rep churns in four months, burning your target list on the way out. SaaStr's rule: move out of founder-led selling around \$1M ARR, and for **vertical** SaaS not before roughly \$10M ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/what-are-the-best-ways-to-transition-from-the-founder-led-sales-stage/)). [[13_REVENUE_GENERATION|Revenue Generation]] §7 sets the Indian milestone: **₹0 → ₹20L ARR, founder-led only, no rep.**

**3. The arithmetic.** A CMO in India averages roughly ₹25–30 lakh a year, funded-startup bands ₹25–40 lakh ([Glassdoor India](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Salaries/india-chief-marketing-officer-salary-SRCH_KO0,27.htm)); Indeed India reports a far lower ₹61,957/month median off five samples ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/career/chief-marketing-officer/salaries)) — the label spans a Raipur agency owner and a listed-company executive. Take the *low* number and it is still ₹7.4 lakh a year against a marketing budget of ₹30–50k/month. **VERIFY LIVE** with two Raipur/Bhilai recruiters before this reaches a board note.

**4. Nobody in Indian SMB SaaS did it the other way.** Zoho was bootstrapped from a Chennai apartment to a billion dollars, no outside investors and no early brand spend ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/boardroom/the-bootstrapped-buddhist-how-sridhar-vembu-built-zoho/43383/1)). Vyapar was an engineer's side project written for his cousin's furniture unit; the founders quit their jobs only after organic downloads appeared ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/smbstory/msme-accounting-software-gst-bengaluru-vyapar)). Khatabook went from 40,000 merchants to 400,000+ in about eighteen months and, per Inc42, "unlike other platforms… which spend a lot in getting downloads and installs, Khatabook used the power of word-of-mouth marketing" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/sequoia-surge-backed-khatabook-is-helping-indian-shopkeepers-get-cashback/)). Pump owners trust pump owners. No CMO manufactures that; one satisfied dealer on GE Road does.

## 5. Why an engineer picks this up faster than a "marketing person" expects

Marketing at your stage is **measurement and iteration, not charisma** — the same test loop you run daily, with a slower feedback cycle and messier instrumentation. Hypothesis, one variable, measure, keep or kill.

The parts that *look* like charisma are not. A pitch is a script iterated thirty times ([[M05-pitch-scripts-and-objection-handling|M05]]); an objection response is a lookup table; a marketer's "instinct" is someone who has read four hundred customer sentences where you have read four.

What does not transfer: **tolerance for repetition** (the same eight-minute conversation, forty times), **tolerance for rejection** (Indian B2B cold conversion runs around 2% — [[12_OWNER_ACQUISITION|Owner Acquisition]] §5), and **writing in the customer's words, not the product's** — "real-time reconciliation" is a feature; "aapka ₹40,000 ka jhagda dobara nahi hoga" is a benefit. [[02_Marketing_Tutorial|The marketing tutorial]] §7 is a 25-hour path to "literate founder-marketer". Do exercise 1 first, then read.

## 6. The one page the rest of this course hangs on

> **Marketing is finding the people whose problem you solve, saying it in their words where they already are, and making it easy to say yes. Sales is the conversation that finishes it. For a two-sided product: sell to the dealer, seed the customer, and count both.**

And the rule that decides what you do next, in any week:

```
Nobody has bought it yet      -> go to the pump yourself, this week      (lesson 06)
One has bought it             -> get the proof and the referral           (lesson 08)
Ten have bought it            -> write the script down, measure the channel (lessons 10-11)
A hundred have bought it      -> hire, repeat the district, then go big   (lesson 12)
And in every case             -> both sides live, or it doesn't count     (lessons 02, 07)
```

Where this course meets its neighbours — theory there, field artefact here: ICP is [[C09-icp-and-customer-discovery-guide|C09]] → [[M02-icp-and-persona-field-cards|M02]]; positioning is [[C08-positioning-and-messaging-canvas|C08]] → [[M04-positioning-and-message-house|M04]]; founder-led sales is [[07-customers-markets-and-founder-led-sales|CEO lesson 07]] / [[C10-founder-led-sales-pipeline-and-script|C10]] → [[M07-sales-pipeline-tracker|M07]]; the company scorecard [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]] is fed by [[M13-marketing-scorecard-and-cac-calculator|M13]].

## 7. At VSYST — applying this now

No marketing person, and none for two quarters. The split for Weeks 0–12, so nobody waits for somebody:

- **CEO (technical, ships code)** — owns the **number** and the Monday scorecard, and runs the demo on every visit. Build time is scarce, so his marketing hours are fixed and small: 20 minutes Monday, two afternoons in the field, 30 minutes Friday.
- **Domain-expert director (fuel distribution)** — owns the **conversation**. He is the credibility in the room: he knows what a decant, a density reading and a shortage claim actually cost. He leads the pump visit, the association and OMC relationships, and every referral ask.
- **Third director** — owns the **list and follow-up**: the Raipur target list ([[M03-target-list-builder|M03]]), WhatsApp replies inside 24 hours, an honest pipeline sheet until ERPNext CRM is live.
- **Budget** — ₹30–50k/month, barely touched this month: fuel, chai, printing, one Hindi demo video. Founder hours are the constraint (HC7).
- **Assets you already have** — the live IOCL conversation via Amey ([[discussion-document|IOCL discussion document]]) and Easebuzz onboarding: trust signals in a pump owner's office, not press releases.
- **This week, do not** post a marketing job, brief an agency, buy a Google ad, or redesign the logo. All four are the pretty-things trap wearing a business case.
