# 05 — Legal and Governance Foundation

_Phase 2 · Foundation · Days 1–30. After this lesson you can assemble VSYST's company binder (every incorporation, tax, bank, register, certificate and contract the company must have, each with an owner and a location), run the board and ROC mechanics of a small private company without missing a date, close the gaps in the founders' agreement and IP ownership, stand up the contract library, and hand the CS a governance calendar v0 that becomes the full compliance calendar in lesson 13._

## 1. Why the COO owns the paper

A Private Limited company is **a legal person that exists only on paper** — a Certificate of Incorporation, a memorandum and articles, a set of registers, minutes of the meetings its board held, and a trail of forms filed with the Registrar of Companies (ROC). Nobody at the ROC checks whether the product ships; they check whether the paper exists and the dates were met. Miss enough of them and the consequences are concrete: **daily late fees with no cap, directors disqualified for five years, the company struck off** — with the founders' DINs, and their next company, caught in the blast.

At VSYST the CS (a company secretary in practice) and the CA file the forms. **The COO owns the mechanism around them** — the binder that says what exists and where, the calendar that says what is due when, and a named owner for every line. This is the first place the course's rule bites: _good intentions don't work; mechanisms do._ "The CA handles it" is an intention. A calendar row with a form number, a due date, an owner and a status is a mechanism.

Who does what, written down once:

## 3. Governance mechanics for a small company

Most of this is what your CS already knows. You need to know it well enough to schedule it, supply it and catch a miss.

**Small-company status.** The Companies Act gives "small companies" real relief — fewer board meetings, no cash-flow statement, the abridged annual return MGT-7A signable by a director alone, lower penalties. The thresholds were raised effective **1 December 2025** to paid-up capital ≤ ₹10 crore **and** turnover ≤ ₹100 crore ([Fox Mandal — MCA notifies revised small company thresholds](https://foxmandal.in/News/mca-notifies-revised-small-company-thresholds/)). VSYST is almost certainly inside them — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CS, because the reliefs below depend on it.

| Rule                    | What it means for you                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Source / VERIFY                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Board meetings**      | Default is at least four a year, gaps ≤120 days (s.173(1)); a small company needs **at least one meeting in each half of the calendar year, at least 90 days apart** (s.173(5)). Three habits keep the table honest:

- [ ] Board meetings go on the shared calendar for the whole year each April, with the CS invited; agenda and papers circulate seven days before.
- [ ] Every board decision that authorises spending, borrowing, a bank change, a director's related-party dealing or a share allotment is a **minuted resolution**, entered in the minutes book and copied to the binder.
- [ ] Directors sign their own DSC-signed forms. The CS prepares; the director reads the PDF and signs. DSC tokens and PINs are never left with the CS's office — a common, dangerous shortcut.

## 5. Founders' agreement, vesting and the IP chain of title

Three founder-directors who are family will be tempted to skip this. Don't. The [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]] calls the missing founders' agreement "the #1 early-startup killer" and co-founder conflict a bigger killer than competition; the agreement is what lets you disagree safely.

**Founders' agreement — what it must settle** (the lawyer drafts; you decide the answers first):

- [ ] Equity split and **vesting** (the ops plan's ~4 years, with a cliff) — so a founder who leaves early does not keep a full share.
- [ ] Roles and time commitment; what happens if a founder steps back or takes a job.
- [ ] Decision rights by area and spend limits — exactly the [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]] and [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]] work from lesson 03; the agreement should reference them, not duplicate them.
- [ ] Founder money: loan (DPT-3, interest, repayment) or capital? Decide with the CA (Finance Phase 6).
- [ ] IP assignment (below), confidentiality, non-solicit to the extent enforceable in India (the lawyer's call), dispute resolution and jurisdiction (Raipur).
- [ ] Exit, buy-back and valuation method if a founder leaves; death and incapacity clauses.

**IP chain of title.** The Copyright Act's default (s.17) is that an **employer owns work made under a contract of service — but a contractor owns what they create unless it is assigned in writing**. Founders are directors, not necessarily employees; a freelancer who wrote part of the app owns that part until a deed says otherwise. Startup checklists put "IP assignment from all founders and contractors before writing a single line of code" in week 0 ([Stripe — startup checklist for founding teams](https://stripe.com/resources/more/checklist-for-business-startups-what-founding-teams-need-to-do-first)); DZZLO's code already exists across three repositories, so your job is retroactive: list every person who has committed code, designed a screen or written copy, and get a signed **IP assignment deed** from each — founders included — with present-assignment language covering past and future work; **VERIFY LIVE** the wording with the lawyer. This is an investor's first diligence question, and partners now in discussion (IOCL, the bank/gateway) will eventually ask it too.

**ESOP, for later.** A private company can adopt an ESOP by ordinary resolution (Rule 12), files MGT-14 within 30 days, needs a minimum one-year vesting and keeps register SH-6 — and DPIIT-recognised startups may include promoter-directors for ten years from incorporation, which ordinary companies cannot ([EquityList — ESOPs under the Companies Act](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/esop-companies-act)). Nothing to do now except reserve the pool decision in the founders' agreement.

E-signatures are valid under the IT Act (electronic signatures under s.3A, legal recognition under s.5, Aadhaar eSign notified in the Second Schedule), with a short list of excluded documents — wills, powers of attorney, immovable-property conveyances, negotiable instruments other than cheques; stamp duty is separate, via e-stamp ([Leegality — the law around Aadhaar eSign](https://www.leegality.com/blog/law-around-aadhaar-esign)). **Trademark and brand.** File TM-A for the DZZLO name and logo in the classes your agent recommends for software and SaaS. Alongside: the domain registrar account, DNS, the Google Play and App Store developer accounts (`in.vsyst.dzzlooms` lives there), the GitHub organisation, social handles and the brand file — each owned by a **company** identity with two admins, never a personal Gmail (lesson 07 makes this a rule).

## 9. At VSYST — applying this now

- **The binder audit comes first, and it is an audit, not a build.** VSYST has existed since 2021 and has held DPIIT recognition since February 2022, so INC-20A, the first auditor, share certificates and several years of AOC-4/MGT-7A are either done or overdue. Run T02 in one sitting with the CS on a call: for each row, _have / missing / unknown_. Any ROC arrears go to the CS this month — CCFS-2026's window closes 31 August 2026 (**VERIFY LIVE**).
- **The office is inside the family petrol pump.** Registered-office proof, the S&E question and any rent or shared-services arrangement between the company and the family business are exactly the related-party facts MBP-1 and MBP-4 exist for — have the CS paper them properly rather than leave them informal.
- **Founder loans and salaries are almost certainly how money has entered.** Make the DPT-3 schedule and the director-loan documentation a binder row today (Finance Phase 6 and 7 cover the accounting side).
- **The IP question is live.** Three repositories, one React Native codebase, freelancers "as needed", and partner talks with IOCL and a bank/gateway: sign the founders' and contractors' IP assignment deeds before those conversations reach diligence.
- **Contracts before customers pay.** The subscription decision (per GSTIN, billed on the web) means the MSA/SaaS terms with DPDP clauses and a DPA must exist before the first invoice — put the lawyer's brief on this month's list.
- **Use what you already have.** DPIIT recognition halves the trademark fee; Udyam makes large buyers pay inside 45 days; the CG 2025–30 policy and 36Inc are a Raipur-specific advantage most startups here never claim.
