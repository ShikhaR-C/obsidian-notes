# 00 — Course Map and Master Timeline

_Phase 0 · Orientation · Read this before Week 0. After this page you know exactly what to do in week 1, week 2, month 3 and month 12 — which task, in which order, owned by whom, using which template, taught in which lesson, and the exit criterion that says you may move on._

This is the **map**. The lessons are the territory. Everything the course teaches is here as a dated row: the task, why it matters, who owns it, the template it produces, the lesson that teaches it, and the test that says it is done. Nothing on this page is new material — if a row raises a question, the lesson in its **Lesson** column answers it.

## 1. How to use this page

**Do the exercises in timeline order, not table-of-contents order.** The lessons are grouped by topic because that is how topics are learnt; the company is built in a different order, because paper and money must exist before people, and a rhythm must exist before anything worth measuring does. You will therefore read [[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|lesson 13]] long before month six — the first GST date does not wait for your phase plan — and you will not open [[18-automation-and-ai-in-operations|lesson 18]] until month eight, because there is nothing worth automating yet.

Four rules govern the sequence, and every table below obeys them:

1. **Legal and money rails before people.** You cannot hire a person you cannot pay, contract or insure. Days 1–14 are paper and rupees.
2. **Cadence before dashboards.** A meeting that reads five rough numbers every Monday beats a beautiful dashboard read by nobody. Install the heartbeat ([[08-the-operating-cadence|lesson 08]]) in month one; build the KPI tree ([[16-metrics-dashboards-and-scorecards|lesson 16]]) in month seven.
3. **SOPs before delegation.** Handing over a task nobody has written down is not delegation, it is abandonment. [[15-sops-and-playbooks|Lesson 15]] precedes [[17-delegation-decision-rights-and-org-design|lesson 17]]'s handovers.
4. **Delegation before automation.** Never automate a mess: eliminate → simplify → standardise → automate → delegate. A process a human cannot run reliably is a bug you are about to run at machine speed.

**How to read the columns.** _Why it matters_ is the failure the row prevents — read it when you are tempted to skip one. _Owner_ is the single accountable name; external professionals own their own filings, never your calendar. _Artefact or template_ names the toolkit file or [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx) tab the task produces — the whole toolkit is indexed at [[COO-Docs/toolkit/index|COO Toolkit]]. _Exit criterion_ is the test: if you cannot demonstrate it, the row is not done.

**On the day numbers.** Day 1 is your first day in the seat, in calendar days. Weekday-tied tasks — the Monday fill, the Friday review — land on the nearest one. Where a row waits on the CS, the CA, a broker or a bank, send it and book the follow-up the same day; their turnaround is not your delay to absorb silently.

**On the eighteen months.** That is the pace for a seat shared by three people wearing several hats at a bootstrapped, pre-revenue company. A full-time seat or a hire compresses the windows — but the _order_ does not change. Skipping ahead is how companies end up with a dashboard and no bank mandate.

## 6. Days 15–30 — tools, then the heartbeat

Two builds in one fortnight: the **tool map** (so the company stops debating tools and starts owning them) and the **cadence** (so problems surface on a schedule instead of by accident). Tools first only because the cadence needs a chat channel, a calendar and a sheet to live in.

The cadence sits at the end of this window because days 1–14 are consumed by paper and rupees — but nothing stops you starting the Monday slot in week one. **A meeting improves by being run, not by being planned**; the fourth one will be good even if the first is nearly empty.

## 11. The first 30 days, day by day

One action per day — **the single most important one**, drawn from §3–§6. Print this. Most days need 60–120 minutes of focused work; the days marked with an external party need a call booked, not a full day blocked. Where a task is tied to a weekday, it lands on the nearest one.

Week 0 happens before Day 1 and is not optional: lessons 01–04 read, [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]] signed, [[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan|T30]] written, [[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]] opened.

**Week 1 — the paper**

- **Day 1.** Open [[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]] and mark every row _have / missing / unknown_ with its location and owner. Do not fix anything yet.
- **Day 2.** Stand up the identity root: company-domain email, the `ops@` / `support@` / `billing@` aliases, two-factor on every director account.
- **Day 3.** The CS call (30 min). Turn every "unknown" into a "have" or a dated task. Ask specifically about ROC arrears.
- **Day 4.** Statutory registers: which exist, in what form, last entry date, who updates. Send the CS a first-entry task list.
- **Day 5.** Fix both board-meeting dates for the half-year and set the papers deadline seven days before each. Invites out with the CS attached.
- **Day 6.** The IP question, in writing, to the lawyer: does the company own the DZZLO codebase today, and if not, what closes the gap? Same call: founders' agreement and vesting.
- **Day 7.** Build **governance calendar v0** into [[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]] and send it to the CS for correction in writing. Week-1 test: zero "unknown" cells in the binder.

**Week 2 — the money**

- **Day 8.** Read [[06-money-rails-and-finance-operations|lesson 06]]. Get the current account onto maker-checker and list who currently holds what banking access.
- **Day 9.** Sit with the CEO and set the **DoA thresholds** into [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]]. Mirror them into the bank's limits the same day. Log it in [[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]].
- **Day 10.** Write the one-page expense, reimbursement and petty-cash policy. Name the claim format and the payment day.
- **Day 11.** Build the vendor payments calendar from three months of bank statements. Ask every vendor their Udyam/MSME status — most have never been asked.
- **Day 12.** Fix the invoicing and collections routine and add the two AR rows to [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]. They read zero today; that is the point.
- **Day 13.** Populate the **13-week cash forecast** in the `Runway & 13-Week Cash` tab. Find the lowest projected balance and say the date out loud.
- **Day 14.** Send the CA the monthly close checklist with two questions: what do you need from us and by which working day, and when do we get the numbers back?

**Week 3 — the tools**

- **Day 15.** Fill [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]] with the entire current stack. Compute the cost per person per month even if half the cost cells are blank.
- **Day 16.** Access audit and 2FA sweep with the CEO/CTO, system by system. Remove what is unjustified; seal the root and owner accounts.
- **Day 17.** Create the break-glass account and move every shared credential into the password manager. Nothing stays in chat.
- **Day 18.** Write three tool decision records into [[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]] — internal chat, payroll, e-signature. "Defer, with a trigger" is a legitimate decision.
- **Day 19.** **Test a restore.** Restore the database to a scratch cluster, run one query that proves the data is there, time it, then delete the cluster.
- **Day 20.** Publish the where-things-live note: folder structure, naming, what may never enter the vault, and what data may go into AI tools.
- **Day 21.** Copy every renewal date out of the register into a company-owned calendar with a 60-day alert on each.

**Week 4 — the heartbeat**

- **Day 22.** Read [[08-the-operating-cadence|lesson 08]] and put **every ritual in §12 into the calendar** as a recurring invite with its artefact linked. Book the quarterly half-day and the annual planning day now.
- **Day 23.** Write **scorecard v0** with the CEO — seven numbers you can genuinely collect weekly — into [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]. Agree who fills which row by Monday 10:00.
- **Day 24 (Monday).** **Run the first weekly ops meeting** from [[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]. Seed three real issues, each written as one line with a proposed solution. File the notes.
- **Day 25.** Hold the CEO–COO 1:1 and the domain-knowledge 1:1 for the first time, as running documents in [[T04-one-on-one-template|T04]]. The domain slot must produce one written thing.
- **Day 26.** Publish the channel map. Say the sentence that matters out loud: **WhatsApp is external only.**
- **Day 27.** Start the daily check-in thread. Answer the first "blocked" line within the hour, so the norm is set by example.
- **Day 28.** Enter the first three rows into the decision log with review dates — the cadence itself, the channel map, and one live decision from Monday.
- **Day 29 (Friday).** Write the first [[T28-weekly-business-review|T28]] one-pager and send it to the CEO. Numbers before narrative; no number that is not on the sheet.
- **Day 30.** Day-30 review with the CEO against [[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan|T30]]. Mark month one done or moved, and name month two's three priorities.

## 13. Phase exit criteria

A phase is finished when you can **demonstrate** each line, not when you feel ready. Tick these in your own copy; an untickable line is the next thing you do.

**Phase 1 — Understand the Seat. You may move on when…**

- [ ] You can explain the COO seat at VSYST, and your archetype, in one paragraph without notes.
- [ ] The CEO–COO operating agreement is **signed by both**, with a 90-day review date ([[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]]).
- [ ] You have a written, verbatim answer to "which operational decisions will you stop making?" — including "none yet", if that is the truth.
- [ ] Your charter and 30/60/90 plan carry real dates and the CEO's sign-off ([[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan|T30]]).
- [ ] The decision log exists, with the logging threshold written at the top ([[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]]).
- [ ] You have a "not my job" list with at least ten items on it.

**Phase 2 — Foundation. You may move on when…**

- [ ] The company binder has **zero unknown cells**; every missing item has an owner and a date ([[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]).
- [ ] A payment above the single-signature ceiling is genuinely blocked by the bank until the second approver acts ([[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]]).
- [ ] The 13-week cash forecast is populated and refreshed weekly, and you can name the date of the lowest projected balance.
- [ ] Every tool the company uses has a row, an owner and a renewal date in the register, and two-factor is on everywhere ([[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]).
- [ ] A restore has been **performed and timed** — not a backup verified.
- [ ] The weekly ops meeting has run **four times** with notes, to-dos and logged decisions each time ([[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]).
- [ ] Scorecard v0 has been filled on four consecutive Mondays ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]).
- [ ] The CS has confirmed the governance calendar **in writing** and the CA has agreed the close checklist ([[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]]).

**Phase 3 — Build the Machine. You may move on when…**

- [ ] Every dealer conversation arrives in **one queue**, with published hours and an SLA you are meeting ([[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]]).
- [ ] A dealer can go from signup to first invoice against a written SOP that someone other than its author has run.
- [ ] Every open deal sits in a stage whose exit criterion is met, and has a dated next step ([[11-revenue-operations-and-partnerships|lesson 11]]).
- [ ] One release has shipped end to end against the filled checklist, with a 48-hour watch ([[T26-release-checklist|T26]]).
- [ ] Severity levels and an on-call rota exist and have survived one real incident with a postmortem ([[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]]).
- [ ] The full-year compliance calendar has **zero unowned rows**, dormant rows carry their triggers, and the April re-confirmation is booked ([[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]]).
- [ ] The twelve risks are scored and the top three have funded mitigations with owners and dates ([[T15-risk-register|T15]]).
- [ ] Every task only one person can do is listed, each with a named second or a dated plan.
- [ ] The first hire has been onboarded on a checklist, and the offboarding mirror exists ([[T12-onboarding-checklist|T12]], [[T13-offboarding-checklist|T13]]).
- [ ] Cost per tenant and cost per employee per month are real numbers on the scorecard, not estimates.

**Phase 4 — Autopilot. You may move on — or rather, you are done — when…**

- [ ] The SOP index is complete, every row has an owner who agreed to it, and the live SOPs have review dates ([[T08-sop-template|T08]]).
- [ ] At least three SOPs have been **run by someone who did not write them**, from the document alone.
- [ ] The KPI tree has a North Star and input branches, every row has one owner and a reproducible query ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]).
- [ ] The written WBR has gone out eight Fridays running ([[T28-weekly-business-review|T28]]).
- [ ] Three founder tasks have run for a month without the founder touching them.
- [ ] Two automations have run for four weeks, each with an owner, a guardrail and a kill switch ([[18-automation-and-ai-in-operations|lesson 18]]).
- [ ] A quarter has been planned, scored honestly and closed, and six monthly updates have been sent ([[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review|T19]], [[T23-board-and-investor-update|T23]]).
- [ ] Someone other than you fills the scorecard, scribes the meeting and runs the close inputs.
- [ ] **The two-week holiday test has been run and passed** — and what broke has been turned into mechanisms.

## 14. The autopilot test — the course's definition of done

Everything on this page exists to make one thing true: **the founders can be away for two weeks and the company still ships, sells, supports and complies.** That is the definition of autopilot the course uses throughout, and [[20-the-autopilot-test-and-scaling-the-machine|lesson 20]] turns it into an actual test — designed in advance, with a written list of what must keep working and what may not be escalated, then run for real in months 12–18.

Treat it the way you would treat a restore drill rather than a holiday: the value is in what breaks. Whatever gets escalated to you while you are away is a missing SOP, a missing threshold or a missing owner — and each one becomes a row in the next quarter's plan. Passing it once is not the end of the job either; lesson 20's maturity model and annual audit are what keep it true as the company grows from three people to thirty.

## 15. When the timeline bends — compliance dates jump the queue

**This timeline is a default order, not a law.** It assumes a company where nothing is on fire, which will not describe your month two. Three things legitimately reorder it, and only three: a statutory due date, a live incident, and a cash trough visible in the 13-week forecast. Everything else waits its turn.

The first is the one that will actually bite. **Compliance dates do not wait for your phase plan.** Whatever the tables above say about [[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|lesson 13]] belonging to months 4–6, the monthly drumbeat starts the moment a trigger fires: TDS and GST run on fixed dates once you are registered and deducting; advance tax, the AGM, the annual ROC filings, DPT-3 and DIR-3 KYC arrive whether or not you have reached that lesson; DPDP and CERT-In's reporting rule apply to three people exactly as to three hundred. **Every one of those dates is VERIFY LIVE** — deadlines, forms and thresholds move by notification and every Union Budget — which is why lesson 13's central mechanism is not the table but the rule around it: **build the calendar with the CA and the CS, and re-confirm the whole thing with them every April.**

So the practical instruction is this. Build [[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]] in week one as governance calendar v0 — before you have read the lesson that completes it — and give the ops meeting a standing five-minute item: **"what is due in the next 45 days?"** When a filing date lands in the middle of your people-operations month, the filing wins, the row moves, and nothing about that is a failure of the plan. The plan's job is to make sure that when the interruption ends, you know exactly which row you were on.
