# 07 — Tools and IT Foundation

_Phase 2 · Foundation · Days 1–30. After this lesson you can name every tool VSYST runs on and who owns it, apply six rules before adopting a new one, run the company on one identity root with two-factor everywhere, say exactly where each kind of record lives (ERPNext, GitHub, the vault, the workbook), prove that a backup restores, and keep the whole stack on one register with renewal dates and a cost per person per month._

## 1. Why the COO owns the tool map

Three people, a dozen subscriptions, forty logins, half of them on somebody's personal Gmail — that is the default state of every young company, and it is invisible until the day it isn't: a founder is unreachable and the AWS root email is their personal inbox; a contractor leaves and still has the Play Console; a card expires and the OTP vendor stops sending; nobody remembers which Google account owns the Figma team. **Tools are cheap. Sprawl, personal ownership and missing export paths are what cost you** — in hours, in outages, and eventually in a due-diligence data room.

The COO owns three things in this lesson, and none of them requires buying anything this month:

1. **The map** — one table that says which tool does which job, who owns it, roughly what it costs, and how we would leave it (§3, kept live in [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]).
2. **Identity and access** — one root of identity, two-factor everywhere, least privilege, a break-glass account, and an offboarding list that actually revokes things (§4).
3. **Recoverability** — backups that have been restored at least once, on a schedule (§5).

Two terms you will use for the rest of the course. A **system of record** is the single place where one kind of fact is authoritative — if two systems disagree, the system of record wins. VSYST has four: **ERPNext for transactions** (money, customers, HR, support tickets), **GitHub for code**, **the Obsidian vault for knowledge** (SOPs, policies, decisions, meeting notes, this course) and **Google Sheets / the workbook for management numbers** until dashboards exist. WhatsApp is a channel, not a record. An **identity root** is the one account system every other login hangs off — for VSYST that is Google Workspace on the company domain, so that "sign in with Google" plus a group alias, not a person's mailbox, owns every vendor account.

## 2. Selection rules — and the decision record

Every tool decision at VSYST passes six tests. Write them on the first page of the register; the point is not to buy less, it is to **stop debating the same choice twice**.

| Rule                                               | What it means                                                                                                                            | The test                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **One tool per job**                               | Exactly one system of record per kind of fact; no parallel spreadsheet next to ERPNext, no second chat app                               | "Which existing tool already does 80% of this?"                                                                                       |
| **Cheapest that works**                            | Free tier first; monthly before annual; annual only after a quarter of real use                                                          | "What breaks on the free tier, and does it matter this year?"                                                                         |
| **Exportable data**                                | You can get everything out in an open format without asking the vendor                                                                   | Test the export before you depend on the tool (§3, last column)                                                                       |
| **Company-owned admin — never a personal account** | The owner/admin login is a company Workspace account or a group alias; billing is the company card or account                            | "If this person left tomorrow, does the company still control the account?"                                                           |
| **SSO/2FA**                                        | Sign in with the Workspace account where the tool allows it; two-factor mandatory everywhere                                             | "Can 2FA be enforced for all users, not just recommended?"                                                                            |
| **GST invoice for input tax credit**               | Prefer INR billing from an Indian entity or reseller so the CA can claim ITC; USD-billed tools go on the register with a note for the CA | "Does the invoice carry our GSTIN?" — ask the CA how USD-billed tools without a GST invoice are treated — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CA |

Google Workspace itself is the worked example: the research brief's price notes recommend buying **via an Indian reseller for an INR GST invoice** rather than a USD card charge ([Cloudfy — Google Workspace pricing India, 2026](https://www.cloudfysystems.com/blog/google-workspace-pricing-plans-india)).

**The decision record.** Almost every tool choice is a two-way door — reversible, so decide fast, in writing, and move on ([Amazon's Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions](https://growthmethod.com/two-way-doors/)). The record lives in the decision log ([[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]]) and takes ten minutes:

```
DEC-YYYY-MM-DD-NN · <tool> for <job>
Type: two-way door (reversible) / one-way door (data migration or contract lock-in)
Options considered (at least two): …
Criteria: one job · cheapest that works · export tested · company-owned admin · SSO/2FA · GST invoice
Chosen because: …            Cost/mo (VERIFY LIVE): …
Export path tested on: …     Owner: …            Review date: …
Decided by: …                Logged in: T09 + T14 row updated
```

## 4. Identity and access

The identity root does most of the security work for a small team, and it is nearly free. The IT baseline the compliance guides describe for a new company is short: SSO through an identity provider such as Google Workspace, MFA everywhere, least privilege, encrypted devices, automated off-site backups, and immediate access revocation on offboarding ([PolicyOwn — Startup compliance checklist 2026](https://policyown.com/blog/startup-compliance-checklist-2026)); the security guides for lean teams call the 0–10-employee stage "survival mode" — a password manager, MFA on every account, full-disk encryption, and one person owning security at 10–15% of their time ([Sola Security — Security for startups and lean teams, 2026](https://sola.security/blog/startup-lean-teams-security-guide/)). At VSYST the CEO/CTO owns the technical controls; you own the process ones. Set it up in this order:

1. **Workspace on the company domain.** One account per person; nobody works from a personal Gmail. Two super-admins (COO, CEO), never used for daily mail; a **break-glass account** — a third super-admin that nobody uses, whose password and 2FA backup codes are sealed in Bitwarden with emergency access set for two directors, and on paper in the company binder ([[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]).
2. **Group aliases as owners.** Create `support@`, `billing@`, `ops@` (and `admin@`, `security@` if you like) as Google Groups. Every vendor account — AWS root, the Atlas organisation, the GitHub organisation, Play Console, App Store Connect, OneSignal, 2Factor.in, ERPNext administrator, the payment gateway — is registered to an alias, not a person, so ownership survives people. Invoices go to `billing@` and are filed for the CA.
3. **Bitwarden organisation, shared collections.** Collections by function (Infra, Stores, Finance & Bank, Comms vendors, Support), each shared with the roles that need it; personal vaults for personal logins. **No password in WhatsApp, in a Sheet, in a README, or in a `.env` committed to a repo.** Master passwords are long passphrases; the Bitwarden account itself has 2FA.
4. **2FA mandatory, enforced.** Authenticator app or hardware key; SMS only as a fallback. Enforce it in Workspace, GitHub and every vendor that allows enforcement. Backup codes live in Bitwarden.
5. **Device policy — five lines, signed at onboarding** ([[T12-onboarding-checklist|T12]]): full-disk encryption on (FileVault/BitLocker); screen lock ≤5 minutes; OS and browser auto-updates on; find-my-device on; company data only in company accounts. Test phones and the phone that holds the WhatsApp Business number are company-owned, on a company SIM, listed in the register.
6. **Least privilege per system.** No daily work as root or owner. AWS: root email is an alias with hardware 2FA, IAM users/roles for people and deploys, a billing alert. Atlas: organisation owner is the break-glass identity; project roles for developers; IP access list; separate database users per environment. GitHub: an organisation owned by the company, 2FA required, branch protection on `main`, deploy keys and Actions secrets instead of personal tokens, personal access tokens with expiry. Stores: the company Workspace account holds the Play Console and App Store Connect owner roles; developers get the developer/release-manager role only. ERPNext: roles per module — Sales User, Accounts User, Support Agent — never everyone as Administrator. Bank: maker-checker (lesson 06).
7. **Quarterly access review.** Once a quarter (calendar entry, lesson 08) walk the access matrix below, remove anything unused, rotate anything shared, confirm 2FA is still on everywhere. Quarterly access reviews are the first "foundation-building" control the lean-team guides add after survival mode ([Sola Security, 2026](https://sola.security/blog/startup-lean-teams-security-guide/)).
8. **Offboarding revocation list.** Same matrix, run in reverse within 24 hours of an exit; rotate every secret the person could have seen ([[T13-offboarding-checklist|T13]]).

## 5. Backups and recovery

A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup. Define two numbers per system with the CEO/CTO — **RPO** (recovery point objective: how much data you can afford to lose, e.g. "up to 24 hours") and **RTO** (recovery time objective: how long you can be down, e.g. "4 hours for the API") — then make the mechanism match.

## 7. Where things live — folders and naming

The four systems of record from §1 answer "which tool"; this section answers "which folder", so a new person can find anything in under a minute.

**Google Drive** — shared drives owned by the company (never a founder's My Drive), one per area: `00 Company binder` (scans of everything in [[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]), `01 Finance` (invoices by FY/month, bank statements, CA correspondence), `02 Legal & contracts` (signed PDFs, one folder per counterparty), `03 People` (offers, contracts, IDs — restricted), `04 Vendors & tools` (contracts, invoices, exports), `05 Customers & partners` (proposals, MoUs), `06 Marketing`.

**The vault** — the company tree today is `vsyst-technologies/{docs/tasks, docs/learning, correspondence, sakuradocs}`; a PARA sub-tree (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) already exists inside `docs/learning`. Keep the split honest: **`docs/tasks` = Projects** (engineering plans with an end), **`docs/learning` = Resources** (courses, research), **`correspondence` = live partner threads**, **`sakuradocs` = the operating docs**. This course is `sakuradocs/COO-Docs/` and stays a set of blank masters; filled copies of registers, logs and notes go in a sibling `coo-workbook/` folder as the [[COO-Docs/index|landing page]] says; live SOPs and policies get their own area folder, whose final tree lesson 15 fixes ([[15-sops-and-playbooks|SOPs and playbooks]]). One rule overrides all of this: **the Quartz site is public on GitHub Pages.** No credentials, salaries, customer personal data, contracts or bank details ever go into a published folder — those live in Drive, Bitwarden or ERPNext. The Quartz config ignores folders named `private` and `templates`; confirm in `quartz.config.ts` before you rely on it, and keep anything sensitive out of the repo altogether when in doubt.

**Naming** — kebab-case file stems, ISO dates (`2026-08-19`), one topic per file, the H1 as the first line, and typed identifiers you can grep: `SOP-OPS-003-weekly-scorecard-update`, `POL-004-it-acceptable-use`, `ADR-0012-otp-provider-abstraction`, `DEC-2026-08-19-01`. In Drive: `YYYY-MM-DD <counterparty> <document> vN.pdf`. Six months from now, the search box is your filing system; the names are what make it work.

## 8. The register — and the cost per person per month

The vendor and tool register ([[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]; the `Vendor & Tool Register` tab in [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx)) is the single list of every subscription, contract and renewal. Rules: a row is created **the day** a tool is adopted; renewal date and auto-renew flag are mandatory; a calendar alert fires **60 days before** every renewal (lesson 14); every invoice lands at `billing@` and is filed for the CA with the GSTIN check from §2; the **quarterly tool audit** kills unused seats — the [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]]'s "review tool subscriptions quarterly" habit, now with a checklist. The number the register produces every month is **cost per person per month = total monthly tool and infrastructure spend ÷ people using it (contractors included)**. Watch its trend on the scorecard: it should fall as free tiers are used well and rise only when a paid tool buys back hours you can name.

## 9. AI tools — what data may go where

Every person at VSYST already uses AI tools daily; the policy exists so that speed does not leak data. Classify first, then match tools to classes:

| Data class                   | Examples                                                                                          | Allowed in AI tools?                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public**                   | Published docs, marketing copy, open-source code                                                  | Any tool                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Internal**                 | Draft SOPs, meeting notes, non-sensitive planning, our repo code (secrets excluded)               | Company-paid seats only (Claude Code / Cursor / Team plans); never a personal consumer account                                                                         |
| **Confidential**             | Financials, contracts, pricing decisions, partner conversations (IOCL, bank/gateway)              | Only in tools whose terms exclude training on your data and allow deletion — read the vendor's data-use terms, **VERIFY LIVE**; redact names and numbers where you can |
| **Restricted personal data** | Dealer and customer personal data, phone numbers, ledgers, employee IDs, credentials and API keys | **Never** — unless there is a written processor agreement and DPDP clauses in place (§6), and then only the minimum                                                    |

Four operating rules go with it: company accounts only; **human-in-the-loop for anything that moves money, signs, or is sent to a customer** — IMD's advice to COOs is precisely to champion human oversight because generative AI's non-determinism "can feel alien in an environment founded on repeatability" ([IMD — AI and the COO, 2026](https://www.imd.org/ibyimd/artificial-intelligence/ai-and-the-coo-operational-excellence-in-the-ai-era/)); a one-line log of which agents run with which access (lesson 18 builds the automation register); and a kill switch — one person can revoke every agent's credentials in five minutes. Governance is where most companies are behind: 77% report an AI-governance adoption gap ([Larridin — the COO's emerging role in AI, 2026](https://larridin.com/blog/coo-ai-strategy)). Being three people is the easiest moment to not have one.
