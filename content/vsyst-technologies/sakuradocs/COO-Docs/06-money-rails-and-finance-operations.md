# 06 — Money Rails and Finance Operations

_Phase 2 · Foundation · Days 1–30. After this lesson you can run the COO's side of VSYST's money: a current account with maker-checker, spend approvals against a written DoA, an expense and petty-cash policy, a vendor payments calendar that respects the MSME 45-day rule, a GST-correct invoicing and collections routine with weekly AR ageing and a dunning ladder, the TDS and advance-tax hooks that keep the CA calm, the 13-week cash forecast owned jointly with the founder-CFO, and a monthly close where your inputs arrive on time — all recorded in ERPNext, reviewed monthly against budget._

## 1. The COO's side of money — and the line to the finance course

Two disciplines share the company's money, and this course refuses to duplicate the other one. **[[finance/00_README|Finance for Founders]] is the accounting depth** — the books, the three statements, the filings, the model; the founder-CFO discipline. **This lesson is the rails and routines** — the operational machinery that decides _when cash actually moves_: who may pay, who chases, what an invoice must say, which day the vendor run happens, what the CA receives by the 5th. The finance course's survival law is _profit is an opinion; cash is a fact_ — and the COO owns most of the operational causes of that fact: **collections, cost control and vendor terms.**

## 2. Bank rails — the current account and maker-checker

**The account.** A Pvt Ltd transacts through a current account in the company's name — personal and company money never mix (the entity boundary the finance course opens with). RBI's current-account framework (2020, relaxed October 2021) restricts where _borrowers_ may hold current accounts; a company with banking exposure under ₹5 crore may open one anywhere by giving an undertaking to inform the bank if exposure reaches ₹5 crore, and non-borrowers — VSYST today — are unrestricted ([IndiaFilings — RBI guidelines on current accounts](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/rbi-guidelines-on-opening-of-current-account-2021); [Kotak — RBI current-account rules](https://www.kotak.bank.in/en/stories-in-focus/accounts-deposits/current-account/rbi-circular-guidelines-on-current-account-opening-current-account-rules.html)). Choose the bank on service, net-banking quality and gateway relationships, not nostalgia — and note the Union Bank conversation in flight is a relationship input, "in discussion" only.

**The KYC pack** is the binder paying its first dividend ([[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]): Certificate of Incorporation, MOA/AOA, PAN, a **board resolution** naming the account and its signatories (minuted — lesson 05 §3), director KYC and beneficial-owner details, and registered-office proof. The office sits inside the family petrol pump, so bring the papered arrangement from lesson 05 §9, not an informal explanation.

**Maker-checker from day one.** On corporate net banking, one login _creates_ a payment and a different login _approves_ it. Set it up even with three directors: the COO (or later, an ops associate) is maker; the CEO — or the COO themselves above their own single-signature line — is checker, per the thresholds in §3. This single control kills the two most common small-company disasters: the fat-fingered transfer and the compromised credential that empties the account. Credentials live in the password manager with 2FA (lesson 07); **no shared logins, ever**; keep card/UPI limits on the account low and raise them per transaction when genuinely needed.

**Account structure.** Run two accounts from the start, per [[finance/07-phase-7-bootstrapping-runway-burn|Finance Phase 7]]: **operating** and **tax reserve** — sweep ~20–25% of any revenue with GST/tax implications into the reserve the day it arrives, so government money is never accidentally spent. Signatories are reviewed at every director change (a binder row), and the mandate list at the bank must always match the latest board resolution.

## 3. Who may spend what — approvals and the DoA

Lesson 03 gave decisions four levels; money is where the levels get rupee values. The **delegation-of-authority (DoA) matrix** lists decision type × threshold × who decides × who is informed, and the classic template pairs each spend band with an approver and a second approver ([Tallyfy — DoA matrix](https://tallyfy.com/delegation-of-authority-matrix-template/)); keep the single-signature ceiling low while the company is bootstrapped. A first pass for VSYST — **illustrative; set the real lines with the CEO in exercise 13.1** and keep them in [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]] and the `DoA Matrix` tab of [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx):

| Decision                                | Threshold (illustrative)                  | Decides                                    | Informed / second signature                  |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Routine spend within budget             | up to ₹10,000                             | Function owner                             | COO, in the monthly report                   |
| Spend within budget                     | ₹10,000–₹50,000                           | COO alone                                  | CEO, in the weekly meeting                   |
| Spend above band, or unbudgeted         | ₹50,000–₹2,00,000                         | CEO + COO jointly                          | Decision log ([[T09-decision-log-and-adr     | T09]]) |
| Above the two-signature line            | > ₹2,00,000, or any multi-year commitment | Board (all three directors), minuted       | CS files the resolution if required          |
| New recurring subscription (any amount) | —                                         | COO, with a [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register | T14]] register entry first                   | CEO    |
| Refunds / credit notes                  | up to ₹10,000 COO; above, joint           | —                                          | Logged with reason                           |
| Contracts binding the company           | Any                                       | Per lesson 03 §5.2 levels                  | Always through the checklist in lesson 05 §6 |

Three rules make the matrix real. **The bank enforces it** — §2's maker-checker limits mirror these lines, so the control is technical, not honour-based. **Emergencies are logged, not exempted** — an urgent payment outside the matrix gets made, then logged in T09 within 24 hours with the reason. **Review quarterly** — thresholds that were right at three people strangle a team of eight; the review date sits in the matrix itself.

## 4. Expenses, reimbursements and petty cash

**The expense policy** fits on one page, and its purpose is to make spending boring: what is claimable (travel at defined rates, meals with a cap, software per the DoA, nothing personal), what every claim needs (a GST invoice **in the company's name with VSYST's GSTIN** wherever the vendor can issue one — this feeds §7's input credit), the submission window (within 7 days of spend), the approver (per §3), and the payout promise (next weekly payment run, §5). Publish it before the first contractor asks, not after the first argument.

**The flow**, as a mechanism: spend → photograph the bill the same day → expense claim (a sheet now; ERPNext Expense Claims via Frappe HR once people ops runs on it — lesson 09) → approve per DoA → pay in the weekly run → the CA books it monthly. Two habits keep it clean: claims without bills are returned, not debated, and reimbursements are never paid in cash.

**Petty cash** exists because Raipur reality still occasionally wants cash — courier, chai for a dealer visit, a municipal fee. Run it as an **imprest float**: a fixed amount (₹5,000–₹10,000, illustrative) with one named custodian, a numbered voucher for every spend with a bill stapled to it, a monthly count that must equal float minus vouchers, and a top-up that restores the float against the vouchers. Two prohibitions: **no revenue ever enters petty cash** (all collections go to the bank), and anything above a small per-spend cap (say ₹2,000, illustrative) goes through the bank instead. The monthly count lands in the close checklist (§9).

## 5. Paying out — the vendor payments calendar

The principle is the finance course's: **collect fast, pay slow — ethically.** "Pay slow" means _pay on terms, not on receipt_: if a vendor grants 30 days, paying on day 28 is free working capital; paying on day 2 is a donation; paying on day 40 is a broken promise that costs you the relationship and, eventually, the terms. The mechanism that enforces this is a **single weekly payment run**: one fixed day (say Thursday), when every approved bill that is due within the coming week gets paid in one maker-checker batch. No ad-hoc payments between runs except logged emergencies (§3). One run per week means one approval sitting, one bank session, one chance for errors — instead of twenty.

**The one hard exception: MSME vendors.** If a supplier is a Udyam-registered micro or small enterprise, the MSMED Act's 45-day payment protection applies to you _as the buyer_ — and the Income-tax Act's s.43B(h) makes the stick real: amounts owed to such suppliers beyond the statutory time limit are **deductible only in the year you actually pay them**, a straight hit to taxable profit ([TaxGuru — MSME thresholds and the 45-day rule](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/msme-threshold-limit-effective-1st-april-2025.html)) — **VERIFY LIVE** the current mechanics with the CA under the new Income-tax Act 2025. Dues to micro/small suppliers outstanding beyond 45 days also trigger the half-yearly **MSME-1** return (lesson 05 §4). So the vendor register ([[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]) carries a **Udyam status column**, filled at onboarding, and MSME vendors are flagged in the ERPNext supplier master to be paid inside 45 days without exception — inside 30 if you want margin for error.

**Vendor master hygiene**, because payment fraud arrives by email: bank details are captured once, from a cancelled cheque or penny-drop verification, and any change request is confirmed by a phone call to the number _already on file_ — never the number in the email asking for the change. GSTIN captured and checked at onboarding (§7 depends on it); the contract or PO filed against the vendor row; renewal dates on the register with 60-day-before alerts (lesson 14).

**The calendar itself** — the artefact of exercise 13.2 — is one table of every recurring outflow with its day: the weekly run (Thursday), salaries and any statutory payroll dues (month-end / by the 7th), TDS deposit (by the 7th, §7), GST payment with the return (§7), rent if any, and each subscription's billing date from T14. Put the fixed dates in the `Cadence Calendar` tab of [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx) and the amounts in the 13-week forecast (§8) — the calendar tells you _when_, the forecast tells you _whether_.

## 6. Getting paid — invoicing and collections

The [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]]'s warning is the design brief: invoices due in 15 days routinely arrive in 45–60. Collections is therefore a _system_ — a correct invoice, sent immediately, chased on a ladder, measured weekly — not a hopeful glance at the bank app.

**A correct GST invoice.** Rule 46 of the CGST Rules fixes the mandatory fields; get the template right once in ERPNext and never think about it again: supplier name, address and GSTIN; a **unique consecutive serial number** (≤16 characters, one series per financial year); date; recipient's name, address and GSTIN; **place of supply with the State name** for inter-state supplies; the SAC code; description; taxable value; rate and amount of CGST/SGST or IGST; whether tax is payable on reverse charge; and signature or digital signature. A services invoice must be issued **within 30 days of supply**. Also confirm the HSN/SAC digit requirement for your turnover with the CA (4-digit for smaller AATO, 6-digit above ₹5 crore) — **VERIFY LIVE**, the rule moves.

**Classification and tax.** Software/SaaS services sit in the 9983 family — **998314** (IT design and development) with 998313/998315 nearby — or under **997331** (licensing the right to use software) for pure subscription licences; all at 18%, and practice genuinely varies, so **VERIFY** the SAC with the CA before the first subscription invoice ([IndiaFilings — SAC codes for software services](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/sac-code-for-website-software-development); [Busy — software HSN/SAC](https://busy.in/hsn/software-hsn-code/)). GST 2.0 rationalised the slabs from September 2025 and left IT/software/SaaS at 18% ([PIB — GST Council FAQs](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163560)). **Place of supply** for B2B services is the registered recipient's location (s.12(2), IGST Act): Raipur → a Mumbai dealer = **IGST 18%**; Raipur → a Chhattisgarh dealer = **CGST 9% + SGST 9%** ([gstinvoices.in — IT and software services](https://gstinvoices.in/sac-codes/it-software-services)). E-invoicing (IRP push) binds only above **₹5 crore** AATO — not yet, but the threshold has been proposed lower more than once, so re-check each April ([gimbooks — the ₹5 crore e-invoice rule](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/5-crore-e-invoice-turnover-rule-2026/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**.

**The collections mechanism**, in three parts:

1. **Invoice on the event, not at month-end.** Subscription start, milestone, delivery — the invoice goes the same day. Every day between delivery and invoice is runway donated.
2. **AR ageing, weekly.** One report — who owes what, bucketed 0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+ days — read every Monday in the ops meeting, with a scorecard row ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]; exercise 13.3). The number to watch is the **61+ total and its trend**.
3. **The dunning ladder** — a fixed, polite, relentless escalation, run from ERPNext reminders plus WhatsApp (illustrative; tune per customer class): due−3 days, a friendly reminder with the invoice attached · due date, statement · due+3, WhatsApp nudge from the account owner · due+7, phone call · due+14, a call from a director plus a formal statement of account · due+21, written notice that service pauses per the MSA · due+30, suspension per contract. Every step logged on the invoice. For DZZLO the last rung is credible by design — the subscription decision gates the app server-side, so suspension is a switch, not a lawsuit.

**The number: DSO** (days sales outstanding) — receivables ÷ revenue × days in period; the average time a rupee spends between invoice and bank. Track it monthly from the first paying tenant and drive it down with the two levers the finance course names: **annual prepayment** (a discount for paying the year upfront turns customers into your cheapest financiers — [[finance/07-phase-7-bootstrapping-runway-burn|Finance Phase 7]] §5) and the ladder above, applied without embarrassment. You are not being rude; you are being a company.

## 7. The tax hooks — GST, TDS and advance tax in operations

The CA files; **the COO makes every transaction filing-ready on the day it happens.** That is the entire section in one sentence. The full calendar lives in lesson 13 and [[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]]; here are the hooks that bite weekly, **every date and rate VERIFY LIVE with the CA each April** — the Income-tax Act 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026) renumbered half of what older guides cite.

**GST, output side** is §6: registration status is a decision memo with the CA ([[finance/05-phase-5-indian-accounting-system|Finance Phase 5]]; services threshold ₹20 lakh, but B2B buyers expect a GST invoice for their input credit, so voluntary registration is the SaaS norm — [Razorpay — GST registration limits](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-registration-limits/)). Once registered, the return rhythm: monthly filers do GSTR-1 by the **11th** and GSTR-3B by the **20th**; under the QRMP scheme (AATO ≤ ₹5 crore) GSTR-1 is quarterly by the 13th, tax for the first two months of each quarter goes by PMT-06 by the 25th, and the quarterly GSTR-3B falls on the **22nd — Chhattisgarh is a Category X state** ([ClearTax — GST calendar](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-calendar); [myHQ — GSTR-3B guide](https://myhq.in/blog/virtual-office/gstr-3b-filing-guide/)). Two portal facts worth knowing so you never "fix it later": since the July 2025 tax period the auto-populated GSTR-3B liability is **non-editable** (corrections flow through GSTR-1A) ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gstr-3b-tax-liability-to-be-non-editable)), and returns **cannot be filed more than 3 years** past their due date at all ([TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/43326/gstn-advisory-on-bar-on-filing-gst-returns-after-3-years-from-due-date/)).

**GST, input side — the hook most startups fumble.** Input tax credit on AWS, tools, the CA's own fees and everything else exists only if the vendor's invoice carries **VSYST's name and GSTIN** and the vendor actually reports it. So: company GSTIN on every vendor account (buy Workspace and the like through an Indian reseller for an INR GST invoice — lesson 07), a vendor-bill folder per month, and the §5 onboarding rule that no vendor gets paid before their GSTIN and invoice format are on file. Sloppy vendor invoices are a permanent 18% surcharge on everything you buy.

**TDS — you are the deductor.** When VSYST pays contractors, professionals, rent or commission, income tax is deducted at source and deposited against the payee's PAN. The rates you will actually meet (FY 2026-27, now mapped to s.393 of the ITA 2025 — [TDSMAN — TDS/TCS rate chart FY 2026-27](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/03/tds-tcs-rate-chart-fy-2026-27/)):

| Payment                                      | Old section | Threshold                            | Rate                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Professional fees (designer, consultant, CA) | 194J        | ₹50,000/yr                           | 10% (2% for technical services)            |
| Contractor / job work                        | 194C        | ₹30,000 single / ₹1,00,000 aggregate | 1% (individual/HUF) / 2% (others)          |
| Rent                                         | 194I        | ₹50,000/month                        | 2% plant & machinery / 10% land & building |
| Commission                                   | 194H        | ₹20,000/yr                           | 2%                                         |
| Any payee without PAN                        | —           | —                                    | 20% (or the rate, whichever is higher)     |

The operational hooks: every contractor is onboarded with **PAN + GST status before the first payment** (T14 and the contractor agreement, lesson 05 §6); the PO/agreement notes the TDS section; deduction happens at payment in ERPNext; the deposit goes by the **7th of the following month** (30 April for March) — a standing date on the §5 calendar; and quarterly returns fall due **31 Jul / 31 Oct / 31 Jan / 31 May**. From Q1 FY 2026-27 the return forms are renumbered — 24Q → **Form 138**, 26Q → **Form 140**, and the certificates Form 16 → **130**, 16A → **131** — with late filing at ₹200/day capped at the TDS amount ([TaxUpdate India — new TDS/TCS forms FY 2026-27](https://taxupdate.in/income-tax/811/new-tds-tcs-return-forms-fy-2026-27-form-138-140-143-144-due-31-july-2026/)). The COO's job is not to compute any of this — it is to make sure the CA _can_: payee master data complete, payments through the bank, the 7th never missed.

**Advance tax**, once there is taxable profit, falls due **15 June (15%), 15 September (45%), 15 December (75%), 15 March (100%)** cumulatively ([ClearTax — advance tax](https://cleartax.in/s/advance-tax)). The CA computes the instalment; the COO calendars the date and keeps the tax-reserve account (§2) funded so the payment is a transfer, not a scramble.

## 8. The 13-week cash forecast — owned jointly

The single runway number (cash ÷ burn) answers "how long do we live on average?" The **13-week cash forecast** answers the question that actually kills companies: "**in which week do we go below zero?**" One quarter, week by week: expected cash in (collections by _when they will actually arrive_, not invoice date; prepayments), expected cash out (the §5 calendar: salaries, the weekly vendor run, TDS on the 7th, GST, the annual bills that land like meteors), net movement, opening and closing balance per week. The standard format is exactly this — a rolling 13-week grid of inflows, outflows and closing balance ([Graphite — why you need a 13-week cash flow forecast](https://www.graphitefinancial.com/blog/why-you-need-13-week-cash-flow-forecast); [Abacum — the 13-week cash flow](https://www.abacum.ai/blog/13-week-cash-flow)). What you are looking for is the **trough** — the lowest weekly closing balance — because "fine on average, broke in week 6 when the AWS annual bill and salaries collide" is precisely the failure a monthly view hides ([[finance/07-phase-7-bootstrapping-runway-burn|Finance Phase 7]] §6 built this habit; this lesson gives it an owner and a slot).

**Jointly owned means split, not shared.** The founder-CFO seat owns the truth of the balances and the accounting treatment; the COO owns the _drivers_ — when each receivable will really land (§6's ladder tells you), what the vendor run will take (§5's calendar), which renewals hit this quarter (T14). The mechanics: the sheet is the `Runway & 13-Week Cash` tab of [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx) ([[T20-budget-vs-actual-and-cash-forecast|T20]] holds the rules); the COO updates it **every Monday before the ops meeting**; week 1 actuals replace last week's guess, and any weekly line that missed by more than ~10% gets a one-line "why" — that is how the forecast learns. The **red line** from Finance Phase 7 (e.g. "below five months' runway, finding cash becomes the only job") is written at the top of the tab, and crossing the projected red line anywhere in the 13 weeks is an automatic agenda item, not a feeling.

## 9. The monthly close and budget vs actual

**The close** is the monthly ritual that locks the books for the month — every transaction recorded, every account reconciled, statements produced. The CA runs it; it runs _on your inputs_, and a close that finishes by mid-month instead of next quarter is almost entirely a function of the COO supplying those inputs on schedule. The checklist below is the draft for exercise 13.4 — agree real dates with the CA and file the agreed version in the binder and [[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]] (days are working days after month-end, illustrative):

| By        | The COO supplies                                                                                               | From                               |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Day 2     | Bank statements for all accounts; petty-cash count and vouchers (§4)                                           | Bank portal; custodian             |
| Day 3     | Sales invoice register and payments received; AR ageing                                                        | ERPNext (§10)                      |
| Day 3     | All vendor bills for the month (chased in the Friday run-up, not on day 3); expense claims closed and approved | ERPNext / the bill folder (§7)     |
| Day 5     | Contractor payment list with PANs and TDS deducted; payroll register once salaries exist                       | ERPNext; payroll sheet (lesson 09) |
| Day 7     | Confirmation TDS is deposited; GST workings handed over per the §7 rhythm                                      | Bank challan; CA                   |
| Day 10–12 | — the CA returns: P&L, balance sheet, GST/TDS filings status, and open queries answered within 48 hours        | CA                                 |

**Budget vs actual (BvA)** is the second half of the ritual. The annual plan (lesson 19) gives each cost category a monthly budget; the close gives you actuals; the BvA sheet (`Budget vs Actual` tab; [[T20-budget-vs-actual-and-cash-forecast|T20]]) shows the variance. The rule that keeps it useful: **any line off by more than 10% and more than a threshold ₹ amount gets one written sentence** — cause, and whether it repeats. Review BvA with the CEO in the monthly business review (lesson 08); it is the earliest honest signal that the plan and reality are diverging, and it feeds the quarterly re-plan ([[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review|T19]]).

**Founder money, one paragraph of hygiene** (the depth is the finance course's): directors take the modest, real salary Finance Phase 7 §9 prescribes — booked properly, never ad-hoc withdrawals; money a founder puts _in_ is documented as a director's loan or capital with the CA (Phase 6), because the DPT-3 return discloses director loans every June (lesson 05 §4); and nothing moves between the company and the family petrol pump without paper (lesson 05 §9). The COO's contribution is simply refusing exceptions — the books stay clean because the rails allow nothing else.

## 10. ERPNext as the system of record

Everything in this lesson lands as a **document in ERPNext** — that is what "system of record" means: if it isn't there, it didn't happen, and no parallel spreadsheet is allowed to disagree with it (the [[ERPNext-Implementation-Guide|ERPNext Implementation Guide]]'s go-live rule: dual systems kill adoption; pick a cutover date and mean it). One distinction to keep permanently clear: **DZZLO OMS issues the dealers' invoices to _their_ customers; ERPNext holds _VSYST's own_ books** — the product's documents and the company's documents never mix.

The COO owns these document chains (the guide's Phase 4 workflows):

| Chain              | Documents                                                                | COO's rule                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sales cycle**    | Customer → Quotation → Sales Order → Sales Invoice → Payment Entry       | Every subscription and pilot flows through it from day one (lesson 11 adds Lead → Opportunity in front); the §6 invoice template lives here |
| **Purchase cycle** | Supplier → Purchase Order → Purchase Invoice → Payment Entry             | Every vendor from §5, with GSTIN, Udyam flag and TDS section on the supplier record                                                         |
| **Money records**  | Payment entries, the AR/AP reports, expense claims (via Frappe HR later) | AR ageing pulled for every Monday meeting; the close inputs (§9) are exports, not retyping                                                  |

The CA reviews the ledgers and account mappings once the first cycles run (guide, Phase 4's accounting-review week), and the automation layer comes free later: overdue-invoice reminders and approval workflows above a threshold are built-in ERPNext features (guide, Phase 6) — which is how §6's dunning ladder eventually runs itself (lesson 18). Until the cutover, the workbook tabs are the interim record; after it, they read _from_ ERPNext, never beside it. Tally remains where lesson 07 put it: at the petrol pump and with the CA — not VSYST's system of record.

## 11. Later — subscriptions, e-mandates and the gateway

Nothing in this section is to build now; all of it shapes decisions you will make within the year, so it goes on record. The monetisation direction is decided: **company-level subscription per GSTIN, users unlimited, all billing on the web, the app gated server-side — prices undecided** (never state any). The operational consequences, when billing switches on:

- **Recurring payments run under RBI's e-mandate framework** (consolidated 21 April 2026): additional-factor authentication (AFA) at mandate registration; subsequent auto-debits **up to ₹15,000 without AFA** (₹1 lakh for specified categories like mutual-fund SIPs and insurance — not SaaS); a **pre-debit notification at least 24 hours** before every charge; cancellation and modification with AFA; and the rules cover cards, PPIs and **UPI Autopay** alike ([AMLEGALS — the e-mandate framework 2026 checklist](https://amlegals.com/upi-autopay-and-recurring-payments-compliance-checklist-under-rbis-e-mandate-framework-2026/); [Razorpay — recurring payments and UPI Autopay](https://razorpay.com/blog/master-recurring-payments-upi-autopay-guide/)). The design constraint hiding in that: **a plan above ₹15,000/month means AFA on every debit or split billing** — a pricing-mechanics input for the [[app-store-economics/08-dzzlo-subscription-strategy|subscription strategy]], and a number to **VERIFY LIVE** when the pricing work starts.
- **Gateway onboarding is a paperwork exam the binder passes in a day.** RBI's Payment Aggregator Directions (15 September 2025) require full merchant KYC, with a lighter route for small merchants; expect the gateway to ask for COI, PAN, GSTIN, a cancelled cheque, director KYC, and a live website with terms, privacy, refund and contact pages ([AuthBridge — RBI PA Master Direction](https://authbridge.com/blog/rbi-payment-aggregator-master-direction-2025/)). The Easebuzz / Union Bank conversation stays "in discussion" — the COO's contribution is lesson 11's partnership discipline: one owner, a tracker, a written next step.
- **Annual prepay remains the goal** regardless of rails (§6, §8): one AFA-authorised payment a year beats twelve mandate debits for cash, DSO and support load alike.

When this switches from "later" to "now", it becomes a project charter ([[T18-project-charter|T18]]) with the CA and the gateway in the loop from day one.

## 12. At VSYST — applying this now

- **Start with an audit, not a build — the company has existed since 2021.** In one sitting, answer: which current account(s) exist and at which bank; who the signatories are and whether the mandate matches the latest board resolution; whether maker-checker is actually enabled on net banking or everything rides on one login; where vendor bills arrive today (almost certainly WhatsApp) and where they go; who, if anyone, has ever raised a VSYST invoice and from what template. Every "don't know" becomes a binder row ([[T02-company-binder-checklist|T02]]).
- **Open the tax-reserve account and set the sweep rule now**, while it is trivially cheap to do — pre-revenue, the habit costs nothing, and the first subscription rupee arrives into rails that already behave (§2).
- **The product is free so far; build the muscle on the spend side.** With little AR to chase, this month's mechanisms are §3 (DoA signed), §5 (weekly run, MSME flags on every vendor) and §7 (contractor PAN/TDS discipline — freelancers "as needed" is exactly where 194J/194C surprises live). The collections machinery (§6) gets built into the ERPNext sales cycle now and rehearsed on any pilot or partner revenue before it must perform at scale.
- **Respect the boundary with the pump.** Shared office, shared family, two sets of books: every flow between VSYST and the petrol pump business is papered (lesson 05 §9), and nothing of the pump's cash ever touches VSYST's accounts or petty cash. The MBP-1 disclosures exist for precisely this geometry.
- **Founder money is already in — document it backwards.** The DPT-3 schedule and director-loan paperwork (lesson 05 §4, Finance Phase 6) are this month's job with the CA, not a someday job.
- **Four mechanisms, one month:** maker-checker on the bank; T22 signed; the Thursday payment run with the calendar on the wall; the close checklist agreed with the CA. Install those and VSYST's money runs on rails a stranger could audit — which is the definition of done for this lesson.
