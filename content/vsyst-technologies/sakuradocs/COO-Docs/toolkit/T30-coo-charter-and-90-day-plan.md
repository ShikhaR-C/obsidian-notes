# T30 — COO Charter and 90-Day Plan

_Toolkit · fills exercise 13.1 in [[04-how-to-become-a-coo-in-2026|04 — How to Become a COO in 2026]]; lessons [[01-what-is-a-coo|01]]–[[03-the-coo-core-value-and-the-ceo-coo-contract|03]] all end here · Owner: you write it, the CEO signs it · Cadence: Week 0; re-read at day 90 and refreshed annually · No workbook tab — the plan's dated items go on the `Cadence Calendar` tab of [vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Two documents that only work together. The **charter** says what the seat is — its mission, what it owns, what it explicitly does _not_ own, what it is measured on. The **90-day plan** says what the seat will do first, and what will exist at the end of it. Without the charter the plan is busywork; without the plan the charter is a job title.

The advice a founding COO gives from the inside is exactly this pairing: **define the mission in week one**, and don't spend the first months rebuilding standard artefacts that already exist ([Between Two COOs — what a COO does at an early-stage startup](https://betweentwocoos.com/blog/what-does-a-coo-do-early-stage-startup-allie-fleder/)). This toolkit is why you never have to rebuild them.

## When to use

- **Week 0**, before anything else in the course — it is the first artefact the seat produces.
- **When a COO is hired later**, as the document they are handed on day one, and the one they rewrite in week one.
- **At day 90**, read against reality: what shipped, what slipped, what the seat actually turned out to own.
- **Annually**, and any time the remit moves — a new function arriving, a hire taking a piece of it, a partnership changing the company's shape.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Mission in one sentence, written in week one.** Not "run operations" — something you could fail at. The house version: _build and run the machine that turns the company's promises into kept promises, so the founders can steer instead of push_ ([[index|the landing page]]).
2. **The "does not own" list is the more valuable half.** Ambiguity, not skill, is what kills CEO–COO partnerships ([[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]]); the fastest cure is naming what stays with the CEO/CTO in writing. At VSYST that is product direction, engineering, architecture, the code in releases (you own the _process_ around them), and the relationships only a founder can hold ([[01-what-is-a-coo|lesson 01]] §9).
3. **Ask the CEO which decisions they will stop making — and write the answers into the charter.** If the answer is "none yet", you have a title, not a seat ([Alder Koten — why COOs fail in founder-led companies](https://charliesolorzano.me/2026/03/17/coo-founder-led-company-failure/)). Each answer becomes a row in [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]].
4. **KPIs must be real rows, not adjectives.** Take them from the COO scorecard in [[03-the-coo-core-value-and-the-ceo-coo-contract|lesson 03]] §3 — on-time delivery of the plan, scorecard health, cash and collections, hiring hit rate, incidents and SLA, compliance zero-misses, founder hours freed, the autopilot level — and put them in [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]] as a block separate from the company scorecard, because this one measures _you_.
5. **The standard 90 days are listen → analyse → implement**: days 1–30 read everything and map what is, is not, should and should not be happening; days 31–60 analyse metrics, money and process (roughly a third of the first two months goes to analysis) and form a view of the team; days 61–90 present the view and start implementing ([Forbes Coaches Council — a new COO's 90-day plan](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbescoachescouncil/2020/12/14/are-you-a-new-coo-start-out-strong-with-this-90-day-plan/); [COO transition roadmap](https://www.chiefoperatingofficer.org/blog/coo-transition-plan); [My first 90 days as COO](https://www.lizettewarner.com/post/my-first-90-days-as-coo)). In a founder-led company, add: **observe and document before changing anything**, and take the least emotionally loaded domains first — vendors, finance ops, compliance — leaving what is close to the founder's heart until credibility exists (Alder Koten).
6. **Compress the arc only if you have earned the right to.** A founder formalising the seat already knows the company, so days 1–30 can produce artefacts instead of only notes — that is the VSYST version below. An outsider should not compress it.
7. **Every window ends in an artefact and an exit criterion**, never a mood. "Understand finance" is not a milestone; "13-week cash forecast live, owned jointly with the CA" is.
8. **The CEO signs it.** A charter countersigned in Week 0 is what makes the seat exist; an unsigned one is a wish list. File it in the vault next to [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]] and re-read both quarterly.

## Template (a) — the charter, one page

```
COO CHARTER — <company>            written <date> · reviewed <date+90d>
Signed: COO ____________   CEO ____________

MISSION (one sentence, failable)
  ...

I OWN (outcome — not activity — one line each; each must have a number)
  1. ...            2. ...            3. ...            4. ...

I DO NOT OWN (say it plainly; who does)
  - ...  -> CEO/CTO        - ...  -> domain-expert director
  - ...  -> the CA / CS (external)

DECISIONS THE CEO STOPS MAKING (from the week-one conversation)
  - ...  -> now mine up to <limit>, per T22 row <n>

HOW I AM MEASURED (rows in T05, reviewed monthly/quarterly)
  <metric> · <definition> · <source> · <target> · <reviewed>

PARTNER: <the CEO>   ·   BACKUP FOR MY SEAT: <name/role>
ESCALATION: anything that could hit cash, a customer, a filing, a person
or the product's reputation is said the same day (T01 no-surprises rule).
```

## Template (b) — the 30/60/90 plan

One table, three windows, an exit criterion per window. Keep it to one page; put every dated item on the `Cadence Calendar` tab.

| Window         | Mood                        | What you do | Artefacts produced | Exit criterion |
| -------------- | --------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------ | -------------- |
| **Days 1–30**  | Listen and record           | ...         | ...                | ...            |
| **Days 31–60** | Analyse and lay foundations | ...         | ...                | ...            |
| **Days 61–90** | Cadence and first builds    | ...         | ...                | ...            |

At day 90, write half a page against it: what shipped, what slipped and why, what the seat actually turned out to own, and the three things days 91–180 must produce. That half page is the first entry in your own record — and the format the monthly update ([[T23-board-and-investor-update|T23]]) will reuse for the rest of the company's life.

## VSYST example — the filled 90 days

VSYST's arc is compressed: you are a founder formalising a seat, not an outsider earning trust, so days 1–30 already produce artefacts ([[04-how-to-become-a-coo-in-2026|lesson 04]] §8). The three windows map onto the course itself — which is the point of the course.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Days 1–30** — make the company real, runnable and recorded           | Read lessons 01–04 and sign the seat; audit every paper, every login and every rupee that leaves the bank; fix the tool map; start the heartbeat | [[T30-coo-charter-and-90-day-plan\|T30]] + [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement\|T01]] signed; [[T02-company-binder-checklist\|T02]] complete ([[05-legal-and-governance-foundation\|05]]); [[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix\|T22]] drafted and [[T20-budget-vs-actual-and-cash-forecast\|T20]] live ([[06-money-rails-and-finance-operations\|06]]); [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register\|T14]] filled ([[07-tools-and-it-foundation\|07]]); [[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda\|T03]], [[T05-kpi-scorecard\|T05]] v0, [[T04-one-on-one-template\|T04]] running ([[08-the-operating-cadence\|08]]) | The binder is complete, no company account sits on a personal login, the ops meeting has run four weeks without a skip, and the scorecard was filled by 10:00 every Monday                    |
| **Days 31–60** — build the two functions that touch customers          | People ops from zero; move dealer support into one queue; make the pipeline a list rather than a memory                                          | First six policies + [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process\|T10]], [[T11-offer-letter-and-contract-checklists\|T11]], [[T12-onboarding-checklist\|T12]], [[T21-headcount-plan\|T21]], [[T27-policy-handbook-toc\|T27]] ([[09-people-operations\|09]]); [[T25-customer-support-sop\|T25]] + `SOP-CUS-001` dealer onboarding ([[10-customer-operations-support-and-success\|10]]); CRM stages and the weekly pipeline review ([[11-revenue-operations-and-partnerships\|11]])                                                                                                                     | Every dealer message lands in one queue with an SLA; activation (first customer linked, first invoice, 14 days) is measured; the partnership conversations each have an owner and a next step |
| **Days 61–90** — wrap the product, the law and the money in mechanisms | Put process around shipping; build the compliance calendar with the CA/CS; put every vendor on one list; then plan the first quarter properly    | [[T26-release-checklist\|T26]] + [[T17-incident-postmortem\|T17]] ([[12-product-and-engineering-operations\|12]]); [[T16-compliance-calendar\|T16]] + [[T15-risk-register\|T15]] ([[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance\|13]]); vendor audit and cost-per-person ([[14-vendors-procurement-and-cost-control\|14]]); the first quarterly half-day — [[T19-quarterly-plan-and-review\|T19]] + [[T06-okr-planning-sheet\|T06]]                                                                                                                                                            | A release has shipped through the checklist; no statutory date is unowned; the next quarter has 3–5 rocks with owners; the CEO's calendar shows less operations than it did on day 1          |

The number that says it worked is not how many templates are filled — it is **founder hours freed** (lesson 03 §3, metric 7), measured by a calendar audit in week 1 and again in week 13.
