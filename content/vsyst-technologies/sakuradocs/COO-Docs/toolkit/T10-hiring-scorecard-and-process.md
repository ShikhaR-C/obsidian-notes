# T10 — Hiring Scorecard and Process

_Toolkit · fills the exercises in [[09-people-operations|09 — People Operations]] · Owner: hiring manager + COO · Cadence: one scorecard per role, written before sourcing; tracker updated at the weekly pipeline check · Workbook tab: `Hiring Pipeline` in [vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

Hiring is the highest-stakes recurring decision a small company makes, and the default method — read a CV, "let's chat", decide on feel — measures confidence and likeability, not the job. The fix is Smart & Street's _Who_ method: **write the scorecard before you meet anyone** — the role's **mission** (one sentence of why the seat exists), its **outcomes** (3–5 measurable, dated results the person must deliver) and the **competencies** (how they must work, including the company's values) — then run every candidate through the same structured process against it ([Who — the A Method for Hiring, excerpt](https://geoffsmart.com/books/who-the-a-method-for-hiring/who-the-book-excerpt/)). a16z uses the same mission/outcomes/competencies document for executive hires ([a16z — executive hiring](https://a16z.com/executive-hiring/)). The scorecard turns "did we like them?" into "did we see evidence they can deliver these outcomes?" — a question interviewers can actually score. [[09-people-operations|Lesson 09]] §3 teaches the process; this template is the paper it runs on.

## When to use

- **Every role, including contractors.** Contractors convert to employees through the same scorecard and process as any hire (lesson 09 §2) — "we already know them" is how mis-hires happen twice.
- **Before sourcing, never after.** The rule is absolute: **no scorecard, no posting.** The job description is the scorecard rewritten in public language.
- **Only when a trigger fires.** Roles open when the metric trigger in [[T21-headcount-plan|T21]] fires, not when the founders feel busy.
- **Keep the role open rather than lower the bar.** A scored process makes the weak hire visible before the offer; the ops plan's law — hire slow, fire fast — applies from candidate one. If nobody clears the bar, fix the sourcing or the salary band, not the rubric.
- One scorecard per role; the tracker below runs continuously and is read in the weekly pipeline check.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Scorecard first, with a salary band.** Hiring manager and COO write it together and sign it; outcomes must be measurable and dated ("by day 90…"), competencies include VSYST's values, and the band comes from the [[T21-headcount-plan|T21]] benchmarks — argued once, before any candidate anchors it.
2. **Same questions, every candidate.** Interviews are structured: questions from the bank below mapped to scorecard competencies, asked in the same order. No unstructured "let's chat" rounds.
3. **Score independently, in writing, before discussing.** Each interviewer enters 1–4 per competency straight after the interview, before hearing anyone else's view.
4. **A paid, real work sample** — 2–4 hours, from the actual job — is the most predictive stage; never skip it for a likeable candidate.
5. **Two references, called, with the T10 script** — including at least one name the candidate did not volunteer ("who was your manager there? may we call them?").
6. **The decision meeting is short and rule-bound** (below). Update the `Hiring Pipeline` tab within a day of every stage; every candidate gets a written yes/no within the stage SLA.
7. **Keep personal data out of the shared sheet.** Candidate A/B/C codes in the tab; CVs and contact details stay in the inbox/ATS (a DPDP habit that costs nothing now — [[13-compliance-calendar-risk-and-insurance|lesson 13]]).

## Template

**(a) The role scorecard** — copy per role, one page, signed before sourcing:

```
# Scorecard — <role> (<location · employee/contractor>)
Written by: <hiring manager + COO> · date · salary band: ₹___–___ /mo · reports to: <role>

Mission (one sentence — why this seat exists):
...

Outcomes (3–5, measurable, dated):
1. By day 90, ...
2. By month 6, ...

Competencies (5–8, incl. values — each gets a 1–4 score per interviewer):
- <role skill> · <role skill> · <communication/language needs>
- Values: <e.g. writes things down · keeps promises to customers · says "I don't know">
```

**(b) The process stages** — owners and SLAs (illustrative; the full table is lesson 09 §3):

| Stage                                 | Owner                | SLA                          | Rule                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Scorecard                             | Hiring manager + COO | Before sourcing              | No scorecard, no posting                                                   |
| Source → Screen (20-min call)         | COO / hiring manager | Screen within 3 working days | Score before next stage                                                    |
| Interview 1 (structured, 45–60 min)   | 2 interviewers       | Within a week of screen      | Independent written scores                                                 |
| Work sample (paid, 2–4 h, real task)  | Hiring manager       | Reviewed within 2 days       | The most predictive stage                                                  |
| Interview 2 (values + remaining gaps) | The other director   | Within 3 days                | Same question bank                                                         |
| References (two, called)              | Hiring manager       | Before offer                 | One un-volunteered name                                                    |
| Decision meeting                      | CEO + COO            | 30 min, same day             | Scores in writing before discussion                                        |
| Offer (verbal → written)              | COO                  | Within 2 days                | Probation stated up front; then [[T11-offer-letter-and-contract-checklists | T11]] |

**(c) Question bank** — same questions to everyone; probe with "what exactly did _you_ do?" and "how did it turn out, in numbers?" (adapted from the _Who_ interviews):

- Walk me through your last role: what were you hired to do, and against what target?
- What are you proudest of there? What went badly — and what did you do about it?
- Who was your manager? How will they rate you, 1–10, when we call — and why?
- Tell me about the last process you wrote down or improved. What happened to it after you left?
- (Per competency) Tell me about a time you <competency in action>. What was the situation, what did you do, what resulted?
- (Values) Tell me about a promise to a customer you could not keep. What did you do?

**(d) Scoring rubric — 1 to 4, no halves:**

| Score | Meaning                                                    |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | No evidence, or contrary evidence — below the bar          |
| 2     | Partial evidence; would need coaching to reach the bar     |
| 3     | Clear evidence they meet the bar — the hiring standard     |
| 4     | Exceptional evidence; could raise our bar and teach others |

Hire only at an average ≥ 3 with no competency at 1. A sheet full of 2s is a no, however likeable.

**(e) Reference-check script** (10 minutes, by phone):

```
1. Confirm: role, dates, reporting line.
2. In what context did you work with <candidate>?
3. What were they hired to do? Did they do it?
4. Biggest strengths? Areas where they needed support?
5. Rate their performance 1–10. What would have made it a 10?
6. Would you re-hire them for <this role's mission>? Why / why not?
```

**(f) Decision-meeting rules:** scores on the table in writing before anyone speaks; the hiring manager speaks last; a strong "no" with a stated scorecard reason blocks the hire; decide the same day; the decision and reason go into the tracker, and a borderline "yes" is a no.

**(g) Candidate tracker** — one row per candidate per role, identical columns to the `Hiring Pipeline` tab in [vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx): **Role · Candidate · Source · Stage · Interviewer(s) · Scorecard avg (1–4) · Next step · Next-step date · Days to next step · Decision · Notes**, with Stage one of Sourced → Screened → Interview 1 → Work sample → Interview 2 → References → Offer → Hired / Rejected / Withdrawn. Reviewed in the weekly pipeline check; every row must have a next step and a date.

## VSYST example (illustrative)

An extract from the scorecard for VSYST's first hire — the **Support/Ops Associate (Raipur)** from lesson 09 §2, contractor-first (numbers illustrative):

- **Mission:** take dealer support and onboarding off the technical founder so tickets, WhatsApp and activation run without touching the CEO's day.
- **Outcomes:** by day 90 — owns first response on all support channels within the [[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]] SLA; runs dealer onboarding end-to-end with ≥ 80% of new tenants activated (first customer linked and first invoice) within 14 days; the FAQ's top-10 answers written in Hindi and English; by month 6 — runs SOP-OPS-002 (the Monday scorecard fill, [[T08-sop-template|T08]]) unaided.
- **Competencies:** fluent Hindi (Chhattisgarhi a plus) on the phone and WhatsApp; writes clearly and briefly; process discipline (follows a checklist, then improves it); empathy with dealer reality — petrol-pump or distribution exposure preferred; values: writes things down, keeps promises to customers.
- **Work sample (paid):** handle five sample WhatsApp tickets (OTP not received, rate-confirmation missed, invoice mismatch, login/invite issue, statement PDF) and write one FAQ entry — exactly the tab's example row.
