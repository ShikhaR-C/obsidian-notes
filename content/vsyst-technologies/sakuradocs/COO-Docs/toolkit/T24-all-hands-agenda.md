# T24 — All-Hands Agenda

_Toolkit · fills the all-hands in §6 of [[08-the-operating-cadence|08 — The Operating Cadence]] · Owner: the COO runs it, the CEO opens it · Cadence: monthly, 45 minutes, last Friday — started the month the first person outside the directors joins · No workbook tab — the ritual sits on the `Cadence Calendar` tab of [vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx](vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx)._

## Purpose

The all-hands is the monthly forty-five minutes in which **everyone in the company sees the same numbers, hears the same priorities, and can ask anything.** That is its whole job. It is not a status meeting — the weekly ops meeting does status and problem-solving ([[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]) — and it is not a motivational event. It is the mechanism that makes transparency a default rather than a favour, which matters most at a family-founded company where information otherwise travels by dinner table.

Two ideas do the work. **One version of the numbers**: the scorecard shown here is the same sheet the directors read on Monday, unedited ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]). And **no surprises**: nobody learns news about their own work, their own team or their own company in this room for the first time.

## When to use

- **Monthly, from the month the first person outside the three directors joins.** Until then the ops meeting _is_ the all-hands — start the ritual with the first hire so transparency never has to be retrofitted ([[08-the-operating-cadence|lesson 08]] §6).
- **Same slot every month** — last Friday works, after the monthly business review, so the numbers presented are the ones just discussed.
- **Immediately after anything big**, as a short special edition: a partnership signed, a major incident, a departure, a pricing decision. A rumour that reaches the team before the all-hands is a failure of this mechanism, not of the team.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **Same numbers everyone sees.** No management version and team version. If a number is too sensitive to show, say _why_ it is not shown — do not show an edited one.
2. **No surprises, in either direction.** Anything affecting a person's work is said to them first, in a 1:1 ([[T04-one-on-one-template|T04]]), before it is said here. Bad news travels first and fastest.
3. **Every question is answered or logged.** "I don't know" is a fine answer if it comes with an owner and a date; the log is read out at the next all-hands, unanswered items first. Nothing kills an all-hands faster than a question that evaporated.
4. **Forty-five minutes, ends on time, agenda published the day before** with a place for questions to be submitted in advance — which is how the quiet people ask anything at all.
5. **One customer story, every month, with a real dealer's name and what changed for them.** In a company where most people never meet a dealer, this is the only regular contact with the reason the company exists.
6. **Recognition is specific and named**, and it points at a behaviour the company wants repeated — "wrote the SOP so the next person doesn't have to ask", not "great work everyone".
7. **Notes and the deck go into the vault the same day**, for whoever was on a dealer visit. Attendance is expected, not enforced; the record is the mechanism.
8. **It is not a decision meeting.** Decisions belong to the ops meeting, the quarterly or the DoA ([[T22-delegation-of-authority-matrix|T22]]); anything that turns into a debate here is captured and taken to the issues list.

## Template — the 45-minute monthly all-hands

```
ALL-HANDS — <Month YYYY>        45 min · last Friday · runs: COO · opens: CEO
Agenda posted <yesterday> · questions box open until the start · notes in the vault after

 5  Where we are going        CEO: the one-sentence direction, and what changed this month
10  The numbers               the same scorecard the directors read Monday; on-track /
                              off-track; ONE line of why for each off-track row
 8  Wins and a customer story a real dealer, named, and what changed for them
 7  Priorities this quarter   each rock: owner, on track / off track, what help it needs
 5  People and hiring         who is joining, roles open and their triggers, policy changes
 8  Q&A                       submitted questions first, then live; answered or LOGGED
                              with an owner and a date
 2  Recognition               specific, named, pointing at a behaviour to repeat

After: notes + deck in the vault the same day; the question log updated;
anything that turned into a debate goes to the ops meeting's issues list.
```

## The 20-minute variant (3–5 people)

At the size VSYST will be for a while, forty-five minutes is theatre. Run twenty, keep every ingredient, drop the ceremony — and keep the _slot_ even in a quiet month, because a ritual skipped twice is a ritual gone.

```
ALL-HANDS (small) — 20 min
 5  Numbers          scorecard, on/off track, one line per red
 5  Wins + one customer story
 4  This quarter     the rocks, owners, what's stuck
 2  People           anyone joining, any role opening, any policy change
 4  Q&A              answered or logged
```

## VSYST example (illustrative)

The first real all-hands at VSYST is four or five people — three directors, the Support/Ops Associate, perhaps a field-sales contractor — standing in the office inside the family's petrol pump, with the scorecard on a laptop screen. What makes it worth doing at that size:

- **The associate sees the whole board, not just their queue.** FRT and CSAT sit next to activation, runway and the pipeline, so "my tickets" becomes "our fourteen tenants".
- **The customer story is told by the domain-expert director**, in Hindi if that is the room's language, about a dealer whose morning got shorter because the ledger was already right. Thirty years of context, five minutes a month, and nobody outside the family would otherwise hear it.
- **The hiring slot names the trigger, not the vacancy** — "field sales opens when the district pipeline passes thirty leads; we are at nineteen" ([[T21-headcount-plan|T21]]). People who understand the trigger stop asking when help is coming and start moving the number.
- **The question log is the honest part.** In month one it will contain something awkward — about pay bands, about whether the app will ever charge, about what happens if a partnership lands. Answer what you can, log the rest with a date, and answer it next month. Doing that twice buys more trust than any offsite.
