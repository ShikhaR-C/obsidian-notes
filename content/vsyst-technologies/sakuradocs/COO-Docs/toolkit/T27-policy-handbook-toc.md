## Purpose

A policy is **a mechanism for a recurring people decision** — written once so it is not re-argued, and not re-argued differently for different people. This template is the _table of contents_, not the policies: it names every policy a small Indian Pvt Ltd eventually needs, says in a line what each is for, flags which are legally required and at what headcount, and fixes the order to write them in. Twelve one-page policies written over a year is achievable; a handbook attempted in one sitting never ships.

The starter set is not invented here — India-specific practice lists roughly this spread (handbook/code of conduct, leave and holidays, POSH, IT and acceptable use, data protection and confidentiality, remote work, expenses and travel, performance and probation, exit/notice, anti-bribery, ESOP), and pre-first-hire checklists elsewhere name the same essentials before anyone joins ([PolicyOwn — startup compliance checklist 2026](https://policyown.com/blog/startup-compliance-checklist-2026)).

## When to use

- **Months 2–3, before the first hire** — the first six are pre-hire work, not post-hire tidying.
- **Whenever a headcount threshold is crossed** (10 employees, 20 workers) — several rows below change from optional to mandatory at those lines.
- **When the same people question is asked twice** — that is the twice rule pointed at policy.
- **Every April**, alongside the compliance calendar rebuild ([[T16-compliance-calendar|T16]]), because thresholds and rules move.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One page each, in the vault, plain language.** Policies that read like statutes are not read. Each carries an owner, a version, an effective date and a next-review date, like an SOP ([[T08-sop-template|T08]]).
2. **The "Mandatory in India?" column is a VERIFY LIVE column, every row.** The four Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025 with Central Rules notified 8 May 2026, and Chhattisgarh's state rules status is itself a thing to confirm ([KPMG Flash Alert 2026-127](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-127.html); [LKS — state rules tracker](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/rules)). Confirm each row with the CA for tax and social security and a labour consultant or lawyer for the rest — never from this table alone.
3. **Policies must match the appointment letter.** Appointment letters are now statutory in prescribed format, so probation, notice, leave and confidentiality must say the same thing in both places ([[09-people-operations|lesson 09]] §4 and [[T11-offer-letter-and-contract-checklists|T11]]) — a contradiction between the two is what a dispute is made of.
4. **Write only what you will enforce.** An unenforced policy is worse than none: it teaches people that written rules are decoration. Kill rows you cannot run yet and say so.
5. **Every joiner acknowledges the live set on day 1**, recorded in [[T12-onboarding-checklist|T12]] — the acknowledgement, not the document, is what makes a policy operative.
6. **Approval matches the risk.** Operational policies are the COO's; anything with a statutory or cultural weight goes to the CEO or the board, and POSH is noted in a board minute (the Board's report now carries a POSH and maternity disclosure — [[05-legal-and-governance-foundation|lesson 05]]).
7. **The handbook lives in the vault, not in email attachments**, with one link that is always current; changes are announced, dated and versioned.

## The order to write them, and who approves

**Write the first six first** — rows 1–6, in that order, one page each, before the first hire ([[09-people-operations|lesson 09]] §7). They cover the legally undeniable (POSH), the two things every employee asks about in week one (leave, expenses), the one that protects the product (IT and security), the one that says who you are (code of conduct) and the one that describes where work happens. Rows 7–11 follow over the next two quarters, in the order their triggers fire — DPDP work ahead of May 2027, grievance ahead of 20 workers, performance and exit ahead of the first confirmation and the first departure. Row 12 waits until there is equity worth granting.

Approvals in one line: **the COO owns the operational policies; the CEO signs the ones about behaviour; the board minutes POSH and anti-bribery; shareholders approve the ESOP scheme.** Record each approval in [[T09-decision-log-and-adr|T09]] with the version number, so "which version did we approve?" has an answer.
