# T04 — One-on-One Template

_Toolkit · fills the exercises in [[08-the-operating-cadence|08 — The Operating Cadence]] and [[09-people-operations|09 — People Operations]] · Owner: every manager — the COO first · Cadence: weekly 30–45 min (biweekly only for proven seniors), fixed slot, moved but never cancelled · Workbook tab: none — each 1:1 is a running doc in the vault._

## Purpose

The 1:1 is **the highest-leverage meeting a manager runs**: recruiting, training and 1:1s are where one hour of yours changes many hours of someone else's work ([High Output Management — notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/high-output-management-andy-grove)). Its two design rules come from the operator canon: **the agenda belongs to the report, not to you** (Grove — it is their meeting; you go second), and **the cadence is fixed** — 1:1s sit on the calendar every week, not "when there is time", and issues arrive written with a proposed solution, "writing vs talking" ([Mochary — The Great CEO Within](https://blas.com/the-great-ceo-within/)). A 1:1 is **not a status meeting**: numbers live on the scorecard ([[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]) and shipped/blocked/next lives in the stand-up. It exists so blockers, feedback and growth surface **while they are small** — the continuous conversations-feedback-recognition loop that goal systems assume is happening ([OKRs and CFRs — Quantive](https://quantive.com/resources/articles/okrs-and-cfrs)).

## When to use

- **From the first report** — employee or contractor with recurring scope. Two people reporting to you = two running docs, this template each.
- **Weekly, 30–45 minutes, default.** Grove's **task-relevant maturity** sets the depth: someone new to a task needs weekly, structured, close; someone experienced needs goals and space — and the same person can be mature on support tickets and new to writing SOPs at once ([Grove notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/high-output-management-andy-grove)). Go biweekly only after a quarter of green.
- **The CEO–COO 1:1 is a different meeting** with its own skeleton in [[T01-ceo-coo-operating-agreement|T01]] section D — don't merge the two.
- Never cancelled — **moved at worst**. A cancelled 1:1 tells the report exactly where they rank.

## How to fill (rules)

1. **One running doc per person**, shared and editable by both, newest week on top — e.g. `1on1/<name>.md` in the vault's private area. Both of you add agenda items during the week, so the meeting starts full.
2. **Their agenda first.** Your items wait until theirs are done; most weeks that leaves you ten minutes, which is correct.
3. Anything that needs a decision arrives **written, with a proposed solution** (Mochary's rule). You react to a proposal instead of discovering a problem.
4. **No status.** If an update has a number, it belongs on [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]]; if it is "what I did", it belongs in the stand-up. Reclaim the time for blockers and coaching.
5. End every 1:1 with **feedback both ways — one thing each** — and **actions with owner + date**, reviewed first thing next week. Actions that roll over twice go to the ops meeting issues list ([[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]).
6. **Growth every fourth week:** spend a third of the slot on their development — skills, exposure, next role — not on this week's work.
7. Notes are typed into the running doc **during** the meeting, in front of them. No private shadow notes except legally sensitive matters (pay, performance concern), recorded factually.

## Template

Running-doc skeleton (copy once per person; add a dated block each week on top):

```
1:1 — <name> x <manager>        cadence: weekly <day/time>, 30–45 min
Running doc — newest week first. Both edit. Their agenda first.

## YYYY-MM-DD
1. Their agenda (they write before the meeting; they go first)
   - ...
2. Blockers — what is slower or harder than it should be? where are you waiting on me?
   - ...
3. Priorities — top 3 until next week; what gets dropped to make room?
   - ...
4. Feedback both ways (one each: keep / change)
   - them -> me: ...
   - me -> them: ...
5. Growth (every 4th week) — skills, exposure, next role, training
   - ...
6. Actions (owner + date — reviewed at the top of next week's 1:1)
   - [ ] ...
```

Questions bank — use one or two per section when the page is blank, not all of them:

| Section               | Questions that work                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Opening               | "What's on your mind this week?" · "What would make this meeting worth it for you?"                                                        |
| Blockers              | "What's slower or harder than it should be?" · "Where are you waiting on me or the CEO?" · "What almost went wrong this week?"             |
| Priorities            | "What's the one thing that matters most by Friday?" · "If you had to drop one thing, which one?"                                           |
| Feedback to you       | "What should I do differently for you?" · "What am I doing that wastes your time?" · "What would you change about how we run the company?" |
| Growth                | "What do you want to be doing more of in six months?" · "What did you learn this month?" · "Which SOP could you own end to end?"           |
| Listening for signals | "What are dealers complaining about that I don't hear?" · "What's the team joking about lately?"                                           |

## Red flags

- **Their agenda is empty two weeks running** — they have stopped bringing problems. Ask smaller questions; check whether a past item was punished.
- **Every item is status** — move it to the stand-up and the scorecard; the 1:1 has collapsed into reporting.
- **You talk more than half the time** — it has become your meeting.
- **Actions roll over twice** — overload or wrong actions; take it to the issues list, not to a bigger lecture.
- **Cancellations creep in** (either side) — the calendar is telling the truth about priorities; fix the slot or the relationship.
- **"All good" for a month straight** — the meeting has become a formality; the next surprise will be a big one. Feedback both ways, every week, is the vaccine.

## VSYST example (illustrative)

Tuesday 14:00, COO × Support/Ops Associate, week 6. **Their agenda:** drowning in "OTP not received" WhatsApp messages; unsure what to promise a dealer asking for a Hindi invoice PDF. **Blockers:** no canned replies exist — every ticket is typed fresh; waiting on the COO for the Helpdesk trial login. **Priorities:** clear the ticket backlog to zero by Friday; onboard the two new tenants stuck without a customer link. **Feedback:** them → you: "the ops meeting overruns and eats my queue time" (action: timekeeping tightened per [[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]); you → them: the Hindi FAQ draft was exactly right — do the next three. **Actions:** COO shares Helpdesk login by Wednesday; associate drafts the OTP reply macro and adds the case to [[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]] by Friday; "Hindi invoice PDF" logged as a product request for the roadmap list. Growth week next week: they want to learn ERPNext reports — 30 minutes booked with the ERPNext admin.
