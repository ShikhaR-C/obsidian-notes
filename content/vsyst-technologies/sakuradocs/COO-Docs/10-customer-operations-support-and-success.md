# 10 — Customer Operations: Support and Success

_Phase 3 · Build the Machine · Months 2–6. After this lesson you can run every dealer conversation through one queue with hours, SLAs and severity levels, answer DZZLO's five commonest tickets from a written SOP, onboard a dealer from signup to first invoice against a 14-day activation clock, run a success cadence that catches churn signals before the cancellation, feed the complaint log into the roadmap, and put the four support numbers on the scorecard._

Today, "customer operations" at VSYST is the technical founder's personal WhatsApp: a dealer messages at 6:40 AM about an OTP, the founder answers between commits, nothing is counted, and the same question will arrive again next week from someone else. That is correct for the first handful of tenants — and it is not a system. This lesson builds the system around it, so that answering dealers becomes a **queue with numbers**, onboarding becomes a **checklist with a 14-day clock**, and quiet churn becomes a **signal you see three weeks early**.

## 1. Customer operations is two jobs — support and success

**Support** is reactive: a dealer or their customer has a problem now — answer it fast, fix it, and leave a written trace. **Success** is proactive: make sure each tenant reaches the moment the product pays for itself, and notice when usage says they are drifting away — before the cancellation call. Confusing the two is the classic small-SaaS failure: a team that heroically answers tickets while tenants silently stop invoicing has great support and no success.

Both jobs point at the same person: **the dealer is the paying tenant; the customer rides on the dealer's tenant** ([[00_OVERVIEW|Product Overview]]). DZZLO's design already says it — the dealer carries the login and sets the rates; the customer confirms, orders and pays through the dealer's world. So support priority follows the money: a dealer blocked from invoicing outranks a customer's cosmetic complaint, and "success" is measured tenant by tenant, not user by user.

Two rules govern the build order. First, the [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]]'s product rule: **the founder answers every support message early** — nothing teaches the product's real failure modes faster, and the first hundred tenants are R&D, not revenue ([[company/README|Company Playbook]]). Second, this course's twice rule: every ticket the founder answers **leaves something behind** — a macro, an FAQ entry, a checklist step — so that handing the queue to the first Support/Ops Associate ([[09-people-operations|lesson 09]]) is a document transfer, not a brain transplant. The founder-first phase is a deliberate stage of the mechanism, not the absence of one.

## 2. One channel, one queue

The [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]]'s support rule is one line: **one clear support channel, and a promised response time you can keep.** For a WhatsApp-native dealer base that means:

- **One WhatsApp Business number** — a company-owned number on a company SIM and phone (lesson 07's register lists it), published everywhere a dealer looks: the app, the invoice PDF footer, the store listings, the visiting card. Dealers will use WhatsApp whatever you publish, so make the official number the easy one.
- **One `support@` alias** — the email face of the same queue, for store correspondence, formal complaints and anyone allergic to chat.
- **Both feed one queue: Frappe Helpdesk** on the ERPNext site — the [[ERPNext-Implementation-Guide|ERPNext guide]]'s add-on section installs it at `/helpdesk` (it does not require ERPNext, shares the site's login, and is free with unlimited agents; hosting from ~₹410/month — **VERIFY LIVE** on [Frappe Helpdesk pricing](https://frappe.io/helpdesk/pricing)). The `support@` alias forwards in natively; WhatsApp messages are logged as tickets by the person on the number (copy-paste with the dealer name and phone — thirty seconds) until a WhatsApp Business API integration does it automatically.

Why one queue matters more than which tool: **a conversation that lives only in a chat thread cannot be counted, assigned, escalated or learned from.** The queue is what turns support from a feeling ("we're drowning") into numbers (§11) and mechanisms (§5). The enemy is the **personal number** — every dealer who has a founder's personal WhatsApp will use it forever; from the first onboarding, the official number is the one printed, and messages to personal numbers get a polite forward-and-reply-from-the-queue, every time, until the habit moves.

## 3. Hours and the SLA table

An **SLA** (service-level agreement) is a published promise about response and resolution times. Publish one even though nobody is forcing you to, because it converts "we reply fast" into a number you can miss and fix. Two clocks shape VSYST's hours:

- **The business clock:** dealers work roughly 7 AM–9 PM, seven days — fuel does not take Sunday off. Support hours must cover the trading morning.
- **The product clock:** the rate-confirmation window runs **10 PM–6 AM**, and unconfirmed rates lock at 6 AM ([[00_OVERVIEW|Product Overview]]). The window's _edges_ — 9–11 PM when dealers set rates, 6–9 AM when disputes surface — are when "urgent" messages actually arrive.

A keepable v0, set by decision record and revised with evidence (illustrative — agree yours in exercise 13.2):

| Severity | First response (FRT) | Resolution target         | Coverage                                                      |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| P1 (§4)  | 30 min               | 4 hours, workaround first | 07:00–22:00 all days; P1s outside hours via the on-call phone |
| P2       | 2 working hours      | 1 working day             | 09:00–19:00 Mon–Sat                                           |
| P3       | 4 working hours      | 3 working days            | 09:00–19:00 Mon–Sat                                           |

Rules: **promise less than you can do, then beat it** — a 30-minute promise kept beats a 5-minute promise broken; the SLA clock runs from the message hitting _any_ official channel, not from when it was noticed; and the auto-reply outside hours states the hours and the P1 escalation path, so silence never reads as absence.

## 4. Triage — P1 to P3, and escalation to engineering

**Triage** is the first decision on every ticket: how bad, who owns it, what happens next. Three levels are enough:

| Level                                               | Definition                                                                                          | DZZLO examples                                                                                                                       | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **P1 — outage / money-trust broken**                | Many tenants blocked, or data that money depends on is wrong                                        | API down; OTP vendor outage blocking all deliveries; push notifications dead during the rate window; a ledger showing wrong balances | Support declares the incident, engineering is called (not messaged) immediately, status message goes to affected dealers within 30 min, incident timeline starts ([[12-product-and-engineering-operations\|lesson 12]]) |
| **P2 — one tenant blocked, or wrong but contained** | A dealer or customer cannot complete a core-loop step; a suspected calculation error on one account | One dealer cannot generate invoices; a customer's statement disagrees with the dealer's ledger; TCS applied unexpectedly             | Worked in queue order against the P2 SLA; engineering pulled in via a linked issue if the SOP's checks don't resolve it                                                                                                 |
| **P3 — how-to / cosmetic / request**                | Confusion, training gaps, feature asks                                                              | "Where do I see yesterday's rate?"; "Can the invoice show my logo?"                                                                  | Answered from macros/FAQ; feature asks logged to the complaint log (§10), never promised on the spot                                                                                                                    |

**Escalation to engineering is a protocol, not a shout.** A ticket crossing to engineering carries: tenant, user and phone; the exact action and error (screenshot); time window; what the SOP's checks already ruled out. It becomes a GitHub issue linked to the ticket, and _support keeps the dealer conversation_ — engineering fixes, support communicates. For P1s, the incident machinery from [[12-product-and-engineering-operations|lesson 12]] takes over: severity call, one owner, dealer-facing status updates on WhatsApp in plain language ("problem, what works meanwhile, next update at HH:MM"), and afterwards a **blameless postmortem** — summary, timeline, root causes, corrective actions with owners and dates — using [[T17-incident-postmortem|T17]]; the blameless discipline is Google SRE's, and it is what keeps people reporting problems instead of hiding them ([Google SRE — postmortem culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/); [incident.io — postmortem practices](https://incident.io/blog/sre-incident-postmortem-best-practices)). The postmortem's action items land back in this lesson: a new alert, a new macro, a new onboarding step.

## 5. The support SOP — and the five tickets every week brings

The support SOP ([[T25-customer-support-sop|T25]], written in [[T08-sop-template|T08]]'s standard shape) is the document that lets anyone competent run the queue: channels and hours, the SLA and severity tables, the triage decision tree, ticket fields (tenant, user, scope, severity, category), macros, escalation, and the weekly metrics review. Runbook practice gives the threshold for what goes in it: **troubleshoot the same problem twice and the steps belong in the document** ([Upstat — runbook vs SOP](https://upstat.io/blog/runbook-vs-sop)).

Seed it with the five tickets DZZLO's core loop generates — each mapped to first checks and a macro, so day one of the SOP already answers most of the week (details live in T25; this is the shape):

| Ticket                         | Where it comes from in the loop                                                                                  | First checks                                                                                                                      | Usual resolution                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **"OTP not received"**         | Driver-OTP delivery verification at dispatch; login OTPs on the invite flow (2Factor.in SMS)                     | Right phone number on the driver/user record? Vendor delivery status in the 2Factor panel? One user or many (many = possible P1)? | Correct the number and resend; vendor-side delay → wait-and-retry macro; systemic failure → escalate, and follow the SOP's documented fallback for confirming the delivery so the vehicle is not stranded                                       |
| **"Rate confirmation missed"** | The 10 PM–6 AM confirmation window; unconfirmed rates auto-lock at 6 AM                                          | Did the push arrive (OneSignal status)? Was the customer's app logged in? Which user scope tried to confirm?                      | Usually training — walk the customer's order-placer through where confirmation lives; repeated misses for one tenant → a success signal (§9), not just a ticket                                                                                 |
| **"Invoice mismatch"**         | Three-tier invoices (PRODUCT / CASH_REIMBURSE / GST), voucher allocation, TCS auto-trigger at the ₹50 lakh flags | Which invoice type is being compared to what? Payments allocated against the right invoices? Did TCS switch on mid-year?          | Most "mismatches" are a customer comparing a PRODUCT invoice against a total that includes reimbursements, or surprise TCS — explain from the ledger; a _real_ discrepancy is P2, goes to engineering, and is never hand-edited in the database |
| **"Invite / login issues"**    | Phone-OTP invite flow; user scopes (owner, order-placer, accountant, view-only)                                  | Invite sent to the right number? Already used? Is the person in the scope they think?                                             | Reissue the invite; fix the scope with the dealer's authorisation — scope changes are the dealer's call, not support's                                                                                                                          |
| **"Statement PDF"**            | Customer-side ledger and downloadable statements; month-end reconciliation                                       | Period requested? App version? Does the on-screen ledger load?                                                                    | Regenerate/resend; teach the monthly-statement path; recurring generation failures → engineering                                                                                                                                                |

**Macros speak the dealer's language.** Every macro has an English line and a Hindi line where natural (dealers forward these to their own staff), names the person replying, and never blames the user. T25 carries the canned texts; the rule here is that macros are living — the founder edits them weekly in the founder-first phase, because a macro that answers imprecisely generates the next ticket.

## 6. The knowledge base — an FAQ in the dealer's language

The knowledge base is the queue's pressure-release valve, and its build rule is the course rule: **asked twice = an FAQ entry.** Three properties matter more than polish:

1. **Vernacular first.** The dealer's accountant and the customer's order-placer read Hindi more comfortably than English; write each answer in Hindi with an English mirror, in the words dealers actually use (the domain-expert director reviews phrasing — the weekly knowledge slot from [[08-the-operating-cadence|lesson 08]] is where).
2. **WhatsApp-forwardable.** The unit of the knowledge base is not a web page; it is a **message** — a short text with numbered steps, a screenshot with two arrows, a 60-second screen-recording voice-over. Support answers a P3 by forwarding the asset, not retyping it. A simple public FAQ page (linked from the store listings) mirrors the same entries for the search-inclined.
3. **Born from tickets, reviewed monthly.** Every FAQ entry cites the ticket category it retires; the monthly support review (§11) checks which entries actually reduced repeats and which need rewriting. Start with the five tickets of §5 and the onboarding questions of §7 — that is the whole v0.

Internally, the master copy of every entry lives in the vault (source of truth, versioned); the forwardable renditions live on the support phone, organised so the person on the queue finds any asset in ten seconds.

## 7. Dealer onboarding — signup to first invoice in 14 days

Onboarding is where success is won or lost, and it has one number: **activation — the tenant has ≥1 customer linked and ≥1 invoice raised within 14 days of signup.** A dealer who reaches that point has replaced a piece of their old routine with DZZLO; a dealer who hasn't is a login, and logins churn silently. Activation is the classic controllable input metric — the thing you can act on this week that predicts the lagging numbers ([Commoncog — the Amazon WBR](https://commoncog.com/the-amazon-weekly-business-review/)); [[16-metrics-dashboards-and-scorecards|lesson 16]] builds VSYST's North Star on it.

The dealer-onboarding SOP (exercise 13.3 writes it into [[T08-sop-template|T08]]) is a checklist with one owner and day-stamps:

- [ ] **Day 0 — signup and setup:** KYC basics on file (GSTIN, PAN — the schema validates them); tenant created; products and rates configured; vehicles and drivers entered with the phone numbers OTPs will hit; the dealer's top 5 customers created with credit terms.
- [ ] **Day 0–1 — training at the counter:** one visit (or call for remote districts): set tonight's rate together, place a test order, run one dispatch with a live driver OTP, raise one real invoice, record one voucher. **The dealer's own staff drive; you narrate.** Leave the FAQ pack and the official support number saved in their phone.
- [ ] **Day 1 — first customer invited:** at least one real customer's order-placer invited through the phone-OTP invite flow, and rate confirmation demonstrated on the customer's phone.
- [ ] **Day 3 — check call:** rates being set nightly? First orders in? Blockers? (The commonest: customer phone numbers wrong, drivers not entered, an accountant who wasn't shown the ledger.)
- [ ] **Day 7 — usage check against the tenant's numbers:** rates set, orders, invoices. If zero invoices, diagnose honestly — training gap (fix on a call), data-entry hump (sit with them), or a mis-sold dealer who never intended to switch (a pipeline lesson for [[11-revenue-operations-and-partnerships|lesson 11]], logged).
- [ ] **Day 14 — activation verdict:** activated → into the regular success cadence (§9) with a congratulations message; not activated → one honest rescue conversation, then a decision — extend with a specific plan, or park them and record why.

Two design notes. The checklist runs on the _dealer's_ premises and clock — Raipur field reality, where the sale and the onboarding are often the same two visits ([[company/README|Company Playbook]]). And every failed activation is a defect report on this SOP: the fix lands as a new step or a new FAQ asset, not as "that dealer wasn't serious".

## 8. Customer-side onboarding — WhatsApp-first, zero-install

The customer side follows a different physics, fixed in the [[company/README|Company Playbook]]'s commandments: **"Dealer = powered-on side. Customer = WhatsApp-first, zero-install. The dealer carries the login; the customer gets the receipt."** And its guard-rail: **single-player value** — DZZLO must pay for itself for the dealer even if zero customers ever join the customer-side flow. Operationally that means:

- **Never gate dealer activation on customer adoption.** The Day-1 "first customer invited" step is a push, not a dependency; a dealer raising invoices with no customer app users is still activated.
- **The dealer is the customer's salesperson.** A transporter downloads an app because their fuel supplier says "confirm tonight's rate here or the morning trip waits" — not because VSYST asked. Support's job is to arm the dealer: a forwardable Hindi invite message, a 60-second "how to confirm your rate" video, the invite flow demonstrated at onboarding.
- **Zero-install first.** The customer's first experiences of DZZLO should require nothing: a statement PDF and rate message reach them on WhatsApp _from the dealer_. The app (phone-OTP invite, no passwords — [[00_OVERVIEW|Product Overview]]) is the upgrade path once they want to place orders and see the ledger themselves, and the multi-user scopes mean the owner, the order-placer and the accountant each get exactly the access the dealer intends.
- **Customer-side tickets are dealer-context tickets.** A confused transporter is logged under the dealer's tenant — because the fix (rate window training, a scope change, a re-invite) almost always routes through the dealer relationship.

## 9. The success cadence and churn signals

Success runs on two appointments inside [[08-the-operating-cadence|lesson 08]]'s rhythm — no new meetings:

- **Weekly — the new-tenant check (10 minutes, in the ops-meeting prep):** every tenant inside its first 60 days, against three columns — rates set this week, invoices this week, days since last activity. Anything red becomes a named to-do ("call dealer X today, script Y"), not a worry.
- **Monthly — the tenant health review (30 minutes, feeding the monthly business review):** all tenants on one sheet — invoices/month trend, rate-confirmation rate, active customer links, tickets, last contact. Classify green / amber / red; every red gets an owner and a play.

**Churn in this business is quiet.** A dealer rarely cancels; they drift back to the paper Daily Sales Register and Tally, and the app just goes still. So churn is caught from usage, not from announcements — the signals, and the save plays:

| Signal (from the tenant's own numbers)                     | What it usually means                                                                                      | The play                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rate confirmations dropping** week over week             | Customers stopped engaging, or the dealer stopped setting rates nightly — the loop's front door is closing | Call the dealer the week it shows: is the nightly habit broken (re-train, re-motivate) or did customers disengage (re-invite push, §8)?                        |
| **No invoices in 14 days** from a previously active tenant | The core loop has moved back to paper/Tally                                                                | An honest visit or call — what changed? A staff exit, a season, a missing feature, a bad experience? Fix what is fixable _this week_; log the cause either way |
| **Support silence** — no tickets, no messages, ever        | Not delight; disengagement. Active tenants generate questions                                              | A proactive monthly check-in message with one useful thing (a new FAQ video, a statement tip); measure who replies                                             |
| Repeated P2s or an unresolved money-trust issue            | Trust erosion — the dangerous one for a ledger product                                                     | The COO calls personally; the issue jumps the queue; the postmortem's fix is communicated back to the dealer by name                                           |

Close the loop with the [[company/README|Company Playbook]]'s monthly ritual: a **churn post-mortem** for every tenant lost or written off — what went wrong, what triggered it, what changes in the onboarding SOP, the product, or the pitch. One page, filed, read at the quarterly review.

## 10. The feedback loop — complaint log to roadmap

The [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]] gives the rule: keep a record of every complaint and request, because **patterns are your roadmap.** Mechanically:

1. **Every ticket carries a category and a product-area tag** (rate window, dispatch/OTP, invoices, payments/ledger, statements, invites/access, performance, feature-ask). Thirty seconds at close; the categories are the analysis.
2. **Feature-asks get logged, never promised.** The macro is honest: "logged, and here is how we decide" — the asking dealer's name attached, so the day it ships, they hear first (the cheapest loyalty programme there is).
3. **Monthly, the top themes get counted** — five minutes in the helpdesk dashboard — and the top three go, with ticket counts and dealer names, into the product conversation with the CEO/CTO. Volume is evidence, not a verdict: one P2 pattern that erodes ledger trust outranks ten cosmetic asks. The quarterly planning day ([[19-planning-okrs-and-the-quarterly-rhythm|lesson 19]]) reads the quarter's themes next to the pipeline's lost-deal reasons.
4. **The founder-to-associate hand-off is a staged mechanism**, triggered by the numbers in [[09-people-operations|lesson 09]]'s headcount plan (founder support hours > 10/week for 4 weeks, or > 25 active tenants — illustrative). Stage 1: the associate runs P3s from macros, founder shadows the queue daily. Stage 2: associate runs P2s from the SOP, founder reviews closures weekly. Stage 3: associate owns the queue and the metrics; the founder sees P1s, the weekly numbers, and the monthly themes. The SOP (T25) is what makes each stage a checklist rather than a leap of faith — and the founder _keeps_ reading the monthly themes forever, because that is the product's ear.

## 11. Measuring support — four numbers on the scorecard

Four numbers, as rows in [[T05-kpi-scorecard|T05]] / the `KPI Scorecard` tab, reviewed in the Monday ops meeting like every other row — on track or off track, exceptions to the issues list:

| Metric                                 | Definition                                                                                                                                | Source                  | Illustrative goal / RAG                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **FRT — first response time**          | Median time from ticket creation (any official channel) to first human reply, in coverage hours                                           | Helpdesk report         | Green ≤ 30 min · amber ≤ 2 h · red beyond                                                                          |
| **Resolution time**                    | Median time to resolved, by severity; plus % resolved within SLA                                                                          | Helpdesk report         | Green ≥ 90% within SLA                                                                                             |
| **CSAT**                               | "Did this solve your problem?" asked at closure — on WhatsApp a plain yes/no reply beats any survey link; score = % positive of responses | Helpdesk / manual tally | Green ≥ 85% positive, with the response rate watched alongside                                                     |
| **Tickets per active tenant per week** | Total tickets ÷ active tenants                                                                                                            | Helpdesk ÷ tenant count | Watch the _trend_: rising = product or training problem; near-zero with low usage = disengagement, not health (§9) |

Three habits make the numbers honest. **One owner enters them** (the person on the queue), Monday by 10:00, from the helpdesk's own reports — no metric without a source query (T05's rule). **Severity mix is read next to the totals** — twenty P3s are a training gap; three P2s on invoices are a fire. And **the numbers are for fixing, not for blaming**: the first month's red FRT is a baseline, and the fix is a macro, an alert or a rota — a mechanism, never "reply faster".

## 12. At VSYST — applying this now

- **This month, in order:** (1) the WhatsApp Business number on a company phone and the `support@` alias — published in the app footer, invoice PDFs and store listings; (2) Frappe Helpdesk on the ERPNext site per the [[ERPNext-Implementation-Guide|ERPNext guide]]'s add-on steps; (3) T25 v1 with the §5 five tickets and their macros; (4) the SLA auto-reply; (5) the four scorecard rows. Total cash cost: roughly the hosting you already pay — **VERIFY LIVE**.
- **Use the founder-first phase deliberately.** The technical founder answering tickets is currently the company's best product research; the change this lesson makes is that every answer now lands in the queue (counted) and leaves a macro or FAQ entry (reusable). The COO's role in this phase is _scribe and systematiser_ of the founder's answers — and owner of the hand-off stages in §10.
- **Onboarding is the domain-expert director's stage.** The training visit at the counter — rates, dispatch, the ledger conversation with the dealer's accountant — is thirty years of credibility doing the persuading; the COO's job is that the visit follows the checklist and the checklist absorbs what the visit teaches.
- **Watch the rate-window edges before adding staff.** Before hiring for coverage, measure when messages actually arrive (the helpdesk timestamps tell you); an on-call phone rotation between two people at the 6–9 AM edge may be the entire answer for months — the rota rule lands in [[12-product-and-engineering-operations|lesson 12]].
- **What not to build yet:** no WhatsApp Business API subscription before the volume trigger (§2); no public help-centre software — the vault, a FAQ page and forwardable assets are enough; no CSAT surveys by email (dealers won't answer them — the WhatsApp yes/no is the honest instrument); no chatbot until the FAQ it would serve exists and lesson 18's guardrails are in place.
