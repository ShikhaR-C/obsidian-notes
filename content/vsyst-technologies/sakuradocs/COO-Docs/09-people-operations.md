# 09 — People Operations

_Phase 3 · Build the Machine · Months 2–6. After this lesson you can write a role charter and a hiring plan with metric triggers, run a scorecard-first hiring process, issue an offer, appointment letter and contract that hold up in India, name the statutory thresholds VSYST crosses at 10 and 20 people, pick a payroll setup, publish the first six policies, and onboard, run and exit people on checklists instead of memory._

At three directors and no employees, "people operations" sounds like a problem for later. It is not. The first freelancer invoice triggers TDS; the first employee triggers an appointment letter that is now statutory; the tenth person triggers a POSH committee, ESI, gratuity and maternity obligations at once. **You want the mechanism in place before the trigger fires** — and the sibling [[startup-operations-plan|Startup Operations Plan]] adds the reason that matters more than law: _your first hires copy your behaviour, not your instructions._

## 1. People operations is a system, not "HR"

**People operations** is the set of mechanisms by which the company plans, hires, contracts, onboards, develops, pays and exits people — and stays legal while doing it. Grove's rule on where a manager's time goes applies directly: recruiting, training and 1:1s are the **highest-leverage** activities a manager has, because one hour changes someone else's whole year ([High Output Management — notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/high-output-management-andy-grove)). Slootman adds the bar: hire for demonstrated excellence and **leave a role open until you find it** ([Amp It Up — summary](https://manassaloi.com/booksummaries/2022/08/04/amp-it-up-slootman.html)).

## 2. Role charters and the hiring plan

**Write the seat before you meet the person.** EOS calls this the accountability chart — seats and roles, not names ([EOS Toolbox](https://www.rarebirdinc.com/the-eos-toolbox-six-components-that-work-together/)). A **role charter** ([[T29-org-chart-and-role-charters|T29]]) fits on one page: mission, 3–5 measurable outcomes for 12 months, KPIs, decision rights, reports to, backup. It stops the small-company failure where a hire "helps out" everywhere and owns nothing.

Three rules for the plan:

1. **Contractors before employees.** Pre-revenue, every fixed salary is runway. Start people on a defined scope; convert on evidence, through the same process as any hire.
2. **The hire that frees the founder.** The ops plan's rule — hire for whatever eats the most founder time that someone cheaper could do — is the whole strategy at this stage. For an agency that hire is the project manager; for DZZLO it is the **Support/Ops Associate** who takes tickets and dealer onboarding off the technical founder (lesson 10), then field sales, then a developer, then PM/CS (lesson 17 defends the order). Andy Sparks says the same of the founding COO: do the most important thing nobody has been hired for, hire or delegate someone into your place, move on ([Startup Grind](https://medium.com/startup-grind/what-s-a-coo-do-bbeb0f8648ee)).
3. **Hire on triggers, not hope.** Every row in [[T21-headcount-plan|T21]] carries a metric trigger; nobody is hired because the founders feel busy. Kruze's benchmark for the first _dedicated_ ops hire is roughly 10–12 employees ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/startup-operations-hire/)); until then the COO seat is the ops function.

An illustrative first 18 months (the `Headcount Plan` tab in [the workbook](toolkit/vsyst-coo-workbook.xlsx) computes monthly cost including statutory add-ons):

| Order | Role                           | Type first              | Trigger to open the role (illustrative)                                          | Frees whom                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Support/Ops Associate (Raipur) | Contractor → employee   | Founder support + onboarding hours > 10/week for 4 weeks, or > 25 active tenants | Technical founder — tickets, onboarding, WhatsApp |
| 2     | Field Sales (one district)     | Contractor / commission | Demo requests exceed founder capacity, or first-district pipeline > 30 leads     | Domain-expert director — visits, follow-ups       |
| 3     | Developer / QA                 | Contractor → employee   | Release cadence slipping two months running, or bug-backlog age > 30 days        | CEO/CTO — maintenance, tests                      |
| 4     | Ops Manager / Chief of Staff   | Employee                | Team ≥ 8–10, or the COO's calendar audit shows > 50% "doing"                     | COO — the doing column                            |

"Monthly cost incl. statutory" is not the CTC: add employer PF and ESI where they apply (§5), gratuity accrual, bonus if applicable, insurance and the laptop.

## 3. The hiring process — scorecard first

Copy Smart & Street's _Who_: before meeting anyone, write a **scorecard** — the role's **mission**, its **outcomes** (measurable, dated) and the **competencies** (including the company's values) — then run a structured process against it ([Who — excerpt](https://geoffsmart.com/books/who-the-a-method-for-hiring/who-the-book-excerpt/)); a16z uses the same "mission, outcomes, competencies" document for executive hires ([a16z](https://a16z.com/executive-hiring/)). [[T10-hiring-scorecard-and-process|T10]] holds the template, the question bank and the 1–4 rubric.

| Stage                | What happens                                                                               | Owner                | SLA (illustrative)     | Rule                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Scorecard            | Mission, 3–5 outcomes, competencies incl. values, salary band                              | Hiring manager + COO | Before sourcing        | No scorecard, no posting                      |
| Source               | Referrals first, then boards/LinkedIn/colleges; the JD is the scorecard in public language | COO                  | Weekly pipeline review | Every candidate in the tracker                |
| Screen               | 20-min call: motivation, logistics, two scorecard questions                                | Hiring manager       | Within 3 working days  | Score before the next stage                   |
| Structured interview | Same questions to everyone, mapped to competencies; interviewers score independently       | 2 interviewers       | 45–60 min              | No unstructured "let's chat" rounds           |
| Work sample          | A paid, real, 2–4 hour task from the actual job                                            | Hiring manager       | Reviewed within 2 days | The most predictive stage                     |
| References           | Two, called with the T10 script                                                            | Hiring manager       | Before offer           | Ask for names the candidate did not volunteer |
| Decision meeting     | Scores in writing _before_ discussion; decide the same day                                 | CEO + COO            | 30 min                 | A strong "no" with a stated reason blocks     |
| Offer                | Verbal, then written (§4)                                                                  | COO                  | Within 2 days          | Probation stated up front                     |

Work samples for VSYST's first roles write themselves: triage ten anonymised tickets and draft the replies (support); plan a week of dealer visits in one district and role-play the first (field sales); fix a small real bug with tests (developer). The ops plan's people realities apply throughout: **hire slow, fire fast**; **don't hire friends or family casually** — and if you must, say aloud that the arrangement ends if it isn't working.

## 4. Offer letter → appointment letter → contract

Three documents, in order, each a checklist in [[T11-offer-letter-and-contract-checklists|T11]] (checklists, not drafts — the lawyer drafts):

1. **Offer letter** — role, reporting line, CTC and structure, start date, probation, conditions, offer expiry.
2. **Appointment letter** — now a **statutory requirement in a prescribed format for every employee** under the Labour Codes ([KPMG — Labour Codes central rules](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-127.html); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/new-labour-codes)) — **VERIFY LIVE** the format and the Chhattisgarh rules with your labour consultant.
3. **Employment agreement** — designation; CTC with **basic at least 50% of "wages"** (§5); hours and leave; probation and notice; confidentiality; **IP assignment**; non-solicit; references to the POSH policy and code of conduct; background-check consent; Raipur jurisdiction.

The IP clause is not boilerplate for a software company. Under the Copyright Act s.17, **the employer owns work made under a contract of service — but a contractor owns what they create unless it is assigned in writing** (confirm wording with your lawyer). Every freelancer who has touched DZZLO code, design or copy needs a signed assignment — lesson 05 puts it in the company binder; this lesson makes it a step in every engagement.

**Employee or contractor?** The tests are control and integration; misclassification risk rises under the Codes, which add gig and platform-worker provisions ([TDSMAN — TDS/TCS rate chart FY 2026-27](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/03/tds-tcs-rate-chart-fy-2026-27/)). The operational differences:

| Item                        | Employee                                           | Contractor / freelancer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Documents                   | Offer + appointment letter + agreement             | Contractor agreement: scope, deliverables, fees, IP assignment, confidentiality, termination                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Tax on payment              | TDS on salary at slab (old s.192 → ITA 2025 s.392) | TDS **194J** 10% professional / 2% technical above ₹50,000 p.a., or **194C** 1% (individual/HUF) / 2% (others) above ₹30,000 single / ₹1,00,000 aggregate; **20% if no PAN** — **VERIFY LIVE** with the CA (sections renumbered under the Income-tax Act 2025) |
| GST                         | None                                               | If GST-registered, the invoice carries 18% GST; you claim ITC                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| PF / ESI / gratuity / bonus | Yes, once thresholds are crossed (§5)              | No — but see misclassification                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Policies                    | Per handbook                                       | Per contract; POSH covers anyone at the workplace                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| IP                          | Employer's by default (s.17)                       | **Only if assigned in writing**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

Deducted TDS is deposited by the **7th of the following month** and reported quarterly; the return forms were renumbered from Q1 FY 2026-27 (24Q → Form 138, 26Q → Form 140) ([TaxUpdate India](https://taxupdate.in/income-tax/811/new-tds-tcs-return-forms-fy-2026-27-form-138-140-143-144-due-31-july-2026/)) — **VERIFY LIVE**; the CA files, you ensure every contractor's PAN and invoice are on file before payment (lesson 06).

**E-sign everything.** Electronic signatures are recognised under the IT Act 2000 (s.3A, s.5), Aadhaar eSign is a notified method, and only a short list — wills, powers of attorney, property conveyances, most negotiable instruments — is excluded ([Leegality](https://www.leegality.com/blog/law-around-aadhaar-esign)). Indicative cost: Zoho Sign India from ~₹500/user/month plus ₹15–30 per Aadhaar eSign; Leegality ~₹10–40 per eSign at volume — **VERIFY LIVE** on the vendor pages ([itforsme — Zoho Sign India](https://www.itforsme.in/pricing/zoho-sign-india/)). Stamp duty is a separate, state-level question for the CS.

## 5. Statutory basics for India and Chhattisgarh — VERIFY LIVE, all of it

The **four Labour Codes** (Wages; Industrial Relations; Social Security; Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions) came into force on **21 November 2025**, with the **Central Rules under all four notified on 8 May 2026**; Chhattisgarh is listed among the states with final State Rules — **VERIFY LIVE** with your labour consultant ([KPMG Flash Alert 2026-127](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-127.html); [LKS — state rules tracker](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/rules)). The change that touches every CTC: a uniform definition of **"wages"** under which excluded allowances (HRA, overtime, bonus and the like) are **capped at 50% of total remuneration** — the excess is deemed wages, raising the base for PF, gratuity and bonus. So: basic at ≥50%. Other headline rules: 48 hours/week, overtime at 2×, a grievance committee at 20+ workers, annual health checks for staff aged 40+, crèche at 50+ ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/new-labour-codes)).

## 8. Onboarding and offboarding

**Onboarding** decides the first 90 days. The checklist ([[T12-onboarding-checklist|T12]]) has four blocks, each with an owner:

- [ ] **Pre-day-1:** signed offer / appointment letter / agreement; PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, previous Form 16; laptop imaged and encrypted; Google Workspace account and group aliases; Bitwarden vault; GitHub / AWS / Mongo / store access at least privilege, from the access list in [[T14-vendor-and-tool-register|T14]]; buddy named.
- [ ] **Day 1:** welcome by the CEO (30 min); policies acknowledged; tour of the vault (this course, the SOP index, the scorecard); the **first-week plan** in writing; 2FA on every account before lunch.
- [ ] **Week 1:** shadow the buddy on real work (support: sit on the queue; field sales: a day at the pump with the domain-expert director); one real task shipped by Friday; end-of-week 1:1.
- [ ] **30-60-90:** the role charter's outcomes split into three check-ins — at 30 days "can they do it with help?", at 60 "alone?", at 90 "can they improve it?" — with a short written note each time.

Security-minded IT onboarding lists say the same: accounts, device, MFA, policy acknowledgement on day one ([iFeeltech](https://ifeeltech.com/blog/new-employee-it-onboarding-security-checklist)). Measure **time-to-productivity** — days to the first unassisted outcome.

**Offboarding** ([[T13-offboarding-checklist|T13]]) mirrors it, and its first rule is _same-day access revocation_ — lean-team security guides treat immediate revocation as a baseline control ([Sola Security — 2026 guide](https://sola.security/blog/startup-lean-teams-security-guide/)). Then: device return and wipe; knowledge handover (their SOPs updated, tickets reassigned, shared logins rotated); full-and-final settlement with the CA (salary, leave encashment per policy, gratuity if applicable, TDS); a 20-minute exit interview, written down; announcement to the team and, for customer-facing roles, to the dealers they served. The ops plan's harder line belongs here: if your gut says no at week 6 it will still say no at month 6 — use the probation you wrote.

## 9. Running people: 1:1s, feedback and light reviews

The 1:1 is the highest-leverage meeting a manager has, and its agenda belongs to the report ([[T04-one-on-one-template|T04]]). Grove's rule for _how closely_ to manage is **task-relevant maturity**: someone new to a task needs structure and frequent checks; someone experienced needs goals and space — and the same person can be mature in one task and new in another ([High Output Management — notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/high-output-management-andy-grove)). Mochary adds the fixed cadence: 1:1s and the team meeting on the calendar every week, not when there is time ([The Great CEO Within — notes](https://www.nateliason.com/notes/the-great-ceo-within-matt-mochary)).

Feedback runs both ways, continuously. Doerr pairs OKRs with **CFRs — Conversations, Feedback, Recognition** ([Quantive](https://quantive.com/resources/articles/okrs-and-cfrs)). The **light performance review** for a team under ten is quarterly, 30 minutes, written, against the role charter's outcomes and the values in the scorecard: delivered, keep, change, support needed. Not a ranking; not tied to pay in the first year (lesson 19 says the same of OKRs). People numbers for the scorecard: open roles vs plan, time-to-hire, time-to-productivity, later eNPS and regrettable attrition — turnover is a classic COO KPI ([Cowen Partners](https://cowenpartners.com/coo-performance-metrics-how-to-measure-the-effectiveness-of-your-coo/)).

## 10. ESOP basics — for later, but know the shape now

You will not grant options in month two; you will be asked about them by the first serious hire. For a private company: options are issued under **s.62(1)(b)** of the Companies Act with **Rule 12** of the Share Capital & Debentures Rules; private companies may approve the scheme by **ordinary resolution**; file **MGT-14** within 30 days; **minimum one-year vesting**; promoters and directors holding over 10% are excluded — **except in DPIIT-recognised startups for ten years from incorporation** (VSYST is DPIIT-recognised; check the window with the CS); maintain the **SH-6** register; tax falls as a perquisite at exercise and capital gains on sale, with a deferral for eligible 80-IAC startups — **VERIFY LIVE** every element with the CS and CA, including the renumbered Income-tax Act 2025 section ([EquityList](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/esop-companies-act); [Startup Movers](https://www.startup-movers.com/blog/esop-for-founders-promoters)). Two habits from the Indian data: express grants in absolute rupee value, not only a percentage ([TheHireHub](https://www.thehirehub.ai/blog/how-to-hire-a-coo-india-2026)), and write the plan in plain words — an iSPIRT survey found 67% of employees never read theirs ([Qapita](https://www.qapita.com/in/blog/how-esops-are-powering-indias-startup-revolution)). The cap-table side is [[finance/08-phase-8-fundraising-and-cap-table|Finance Phase 8]].

## 11. Culture as mechanisms

Culture is what the mechanisms reward. Four that cost nothing: **values as competencies in every scorecard** (§3), so you hire for them; **rituals** — the weekly ops meeting ([[T03-weekly-ops-meeting-agenda|T03]]), the monthly all-hands with a recognition slot ([[T24-all-hands-agenda|T24]]), a Friday demo; **recognition** that names the behaviour, not just the result; and **writing things down** — the vault is where a new person learns how the company thinks. The rest is example, which is why the ops plan's warning about first hires is the real culture policy.

## 12. At VSYST — applying this now

- **Contractors first, papered properly.** Every freelancer who has touched code, design or copy signs a contractor agreement with IP assignment and confidentiality; PAN on file before the first payment; TDS under 194J/194C deducted and deposited by the 7th — the CA confirms sections and rates.
- **The first hire is the Support/Ops Associate in Raipur** — the hire that frees the technical founder from tickets and dealer onboarding (lesson 10). Write the charter into T29 and the trigger into T21 now, so the decision is made by a number later.
- **Below ten people the paperwork is still real:** appointment letters in the prescribed format; the POSH policy with the Local Committee route; ask the Raipur Labour Department about a Shops & Establishments certificate even below ten workers; confirm LWF and Professional Tax with the CA.
- **Payroll waits for the first employee;** contractor payments run through ERPNext's purchase cycle. Group health from Onsurity/Plum-style plans covers teams from three members (indicative entry price ~₹145/employee/month — **VERIFY LIVE**, [Onsurity](https://www.onsurity.com/blog/group-health-insurance-cost-per-employee-in-india/)); the insurance decision sits in lesson 13.
- **The office is inside the family's petrol pump.** The remote/hybrid policy says where work happens, and onboarding uses it: day two of any customer-facing hire is spent at the pump with the domain-expert director, watching a rate get set and a tanker dispatched.
- **Directors' own pay** follows lesson 06 and Finance Phase 7 — the same maker-checker as everyone else's.
